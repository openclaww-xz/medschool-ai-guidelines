# Run 26 Synthesis — National/International Framework Bodies: Who Leads, Who Follows, Who Is Vague

Scope: all 26 sources in `raw/national-frameworks/`, each read in full (see per-file notes in this directory). Bodies represented: AMA (5 docs), AAMC (3), ACGME (2), NBME/USMLE (2), FSMB (2), ACP (2), AMEE (2), plus AAP, AAFP, ACC, ACR–SIIM, LCME, Macy Foundation, WHO Europe (1 each).

## 1. The intellectual leaders (whose language others borrow)

**Tier 1 — AMA is the corpus's conceptual center of gravity.** Its Sept 2023 "ethics, evidence and equity" companion document is the earliest substantive education-specific framework in the corpus and supplied the vocabulary and architecture that recur everywhere else: the role-partitioned developer/deployer/educator responsibility model, the quintuple-aim transposition to education, the "must retain authority over high stakes decisions" clause, and the AI-for-education vs training-for-AI scope split (restated verbatim on its own ChangeMedEd hub and echoed by AAMC's principles framing). AMA also coined the corpus's stickiest framing devices: "Augmented Intelligence" (avoiding "artificial"), the "AI literacy as anatomy-and-physiology" analogy (Nov 2025), and the "precision education" vocabulary from its learning-analytics guide. Its CME Report 4-A19 and AI Principles give it the deepest formal policy machinery (HOD-adopted, multi-year). Evidence of outbound borrowing: AAMC principles and AMA hub cross-cite each other, but AMA's documents cite outward more (NAM, BEME 84, Academic Medicine competencies) and are cited by ChangeMedEd's canon — AMA functions as the field's aggregator-of-record through the Ed Hub / ChangeMedEd distribution channel.

**Tier 1 (parallel) — WHO, as the global source-code.** WHO/Europe's readiness report is downstream of WHO HQ's 2021 Ethics & Governance and 2023 Regulatory Considerations guidance, which remain the normative anchors that regional and national instruments (EU AI Act discourse included) reference. Within this corpus, WHO Europe contributes the only system-level governance vocabulary — hard/soft law, liability regimes, precertification, postmarket surveillance, drift monitoring ("Monitor AI systems continuously to detect bias, performance drift and potential harms"), data hubs, sandboxes — plus 41 citations and 50-country data. Its clause inventory is the imperative-densest in the corpus aimed at states. NBME's ethics framework explicitly cites the European Commission Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI — the same OECD/EU lineage WHO operationalizes.

**Tier 2 — NBME is the niche pioneer of assessment.** Its Feb 2023 USMLE ChatGPT post was the corpus's first document (construct-representativeness defense: "insufficient evidence to support the current claims that AI can pass the USMLE Step exams"), and its Mar 2024 AI-in-assessment executive summary is the most technically specific education document anywhere in the set (fine-tuning, F-score human-agreement, hallucination named and called "imperative" to address, "AI is never neutral or impartial," AI bias folded into "threats to validity"). When other bodies talk about assessment validity, they are talking in the space NBME opened.

**Tier 2 — AAFP as the must-clause pioneer.** Its Oct 2023 eight-principle policy has the densest inventory of "must" statements in the corpus (12+), nearly all aimed at vendors: training-data transparency, implicit-bias processes, differential-outcome monitoring, liability acceptance under an FDA/ONC-style risk model. The "dyad expanded to a triad with AI" and quintuple-aim anchoring predate and prefigure AMA/AAMC/FSMB language. It is the clearest case of a body whose language (vendor obligations, clinician-accountability, triad) others absorbed.

**Tier 2 — ACP as the ethics-completeness leader.** Its 2024 AI position paper (~12.7k words) and 2026 ethics/professionalism paper (~6.7k words) are the corpus's most comprehensive clinical-ethics treatments — oversight, equity, privacy, liability, patient-physician relationship, with position-paper rigor and full citations. Others borrow the structure (principles → policy statements → recommendations); ACP executes it at full length.

## 2. Followership (adopting others' frames competently)

- **AAMC** — progressive-structured institutional voice whose seven principles (2025 v2.0) are governance-clean but technically thin (no hallucination/drift/assessment-validity content; one citation; disclosed ChatGPT-4.0 first draft). It borrows AMA's ethics/evidence/equity triad spirit and micro/meso/macro layering is its one original contribution. Its GIR survey, however, gives it the field's best empirical product (69% education policies vs 51% clinical; 74% governance programs) — follower in vocabulary, leader in data.
- **FSMB** — the most consequential follower: its May 2024 House-of-Delegates-adopted recommendations (Ethics and Professionalism Committee, Woodland chair) and full report translate AMA/AAFP ethics vocabulary into state-board-enforceable form ("requiring continuing medical education focused on AI in healthcare"). It adds regulatory teeth, not new concepts.
- **ACGME** — two years of sophisticated plenaries (2024: deskilling, "humans suck at vigilance," professional identity; 2026: agentic AI in the GME office with ROI metrics) and zero published requirements. It borrows the stage's ideas and operationalizes them internally; institutionally it follows.
- **ACC** — its 2025 RFI comments borrow the assistive-vs-autonomous and clinician-center vocabulary but contribute two genuinely original legal-economic arguments: no AI should be presumed the "standard of care" (liability-deviation asymmetry), and reimbursement economics. A follower with two novel clauses.
- **Macy Foundation** — not a vocabulary maker but the field's evidence funder: RCT-mandated demonstration projects, open release of curricula, and the sharpest evidence claim on any short page ("multiple studies now show that high-performing artificial intelligence technologies don't always improve doctor performance"). It funds what the leaders assert.

## 3. The vague (gestural, pre-normative, or empty)

- **AAP** — thinnest document in the corpus: a webinar hub page with zero normative clauses and "potential" in nearly every line. Awareness-stage dissemination, no policy.
- **LCME** — not vague in diagnosis (AI's impact on "medical education, assessment, and healthcare delivery" is its #2 strategic challenge) but vague in output: after June-2024 challenge identification, the only drafted text is one clause in revised Element 7.2 ("appropriate use of artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies in diagnosis and patient management"), in public comment to Jan 2026. The only body with binding power, moving at accreditation speed.
- **AAMC principles (as a text)** — see above; vague on technology specifics even as it leads institutionally.
- **NBME guidelines** — promised ("as our work to develop a set of ethical guidelines moves forward"), not yet issued in these documents.
- **USMLE examinee rules** — the Feb 2023 post sets no rules about examinee AI use at all.

## 4. Cross-cutting findings

1. **A two-tier intellectual economy.** Concept generation concentrates in AMA + WHO (+ AAFP's must-contract, NBME's psychometrics, ACP's completeness); everyone else adopts. The borrow-direction is visible in citation networks: AMA hub → AAMC principles + BEME 84; NBME → EU Commission + Biden EO; FSMB report → AMA/AAFP framing; WHO Europe → WHO HQ + EU law.
2. **The education/clinical governance inversion** (AAMC GIR): education has MORE policy than clinical missions (69% vs 51%) — the frameworks are ahead of practice in exactly the domain this corpus governs.
3. **Assessment validity is the least-governed frontier.** Only NBME/USMLE engage it substantively; LCME names it as a challenge but has drafted nothing; AMA/AAMC/AMEE treat assessment as a privacy or fairness context, not a construct-threat. AMEE Guide 178 is the sole sustained international treatment.
4. **Must-density vs enforceability are inversely distributed.** The bodies that could bind (LCME, FSMB-through-boards) publish the fewest/softest clauses; the bodies with no enforcement (AAFP, AMA companion doc, WHO) publish the most "must" statements. AAFP's musts land on vendors who never agreed to them; WHO's imperatives land on states that mostly don't comply (64% have no AI health policies at all).
5. **Hallucination is named only by assessment bodies** (NBME "imperative"; USMLE's "misinformation"); drift only by WHO (and AAFP's "continually adopt latest best practices" gesture); agentic AI only by ACGME-2026; ambient scribes only by AAMC-GIR (DAX) and Macy (Communication Compass); the standard-of-care/liability-deviation problem only by ACC. No single body integrates all failure modes — the corpus's knowledge is fragmented by specialty.
6. **Timeline of ideas:** USMLE (Feb 2023, defense) → AAFP (Oct 2023, vendor contract) → AMA companion (Sept 2023, education framework) → FSMB (May 2024, regulation) → NBME (Mar 2024, assessment internals) → ACP (2024, full ethics) → AAMC principles (2025, institutional) → WHO Europe (2025, system readiness) → AMA literacy policy + LCME Standard 7 (2025-26, mandates emerging). The arc runs from psychometric defense to institutional governance to regulatory mandate — and the mandate stage is only now beginning (LCME in comment; FSMB CME requirement; AMA advocating for recognition in standards).
