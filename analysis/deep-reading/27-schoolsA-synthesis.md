# Run 27 — Deep-Read Synthesis: Pioneer-Candidate Schools, Batch A

Thirteen school-policy files fully read (15,579 words of source). Per-file notes in this directory mirror source filenames. Ranking below runs from most to least pioneering. "Pioneer" = permissive-by-default learning posture + real tool investment or assessment redesign + technical/evidentiary sophistication; "conservative-naive" = prohibition-default, generic clauses, no tooling, no evidence of understanding the technology.

## Spectrum Ranking

### 1. UVA SOM — MD Program Policy on Generative AI — **pioneer**
Most pedagogically and operationally fused policy in the batch. Two-tier tool taxonomy with contractual "safe harbor" concept; named current inventory (CoPilot, Google AI/Gemini Notebook, Dax CoPilot, small-scale Claude pilot, OpenEvidence, UpToDate Expert AI, DeepSeek, Perplexity, Elicit, Mapify); AI-assisted vs AI-generated disclosure-threshold calibration; the corpus's best de-identification clause ("Reliable de-identification is harder than it looks—metadata, rare diagnoses, and identifiable features can still identify a patient"); UWorld/Anki IP clause aimed at real student behavior; six worked acceptable-use scenarios that operationalize the rules. Supportive opening framing ("supports medical students to use generative AI... to augment learning"). Updated July 2026. Capped only by absence of a formal assessment-classification scheme and university-guidance precedence.

### 2. UCSF PharmD — AI in Assessments and Deliverables — **pioneer**
The assessment-redesign benchmark: a mandatory four-level classification taxonomy (AI-1 Disallowed → AI-4 Unregulated) with instructor blanket or per-item classification, default-to-AI-1, submission-as-attestation clauses, and a documentation appendix with citation template and worked example. Externally evidence-cited (Sentient Syllabus, UNESCO, Monash). Anticipated embedded AI ("AI products are increasingly integrated into standard software packages") in Oct 2023. Strong pedagogical rationale ("Learning is not simply memorization of facts—it is building flexible knowledge structures"). No secure deployment of its own — the only gap vs #1.

### 3. Icahn (Mount Sinai) — Using AI in Teaching, Learning, and Discovery — **pioneer**
Widest governance scope (education + research + discovery) with a standing AI Committee; dual enterprise tools (ISMMS Gemini + ChatGPT Edu instance); mandatory syllabus AI-level statements with "full/limited/no use" template language plus a six-category permitted-use menu (credited to University of Wales); anti-detection stance with licensed iThenticate alternative; research-infrastructure depth no peer shows (Minerva cluster, Mount Sinai Data Warehouse/Epic extraction, re-identification risk, FAVES principles, IRB protocols describing AI methods, Mentor–Learner Agreement instrument). Sophisticated data epistemics: licensed platforms still require terms/consent/risk review; training-restriction settings "change regularly and cannot be relied upon." Consultative pre-approval default (discuss with director before non-explicitly-permitted use) is less permissive than UVA/Stanford defaults.

### 4. Stanford SoM — MD Handbook 3.32 GenAI Policy — **pioneer**
Named secure deployments (Stanford Healthcare Secure GPT; Stanford AI Playground) plus risk-classification links; enforcement realism unique in the corpus ("restricting the use of AI tools cannot be strictly enforced... students will be held accountable for demonstrating competency... without the aid of AI tools"); strictest citation spec (tool, model/version, date, query, output excerpt); anonymized-input worked example vs progress-note counterexample; "discouraged" (not prohibited) default for evaluated activities; curriculum-integrated AI literacy; covers MSPA. Updated Aug 2025.

### 5. UNC GMB — Student GenAI Usage Guidance (Jul 2023) — **pioneer (vintage caveat)**
The most permissive coursework default in the batch ("In principle, you may submit material that contains AI-generated content... if this use is properly documented") under an inevitability philosophy ("society's acceptance and use of generative AI is inevitable"); eight assignment-type-specific rules; attestation + usage-table disclosure instrument with conversation links; anti-fabrication clause on AI images resembling scientific data; disclosed ChatGPT drafting; blunt accountability ("If you cannot verify it as factual, you should delete it"). No tooling (2023-era) and PhD-program scope keep it below the four above despite its design courage.

### 6. UNC OMSE — Artificial Intelligence Policy (Jul 2026) — **progressive-structured (near pioneer)**
Default-permissive allow-listed learning uses (differentials, study aids, exam prep, brainstorming) — one of only two inverted defaults in the batch; every restriction competency-milestone-tagged (MK/PC/PBL/IC/PR/SBP); UNC PromptLab institutional gateway + CoPilot named; grammar/punctuation carve-out vs citation-manager disclosure nuance; unique AI-meeting-summary consent clause (Zoom). Lacks deployment depth, external citations, and a classification scheme.

### 7. Perelman (Penn) — UME GenAI Policy (UME.AC.119, Jul 2026) — **progressive-structured**
The most administratively complete artifact: numbered policy, 16 named contributors, annual review, reporting channels with anti-retaliation, AAMC checklist grounding, PMACS approved-tools matrix, equity clause (assignments must not disadvantage learners without premium tools), AI-detector discouragement on accuracy + FERPA grounds, disclosed ChatGPT Edu/CoPilot drafting. Held below progressive-frontier by its prohibition-default student core ("not permitted... unless explicitly authorized") and no permissive learning-use statement or assessment taxonomy.

### 8. U-M Medical School — Acceptable AI Use Guideline — **progressive-structured**
Pioneer-grade infrastructure: homegrown U-M GPT / Maizey / GPT Toolkit approved for HIGH-classification data incl. PHI with no-training/no-external-sharing guarantees; data-classification regime; mandatory AI training course (UMHS-10001); named author (Deputy CIO) + CISO endorsement; live tool blocking; DS-18 AI-code standard. But it is an IT workforce guideline, not an education policy: no pedagogy, citation format, or assessment thinking; academic use wholly deferred to instructors.

### 9. Geisel (Dartmouth) — GenAI Educational Environments (UME-CNTRL-0019) — **cautious-informed**
Best-documented governance lineage (draft→MEC→DAB→Faculty Council, all dated); AAMC principles reproduced verbatim; LCME 7.4/7.7 quoted; disclosed GPT-4.1-mini first draft; instructor-side AI encouragement. But student substance is conventional prohibition-default; definitions technically sloppy ("OpenAI" as a tool example, "HIPPA"); no tools, no assessment redesign.

### 10. USC Keck — Medical Student GenAI Tools Policy (Dec 2025) — **cautious-informed**
Current, committee-approved (MECC), coherent minimal policy covering the standard clause set (prohibition-default for evaluated work, citation, verification, PHI, curricular-material IP, journal policies), uniform across pre-clerkship/clerkship/post-clerkship; violations tied to Code of Professional Behavior. No permissive learning-use statement, no tools, no pedagogy — competent containment.

### 11. Yale — Standards for Using AI Tools in Health Sciences — **cautious-informed (provisional; incomplete capture)**
University-level health-sciences guidance stub (~250 words of operative capture): PHI-focused, references "Clarity, the University-supported AI platform" and a clinical-decision-support-vs-platforms distinction, but all substantive standards sit behind uncaptured accordions. Underlying substance likely higher; rated on what is captured. Re-crawl recommended.

### 12. Duke — Guidelines and Tools (AI at Duke) — **cautious-informed (provisional; hub page)**
University hub page, not a SoM policy: "unauthorized use of generative AI is considered academic misconduct" per Duke Community Standard with instructor opt-in; real support ecosystem exists behind links (CTL assignment-design guidance, AI Ethics Learning Toolkit, Co-Lab, Coursera) but zero clinical/PHI/HIPAA content in the artifact. SoM-specific depth may exist elsewhere; this capture shows only scaffolding.

## Batch Patterns

- **Tool investment is the strongest pioneer separator**: UVA (Dax/Claude pilot), UCSF (Azure ChatGPT Enterprise, PHI-approved), Stanford (Secure GPT + AI Playground), Sinai (Gemini + ChatGPT Edu + Minerva), U-M (GPT/Maizey high-data tier), Penn (PMACS matrix). Every top-5 policy names at least one provisioned platform.
- **Assessment taxonomy is the second separator**: UCSF PharmD (AI-1..4), Sinai (six-category menu + mandated syllabus statements), UNC GMB (eight assignment types) — the three policies that restructure the assessment contract rather than prohibit.
- **Anti-AI-detector stances** appear at Penn and Sinai (FERPA/accuracy grounds) — an evidence-informed position entirely absent from cautious policies.
- **AI-assisted policy drafting disclosed by four schools** (Penn — ChatGPT Edu/CoPilot; Geisel — GPT-4.1-mini; UNC GMB — ChatGPT; indirectly Stanford via university guidance) — transparency about the policy's own provenance correlates with higher sophistication.
- **Default polarity splits roughly even**: allow-by-default for learning (Stanford, UNC OMSE, UVA) vs prohibit-unless-authorized (Penn, Keck, Geisel, Sinai's consultative middle). Polarity does not track tool investment — Penn and Sinai pair prohibition-consultative defaults with elite infrastructure.
- **De-identification sophistication escalates with rank**: UVA (metadata/rare-diagnosis re-identification), Sinai (EHR-warehouse re-identification), Stanford (worked anonymization example) vs simple "no PHI" statements at the cautious end.
- **Two incomplete captures** (Yale, Duke) are both university-level pages; both flagged for re-crawl before final corpus classification.
