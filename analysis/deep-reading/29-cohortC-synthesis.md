## Regional/state-school patterns vs private elites
1. **The sophistication center of gravity is NOT the private elites.** The two most complete documents in this cohort are from small/public state schools (JABSOM, UVM) or state systems (Utah, UAMS, OHSU). Feinberg (the one elite private) is excellent per-word but minimal — 5 clauses, no definitions, no governance. Elites wrote early (Feinberg 9/2023) and concise; state schools wrote later (2025–2026) and encyclopedic.
2. **Lineage flows downhill and sideways, not from the top.** LSUHSC-NO cites McGovern (a *center-level* policy), Feinberg, UCSF, UVA, UAB. OHSU GME-48 credits **UT Health San Antonio** — a state school — as its source. The diffusion network is state-school-to-state-school; "elite origin" is not the dominant propagation pattern. MUSC openly adapts an academic-publishing scale (Perkins et al. AIAS) under CC license and is itself being mirrored (Marshall University hosting).
3. **State statutory environments shape state-school policies visibly**: Utah GRAMA records law + Chief AI Officer; Nevada NSHE + State AI Policy + DIR-certified training (TTUHSC analog: Texas DIR AI literacy mandate); SLU's GDPR/DPO (Madrid campus); Vermont state breach law embedded in UVM's taxonomy; Hawai'i statutes and Native-Hawaiian values framing at JABSOM. Private Feinberg has no statutory surface at all. State schools carry compliance plumbing the privates skip.
4. **Enterprise-first vs school-first maturity**: state flagships (Utah, UAMS, Kentucky CATS AI, TTUHSC) build university/system-wide governance (AI committees, tool inventories, impact assessments) that med-school policy then references; the private elite (Feinberg) builds only the med-school layer. Counter-pattern: Utah SOM's own handbook clause (one prohibition-default paragraph) lags its own university's pioneer guideline by miles — institutional maturity ≠ curricular propagation.
5. **Institutional self-scrutiny is a state-school phenomenon**: LSUHSC-NO and JABSOM both disclose ChatGPT-assisted drafting of the policy itself; OHSU logs a version table with a governance-committee amendment. No elite-private document does either.

## GME vs UME depth
1. **GME policies are operationally deeper per clause.** OHSU GME-48 and UKY GME cover terrain UME policies never touch: AI scribe patient consent + documentation of consent; milestone-gated program restriction of trainee AI use; resident applicant screening controls (OHSU: allowed with institutional approval; UKY: never independently screen/score/rank; both name or imply tools — UKY names Thalamus Cortex); CCC/evaluation-data protection; AI-assisted documentation with mandatory faculty oversight; DIO escalation paths.
2. **UME policies are deeper on assessment/integrity mechanics**: disclosure statement templates (OHSU PA, JABSOM, UNR), citation formats (McGovern, UAMS), phase-differentiated permission matrices (LSUHSC-NO preclerkship vs clerkship), logging-evidence procedural fairness (JABSOM), curricular-material IP protection (JABSOM, Feinberg, LSUHSC-NO), MSPE disclosure cascade (JABSOM faculty-comment summarization).
3. **The clinical-documentation line is the shared spine but is drawn differently**: EHR-boundary bans on external AI note generation appear in UME (Feinberg, LSUHSC-NO clerkship, UVM, JABSOM via clinical-reasoning ban, OHSU PA transcription-only) and GME (UKY, OHSU with faculty-review allowance). GME trusts residents with approved ambient documentation under supervision; UME mostly forbids student generation outright (OHSU PA) or permits only EHR-embedded function (Feinberg). Clinical-reasoning firewalls for students (no AI differentials/plans: JABSOM, OHSU-PA discouragement) contrast with MUSC placing "Practice Clinical Decision-Making" in its second-most-permissive tier — the widest philosophical spread in the cohort.
4. **Only JABSOM unifies UME+GME** in one learner policy; everywhere else GME and UME are separate instruments with separate enforcement (ERRC vs CCC/DIO), and GME policies are newer (2026) than their UME siblings.
5. **Detection-tool stance is the sharpest generational marker**: pro-detection (McGovern/Turnitin, 2023-vintage) → skeptical-but-permitted-with-FERPA-deidentification (JABSOM, SLU) → outright evidentiary limits (Utah "not conclusive evidence"; UAMS "inaccurate and unreliable" + committee approval before any grading-adjacent AI). The 2023 adopters still read conservative-naive; the 2025–26 state-school documents treat detection as a due-process problem.

## Cross-cutting findings
- Permission default is now the norm: syllabus-grants-permission (Utah SOM, SLU, Feinberg) or enumerated-default rules (JABSOM Appendix A, LSUHSC-NO phases, MUSC tiers); pure prohibition with no permitted uses survives only in the thinnest documents (UF COM).
- Curricular-material IP has become a standard protected class (JABSOM PBL cases; Feinberg slides/feedback; LSUHSC-NO exam questions; OHSU-PA curricular-study carve-out).
- Governance committees are the state-school signature (OHSU AI Governance Committee, UAMS Chancellor's AI Governance Committee w/ 24h reporting, TTUHSC President's Cabinet committee, Utah OAI) — privates route through curriculum committees instead.
