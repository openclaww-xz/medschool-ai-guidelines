# AAFP — Ethical Application of Artificial Intelligence in Family Medicine

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: American Academy of Family Physicians. A formal policy document ("the AAFP sets forth an initial set of principles that we believe must be followed"). Reference line "(July 2023) (October 2023 COD)" indicates **Congress of Delegates adoption, Oct 2023** — early among specialty societies. No drafting committee named.
- Powers claimed: speaks for family medicine; principles addressed **to AI/ML vendors ("Companies") more than to physicians** — nearly every "must" lands on the developer. It binds AAFP advocacy positions; it aspires to bind industry conduct. Audience includes practices deciding whether to adopt tools.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **conditional embrace with vendor obligations** — adoption is desirable only if an eight-principle contract is met. Framing quotes:
  - "AI/ML has the potential to support family medicine by supporting the **four C's of primary care (first contact, comprehensiveness, continuity and coordination of care)** and enhancing capacity and extending capabilities."
  - "**AI/ML should be evaluated with the same rigor as any other tool utilized in health care.** Because AI/ML has the potential to profoundly disrupt the practice of medicine and the patient experience at scale..."
  - "...principles that we believe **must be followed** in the domain of AI/ML if they are to be applied in family medicine."

## (c) Complete clause inventory — the eight principles (verbatim normatives)
1. **Preserve and Enhance Primary Care**: "As the patient-physician dyad is expanded to a **triad with AI**, the patient-physician relationship **must at the minimum preserved, and ideally, enhanced**. When AI/ML is applied to primary care, it **must enhance the 4 C's** of primary care and expand primary care's capacity and capability to provide longitudinal care that achieves the **quintuple aim**."
2. **Maximize Transparency**: "AI/ML solutions **must provide transparency to the physician and other users** so that the solution's efficacy and safety can be evaluated. **Companies must provide transparency around the training data** used to train the models. Companies **should provide clear, understandable information describing how the AI/ML solution makes predictions**. Ideally, this would be for each inference but at least provide a conceptual model for decision-making, including the importance of data leveraged for the inference."
3. **Address Implicit Bias**: "**Companies providing AI/ML solutions must address implicit bias in their design.** We understand implicit bias cannot always be completely eliminated. Still, the company **should have standard processes in place to identify implicit bias** and to mitigate the AI/ML models from learning those same biases. In addition, when applicable, companies **should have processes for monitoring for differential outcomes, particularly those that affect vulnerable patient populations**."
4. **Maximize Training Data Diversity**: "To maximize the generalizability of AI/ML solutions, **training data must be diverse and representative** of the populations cared for by family medicine. **Companies must provide clear documentation on the diversity of their training data.** Companies **should also work to increase the diversity of their training data** to not increase or create new health inequities."
5. **Respect the Privacy of Patients and Users**: "It is critical for patients and physicians to **trust companies will maintain confidentiality** of data from them. **Companies must provide clear policies around how they collect, store, use and share data** from patients and end-users. **Companies must get consent for collecting any identifiable data** and the consent **should clearly state how the data will be used or shared**."
6. **Take a Systems View in Design**: "an AI/ML solution... **must be designed to be an integrated component of the system**... the company **must understand how the AI/ML solution will be used within a workflow**. The company **needs to have a user-centered design approach**. Since the vast majority of AI/ML solutions in health care **will not be autonomous**, the company **must understand and leverage the latest science around human/AI interaction** as well as quality assurance."
7. **Take Accountability**: "the **company must take accountability for assuring the solution is safe**. For those solutions design for use in direct patient care, **they must undergo a similar rigorous evaluation as any other medicine intervention**. We also believe that **companies should take on liability where appropriate**, based on a **risk-based model** that accounts for the role played by AI/ML and the situation... A good starting point for such a risk-model is the **Food & Drug Administration (FDA) and Office of the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology (ONC) framework for software as a medical device**."
8. **Design for Trustworthiness**: "**Companies must implement policies and procedures** that ensure the above principles are appropriately addressed. Companies **must strive to have the highest levels of safety, reliability and correctness**... Companies **should consider how they can maximize trust... throughout the entire product lifecycle**. AI/ML will continue its rapid advancement, so **companies must continually adopt the latest state of the art best practices**."
Context clauses: "Family physicians also **mitigate health inequity, including systemic racism**, by collaborating with community stakeholders"; AI "can be leveraged to help achieve the **quintuple AIM** if applied appropriately."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Technology understanding: solid for 2023 — training-data transparency, differential-outcome monitoring, human/AI interaction science, non-autonomy assumption, product-lifecycle trust. No hallucination/drift/agentic vocabulary, but "continually adopt the latest state of the art best practices" gestures at drift.
- Evidence: cites FDA/ONC software-as-medical-device framework as risk-model starting point — the only corpus document pointing at a concrete regulatory scaffold.
- Mandates: **the densest "must" inventory relative to length in the corpus** (12+ must-statements) — but all aimed at companies, unenforceable by AAFP. It is a demand document, not a rule.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- **Dyad→triad reframing** ("patient-physician dyad is expanded to a triad with AI") — rhetorically distinctive, since borrowed by others.
- Quintuple aim anchoring; four C's as the test of acceptable AI — primary-care-specific value frame.
- Vendor-directed obligations (training-data documentation, liability acceptance) go further than AMA/AAMC; anticipates FSMB liability theme and ACC's clinician-autonomy theme.
- Equity framed as anti-racism practice ("systemic racism") — strongest equity language among US bodies.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** — early (Oct 2023), dense, specific, risk-model-aware; embrace contingent on an explicit vendor contract. Not pioneer only because its conceptual vocabulary (transparency, bias, accountability) is the standard ethics set rather than novel; but its must-density makes it one of the most demanding texts in the corpus.
