# AAMC GIR — Executive Summary: Generative AI in Academic Medicine (2024 MSIT & 2025 SnippIT surveys, pub. Dec 2025)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: AAMC Group on Information Resources (GIR). "Prepared by the GIR Steering Committee, December 2025." A survey-based executive summary from AAMC's IT-leadership constituent group, addressed to "medical school leadership" in the US and Canada.
- Powers claimed: none binding; it is intelligence plus "strategic imperatives." GIR is a professional-services arm, not a standards body — but its data (response base = medical schools) makes it the field's de facto state-of-adoption census.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **governance-first embrace** — AI adoption is assumed; the work is institutional control. Framing quotes:
  - "Proactive, informed leadership in developing robust governance, fostering a skilled workforce, and strategically integrating AI tools will be crucial to leveraging this technology responsibly and effectively."
  - Describes the field as taking "a proactive stance on the responsible integration of AI."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
Descriptive findings (survey facts, quoted for the record):
- "A significant **69% of medical schools report having formal AI policies or guidelines for education**, reflecting a proactive stance on the responsible integration of AI. Clinical missions follow at 51%, indicating an ongoing development curve in patient-facing areas."
- "Formal governance programs to review and approve AI tools are in place at **74% of institutions**, often leveraging existing IT oversight committees while incorporating specialized considerations for the security, privacy, ethical implications, and performance of genAI."
- "Nearly half (**48%**) of medical schools have a five-year strategic plan for AI integration. These plans aim to embed AI capabilities responsibly across all operations, preserve data integrity, and address health disparities through an equity-focused approach."
- "Over 50% of schools provide AI tools for administration, education, and research, with **Microsoft Copilot being the most frequently adopted and supported tool** across all missions. Other commonly used platforms include OpenAI (ChatGPT/GPT-4) and Zoom AI Companion."
- "Specialized tools are also gaining traction, such as **Epic AI Tools, Nuance DAX Copilot for clinical documentation**, and various research-focused AI platforms."
- "**78% of medical schools offer genAI user training** (workshops, resources, introductory sessions); 74% cover ethical use, and 56% provide prompt engineering guidance."
- Mission adoption detail: administration (recruitment, meeting transcription, document prep, legal workflows, policy compliance, event planning); education ("curriculum design, exam authoring, clinical simulations, tutoring, providing feedback on student documentation, and enhancing accessibility"); clinical ("clinical note-taking, **ambient scribing**, diagnostics (e.g., **Viz.AI for stroke detection**), patient communication, radiology imaging, and clinical simulation"); research ("coding, data analysis, image de-identification, research administration, literature review, and simulation-based training").

Normative imperatives (the "Key Imperatives" — the actual directive content):
1. "**Prioritize Comprehensive Governance**: Beyond policies, establishing robust, multidisciplinary governance structures is crucial. This includes formal risk assessment models, **audit capabilities for AI outputs**, and transparent processes for vetting new AI tools to ensure safety, ethics, and regulatory compliance."
2. "**Invest in Dedicated AI Leadership and Expertise**: Many schools are establishing roles like **Associate Dean of AI in Medicine or Director of Clinical AI**. Administrators should ensure that these roles are supported by appropriate qualifications (e.g., an MS in AI, an MD, or a PhD in relevant fields) to drive strategic initiatives."
3. "**Champion Workforce AI Literacy and Training**: A substantial majority of schools (78%) offer introductory AI training... **Sustained investment in these programs is vital** to prepare faculty, staff, and students for the evolving demands of an AI-augmented environment."
4. "**Facilitate Secure Experimentation**: While caution is warranted, 41% of schools provide secure platforms for researchers to experiment with deidentified data. **Encouraging such sandboxes** can accelerate innovation while mitigating risks associated with sensitive data."
5. "**Address Core Concerns Proactively**: Administrators **must actively manage top concerns, including data privacy and security, the accuracy of AI outputs, potential bias, and regulatory compliance**. Clear communication channels (email, website, workshops) for AI policies are essential."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Technologically current: names ambient scribing (DAX Copilot), specific diagnostics (Viz.AI stroke), enterprise tools (Copilot), prompt engineering as a training category. Understands the education-specific uses including "exam authoring" and "feedback on student documentation" — touching the assessment-integrity surface without problematizing it.
- Evidence: entirely self-generated survey data — the strongest empirical grounding in the corpus for adoption claims.
- Mandates: one genuine "must" (#5 "Administrators must actively manage...") plus "should"/"is vital"/"is crucial" hortatives. Mostly advice to deans, not rules.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- The only quantitative census: converts the ethics bodies' claims into percentages (69% education policies vs 51% clinical — the education/clinical governance gap is a finding other frameworks assert only rhetorically).
- Names concrete org design (Associate Dean of AI) and tooling stack — operational specificity no ethicist document has.
- "Audit capabilities for AI outputs" and "sandboxes for deidentified data" are governance mechanics others leave abstract.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** — adoption-positive, data-driven, prescribes governance machinery rather than philosophy. Not pioneer: it follows the ethical lead (AAMC principles, AMA) and reports rather than sets direction; but its metrics will discipline the field.
