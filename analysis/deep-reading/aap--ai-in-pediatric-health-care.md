# AAP — Artificial Intelligence in Pediatric Health Care (resource hub page)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP). This is a **web resource hub page**, not a policy statement. No named committee, no authored document, no policy number. Content consists of a short framing paragraph plus a catalog of webinar titles and presenters (Drs. Srinivasan Suresh, Karim Hanna, Yaa Kumah-Crystal, Naveed Rabbani, Gregg Lund).
- Powers claimed: none. It binds no one. It is a dissemination/education vehicle ("AAP is creating and sharing resources").
- Audience: pediatricians and pediatric specialists.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **embrace-with-guardrails (soft), essentially enablement/dissemination**. The framing sentence: "The development and application of artificial intelligence tools in health care is growing rapidly. AAP is creating and sharing resources to help pediatricians and pediatric specialists understand and utilize these tools to **decrease burden, promote health equity, and improve the overall quality** of pediatric health care."
- Utilization is assumed as the goal; guardrails are not mentioned on the page itself (no risk language, no caveats). The closest to caution is implicit — webinars are framed as "what pediatricians need to know."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
This page contains **no normative clauses** (no must/should/prohibited/permitted statements). Inventory of descriptive/positional statements:
- Positioning: AAP "is creating and sharing resources to help pediatricians... understand and utilize these tools to decrease burden, promote health equity, and improve the overall quality of pediatric health care."
- Offered programming (all descriptive): "Generative Artificial Intelligence: What Pediatricians and Pediatric Specialists Need to Know" (Suresh — "a comprehensive overview of generative AI in health care, with a focus on potential applications in the care of children"); "Generative AI for Medical Education in Pediatrics" (Hanna — "describes and demonstrates AI-enhanced approaches to medical education"); "Generative AI for Clinical Scenarios in Pediatrics" (Kumah-Crystal — "potential use cases for generative AI tools to support patient engagement and understanding in clinical settings"); "Generative AI for Administrative Tasks in Pediatrics" (Rabbani — "potential value of generative AI tools for administrative tasks"); FAQ sessions (Suresh & Lund — "answer pediatricians' questions about generative AI tools in pediatric care"); "AI in Pediatric Health Care Research Part 2" ("how busy clinicians can use AI tools to understand, keep up with, and share the latest in pediatric health care research"); "AI in Pediatrics: Works in Progress" ("how pediatricians and pediatric specialists are using AI tools and technology in innovative ways... building and implementing tools").
- Recurring qualifier: nearly every item uses "potential" — potential applications, potential use cases, potential value. The word "potential" appears in 3 of 7 webinar descriptions, signaling pre-normative exploration.

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Technology understanding: **low on this page**. No mention of hallucination, drift, agentic AI, ambient scribes, bias, or data privacy. It does implicitly recognize distinct use domains (clinical, education, administrative, research) — a domain taxonomic instinct.
- Evidence: none cited.
- Mandates: none; purely gestural/dissemination. The sophistication lives in the webinars it points to, not the page.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- Only pediatric-specific national body in the set — child-specific framing ("care of children," "support children and families") is unique.
- Thinnest document in the corpus: a pointer page rather than a policy. Where AAFP, ACP, AMA issue position papers, AAP's footprint here is continuing-education-style webinars. Note: AAP Council on Clinical Information Technology has separate policy; this page is not it.

## (f) Provisional classification
**conservative-naive** (as a framework document) — not in the pejorative sense of opposition, but in the structural sense: it offers no normative content, no risk framework, no governance claims; it is awareness-stage dissemination. Its sophistication is limited to recognizing that pediatricians need literacy. If the underlying webinars were read, classification might rise; on this document alone it is pre-policy.
