# ACGME — ACGME2024 Session Summary: The Generative AI Revolution and Innovations and Opportunities in Medical Education (Mar 25, 2024)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: ACGME (accreditor of GME). A **blog summary of a conference plenary**, not policy: 2024 ACGME Annual Educational Conference Featured Plenary with four named experts — Robert M. Wachter, MD (UCSF chair), Jesse Burk-Rafel, MD, MRes (NYU Grossman), Eva Aagaard, MD, FACP (Washington University vice-chancellor), Eric Holmboe, MD, MACP, FRCP (Intealth CEO; until end-2023 ACGME chief research/milestone officer).
- Powers claimed: none formally; but ACGME's choice of plenary topic and framing signals accreditation-community attention. It binds no one.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **embraced inevitability with vigilance warnings**. Framing quotes:
  - Wachter to educators: "**your [learners] are already doing this, and are getting pretty good at it.**" — the strongest "it's already here" statement in the corpus.
  - Wachter's trajectory thesis: AI erred by "focusing on diagnosis (the 'hardest' problem)" early, causing caution; he "foresees the opposite progression now, by starting with lower stakes areas such as paperwork before evolving to higher stakes components like diagnostics."
  - Hemingway framing: change comes "gradually, then suddenly." Holmboe: "It is here... it can really make a difference."
- Counterweight: Aagaard — "Medical education is behind in AI" — urging adoption "with consideration to risk and benefit of use, how to gauge accuracy of AI output, and even basic AI literacy."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
Normative/advisory statements by theme:
**Deskill and vigilance (Wachter)**
1. "Dr. Wachter urged human vigilance and preventative steps to protect against what he described as **'deskilling.'**"
2. "AI in medicine in the foreseeable future, even with the breakneck pace of evolution... will be **useful but not fully trustworthy**."
3. "**'humans suck at vigilance… if they are not actively engaged.'**" — verbatim warning on automation complacency.
**Equity/resources (Burk-Rafel)**
4. Called AI "**the 'great leveler' for those with more limited resources**. He addressed the use of AI in areas such as admissions, assessment, simulation, and even self-directed learning."
5. "**'What is the critically, uniquely human element that AI cannot replace?'**... Education... can utilize AI in a way that is safe and fair, **honoring the social contract**."
**Catch-up and ethics (Aagaard)**
6. "She urged medical educators to think about how to incorporate AI into education and training, but with consideration to **risk and benefit of use, how to gauge accuracy of AI output, and even basic AI literacy**."
7. "Dr. Aagaard noted the importance of ethics in this area, as well as the need to address **how AI could affect the professional identity of physicians**." — professional-identity formation as an AI issue appears here first in the corpus.
**Assessment and bias (Holmboe)**
8. "tools are already being developed for AI to help **identify struggling learners early** in the process and **cautioned against bias in AI and the need for vigilance**."
9. "**AI should be an enhancement, but humans must remain in the loop**, he emphasized."
10. Closing: Holmboe "arguing for ethics in AI's relationship with medicine, especially on bias," Aagaard "stressing the need for human vigilance to prevent such problems."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Technology understanding: high for 2024. Live generative demo (voice answering a "difficult, multi-part scenario" on resident feedback "just seconds after" presentation); deskilling; automation-vigilance decay ("humans suck at vigilance"); early-warning learning analytics; bias.
- Evidence: anecdotal/demo-based, no citations. Session genre.
- Mandates: none — advisory only. But the concepts (deskilling, human-in-the-loop, professional identity, leveler equity) are the ideas other bodies will formalize.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- "**Deskilling**" and "humans suck at vigilance" — the most candid failure-mode language anywhere in the corpus; most bodies say "accuracy concerns," Wachter names the human-factors mechanism.
- "Great leveler" equity claim (AI narrows resource gaps) is distinctive; elsewhere equity = access/bias risk.
- Professional identity formation as an explicit AI concern is unique to this session.
- Notably skeptical of AI-for-diagnosis-first history — a historically informed adoption path (paperwork → diagnostics).

## (f) Provisional classification
**cautious-informed** — intellectually ahead of the policy bodies (deskilling, vigilance decay, identity) but institutionally gestural: a conference blog, no requirements. The ACGME's actual accreditation silence (as of this document) contrasts with the sophistication of its stage.
