# ACGME — ACGME2026 Session Summary: Harnessing AI (Agents) in the GME Office (Mar 19, 2026)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: ACGME. Blog summary of the ACGME2026 Featured Plenary "Harnessing AI Agents in the GME Office: Innovations and Future Directions," led by **ACGME Chief Information Officer Bruce Metz, PhD, CHCIO**, with panelists Woodson Jones, MD (UT Health San Antonio, vice dean/DIO), Hadar Duman, MHI (director of Accreditation, U. Washington), Jie Li, PhD (Stanford Health Care GME), Pedro Tanaka, MD, PhD, MACM (Stanford associate DIO).
- Powers claimed: none binding; but the CIO's institutional voice signals ACGME's internal adoption trajectory. Audience: GME offices and DIOs.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **inevitability-with-intentionality**. Framing quotes:
  - Metz: "**AI is here whether organizations are ready or not. The real choice... is whether institutions move forward reactively or with intention and strategy.**"
  - "Rather than viewing AI as a threat, Dr. Metz encouraged attendees to consider how **AI agents can expand organizational impact while preserving, and even strengthening, humanity** in GME."
  - Guiding metaphor: "**The Bridge**" — "inviting GME leaders to cross thoughtfully into AI adoption."
- Ethics stated as staged governance: "AI implementation **should be staged, governed, and supported by strong guardrails** to ensure safety, ethics, and accountability." Goal: "**not replacement but elevation**."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
Normative/advisory statements:
1. Metz: "AI is here whether organizations are ready or not" — inevitability premise.
2. "The real choice... is whether institutions move forward **reactively or with intention and strategy**."
3. "AI implementation **should be staged, governed, and supported by strong guardrails** to ensure safety, ethics, and accountability." — the clearest should-clause; "underscoring the importance of **human oversight throughout every stage of adoption**."
4. Elevation thesis: "The ultimate goal... is not replacement but elevation: allowing people to spend more time on judgment, learning, and care."
5. Jones: "**'AI is a tool that is inevitable. Responsibility over speed will free, not fail, people.'**"
6. Jones on selection: "the importance of **selecting scalable, secure solutions aligned with institutional needs and values**"; his dream: "reducing drudgery, enhancing efficiency, and allowing more time for creativity!"
7. Duman (UW case): AI agents "assist with reviewing and summarizing materials and generating drafts for staff to refine" — quantified: 45–60 min tasks → ~60 seconds; "reduced labor from eight dedicated team members to two, cut more than 100 hours of work down to 40, and improved consistency."
8. Duman's guardrail: "**'human accountability and manual oversight remain essential to ensure quality, accuracy, and legal compliance.'**"
9. Li (Stanford ADS case): "standards-based AI approaches can enhance oversight and accreditation by handling document review more efficiently" — "a **64 percent reduction in labor** while producing cleaner, more standardized outputs."
10. Tanaka (PRISM): "AI-assisted qualitative analysis to identify key strengths and areas for improvement across programs... reduced analysis timelines from weeks to hours, while improving consistency, scalability, and objectivity... measurable improvements in program experience and satisfaction."
11. Duman: "**'AI is a toddler. We have to engage now to shape how it turns out as an adult.'**"
12. Li: "**'People should allow AI to handle the robotic work so we can focus on being humans.'**"
13. Tanaka: "**'We need to be fully engaged in the AI implementation process.'**"
14. Closing call: "**Be proactive, intentional, and collaborative in AI adoption.**"

## (d) Sophistication markers
- **Agentic AI fluency unique in the corpus**: this is about AI *agents* in GME administration (drafting, document review, accreditation data system updates, qualitative synthesis) — no other document engages agents operationally.
- Evidence: self-reported institutional metrics (60x task speedup, 8→2 staff, 64% labor reduction, weeks→hours). Real numbers, unverifiable provenance — vendor-case-study genre risk.
- Mandates: hortative only; the "should be staged, governed, guardrailed" clause is the normative core. Human oversight "throughout every stage" is repeated as the fixed constraint.
- Bias is absent here (unlike the 2024 session) — administrative AI framing drops the equity thread.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- Two-year arc visible inside ACGME: 2024 plenary = wonder + warnings (deskilling); 2026 plenary = **deployment case studies with ROI numbers**. ACGME shifted from ethics talk to operations talk without ever issuing requirements.
- "AI is a toddler" and "responsibility over speed" are quotable distillations others may borrow.
- Notable for what it does NOT contain: no assessment-validity concern, no learner-facing rules — GME-office back-office focus only.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** operationally (staged governance, human oversight, metrics) but institutional-policy-empty: ACGME has now convened twice without publishing requirements. As a framework document: **cautious-informed** — knowledgeable, guardrail-fluent, committing to nothing.
