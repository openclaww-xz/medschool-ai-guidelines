# Adoption, perceived value, and barriers to genAI in emergency medicine (Latin America, cross-sectional survey)

## Research question
How are EM professionals in Latin America using generative AI: use patterns, perceived value, risks, institutional readiness, and training needs?

## Methods
Voluntary anonymous cross-sectional online survey (Spanish), convenience sampling via professional societies, institutional networks, social media, peer referral; piloted with 5 clinicians, no formal psychometric validation; chi-square analyses; Apr–Oct 2025.

## Sample
123 respondents from 16 Latin American countries (Dominican Republic 24.8%, Mexico 19.5%, Colombia 14.2%); 42.6% public, 28.7% mixed institutions; 61.3% >10 yrs EM experience; 97.5% internet access at work.

## Key findings (with counts)
- 78.3% had used genAI professionally; 76.9% familiarity ≥3/5.
- Platforms: ChatGPT 81.0%, Gemini 31.9%, Copilot 24.1%, Perplexity 11.2%, Claude 2.6%.
- Use cases: research/lit review 66.1%, educational material 59.1%, clinical notes 40.9%, diagnostic support 38.3%, patient communication/translation 32.2%, radiology 16.5%.
- Risks: misinformation 56.2%, erosion of clinical judgment 56.2%, bias 53.7%, liability 46.3%, confidentiality 33.9%.
- Barriers: lack of training 54.2%, legal concerns 39.8%, cultural resistance 38.1%, restrictive/absent institutional policies 34.7%.
- Governance: only 7.4% reported their institution had clear AI policies; 77.0% no policy; 15.6% unsure (uniform across countries, p=0.47).
- Training demand 88.4% (69.7% prefer virtual); 65.6% support integrating AI into clinical protocols.

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Policy vacuum attributed to institutions (hospitals) and health ministries; recommendations table assigns institutional policy to "hospitals and health ministries," training to "professional societies and academic institutions," regional coordination to PAHO and EM bodies. Adoption is bottom-up (individual clinicians) running far ahead of any formal governance — the paper explicitly frames it as "bottom-up innovation vs top-down regulation."

## Limitations
Convenience sample inflating digitally engaged clinicians; self-report; no country representativeness; cross-sectional; no psychometric validation; no objective outcomes.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
Strongly corroborates the Clin Teach policy-gap study's core finding (most institutions lack policy) and extends it internationally: 92.6% governance gap vs Clin Teach's 39% of US schools without any policy. Matches our corpus pattern of informal frontline adoption preceding institutional policy (seen in LatAm/low-resource context). Use-case ranking (research/education > documentation > diagnosis) mirrors our medical-student survey findings. Notably for our framework corpus: this shows the AMA/AAMC-style national guidance that exists in the US is entirely absent in this region — policy leadership void at national level.
