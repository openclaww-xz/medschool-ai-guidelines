# AFMC — Response to Federal AI Strategy ("AI for All"), June 12, 2026

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: Association of Faculties of Medicine of Canada, "representing Canada's **19 medical schools** and the academic medicine community" — the national voice of Canadian medical deans.
- Genre: public advocacy/response letter to the federal "AI for All" National AI Strategy (introduced June 3, 2026). Contact: advocacy@afmc.ca. Non-binding; power is representational and lobbying.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **harm-centric advocacy for stronger state regulation** — the only document in the international corpus whose primary object is AI-enabled *misinformation*, not AI use in education or practice.
- Framing verbatim: "The rapid evolution of AI technologies has **significantly accelerated the creation and spread of health misinformation and disinformation** online. From AI-generated deepfakes to increasingly sophisticated chatbots and synthetic content, Canadians are being exposed to inaccurate, misleading, or harmful medical information at an **unprecedented scale and speed**." And: "These risks are not theoretical – they are **already affecting patient decision-making, vaccine confidence, adherence to evidence-based treatments, and overall public trust in health systems**."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
1. Welcome clause: AFMC welcomes federal attention to "online and artificial intelligence (AI)–enabled harms"; underscores "urgent need for robust, coordinated action to protect Canadians from **medical misinformation** and related digital harms."
2. Threat characterization clause (verbatim in (b)): deepfakes, chatbots, synthetic content; effects on patient decisions, vaccine confidence, treatment adherence, trust.
3. Harm clause: misinformation "can delay appropriate care, encourage the use of ineffective or unsafe therapies, and undermine confidence in clinicians, public health authorities, and scientific institutions. For **vulnerable populations – including children, minorities, and those with limited health literacy** – the impacts can be especially severe."
4. Five legislative demands ("AFMC strongly supports... we call for:"):
   a. "**Modernized online safety legislation** that explicitly addresses health and medical misinformation, including mechanisms to **hold platforms and content creators accountable** for harmful content;"
   b. "**Stronger privacy and data protections** to ensure that personal health information is not exploited or misused by AI systems in ways that could harm individuals or enable targeted disinformation;"
   c. "**Clear standards for transparency and labelling** of AI-generated health content, including **watermarking or disclosure requirements** to help Canadians distinguish between verified and synthetic information;"
   d. "**Enhanced safeguards for children and vulnerable populations**, who are disproportionately affected;"
   e. "**Effective enforcement mechanisms, including independent oversight**, to ensure that legislative commitments translate into meaningful protections."
5. Limits-of-legislation clause: "legislation alone will not be sufficient." Complementary measures:
   a. "**Investment in health and digital literacy**, equipping Canadians with skills to critically assess online medical information;"
   b. "**Support for trusted, evidence-based health communication**, including partnerships with academic institutions, clinicians, and public health organizations;"
   c. "**Ongoing research and monitoring** to understand the evolving landscape of AI-enabled harms and inform adaptive policy responses."
6. Leadership clause: "Canada has an opportunity to **lead globally** in building a digital ecosystem that fosters innovation while safeguarding public health... Achieving this balance requires a clear commitment to protecting Canadians from the harms of medical misinformation."
7. Partnership clause: "AFMC stands ready to work with governments, regulators, and partners across sectors to advance policies and practices that uphold scientific integrity, protect patients, and strengthen trust in health information in the digital age."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Technically current on the misinformation vector: names **deepfakes, synthetic content, targeted disinformation enabled by data misuse** — a threat model absent from every education-focused document in the corpus.
- Policy-sophisticated: pairs platform accountability + watermarking/disclosure + independent oversight — the standard regulatory toolkit, applied to health content.
- Understands the limits of law ("legislation alone will not be sufficient") and specifies non-statutory complements (literacy, trusted communication, monitoring).
- No engagement with AI's *educational or clinical* upside or downside — a deliberately narrow aperture.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- The corpus's only document demanding **more state regulation** rather than issuing self-governance — Canada's national medical-education voice operating as an external lobbyist, complementary to the Royal College's internal task-force model.
- Misinformation/watermarking/platform-accountability territory is untouched by GMC, MOH Singapore, HEE, or any school policy here.

## (f) Provisional classification
**cautious-informed** — risk-aware, technically current, demanding precautionary state action; not naive (threat model is accurate), not progressive about adoption (silent on educational AI entirely).
