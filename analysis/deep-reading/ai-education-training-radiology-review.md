# AI in education and training of radiology: scoping review (Frontiers in Radiology, 2026)

## Research question
How has AI been examined in radiology education: educational approaches, learner groups, reported outcomes, and methodological limitations?

## Methods
PRISMA-ScR scoping review; Scopus/PubMed/IEEE Xplore, 2020–2025; PCC framework; outcomes coded into skill development / performance / engagement; multi-reviewer consensus assignment.

## Sample
2,095 records → 29 included publications (14 reviews excluded). Publication volume grew ~6x (Scopus 91→549/yr). Learners span medical students, residents, radiographers, medical physicists, dentists, practicing radiologists; 23 empirical, 6 non-empirical (frameworks/consensus).

## Key findings (with counts)
- Priorities: skill development 55.2% (16/29), performance 27.6% (8), engagement 17.2% (5).
- Effect direction: ~86% beneficial (14 "increased" + 11 "positive"); only 4 negative (low AI literacy, career-deterrence perceptions in radiology).
- Consistent readiness gap: enthusiasm exceeds preparedness across all learner groups; knowledge often acquired via social media/informal sources.
- Barriers ranked: lack of formal AI education (most consistent), faculty expertise, crowded curriculum, funding/infrastructure, no standardized competency frameworks, no external requirements (boards/ACGME) forcing prioritization.
- GenAI/LLM studies show promise (exam support, patient FAQs) with hallucination caveats — supplementary not primary training resource.
- Evidence: mostly cross-sectional self-report, single-site, short-term; few objective competency assessments or longitudinal designs.

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Notes absence of external mandates: "lack of external requirements (e.g., board/ACGME) may reduce prioritization" — i.e., specialty boards and ACGME are the missing policy leaders; ESPR AI Task Force cited as a specialty-society-led roadmap exemplar. Argues AI education should be a strategic institutional priority.

## Limitations
Heterogeneous interventions/outcomes; self-report dominance; single-center small samples; no longitudinal competency evidence; review excluded reviews from synthesis.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
Corroborates our national-framework corpus finding that ACGME/LCME have NOT issued AI-specific accreditation standards (LCME strategic re-visioning notes; ACGME session notes) — this review independently confirms the accreditation vacuum from the education side. The "no external requirements" barrier directly supports our corpus's core novelty: policy leadership is diffuse, with specialty societies (ACR/ESPR) moving before accreditors. Frontiers AI-writing disclosure present (ChatGPT used for English + schemes).
