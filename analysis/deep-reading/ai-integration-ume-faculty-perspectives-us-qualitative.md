# AI Integration in UME: Faculty Perspectives in the UAE (JMIR Medical Education, 2026)

## Research question
How do medical faculty in the UAE perceive AI integration into undergraduate medical education — practices, readiness, challenges?

## Methods
Qualitative descriptive; semistructured online interviews (30–60 min), Jan–Apr 2025; purposive + snowball sampling; reflexive thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke); FACETS framework + SAMR model as interpretive lenses; COREQ-reported; saturation at 8 interviews + 2 confirmations.

## Sample
10 faculty at 2 UAE institutions (9 public, 1 private); disciplines: EM, IM, public health, OB/GYN, pathology, med-ed; most with extensive curriculum-design roles; self-rated AI understanding moderate–expert (1 expert).

## Key findings (with counts)
- Form: near-monoculture of NLP generative AI (ChatGPT dominant; also Notion AI, Copilot) — accessibility beats specialization.
- Faculty use: assessment/item generation, content authoring, lesson planning, feedback drafting; student use: personalized study plans, practice questions, concept deconstruction.
- Context: universal emphasis on cultural/ethical localization to UAE socioreligious values (family structures, language, norms) — "fundamental requirement," not minor adjustment.
- SAMR: current use overwhelmingly at substitution/augmentation; modification rare; redefinition (AI simulations, adaptive tutoring) aspirational.
- Priorities: faculty development (prompt engineering, ethics, curriculum alignment) and equity/overdependence risk (digital disparities widening performance gaps; misconduct shortcuts).
- Policy call: institutions must establish frameworks for integrity, privacy, equitable access, culturally contextualized.

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Institutional (school-level) frameworks demanded; faculty development as the bottleneck. No reliance on national bodies — local leadership framed as the operative level.

## Limitations
10 faculty, 2 institutions, one country; qualitative transferability not generalizability; fast-moving tools may date findings.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
Corroborates our faculty-development corpus file (institutional readiness as the gap) and our school-policy finding that policy precedes curriculum. The substitution/augmentation ceiling matches our corpus's observation that policies regulate existing assessments rather than redesigning them. The cultural-localization demand is a dimension almost entirely absent from US school policies in our corpus — a divergence between Gulf and US policy framings.
