# AI in Nursing Governance and Regulation: Umbrella Review (SAGE Open Nursing, 2026)

## Research question
What ethical and policy dimensions of AI in nursing governance do systematic/scoping reviews report?

## Methods
Umbrella review (JBI, PRISMA, PROSPERO-registered CRD420261332265); 5 databases, Jan 2010–Dec 2025; dual screening/extraction; JBI quality appraisal; narrative thematic synthesis; CCA overlap analysis.

## Sample
31 reviews (all published 2020–2025 despite 2010+ search window); 22 high, 6 moderate, 3 low quality; overlap analysis on 23 reviews: 694 occurrences, 620 unique studies, CCA 0.54% (slight).

## Key findings (with counts)
- Review-level theme support: privacy/data stewardship 29/31, transparency/explainability 27, bias/fairness 25, accountability/liability 25, consent/autonomy 18, leadership/education/oversight 14, safety/human oversight 9.
- Strong convergence: regulation lags implementation; nurses underrepresented in AI governance; ethical preparedness and AI literacy are priorities.
- Divergence: autonomy enhancement vs dependency/deskilling risk.
- Governance lifecycle proposed: pre-implementation review (intended use, subgroup performance) → implementation (override authority, escalation) → post-deployment (incident reporting, bias audits, drift assessment).
- Four-level responsibility table: regulatory (minimum standards), institutional (multidisciplinary oversight with nursing representation), clinical (document disagreement, communicate uncertainty), education (competencies into curricula/licensure).
- Policy variability across countries; no global consensus on ethical priorities; call for international harmonization.

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Layered assignment: regulators/professional boards set minimums; institutions run oversight; educators/accreditors embed competencies; nurses must be represented in procurement and governance. Accreditors explicitly named for competency integration.

## Limitations
English-only; grey literature excluded; heterogeneous review designs; 8 reviews absent from overlap analysis; convergence ≠ independent confirmation given localized overlap.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
The four-level responsibility model is essentially identical to the architecture our corpus documents empirically (national frameworks → school/institution → clinical environment → curriculum). Nursing literature is ahead of medicine in demanding accreditation-level competency integration — LCME/ACGME have not done this (our run-26/29 finding), making nursing governance a leading indicator. "Governance as lifecycle, not one-time approval" matches Duke ABCDs re-approval structure in our corpus.
