# AI Policies in Bioethics and Health Humanities Journals: Comparative Analysis (BMC Medical Ethics, 2025)

## Research question
What AI policies do leading bioethics and health/medical humanities journals and their publishers hold, and how do they vary in permissiveness?

## Methods
Manual search (8 Mar 2025) of journal homepages/author guidelines/publisher policies for 50 journals (36 bioethics, 14 health humanities) drawn from ASBH, HHC, AMH lists + Google Scholar top-20; one reviewer + 2 verifiers; editor email follow-up for policy-less journals; permissive/non-permissive coding.

## Sample
50 journals (54 identified minus exclusions), publishers incl. Wiley, Springer Nature, T&F, OUP, BMJ, CUP, Sage, JHU Press.

## Key findings (with counts)
- Only 8/50 (16%) journals have their own identifiable AI policy; 27/50 (54%) covered only by publisher-level policy; 15/50 (30%) no identifiable policy at any level.
- Of the 8 with journal policies, 5 (10% of all) explicitly NON-permissive (reject AI-generated text): Christian Bioethics, J Medicine & Philosophy, J Hospital Ethics, Kennedy Institute Ethics J, Voices in Bioethics; only 2 permissive (+1 RHiME HH).
- All 27 publisher policies are permissive-with-disclosure.
- 10/15 editors of policy-less journals responded: all confirmed no policy; 7 in active discussion; 3 personally would not want AI-assisted manuscripts.
- Tensions documented: an editor of two no-AI journals signed the 2023 pro-responsible-use editors' joint statement; OUP (their publisher) is permissive.
- Cross-publisher disagreement on disclosure threshold (Springer: copy-editing exempt; Cambridge: all AI declared) and extent limits (Sage open to substantially generated; JHU Press refuses).

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Policy leadership defaults to PUBLISHERS (the LUS-analogue) when journals don't act — direct parallel to medical schools defaulting to university-system policy. Editors' joint statement (Dec 2023) acted as soft leadership without enforcement. Authors call for a professional society (ASBH) to form an expert consensus group.

## Limitations
Snapshot Mar 2025; public documents only (informal/internal practice unseen); journal-vs-publisher default assumption untested; no assessment of enforcement.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
Structurally identical to the Clin Teach school-policy findings: minority of actors have own policy (16% journals vs 61% schools), generic parent-entity policy fills the void, and contradictory positions coexist between levels. Corroborates our corpus's "authority layering" theme — policy cascades imperfectly from parent organizations, and the lowest-tier actor's specificity matters most for end users.
