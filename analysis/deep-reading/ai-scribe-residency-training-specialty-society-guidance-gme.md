# AI Scribe Use in Residency Training: A Call for Specialty Society Guidance in GME (Advances in Medical Education and Practice, 2026)

## Research question
Does resident-specific guidance on AI scribe use exist among major medical/specialty organizations, and what does the literature show?

## Methods
Jul–Aug 2025 website search of AAD, AMA, ACGME, AAMC, ABMS (keywords "artificial intelligence," "documentation," "scribe," "residency") + direct email confirmation; PubMed search for residency AI-scribe studies.

## Sample
5 organizations (AAD, AMA, ACGME, AAMC, ABMS); 2 qualifying studies from 4 PubMed hits.

## Key findings (with counts)
- 0/5 organizations had resident-specific AI documentation guidance; AAD, ACGME, AAMC, ABMS confirmed by email that none exists.
- Literature: Wright 2025 (47 residents, Abridge pilot: cognitive load 56.3→43.3, p<0.001; documentation burden items improved; time-per-note NS) and Lu 2025 (5 residents vs GPT-4o: quality non-inferior; GPT-4o 40.1 s vs 14.9 min per note, p<0.001).
- Benefits/risks table: time savings and burden reduction vs underdeveloped documentation skills, ambiguous accountability, patient-consent concerns in teaching settings.
- Kaiser Permanente ambient scribe program cited: majority positive, ~15,700 hours saved/year.

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Explicit call: specialty societies (and by implication ACGME) must lead in creating resident-specific AI scribe guidance — currently NO ONE leads for GME documentation. Recommended policy elements: mandatory resident review/sign-off, accountability protocols, consent standards, note-section limits (e.g., A&P).

## Limitations
Letter/scope review, not systematic; 2 small studies only; website-search method may miss internal guidance.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
Directly corroborates our run-29 finding that ACGME has no AI-specific GME policy (only session content), and our observation that GME-level AI policy is institution-by-institution (OHSU, UChicago IM, UKentucky). Slight tension: our corpus shows some GME programs DO have local AI scribe policy (e.g., UC Davis clinical note-taking, UMass Chan clinical learning environment), so the gap is at the national/specialty-society tier, not absolute. The recommended policy elements map almost 1:1 onto provisions we catalogued in institutional policies (mandatory verification, consent, section restrictions).
