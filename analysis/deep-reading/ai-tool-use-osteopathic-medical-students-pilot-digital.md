# AI Tool Use Among Osteopathic Medical Students: Pilot Digital Diary (JMIR Formative Research, 2026)

## Research question
How much of medical students' study time involves AI tools, and how does use vary by training stage and engagement style (active vs passive)?

## Methods
Longitudinal observational cohort; baseline survey + 7 SMS diary entries over 21 days (every 3 days); multiple linear regression + mixed-effects models (random intercept per student); STROBE-reported; Touro University California + Nevada, Apr–May 2025.

## Sample
71/1332 eligible DO students enrolled (5.3% response); 64 with ≥1 diary; 77% preclinical; 381/497 diaries completed (76.7%).

## Key findings (with counts)
- 90% had used AI for studying; 98% of users say AI positively impacts learning; 83% positive on clinical reasoning; 61% want formal curriculum integration.
- Real-time use: AI = 19% of total study time (35.8 of 185.6 min per 3-day diary); ChatGPT 89%, Gemini 31%, AMBOSS AI 16%.
- Clinical students (MS3/4) 31% of study time on AI vs 16% preclinical (adjusted +19pp, p=.003; d=0.97 large; confirmed in MLM p=.02).
- Use predominantly PASSIVE: concept simplification 75%, complex topics 72%, practice questions 52%; active uses lower (mnemonics 35%, lab interpretation 31%, patient simulation 27%).
- Active users log ~108 more total AI minutes (p=.01) but not more per-sitting.
- ICC 0.47: AI use fluctuates within-person day-to-day — state, not trait.
- Study did not measure verification behavior or learning outcomes.

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Institutions should provide responsible-use guidance including critical evaluation/verification — but study notes limited student-specific guidance exists, distinct from clinical/research contexts.

## Limitations
5.3% response, self-selection toward AI-engaged students (inflates estimates); 2 campuses one system (DO); self-report minutes; no validated active/passive instrument; no outcome measures; 3-week window.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
First real-time quantification (vs recall surveys) — 19% of study time is the new baseline datum. Corroborates our corpus's tiered-use policies targeting exactly the passive uses documented here. Notably Touro's own academic-integrity AI addendum is in our corpus — a rare study+policy pair. Passive-dominant use supports the "verification gap" concern that both the AI-literacy framework (56% correct evaluation) and our policy provisions address.
