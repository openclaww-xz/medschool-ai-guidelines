# AMA — Advancing AI in Medical Education Through Ethics, Evidence and Equity (Sep 21, 2023)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: AMA (ChangeMedEd initiative). Companion document to "AMA policy for augmented intelligence" and the "Trustworthy Augmented Intelligence in Health Care" framework, "leverag[ing] the same framework" for education. Built on **prior stakeholder consultation** ("the AMA consulted with key stakeholder groups"). Institutional authorship; no named committee.
- Powers claimed: framework-setting for "developers of educational AI systems," "administrators who deploy," and "educators who integrate" — the three-role architecture is its governance claim. Binds no one; aspirational framework widely cited (AMA's own hub cross-links it).
- Scope note (repeated verbatim on the resources page): addresses "the use of AI to facilitate the process of training health professionals," NOT "providing training in AI."

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **embrace-with-guardrails under a quintuple-aim-for-education vision**. Framing quotes:
  - Vision: "This same model [Quintuple Aim] can be applied to consider the ideal process of education itself and the experience of learners and educators" — an educational quintuple aim: learner experience, learning outcomes, educator work-life, cost, educational equity.
  - Evidence claim (asserted flatly): "**Research demonstrates that AI use improves learning outcomes, quality of life and satisfaction.**"
  - Equity dialectic: "Attention to the risk of bias and co-production with learners leads to AI tools that **mitigate rather than amplify bias**."
- Ethics–evidence–equity is the tripartite lens; "Trustworthy Augmented Intelligence" the brand.

## (c) Complete clause inventory
**Vision clauses (educational quintuple aim)**
1. "AI drives education that is **more personalized to each learner's needs**. **Learners' rights are respected**, and they are **empowered to understand and contribute to the use of AI in their education**."
2. "AI will facilitate an educational program's ability to **respond to individual learner needs at scale**... AI drives learning experiences that meet each learner's **zone of proximal development**." (Vygotskian vocabulary — med-ed-native.)
3. "Educators are **engaged in developing and implementing AI tools** that augment their ability to provide high-quality feedback... Administrative burdens are reduced... **Barriers to adoption, such as lack of education about AI, are overcome and liability and privacy issues are addressed.**"
4. "Oversight of educational programs **ensures compliance with applicable laws and regulations, based on appropriate levels of educational validation and high-quality evidence**."
5. "Educational AI addresses high priority needs and improves outcomes for all learners. **Existing bias in the educational system is combatted** by integrating data across sources to improve equity in assessment and in experience."
**Developer guidance (interrogatives as clauses)**
6. "Ensure that the AI system is **designed for a specific need and is not a tool looking for a use**."
7. "**Impact may diverge from intent.** Learners and front-line educators are important sources of information regarding the actual impact of a tool."
8. "Solicit learner insights during the development process to understand potential ethical concerns."
9. "Consider the appropriate handling of learner academic/performance data, as well as learner personal data."
10. Wearables clause: "Consider whether such interventions **lead to the collection of personal health information or border on rendering clinical care to the learner**." — unique: recognizes educational AI can slide into unlicensed clinical care of the student.
11. "**The developer must acknowledge the need for, and plan a process for, quality assurance**" (bias + ongoing performance monitoring).
12. "The process of development should consider a variety of use cases, envisioning differing learner needs."
13. "Educational innovators **must study the positive and potential negative impacts** on learning or on the experience of learners and front-line educators."
**Administrator guidance**
14. "Such institutional officials **bear responsibility to protect learners**."
15. Checklist: "Have the appropriate legal and ethical considerations been addressed? **What is the protocol to identify and address bias? What is the process for ongoing maintenance and monitoring of the AI tool's performance? Is the AI system being implemented consistently across the organization?**"
16. "**Educational administrators must retain authority over high stakes decisions such as advancement or remediation.**" — the strongest single clause in the corpus: AI must not decide advancement.
**Front-line educator guidance**
17. "Determine that the AI system **meets expectations for ethics, evidence, and equity. It can be trusted as safe and effective.**"
18. Local efficacy: "has been shown to improve learning outcomes **for a learner population like mine**... and I have the resources and infrastructure to implement it in an ethical and equitable manner."
19. "**each instance of use should contribute to studying efficacy.**" — every use is an N-of-1 study; research as routine practice.
20. "The front-line educator **must have a mechanism to express any concerns about results from the AI system and potentially override it if indicated.**" — override right.
**Stakeholder-insight clauses**
21. Transparency demands: intent behind development; "How educators, learners and AI systems will work together to promote trustworthy systems"; "protections such as data privacy and security"; "The program of research to validate educational benefit"; "ongoing monitoring... to mitigate potential negative impacts."
22. "Stakeholders emphasized the importance of **establishing guardrails for validating AI systems and ensuring educational inequities are not exacerbated**... Education and training efforts are needed to **increase the number and diversity of educators with AI knowledge and expertise**."
**Synthesis clauses ("responsible use... entails commitment to")**
23. Systems that: "Address educationally meaningful goals. **Uphold the profession-defining values** of medicine and of medical education. **Promote educational equity. Support meaningful oversight and monitoring** of system performance. **Recognize clear expectations for accountability** and establish mechanisms for holding stakeholders accountable. **Contribute to ongoing study** of the impact of AI in medical education."
**Code anchors**: Code of Medical Ethics Opinions 1.1.6 (Quality), 1.2.11 (Ethically Sound Innovation), 11.2.1 (Professionalism in Health Care Systems) offered as "educational corollary" sources.

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Technologically credible: wearable-data clause (PHI/clinical-care boundary), performance monitoring, bias mitigation-vs-amplification, override mechanisms. No hallucination/drift/agentic terms. "Zone of proximal development" shows genuine learning-science grounding.
- Evidence: claims research demonstrates benefit (#claim 2 — asserted without citation); otherwise embeds study mandates throughout (clauses 13, 19, 23) — an evidence-generating ethic.
- Mandates: several real "must"s (QA planning, impact study, advancement authority, override mechanism) — the most operationally specific education-side framework among US bodies.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- Three-role accountability split (developer/deployer/educator) is AMA's structural contribution; later documents (AAMC principles' micro/meso/macro) parallel it.
- "AI must not decide advancement/remediation" and "every use contributes to efficacy research" are unique hard clauses.
- Wearables→clinical-care-of-learner boundary issue appears nowhere else.
- Terminological discipline: "augmented intelligence" throughout, consistent with AMA policy line since 2018.

## (f) Provisional classification
**pioneer** (education-side) — first (Sep 2023) to give medical education a full role-responsibility framework with enforceable-sounding clauses (advancement authority, override, per-use research duties). Its optimism ("research demonstrates") slightly outruns its citations, but structurally this is the template AAMC's 2025 principles echo in softer form.
