# AMA — Advancing AI in medical education through ethics, evidence and equity (2023-09-21, AMA ChangeMedEd / AMA Ed Hub)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: American Medical Association, on its ChangeMedEd initiative pages; dated 2023-09-21. Written by AMA medical-education staff building explicitly on "AMA policy for augmented intelligence" and the AMA's "Trustworthy Augmented Intelligence in Health Care" framework — self-described as "This companion document leverages the same framework in considering educational applications of AI."
- Scope statement (verbatim): "This document does not address the important goal of providing training in AI for health care professionals to facilitate their duties in providing care; that is addressed elsewhere." I.e., AI-FOR-education here, AI literacy elsewhere.
- Authority: professional-society guidance; no binding force. Purported audience is developers, educational administrators ("Deans of curriculum, assessment, and student affairs; program directors and designated institutional officials; directors of continuing professional development"), and front-line educators in UME/GME/CPD.
- Preceded by stakeholder consultation: "the AMA consulted with key stakeholder groups to elicit their perspectives on the intersection of AI and education."

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: embrace-with-guardrails, structured as an "educational quintuple aim." Framing quotes:
  - "Viewed through the lenses of ethics, evidence and equity, the AMA promotes Trustworthy Augmented Intelligence in Health Care."
  - "This same model can be applied to consider the ideal process of education itself and the experience of learners and educators." (quintuple aim: patient/learner experience, outcomes, cost, work-life, equity)
  - "Research demonstrates that AI use improves learning outcomes, quality of life and satisfaction." (asserted without citation — an evidence marker that gestures rather than cites)
- Distinctive move: role-partitioned responsibility — developer / deployer (administrator) / front-line educator each get their own checklist.

## (c) Complete clause inventory (verbatim normative statements, by theme)

### Vision commitments (quintuple-aim education corollary)
- "AI drives education that is more personalized to each learner's needs. Learners' rights are respected, and they are empowered to understand and contribute to the use of AI in their education."
- "AI will facilitate an educational program's ability to respond to individual learner needs at scale." … "AI drives learning experiences that meet each learner in their zone of proximal development."
- "Educators are engaged in developing and implementing AI tools that augment their ability to provide high-quality feedback to learners." "Barriers to adoption, such as lack of education about AI, are overcome and liability and privacy issues are addressed."
- "Oversight of educational programs ensures compliance with applicable laws and regulations, based on appropriate levels of educational validation and high-quality evidence."
- "Existing bias in the educational system is combatted by integrating data across sources to improve equity in assessment and in experience. Attention to the risk of bias and co-production with learners leads to AI tools that mitigate rather than amplify bias."

### Developers / educational innovators (obligations phrased as questions + imperatives)
- "Educators should be mindful of the responsibilities associated with development"
- "Ensure that the AI system is designed for a specific need and is not a tool looking for a use."
- "Impact may diverge from intent. Learners and front-line educators are important sources of information regarding the actual impact of a tool."
- "Solicit learner insights during the development process to understand potential ethical concerns."
- "Consider the appropriate handling of learner academic/performance data, as well as learner personal data."
- "Consider the appropriate role of evolving data sources. For example, some educational innovations are applying wearable technology. Consider whether such interventions lead to the collection of personal health information or border on rendering clinical care to the learner."
- "The developer must acknowledge the need for, and plan a process for, quality assurance." (monitoring for bias and ongoing performance)
- "The process of development should consider a variety of use cases, envisioning differing learner needs."
- "Educational innovators must study the positive and potential negative impacts on learning or on the experience of learners and front-line educators."

### Deployers: educational administrators
- "Such institutional officials bear responsibility to protect learners."
- Required questions: "Have the appropriate legal and ethical considerations been addressed?" / "What is the protocol to identify and address bias?" / "What is the process for ongoing maintenance and monitoring of the AI tool's performance?" / "Is the AI system being implemented consistently across the organization?"
- Strongest single clause: "Educational administrators must retain authority over high stakes decisions such as advancement or remediation." (human-in-command for high-stakes decisions)

### Front-line educators
- "Determine that the AI system meets expectations for ethics, evidence, and equity. It can be trusted as safe and effective."
- "The AI system has been shown to improve learning outcomes for a learner population like mine, or for educators in similar roles, and I have the resources and infrastructure to implement it in an ethical and equitable manner."
- "To foster collective understanding about the impact of AI in education, each instance of use should contribute to studying efficacy."
- "The front-line educator must have a mechanism to express any concerns about results from the AI system and potentially override it if indicated."

### Transparency (stakeholder-derived)
- Transparency needed on: "The intent behind the development of an AI system"; "How protections such as data privacy and security will be handled"; "The program of research to validate educational benefit"; "How ongoing monitoring of the performance of AI systems to mitigate potential negative impacts will be conducted."
- "Education and training efforts are needed to increase the number and diversity of educators with AI knowledge and expertise."

### Translating principles into practice (summary commitments)
- "responsible use of AI in medical education entails commitment to designing and deploying AI systems that: Address educationally meaningful goals. / Uphold the profession-defining values of medicine and of medical education. / Promote educational equity. / Support meaningful oversight and monitoring of system performance. / Recognize clear expectations for accountability and establish mechanisms for holding stakeholders accountable. / Contribute to ongoing study of the impact of AI in medical education."

### Anchors to binding-adjacent ethics code
- Points to AMA Code of Medical Ethics Opinions 1.1.6 (Quality), 1.2.11 (Ethically Sound Innovation in Medical Practice), 11.2.1 (Professionalism in Health Care Systems) — "for which readers can consider the educational corollary."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Understands education-AI specifically: zone of proximal development, wearable data ("border on rendering clinical care to the learner" — a genuinely sharp pedagogic-ethical insight), bias in assessment, high-stakes decision authority (advancement/remediation). No hallucination/drift/agentic language; "ambient" appears only in an unrelated 2026 news headline ("Patients don't right to ambient AI recordings, court says") in the page furniture.
- Evidence: gestures ("Research demonstrates…") without formal citations; framework grounded in stakeholder consultation rather than literature review.
- Mandates: soft — "must" appears 4 times (developer QA, innovator study, administrator retention of high-stakes authority, educator override mechanism), unusual density of hard modal verbs for a companion-guidance page, but no enforcement.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- First-in-corpus role-partitioned educational framework (developer/deployer/educator) — prefigures the deployer/developer distinction in WHO/EU instrument discourse and the AAMC's later principles.
- Quintuple-aim transposition to education is unique framing language.
- The "wearables may constitute clinical care to the learner" clause and "administrators must retain authority over advancement/remediation" clause are found nowhere else in the corpus.
- Explicitly splits AI-for-education from AI-literacy (cross-references its own literacy policy) — cleaner scope hygiene than most peers.

## (f) Provisional classification: pioneer
Justification: September 2023 — the earliest substantive education-specific AI framework among the US bodies in this corpus, with novel normative architecture (role-partitioned checklists, quintuple-aim education corollary), concrete must-clauses on high-stakes decision authority and educator override, and a research-contribution-per-use ethic. Later AMA documents (CME Report 4-A19, AI literacy policy) and AAMC principles borrow its ethics-evidence-equity triad and its developer/deployer/educator partition, marking it as language others borrowed from rather than borrowed.
