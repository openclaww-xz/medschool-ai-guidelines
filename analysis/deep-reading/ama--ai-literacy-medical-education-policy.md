# AMA — Press release: AMA adopts policy to advance AI literacy in medical education (Nov 17, 2025, Interim Meeting)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: American Medical Association. Policy adopted by the **AMA House of Delegates** at its Interim Meeting (National Harbor, Md., Nov 17, 2025). CEO & EVP John Whyte, MD, MPH is the quoted voice.
- Powers claimed: AMA policy powers — "develop and disseminate model AI learning objectives and curricular toolkits," "collaborate with other medical organizations," "advocate for funding." It binds AMA action, not physicians directly; but AMA HOD policy is the field's most emulated template. Anchored to the new **AMA Center for Digital Health and AI** (launched October 2025) which aims "to ensure physicians play a leading role in shaping emerging technologies."
- Notable: AMA consistently uses "**Augmented Intelligence**" not "Artificial Intelligence."

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **literacy-enablement as precondition of safe adoption**. Framing quotes:
  - Whyte: "As AI becomes increasingly embedded across health care, **we face an urgent need for a standardized educational framework that emphasizes patient safety, transparency, and accuracy**."
  - "Just as medical students learn anatomy and physiology, **they must also understand how AI tools function, their limitations, and their potential to support clinical care**." — the anatomy analogy is the strongest literacy claim in the corpus.
  - Goals: "improve patient care, reduce administrative burdens, and restore physician satisfaction in practicing medicine."
- Secondary risk posture: same meeting passed a **deepfake safeguards resolution** — "create safeguards to protect patients and physicians from deepfake technology in the face of what supporters say is a 'regulatory void.'"

## (c) Complete clause inventory
Normative/commitment statements:
1. Policy aim: "strengthen the physician workforce and improve patient outcomes through **standardized training in medical school and increased access to AI-focused Continuing Medical Education (CME) resources** for practicing physicians."
2. "The policy builds on the AMA's commitment to ensuring AI is implemented ethically and responsibly across health care settings."
3. "**Under the new policy, the AMA will develop and disseminate model AI learning objectives and curricular toolkits** to guide foundational education on the use of AI in clinical practice."
4. "The AMA also will **collaborate with other medical organizations to work toward recognizing AI literacy elements**" (recognition of AI literacy — presumably in accreditation/standards contexts) "and will **advocate for funding and faculty-development resources** to expand AI training initiatives."
5. Literacy content (Whyte): students "must also understand how AI tools function, their limitations, and their potential to support clinical care."
6. Center mission: "embedding physician expertise into the design and implementation of digital health and AI tools, strengthening education and training, and advancing policy and regulatory frameworks... support technologies that enhance patient care and reduce physician burnout."
7. Deepfake resolution: "The AMA will **support organizations that work on federal legislation and regulations regarding deepfake technology** to uphold the integrity of the medical profession against malpractice, increase awareness of the risks associated with deepfake content, and safeguard patient well-being."
8. Deepfake rationale: "Deepfake 'doctors' are garnering millions of views on social media, endorsing products... jeopardizing patient safety." Whyte: "The foundation of the patient-physician relationship is built on accurate information, trust, professionalism, and authenticity, all of which are under direct threat from deepfake content."
9. Dissemination asset: "a seven-part Artificial Intelligence in Health Care series, available for free on the AMA Ed Hub™... Introduction to Artificial Intelligence in Health Care, The Use of AI in Diagnosis, and Navigating Ethical and Legal Considerations of AI in Health Care."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Understands: deepfake threat (only body in the corpus to legislate on it), physician burnout linkage, tool limitations as curriculum content. The anatomy-and-physiology analogy implies AI literacy as **core foundational science**, not elective.
- Evidence: none cited (press release genre); leans on the Center's mandate.
- Mandates: AMA commits its own machinery (toolkits, advocacy, collaboration) — does not mandate schools; "must" appears only in the CEO's normative framing of students.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- Earliest and loudest "AI literacy as anatomy" framing; deepfake resolution is unique in corpus (2025-dated, anticipatory of federal regulatory void).
- Ties literacy to workforce/well-being ("restore physician satisfaction") — a labor-relations framing absent from AAMC/ACP.
- Center for Digital Health and AI gives AMA an institutional engine peers lack.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** — literacy-first enablement with real organizational backing (toolkits, CME, advocacy), plus a targeted risk artifact (deepfake). Not pioneer: it disseminates and convenes rather than setting the conceptual frame (it borrows "responsible/ethical" vocabulary long established by its own 2024 AI principles and others).
