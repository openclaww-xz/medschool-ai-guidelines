# AMA — ChangeMedEd "AI in medical education" resource collection page

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: AMA, via the ChangeMedEd® initiative (AMA's med-ed reform collaboration; the module series co-developed with the **University of Michigan DATA-MD team**). Institutional web curation; named voices: Kim Lomis, MD (VP Medical Education Innovations), Margaret Lozovatsky, MD (VP Digital Health Innovations).
- Powers claimed: none; a resource hub. Its significance is curatorial: it canonizes what AMA thinks educators should read — and it **links to AAMC's Principles and to BEME Guide No. 84**, showing cross-body citation.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **literacy-enablement with dual framing** — AI as tool AND as subject: "AI is playing an increasingly important role at all stages of the medical education continuum, **both as a tool for educators and learners and as a subject of study in and of itself**. AI has the potential to transform the educational experience as a part of **precision education** and transform patient care as a part of precision health."
- The AI-in-education vs education-about-AI distinction is made explicit in the companion-doc note (clause 5 below).

## (c) Complete clause inventory
Statements of position (hub genre — few hard normatives):
1. Dual role: AI is "both as a tool for educators and learners and as a subject of study in and of itself."
2. Module series: "a seven-part online module series developed by the AMA ChangeMedEd® Initiative and the University of Michigan DATA-MD team... introduces learners to critical AI and machine learning concepts in health care and is available at no cost on the AMA EdHub™. Topics... include navigating ethical and legal considerations of AI in health care, practical applications of AI in the health system and the use of AI in diagnosis."
3. Audience: "Targeted toward medical students, the introductory content is also suitable for residents, fellows, practicing physicians and other health professionals."
4. Learning-analytics guide: "The Medical Educator's Guide to Projects Leveraging Artificial Intelligence and Learning Analytics (PDF, free download) **outlines the core issues to consider when designing technological solutions to educational challenges**... provides an overview of key points for discussion with potential technical partners, facilitating essential details in planning and implementation that give projects the greatest chance at success."
5. Companion scope note: "Advancing AI in medical education through ethics, evidence and equity is a companion to the AMA policy for augmented intelligence (PDF) and leverages the same framework when considering the educational applications of AI. This document addresses **the use of AI to facilitate the process of training health professionals** but does not consider issues around the important goal of **providing training in AI for health care professionals to facilitate their duties in providing care**." — an explicit two-problem split, deferring literacy to other workstreams.
6. STEPS Forward: "Join experts discussing the practical uses of AI in medical practice, including **addressing the risks** and exploring its transformational potential... how physicians are navigating this new technology and **leading the way in how AI is designed and implemented**."
7. Curated canon (external): NAM "Artificial Intelligence for Health Professions Educators"; "Teaching artificial intelligence as a fundamental toolset of medicine" (Cell Reports Medicine); "Dr. ChatGPT will see you now" (Montgomery County MD Medical Society); **AAMC Principles for the Responsible Use of AI**; **BEME Guide No. 84 scoping review** (Medical Teacher); "Competencies for the Use of Artificial Intelligence–Based Tools by Health Care Professionals" (Academic Medicine).
8. News canon headlines: "As they push ahead with AI, health leaders must set rules on use"; "Do these 5 things to ensure AI is used ethically, safely in care"; "Using AI in diagnosis means scrutinizing each tool. Here's how."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Technically aware through its canon rather than its own prose: learning analytics, precision education, model scrutiny, algorithm bias CME. No hallucination/drift/agentic vocabulary on the page itself.
- Evidence posture: curates a scoping review (BEME 84) and competencies paper — deferential to evidence synthesis.
- Mandates: none; free-access commitment (no-cost modules) is the main structural gesture.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- The **explicit two-problem taxonomy** (AI-for-training vs training-for-AI) is the cleanest statement of that split in the corpus.
- Demonstrates the citation network: AMA pointing to AAMC principles and BEME — evidence for the synthesis question of who borrows whose language.
- "Precision education" vocabulary (from the learning-analytics guide) is AMA-specific in this corpus.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** as an infrastructure actor: not a policy, but the distribution channel that operationalizes AMA policy (free modules, guides, CME). Its curatorial choices show literacy-enablement philosophy with real artifacts behind it.
