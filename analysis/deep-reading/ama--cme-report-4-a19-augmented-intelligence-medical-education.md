# AMA — Council on Medical Education Report 4 (A-19): Augmented Intelligence in Medical Education (Resolution 317-A-18, 2019)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- **AMA Council on Medical Education**, presented by Carol Berkowitz, MD (Chair); referred to Reference Committee C. "HOD ACTION: Council on Medical Education Report 4 **adopted as amended**, and the remainder of the report filed." Origin: Policy H-480.940 (2018, AMA's first AI policy, from a Board of Trustees report) + Resolution 317-A-18 (Maryland Delegation, "Emerging Technologies (Robotics and AI) in Medical School Education"). The **founding document** of this corpus's timeline — seven years pre-ChatGPT-plus-one.
- Powers claimed: AMA policy; recommendations are "Directives to Take Action" binding AMA's own advocacy machinery; addressed to accrediting/licensing bodies, specialty societies, schools.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **literacy-and-study-first, notably non-alarmist**. Framing quotes:
  - Terminology (still canonical): "'[I]n health care a more appropriate term is **'augmented intelligence**,' reflecting the enhanced capabilities of human clinical decision making when coupled with these computational methods and systems.'"
  - Knowledge-doubling rationale: Densen quoted — "In 2020 it is projected to be 0.2 years—just 73 days... **fundamental change has become an imperative.**"
  - Promise/concern/unknown triad: promise of "enhanced learner evaluations, including the ability to assess competencies prospectively, accurately, and continuously, leading to... **an elimination of the 'stop and test' mindset**"; concerns "such as the possibility of **physician de-skilling** as more cognitive tasks are performed by AI; an **unintentional reinforcement of health disparities**... the potential loss of physician humanism and further deterioration of physicians' bedside skills; and the risk of **overutilization of AI-delivered care, such as the use of technology for the sake of using technology**."
  - Admissions heresy (unique): "The need for this receptivity may in turn **spark a discussion regarding the kind of student who should be recruited to enter the profession**... a premium has been placed on individual mastery of knowledge. Learners who excel at this type of knowledge, however, **may not be the same kind of learners who interact effectively with AI systems.**"

## (c) Complete clause inventory — the 11 adopted recommendations (verbatim)
1. "encourage **accrediting and licensing bodies to study how AI should be most appropriately addressed in accrediting and licensing standards**. (Directive to Take Action)" — the seed of the LCME 2025 revision found in this corpus.
2. "encourage medical specialty societies and boards to consider production of **specialty-specific educational modules** related to AI."
3. "encourage **research regarding the effectiveness of AI instruction in medical education on learning and clinical outcomes**."
4. "encourage institutions and programs to be **deliberate in the determination of when AI-assisted technologies should be taught**, including consideration of established evidence-based treatments, and including consideration regarding **what other curricula may need to be eliminated** in order to accommodate new training modules." — curriculum-tradeoff clause.
5. "encourage stakeholders to provide educational materials to help learners **guard against inadvertent dissemination of bias that may be inherent in AI systems**."
6. "encourage the study of how **differences in institutional access to AI may impact disparities in education** for students at schools with fewer resources."
7. "encourage **enhanced training across the continuum of medical education regarding assessment, understanding, and application of data** in the care of patients."
8. "encourage the study of how **disparities in AI educational resources may impact health care disparities for patients** in communities with fewer resources."
9. "encourage institutional leaders and academic deans to **proactively accelerate the inclusion of nonclinicians, such as data scientists and engineers, onto their faculty rosters**."
10. "That Policy D-295.328, 'Promoting Physician Lifelong Learning,' be reaffirmed."
11. "encourage **close collaboration with and oversight by practicing physicians in the development of AI applications**."
Fiscal note: $1,000.
**Context clauses**
- BOT 41-A-18 quote: "Just as working effectively with EHRs is now part of training... educating physicians to work effectively with AI systems... **will be critical to the future of AI in health care.**"
- Patel et al. mediation quote: "All technologies mediate human performance... **The difference is not one of quantitative change, but one that is qualitative in nature.**"
- Key distinction: "it is important to distinguish between **AI as a topic of study itself** and in the **instruction of learners regarding use of existing tools**... educating students and physicians in the practical use of specific AI technologies is not necessarily equivalent to... understanding how the technology works or how to **evaluate its applicability, appropriateness, and effectiveness**." — the earliest statement of the corpus's recurring two-problem taxonomy.
- Accreditation mapping (2019 snapshot): LCME standards 4.1/4.5/5.4/5.6/5.9/6.3 and COCA 4/4.3/4.4/6.7/7.1/7.6 quoted as adjacent-but-AI-absent; NBME/NBOME exams "do not specifically cover AI"; ACGME CPR VI.A.1 and CLER pathways flagged as vehicles; "None of the member boards of the ABMS currently require education in AI."
- H-480.940 core (quoted in appendix): promote AI that "is designed and evaluated in keeping with best practices in **user-centered design**... is **transparent**; conforms to leading standards for **reproducibility**; **identifies and takes steps to address bias**... safeguards... privacy."
- Evidence base: Kolachalama & Garg finding — MeSH "machine learning" + "graduate medical education" 2010–2017 = 16 publications, "**none of them were actually focused on ML education for medical professionals**"; international attitude surveys (UK GPs: 94% say tech "would never deliver empathetic care," 80% believe it can do documentation; German students 83% buy the promise, 56% doubt definitive diagnosis); institutional examples (Duke DIHI, Carle Illinois, NYU, Hopkins, Stanford, Dell's "chair of diagnostic medicine").
- LCME 2016-17 simulator survey data (78/145 gross anatomy etc.).

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Technology: solid for 2019 — Turing-test definition, AI vs augmented distinction, NLP/ML/deep learning taxonomy, CAD, wearables, adaptive quizzing. Names **de-skilling**, bias, humanism loss, technology-for-technology's-sake. No hallucination (pre-generative era).
- Evidence: 23 references, genuinely scholarly for a policy report; quantified survey data; honest about the literature void.
- Mandates: all "encourage" — even the accreditation ask (#1) is a study directive. Zero requirements; the AMA's 2019 posture is catalytic, not regulatory.
- Distinctive foresight: admissions-criteria question (#recruitment clause) and curriculum-subtraction clause (#4) remain radical even in 2026.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- **The origin point**: H-480.940's "augmented intelligence" and the physician-oversight clause (#11) are the vocabulary every later document reuses (AMA 2023-25, ACP both papers, FSMB). The 2019 accreditation directive visibly bore fruit in LCME's 2025 Standard 7.2.
- Only document to quote accreditor standards exhaustively as a gap map; only one to raise admissions-selection implications.
- Pre-generative vintage makes it a baseline: what changed 2019→2026 is precisely what the corpus's later documents add (hallucination, ambient scribes, agentic).

## (f) Provisional classification
**pioneer** — the earliest, most consequential framing document in the corpus: set the terminology (augmented intelligence), the taxonomy (AI-as-subject vs tool-use), the study-then-standardize sequencing, and the equity/deskill concerns that everyone else inherited. Its non-binding "encourage" style is period-appropriate; its agenda was adopted by the whole field.
