# Ambient AI in Primary Care: Mixed-Methods Survey of UK GPs (BMJ Health & Care Informatics, 2026)

## Research question
How widely are ambient AI scribes adopted among UK GPs, and what are users' experiences (errors, consent, workload, medicolegal concerns)?

## Methods
Anonymous web survey via Doctors.net.uk Omnibus (Aug 7–27, 2025); stratified outreach, convenience sample; descriptive stats + chi-square/z-tests; descriptive coding of one optional free-text item; CROSS reporting.

## Sample
1003 GPs (601 email-route at 21% response + 402 dashboard); verified GMC registrants. 141 (14%) current users, 396 (39%) intending adopters, 466 (46%) not using/not intending.

## Key findings (with counts)
- Adoption 14% (95% CI 12–16.3), 39% intend soon — rapid diffusion signal.
- Users skew: GP partners (+13%), larger practices; fewer locums (−12%) and ≥56-year-olds (−9%); no gender difference.
- Heidi Health dominates: 86.5% of users; Accurx Scribe 12.8%.
- Impact: 80% report decreased documentation time; 70% decreased cognitive workload; 55% rate output "good"; 27% report burnout symptoms.
- Errors: 44% find errors in 10–30% of notes; 41% correct nearly all; 57% correct often/almost always; 86% judge errors insignificant-to-moderate, 14% significant-to-critical.
- Error concentration: multiparty consultations 38%, complex histories 35%, non-English first language 31%, speech impairment 21% — inverse-care-law risk.
- Consent: 63% routinely seek patient consent; 37% do NOT; among consenters, ≤10% of patients decline.
- Medicolegal: 50% slightly concerned, only 9% very/extremely.
- Regulatory context: NHSE April 2025 digital-scribe guidance (clinical safety officer, DPIA, registration); June 2025 CCIO letter — stop using tools not registered as Class I medical devices (Heidi reportedly affected); Ahpra (Australia) embeds consent in code of conduct.

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Strong national-leadership case study: NHS England issued binding-ish guidance and device-registration enforcement within months — a top-down model contrasting with US institutional voluntarism. Clinician accountability for record accuracy unchanged (clinician, not vendor, liable).

## Limitations
Convenience sample of digitally engaged GPs; self-report; single-platform dominance (Heidi); free-text shallow; cross-sectional.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
Corroborates the "errors are routine but manageable with mandatory verification" model our institutional policies encode. The 37% non-consent finding shows policy–practice gaps even under national guidance — supports our implementation-gap theme. NHS device-registration route is absent from US corpus (FDA non-classification), a regulatory divergence worth highlighting in synthesis.
