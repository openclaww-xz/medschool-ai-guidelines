# Ambient AI Scribes as Emerging Infrastructure in the Learning Health System (Learning Health Systems, 2026)

## Research question
Conceptual: what are the systemic/infrastructural implications of outsourcing the "first mile" of clinical documentation to proprietary ambient AI?

## Methods
Commentary synthesizing infrastructure studies (Star & Ruhleder) and learning health system theory; micro–meso–macro tension mapping; no new data.

## Sample
N/A (conceptual). Key cited evidence: >2.5M notes in one year at Kaiser (Tierney, NEJM Catalyst); Topaz hallucination/omission cases; Koenecke 2x speech-recognition error for Black speakers; Epic Sepsis Model external validation failure.

## Key findings (with counts)
- Scribes exhibit 4 infrastructural properties: embeddedness/invisibility, installed-base reliance, propagation (errors flow into dashboards/registries/models/billing), visibility-on-breakdown.
- Speech-recognition legacy: 7–10% word error rates historically, clinically significant errors persist post-review.
- Tensions table: micro (relief vs overreliance; trust vs consent opacity), meso (efficiency vs data quality; deployment speed vs governance capacity), macro (vendor opacity vs transparency; innovation vs regulatory lag).
- Mitigations proposed: "scribe formularies" (staged approval), AI Documentation Stewardship Committees, drift monitoring, model-agnostic labeling standards, contractual transparency.
- No FDA classification/validation norms for genAI documentation tools; turnover cost $500k–$1M/physician context.

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Health systems (meso) must lead with stewardship committees and monitoring because regulators lag and vendors are opaque; regulators (macro) must close classification gaps. Policy framework for source-audio retention/governance explicitly called missing.

## Limitations
Commentary; no empirical data of its own; framework untested.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
Directly corroborates our run-30 health-systems findings (Duke ABCDs oversight tiers, Mount Sinai governance, UCSF procurement gates): the "scribe formulary"/stewardship committee proposals mirror structures already documented in our corpus — evidence the literature is converging on what leading systems already do. Extends our corpus by naming downstream data-quality propagation, which no collected policy addresses.
