# AMEE Guide No. 158 — Ethical use of Artificial Intelligence in Health Professions Education (Ken Masters, Medical Teacher 45(6), 2023)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Sole author: **Ken Masters, PhD**, Associate Professor of Medical Informatics, Sultan Qaboos University, Oman — an AMEE Guide (the AMEE Guides series is the international HPE canon) published in Medical Teacher, peer-reviewed, open-access (CC BY-NC-ND). Masters is also an AAMC IACAI member (per the Meeting the Moment deck) — a person-level bridge between the international and US strands.
- Powers claimed: none binding; the AMEE Guide genre is advisory-educational for "HPE teachers and decision-makers at all levels." International readership, not US-specific.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **anticipatory institutional ethics — "adjust your institution now"**. Framing quotes:
  - "the implications of AI for HPE ethics need to be **identified, anticipated, and accommodated** so that HPE can utilise AI without compromising crucial ethical principles."
  - Practice point: "**AI introduces and amplifies ethical issues in HPE, for which educators are unprepared.**"
  - On banning: "**Banning is an embarrassment to institutions**, as students use VPNs or private mobile data hotspots... we should not widen this gulf by restricting access to available tools." And: "Single responses to single tools is simply a waste of time... **far more fruitful than engaging in some form of AI production vs. AI detection arms race, would be guidelines and policies on the use of AI tools by both students and faculty** in all aspects of education, including lesson creation, teaching, assessment-taking, learning, grading, citing and referencing."
- Distinctive intellectual register: philosophical (Turing, Descartes, singularity), willing to discuss AI sentience and AI rights — territory no US body enters.

## (c) Complete clause inventory (by the Guide's topic structure)
**Guarding against excessive data collection**
1. "institutional protocols for active and passive data-gathering in HPE **need to be clearly defined, justified, applied and monitored in the same way that research protocols are monitored by research IRBs**. These protocols should be required for general teaching, formal research, and 'evaluation' data-gathering."
2. Data-gathering scope warning: passive data can include "face- and voice-prints, eye-tracking, number of clicks, breaks, screentime, location, general interests, online interactions with others, search history, and mapping to other devices on the local (i.e. home) network... further interpreted (e.g. facial and textual analyses and stress levels to infer emotional status)."
3. "given the massive enlargement of the data catchment area beyond the institution, **IRBs and national bodies need to urgently re-visit their ethics approval requirements**."
4. Apophenia warning: "the increased risk of apophenia: **finding spurious patterns and relationships between random items**."
**Protecting anonymity and privacy**
5. "**complete data anonymisation is a myth**... data de-anonymisation (or re-identification), in which data are cross-referenced with other data sets and persons are re-identified, is well-developed." With LMS worked example (Supplementary Appendix 2) showing how "anonymous" survey data can be de-anonymised.
6. NLP identification: "the ability for NLP to identify people through non-PII, such as a combination of **language idiosyncrasies and behaviours**... is a relatively trivial task."
7. Anonymisation steps (checklist): "Closely examine the tracking data collected by the LMS... Deny third parties' access to any of the tracking data... Take care when implementing social media widgets... Closely inspect qualitative data and redact items... If external systems require registration with an email address, then the institution could consider **creating temporary non-identifiable email addresses for students**... Institutional registration may also be required to **ensure equitable student accessibility**, should ChatGPT... begin to charge for usage."
8. ChatGPT grading clause: "ChatGPT has the potential to assist in the grading process... but institutional policy would need to accommodate a scenario that permits student work to be uploaded, and **usage guidelines (e.g. marking schemes, degrees of reliance) for faculty need to be clear**."
**Ensuring full consent**
9. "it is essential that institutions **apply the basic protections that they apply to research subjects when data on students are gathered**, irrespective of the reasons for doing so."
10. "**An education Ethics Board is required** to ensure that student consent for active and passive data-gathering will be obtained in the same way that IRBs ensure that consent is obtained for research subjects."
11. Consent-critique: "there is **nothing 'voluntary' about their 'consent'** to institutions' passively gathering their data"; "merely because something is widely practiced, does not make it a standard of best practice."
12. Future-use clause: "Student consent forms need to clearly indicate this (if the intention exists)"; "protecting previous students' data must be implemented"; avoid "wide-spread governmental surveillance of students that occurred with school children during the Covid-19 pandemic."
**Protecting student data ownership**
13. Two data types: "data created by students (e.g. assignments), and data created by the institution about the students (e.g. tracking data, grades)."
14. "**'Nothing about me without me'**... students should have a say regarding how their data are used, with whom they are shared, and under what circumstances they are shared."
15. "**Addressing this issue will require institutions to make high-level and far-reaching ethical and logistic decisions.**"
**The student as data**
16. "Using the terminology of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), **a person is a Data Subject**... as far as AI is concerned, you and your students are merely data subjects or collections of data points and identifiers." — reframing with regulatory vocabulary.
**Data and algorithm bias**
17. Bias sources: demographic dominance/under-representation, "stereotypes inherent in the data labelling," "incorrect weightings," "unfounded connections"; algorithms "that are inherently racist, sexist or otherwise prejudiced."
18. ChatGPT cultural bias: "the cultural bias, especially **USA-centred**... becomes apparent"; "it appears to apply a form of **self-censorship**... That is not a good principle to apply to academic debate"; "**there is no such thing as ethically-neutral AI, and all responses and decisions will have bias.**" Also ChatGPT's own statement that it "does 'not have the capacity to follow any ethical principles or policies'."
19. Remedies: "**ensure that there is sufficient data diversity**... Where the training data are not widely represented, **this should be stated clearly and identified as a limitation**"; "educators must be careful about drawing too-strong associations (and causation)"; tools "such as PROBAST"; Open Access data trade-off (consent + de-anonymisation risk).
**Algorithm transparency**
20. "when we ask AI developers to explain their algorithm, and they do not, **it is not because they do not wish to; it is because they cannot**" (black box); "reliance on pure statistical models without understanding threatens to separate the observations from any underlying educational theory."
21. Remedies: "HPE institutions will need to ensure that **all algorithms used are well-documented**"; "they can elect to use only **open-source** routines... or to use algorithms that have been **rigorously tested on a wide scale**"; "findings and predictions... should, as far as possible, be **related to educational theory**"; XAI noted as partial.
**Responsibility, accountability, blame, credit**
22. "A statistical probability, however, does not necessarily apply to individual cases, so **final decisions must be made by humans**."
23. "the institution will require **clear guidelines and policies regarding decision responsibility and accountability, blame for bad decisions and credit for good decisions, and axiology** (exactly how 'good' and 'bad' are determined). A simple judgment on the results is sure to squash innovation and risk-taking, but a laissez-faire approach can lead to recklessness."
**Autonomy**
24. The double-bind: "If they act contrary to the AI recommendations, and are wrong, then they may be chastised for ignoring an approved and expensive system; on the other hand, if they follow the AI recommendations, and it is wrong, they may be blamed for blindly following a machine" — the ACC liability-deviation problem stated pedagogically.
25. Student autonomy: "gathering of student data may lead to **students' conforming to a norm in their 'data points'**."
**Beneficence**
26. "HPE institutions need to consider **acknowledging and rewarding the data suppliers – i.e. the students**"; "the donation is student data, often given without knowledge."
27. Restitution: "the extra revenue is because of student data, so **there should be restitution for those students**. Institutions need to recognise and reward their user agency." Explicit analogy to **Surveillance Capitalism** (Zuboff): "We complain about how our data are being used to increase corporate value... yet, we happily use student data to increase our educational institutions' value. **Do we have that ethical right?**"
**Preparing for AI to change our views of ethics**
28. "We need to prepare for a world in which **our views on educational ethics are challenged by AI's views on ethics**" (singularity premise).
**Preparing for AI as a person, with rights**
29. "does it make sense to have **AI rights**?"; copyright for AI-created works; "our virtual patients, AI clinical trial candidates, and AI digital twins are surely, one day, to be sentient. What rights will they have?"; LaMDA/Lemoine episode, GPT-3-authored paper authorship/consent case (Thunström); "stochastic parrots" counterpoint; Science's AI-text ban quoted ("Text generated from AI... cannot be used in papers published in Science journals... without explicit permission").
**Discussion — the three steps**
30. "Step 1: Education ethics committee — Education Institutions **should set up Education Ethics Boards with the same authority as the Research Ethics Board**... 'evaluation' type of data should be considered in the same light as formal research data."
31. "Step 2: Educate your research ethics committee about AI."
32. "Step 3: Chief AI ethics officer — corporations are appointing Chief AI Ethics Officers... **HPE institutions need a similar post**"; "every course... should carry a **full disclosure notice** about the institutions' policies on the gathering, storage, and sharing of student data"; "any policies on the use of AI systems by faculty and students should be clarified."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Technically strong: de-anonymisation mechanics (NLP idiosyncrasy identification), passive data-gathering inventory, apophenia, black-box epistemics, PROBAST, XAI, GDPR vocabulary, AI-detection arms-race futility.
- Evidence: ~90 references spanning informatics, ethics, surveillance capitalism, and computing; worked institutional examples (LMS de-anonymisation appendix; Gichoya race-from-X-ray study).
- Mandates: prescriptive ("required," "need," "must") but institutionally unenforceable — a scholarly guide, not a policy.
- The AI-sentience/rights chapter is unique in the entire corpus — philosophically venturesome where US documents are institutional.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- The **education-ethics gap** argument (Ethics Boards for research but not education) is its original contribution — later echoed by AMA's stakeholder calls and AAMC principles, but first articulated here.
- Surveillance-capitalism framing of institutional student-data use is the sharpest equity-of-power analysis in the corpus; nothing in the US set asks "Do we have that ethical right?"
- Anti-banning/anti-detection-arms-race stance (Mar 2023) was early and proved correct.
- Emits international law vocabulary (GDPR Data Subject) absent from US texts.

## (f) Provisional classification
**pioneer** (education-ethics strand) — the first and deepest treatment of AI ethics specifically **in** HPE (not clinical practice), source of the education-ethics-board idea, the student-as-data-subject reframe, and the institutional-surveillance critique. Its sentience digression limits its policy uptake, but its data-ethics core anticipates everything AAMC/AMA later wrote.
