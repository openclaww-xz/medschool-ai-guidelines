# Amsterdam UMC — "How can you use ChatGPT and AI in a good way?" (Amsterdam Public Health news, March 5, 2024)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: Amsterdam UMC, via Amsterdam Public Health research institute news. Named rule-maker: "the **Health Data Science core team** has drawn up a set of rules" / "has drawn up frameworks for this."
- Genre: operational guidance news item — the institution's actual consumer-GenAI rule set, dated March 5, 2024.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **environment-tiered permission + categorical data bans**. Framing verbatim: "While these tools can be very useful, it is important to keep in mind important factors such as **privacy, data protection and sustainability**."
- Contract-gap diagnosis: "Amsterdam UMC has **not agreed any contracts with the providers of AI products for the consumer market** concerning what happens with Amsterdam UMC data... When it comes to AI consumer products, however, **we have no idea what happens to the data. We also do not know where this data is stored. It is therefore possible that Amsterdam UMC is in breach of laws and regulations as a result**."
- Adoption duty: "generative AI has a lot of potential. As a knowledge institute, that is something we need to investigate" — across "three categories of use: **healthcare, business-sensitive information, and... experimenting with consumer applications such as ChatGPT**."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
1. Three-lane use taxonomy:
   a. **Use in healthcare**: "EPIC and the EVA service centre are working together to introduce AI within EPIC to support healthcare processes. This is the **only place where we are specifically allowed to work with healthcare-related data using AI**." Clarification: "many of the AI initiatives already underway at Amsterdam UMC for specific healthcare applications **do not use generative AI products**... often use AI or algorithms **developed within a controlled Amsterdam UMC environment**. The Health Data Science core team has drawn up frameworks for this."
   b. **Playground for business-sensitive information**: ICT service department "setting up a 'playground'. This **controlled and safe environment** is suitable for experimenting with business-sensitive information" — examples: automating data analyses, document/database connections, minute-taking automation, writing/editing code. "This is a **pilot programme, so the number of participants is limited**."
   c. **Consumer GenAI (ChatGPT etc.)**: permitted with the rules below.
2. **Rules for using ChatGPT / consumer GenAI** (the core rule set):
   a. "**Do not share data that is not accessible from the public domain or a public source.** This means **no personal data** (anything that can be traced back to a person, including images), **no business-sensitive information and definitely no patient information**."
   b. "**Results should be thoroughly checked for potential biases and factually incorrect statements.** Generative AI products in the consumer market, such as ChatGPT, are **language models and not knowledge models**."
   c. "**Results could potentially violate the intellectual property rights of third parties**... trained using a large amount of data, taken from the internet, without taking copyright into account. There are currently many legal proceedings underway, filed by authors, publishers and other parties." Consequence clause: "any results **may not simply be used in documents we publish and that bind us to legal obligations**."
   d. "**Generative AI is not suitable for critical processes such as individual patient care or processes that include financial transactions**."
3. **Sustainability clause**: "Another aspect to take into account is the amount of energy needed to work with AI due to the significant computing capacity required. **Working with AI is not sustainable.** So make sure you have a **clear goal in mind when you experiment**, and be conscientious."
4. **Useful tip 1 — training-data opt-out**: "The paid version of ChatGPT has **settings to prevent your data from being used to train the AI. It is advisable to turn this setting on**."
5. **Useful tip 2 — prompt guidance** (complete recommendation list): describe target audience (management, students, colleagues, patients); describe language level of audience (CEFR A1/A2 beginner, B1/B2 intermediate, C1/C2 advanced); describe goal of the answer (inform, entertain, convince); describe format (bullet-points, dialogue, folder); describe perspective (enthusiastic, critical, expert); "set parameters such as the number of words"; "ask for multiple points of view and positions."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- "Language models and not knowledge models" — the single most precise epistemic characterization of consumer LLMs in the corpus.
- IP/copyright clause with litigation awareness ("many legal proceedings underway") and a binding-document consequence — legally sophisticated; unique in the corpus.
- **Sustainability/energy** as an explicit constraint — appears nowhere else in the 26 files.
- Environment-tiering (EPIC-controlled / playground pilot / consumer tools) is a genuine technical-architecture governance move: same institution, three data-risk zones.
- Practical product knowledge: paid-ChatGPT training opt-out setting; CEFR-structured prompting recipe.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- The intellectual-property rule and the sustainability rule are both corpus-unique. The "no data not already public" rule is stricter and clearer than most confidentiality clauses elsewhere.
- Contrast with its sibling (AI topic overview): the same institution celebrates research AI while categorically banning consumer GenAI from patient data and critical processes — governance precision on the operational side.
- No education/assessment layer here (students are not the audience); this is workforce/operations governance that a medical school would inherit rather than write.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** (operationally) — technically precise, tiered, enforceable rules with real product-level detail; simultaneously **cautious-informed** in posture (consumer GenAI presumed unsafe for clinical data absent contracts). Classified overall: progressive-structured with strong precautionary data governance.
