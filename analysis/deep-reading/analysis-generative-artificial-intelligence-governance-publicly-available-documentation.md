# GenAI Governance Documentation: Global vs Saudi Universities w/ Health Informatics (AMEP, 2026)

## Research question
How does publicly available GenAI governance compare between international (QS-ranked) and Saudi universities offering health informatics programs?

## Methods
Qualitative document analysis + comparative policy analysis; 30 international (QS 2025 subject rankings, health-informatics eligibility) + 16 Saudi institutions; sources retrieved Mar–May 2026; 9-domain deductive framework (UNESCO/WHO/OECD-derived) with inductive refinement; dual coding, Cohen's κ=0.96; Fisher's exact; content-intensity (Absent→Present→Operational→Embedded) + composite maturity score (unvalidated).

## Sample
117 governance sources (111 international, 6 Saudi) across 46 universities; mostly web-based guidance rather than formal policy PDFs.

## Key findings (with counts)
- Availability: 30/30 (100%) international vs 6/16 (37.5%) Saudi universities had public GenAI governance; mean 3.7 docs/international university; 89.2% of international sources 2023–2026.
- Domain gaps significant: Transparency/Disclosure 87.4% vs 33.3% (p=0.005); Ethical Considerations 91.9% vs 50.0% (p=0.014); AI Literacy 81.1% vs 33.3% (p=0.019).
- Comparable domains: Acceptable/Prohibited Use (~99–100%) and Governance/Accountability (~98–100%) — but Saudi framing is compliance/sanctions-oriented vs international human-oversight/disclosure framing.
- Institution level: all 30 international ≥8/9 domains (mean 8.8); Saudi mean 2.1; 10 Saudi = none.
- Cognitive/overreliance provisions: 42.3% of international sources, 0 Saudi.
- Health-informatics-specific program governance: only 2 international institutions (Karolinska HI program guidelines 2025; UCSF Bridges Curriculum GenAI Policy) and 0 Saudi. Clinical-data governance (PHI/HIPAA/GDPR special-category): 8 international universities, 0 Saudi. Cites Stanford MD Policy 3.32, Imperial's AI vision, Harvard/Yale/JHH PHI references.
- Multi-layer governance (≥2 organizational levels): 5 international, 0 Saudi.

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Universities lead; accreditation bodies called on to add discipline-specific AI-governance expectations to program standards; national policymakers (Saudi SDAIA/Vision 2030) called on for model frameworks. Centralized-ministry systems may hold governance at national level without publishing locally — an observability caveat.

## Limitations
Public documentation only (transparency ≠ internal governance existence); 6-source Saudi group; unvalidated maturity score; cross-sectional snapshot; content studied, not implementation.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
Methodologically the closest comparator to our project — a public-document policy analysis with a domain framework — validating our approach. Its findings triangulate our corpus: Stanford 3.32 and UCSF Bridges policy are files WE hold and it independently rates them among the few program-level policies nationally/globally. Its 9-domain framework and intensity ladder are adoptable for our cross-corpus coding. Key novelty contrast: it covers 46 universities in one discipline (health informatics); our 378-source corpus spans the full policy stack (national→school→GME→health system) — no published study does that.
