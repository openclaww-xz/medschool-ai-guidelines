# Bridging the AI Policy Gap in US Medical Schools (The Clinical Teacher, 2026) — ANCHOR STUDY

**Read status note:** raw file contains PubMed abstract only; publisher full text inaccessible (HTTP 403). This note is based on the complete abstract; flagged as partial read.

## Research question
What AI policies have US medical schools enacted for medical students, and what are their sources and content?

## Methods
Cross-sectional document analysis, July 2024, of publicly available AI-related policies for all 199 accredited US MD schools + affiliated larger university systems (LUS); handbooks and policy webpages; dual-reviewer coding for policy source, student specificity, content scope, enforcement; chi-square/Fisher by program type and region.

## Sample
All 199 accredited US medical schools (document-level, not survey).

## Key findings (with counts)
- 121/199 (61%) schools had an AI policy.
- Policy source: 31 medical-school-level, 90 LUS-level (overlap exists). Many schools rely on generic LUS policies rather than med-student-specific guidance.
- Med-school-level policies more often student-targeted, covering coursework, assessments, research, and clinical documentation; emphasizing integrity, ethics, data privacy.
- MD (83%) > DO; South (34%) more likely to have policies (p<0.001).
- Conclusion: gap leaves learners without clear expectations; calls for tailored, student-specific, regularly updated policies.

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Central finding: policy leadership defaults to LUS (generic university policy) when the medical school itself does not act — i.e., no one with medical-education expertise is leading in ~40% of schools. Baseline established July 2024.

## Limitations (from abstract)
Public-document analysis only (non-public policies missed); July 2024 snapshot; enforcement coded but detail behind paywall.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
This is the closest published comparator to our corpus. Our 378-source corpus both corroborates and extends: we hold the actual policy texts (which this study could only code at summary level), cover DO/GME/health-system tiers absent here, and extend the timeline past July 2024. Where this study counts policy presence/absence, our corpus can characterize content, authority, and enforcement — the "what," not just the "whether." Their 61% MD coverage figure and LUS-default finding match our cohort structure (many of our school policies are university-parent documents).
