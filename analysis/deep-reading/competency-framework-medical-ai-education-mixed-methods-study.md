# A Competency Framework for Medical AI Education: Mixed Methods Study (JMIR Med Ed, 2026)

## Research question
(1) What core AI competencies should medical professionals possess for AI-enabled health care? (2) How can a competency framework be used to build a training program?

## Methods
Two-phase mixed-methods: Study 1 — framework built from UNESCO AI competency framework + Miller pyramid, refined via deductive content analysis (κ=0.81) of open-ended survey responses from 24 Singapore experts. Study 2 — 5-module training program evaluated via 2-round Delphi (9 educators; round-1 consensus: IQR≤1, AS>75%, FS>49.23%; round-2: AS>80%), plus 1-day pilot workshop with post-survey (satisfaction/engagement/self-confidence scales, Cronbach α>0.8).

## Sample
24 experts (6 hospital administrators, 8 medical professionals, 10 university instructors; Singapore, invited Aug–Dec 2024); Delphi panel of 9 (2 education, 3 AI, 4 medicine educators; Singapore + UK); pilot: 28 participants (12 Y2, 4 Y3, 7 Y4, 5 Y5 medical students; 57% clinical-stage), 15/28 survey respondents; 4 instructors.

## Key findings
- Final framework: 6 dimensions × 4 Miller levels. Dimensions: patient-centered AI; ethics & transparency; data privacy/security/compliance; technical proficiency; bias mitigation & health equity; GenAI for health care. 19/24 (79%) experts cited AI foundations; 23/24 (96%) application skills; 16 (67%) critical reflection/ethics; 5 (21%) GenAI integration.
- Program: 5 modules (patient-centered/ethical AI; privacy & security; applications; bias & equity; GenAI). Delphi: 16/25 elements at consensus in round 1, all 25 by round 2.
- Pilot (module 3 only): satisfaction 4.00/5 (SD 0.52); engagement 3.80–4.05; self-confidence 3.63 (SD 0.53) — confidence notably lower. Instructors: content needed more time; simplify technical content for nontechnical learners.

## What it says about who leads policy
Governance framing: AI governance "cannot rely solely on external constraints" — competency-building is part of governance. Policy anchors cited: UNESCO framework, HIPAA/GDPR; leadership role for hospital administrators/government agencies (5+ yrs policymaking) implies health-system and national actors lead, with universities implementing.

## Limitations (authors')
Singapore-only experts (cultural generalizability); voluntary pilot with self-selection and 54% response; self-reported, single-module, short-term evaluation; no competency pre/post assessment.

## Relation to corpus findings
Corroborates the Clin Teach policy-gap theme from the curriculum side: even in 2026, a rigorous study opens by noting the "absence of standardized medical AI curriculum frameworks" — institutions still lack shared scaffolding. Our corpus documents what schools actually promulgated (policies/curricula) at population scale, which no expert-panel study can.
