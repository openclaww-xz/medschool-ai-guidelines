# Current Landscape of Medical AI Curriculum Development: Scoping Review (J Multidisciplinary Healthcare, 2026)

## Research question
How have implemented (not theoretical) medical AI education programs been developed, delivered, and evaluated?

## Methods
PRISMA-ScR (Levac); PubMed Central/WoS/Scopus/MEDLINE 2021–Apr 2026; dual review; coding against Kern's Six-Step Approach; only IMPLEMENTED programs included (theoretical/Delphi-only designs excluded); no formal quality appraisal (scoping choice); Bloom's-taxonomy objective coding with κ=0.93.

## Sample
7652 records → 36 implemented programs across 17 countries (14 US; 4 Germany; 13 published 2025, 6 in 2026).

## Key findings (with counts)
- Growth rapid but small: mostly pilots; 17/28 reporting enrollments had <60 learners; withdrawal rates 9.4%–73.3%; only 5 programs confirmed multi-round delivery.
- Development: 26 documented methods — 13 interdisciplinary teams, 12 clinical-experts-only, 7 literature reviews, 6 surveys; 1 by AI experts alone.
- Learners: 27 university-based (11 UME, 8 doctoral); 9 PGME; 7 CME. Disciplines: nursing 6, radiology 5, neurosurgery 2.
- Objectives (Bloom-coded): concentrated at understanding (χ²=164.24, p<0.001) — remembering 5, understanding 20, applying 11, analyzing 12, evaluating 8, creating 8; affective-domain objectives almost entirely absent.
- Content: basic AI knowledge 36/36 (100%), clinical applications 30 (83%), ethics/law 23 (64%), technical skills 18 (50%), communication 4, interprofessional collaboration/quality improvement 1.
- Format: 26 standalone courses vs 11 integrated into existing curricula; 23 elective vs 7 compulsory; 32 modular, only 2 spiral; 13 ≤1 week duration.
- Assessment: subjective self-report dominates (21 programs questionnaires/interviews; 12 objective quizzes/exams); course evaluation by learner feedback in 20/24.
- Persistent gaps named: no standardized frameworks, missing affective objectives, no objective assessment tools, weak curricular integration, insufficient instructional-designer involvement.
- Notes policy anchors (ACP principles, AMEE, EU AI Act 2024) "lack specific guidance for designing courses."

## What it says about WHO leads policy
National/association guidelines (ACP, AMEE, EU) give strategic direction without operational course guidance; development leadership defaults to local expert teams without competency standards or needs assessment. Explicit call for consensus capability frameworks.

## Limitations
Small pool; heterogeneous, sometimes incomplete program descriptions; no quality appraisal; coding subject to interpretation.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
Confirms the corpus-wide pattern: policy documents vastly outnumber implemented curricula; our school policies similarly govern use without specifying taught competencies. The 26-standalone-vs-11-integrated split and elective dominance quantify how early curricular integration is — supporting our synthesis claim that "policy precedes pedagogy." Bloom-skew to understanding matches the AI-literacy framework's complaint that assessment and performance evidence are the weakest links.
