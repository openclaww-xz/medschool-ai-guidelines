# CWRU School of Medicine — Policy on Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence Tools for Medical School Assignments

## Authority / Authorship
- Publisher: Case Western Reserve University School of Medicine; PDF on the curriculum site.
- Policy header: "Approved By: WR2/CSC" (WR2 = the CWRU medical curriculum; CSC = its student curriculum committee); "Date Approved: 8/3/2023"; "Review Period: Annually"; "Responsible Office or Committee: Curriculum Committee"; "LCME Affiliated Elements: N/A."
- One of the earliest formal policies in the corpus (Aug 2023) — predates nearly all cohort-B peers.

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- Effortful-learning framing: "Learning is an active and constructive process that requires effort and struggle. Curricular exercises (e.g., assignments, presentations, assessments, SEQs, reflections, patient notes, Research Thesis, MD Thesis, etc.) are intentionally designed to stimulate this process and challenge students to develop their skills and knowledge."
- Desirable-difficulty logic: "While use of generative AI tools in any manner to complete curricular exercises may make them seem easier, it will also negatively impact the learning value of these exercises and may harm the student's academic progress."
- Controlled-exposure stance: purpose includes "balance technological advancements, and provide a controlled exposure to generative AI."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory
Policy Statement
1. "This policy is needed to uphold the integrity and effectiveness of the learning process, as well as to maintain academic standards and ethical guidelines."

Purpose
2. "The purpose of this policy is to preserve the integrity of the learning process, uphold academic standards, promote ethical behavior, balance technological advancements, and provide a controlled exposure to generative AI."

Scope
3. "This policy applies to medical students enrolled in the University and College Programs at Case Western Reserve University School of Medicine."

Definition
4. "Generative AI (e.g., large language models like ChatGPT) is a technology that can produce text, graphics, images, and other media automatically based on a prompt. It can also rewrite, summarize, and paraphrase existing content. While this technology can be useful for many purposes, it can also undermine the learning process."

Core rules
5. "Therefore, all content that is submitted or presented by a student must be their own work and not the product of generative AI or other individuals."
6. "Students are responsible for the integrity of the content of their work and the underlying references that support that work."

Controlled exposure
7. "Generative AI is a rapidly evolving area, and these tools will undoubtedly become increasingly integrated into software including word processing programs and search engine browsers. To provide students with experience using generative AI, curricular exercises may be created for which use of the AI is required to complete the assignment. Anytime use of generative AI is required or encouraged, this will be clearly stated in the exercise."

## Sophistication Markers
- "Effort and struggle" theory of learning made explicit — the most pedagogically candid rationale in cohort B; anticipates the desirable-difficulties literature without naming it.
- Acknowledges ambient integration (clause 7: AI "will undoubtedly become increasingly integrated into software including word processing programs and search engine browsers") as early as 2023 — foresightful about the impossibility of tool-list-based bans.
- Required-AI assignments (7): curricular exercises may mandate AI use, clearly flagged — a deliberate pedagogical exposure valve inside an otherwise prohibitive policy.
- Curriculum-specific vocabulary (SEQs, Research Thesis, MD Thesis) shows integration with CWRU's actual program architecture; covers both University and College Programs.
- Clean governance metadata (approver, date, annual review, responsible committee).

## Distinctives
- Shortest full-policy document in cohort B after Morehouse (395 words) yet internally coherent: one principle (own work), one exception class (AI-required exercises), zero enumerations.
- No prohibited-use list, no PHI/HIPAA/clinical content, no citation/disclosure mechanism, no enforcement section, no tool names beyond ChatGPT example, no faculty duties.
- Blanket prohibition is outcome-based ("all content… must be their own work and not the product of generative AI") rather than permission-gated — no instructor-permission pathway for ordinary assignments, only purpose-built AI-required exercises.
- 2023 vintage: predates ambient scribes, agentic tools, enterprise AI portals — none contemplated.

## Provisional Classification: cautious-informed
Justification: not naive — it is deliberate, pedagogically articulate, governance-tagged, and contains a forward-looking exposure mechanism (AI-required exercises) plus an annual review commitment. But its substance is a clean default-deny: no permissions architecture, no clinical or data governance, no disclosure design. It reads as the best possible version of a short early ban: cautious-informed, at the permissive edge of that band thanks to clause 7. (Morehouse is conservative-naive by contrast; CWRU is the brief-but-thoughtful counterpart.)
