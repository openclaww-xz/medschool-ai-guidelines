# ECU Brody School of Medicine — Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence (Educational Policy / SOP)

## Authority / Authorship
- Publisher: East Carolina University Brody School of Medicine, Office of Academic Affairs (Educational Policies and SOPs page).
- Document control block: SOP # NA; Version 1.1; Implementation Date March 1, 2026; Last Reviewed/Update February 25, 2026; "Approval by EAC [Educational Affairs Committee] February 25, 2026"; Expiration Date February 25, 2029.
- Owner: "Office of Medical Education, with Compliance and IT Security." Review "every 36 months, or sooner if platform or regulatory changes occur"; emergency-update mechanism (OME interim updates notified within 5 business days, logged on policy page).
- Covers faculty and staff AI use, not only students — a full-community educational policy.

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "This policy establishes a framework for the responsible, ethical, and educationally sound use of generative AI by learners, faculty, and staff."
- Five guiding principles (verbatim): "1. Transparency – AI use must be visible to faculty to ensure authentic learning and assessment. 2. Privacy and Safety – No PHI, near-PHI, or identifiable data in non-approved tools. 3. Proportionality – Disclosure should match assignment stakes. 4. Equity of Access – Students must have free access to one approved tool. 5. Skill Development, Not Replacement – AI should aid, not replace reasoning."
- Rationale line: "Protects fairness, safety, and the learning mission." Tier rationale: "Ensures transparency, fairness, and accountability."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory
Metadata / disclaimer
1. "Policy Disclaimer (former policy): Green = Level 1; Yellow = Level 2; Red = Level 3" (traffic-light legacy from the prior version; the body keeps Level terminology).

Scope
2. "Applies to all educational activities at Brody School of Medicine, including pre-clinical, clinical, and administrative work where AI might be used."
3. "Covers written, multimedia, and clinical documentation. Includes faculty- and staff-generated teaching materials. Excludes research uses governed by separate IRB and research integrity policies."

Key Definitions
4. "Generative AI: Tools that produce new content (e.g., Copilot, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude)."
5. "Predictive AI: Tools that classify, analyze, or predict from existing data."
6. "PHI: Protected Health Information under HIPAA."
7. "Near-PHI: Information that could indirectly identify patients."
8. "Institutional Tool: AI systems reviewed and approved by ECU IT and Compliance."
9. "Non-Approved Tool: Any unapproved AI system; use prohibited for academic or clinical work."
10. "Methods Note: A short disclosure describing AI use in Level 2-tier assignments."
11. "AI Use Tier: The policy classification for permitted AI engagement within a course or activity: Level 1: Open use encouraged. Level 2: Conditional use with disclosure. Level 3: Restricted or prohibited use."

Responsibilities — Faculty / Course Directors / Staff
12. "Declare the AI tier for each assignment or assessment."
13. "Review AI-generated materials for accuracy, bias, and professionalism."
14. "Avoid uploading PHI or identifiable data to non-approved tools."
15. "Ensure equitable access to at least one approved AI tool."
16. "Provide equivalent non-AI learning options when AI use is encouraged."

Responsibilities — Students / Trainees
17. "Follow the declared AI tier."
18. "Submit a brief Methods Note for Level 2 assignments."
19. "Never use AI on Red-tier assignments or in proctored assessments."
20. "Never enter PHI or identifiable peer/faculty content into unapproved tools."
21. "Be prepared to explain submitted work verbally."

Tiered AI Use Framework
22. "Level 1 – AI supports clarity and structure; grammar, outlines, formatting. No disclosure required."
23. "Level 2 – AI contributes to content or summaries; background text, tables, slides. Requires Methods Note."
24. "Level 3 – AI prohibited for high-stakes, confidential, or PHI-related work."
25. "Every graded assignment must list its AI tier (Level 1, Level 2, or Level 3) in syllabi, LMS, or verbally."

Sample Syllabus Language (each verbatim)
26. Level 1: "You may use generative AI for brainstorming, outlining, clarity/grammar improvement, formatting to a provided structure, translation for understanding, drafting routine professional emails, and personal study aids (your own summaries, flashcards, practice questions). If AI does not add new facts or citations and the final wording is yours, no disclosure is required."
27. Level 2: "You may use generative AI to help draft or transform substantive content that appears in your submission (e.g., literature summaries, background paragraphs, tables/figures, slide bullets you keep). Include a brief Methods Note. If the AI added or changed factual content or citations, name one verification step; otherwise write 'N/A — reformative use only.' Be prepared to explain your reasoning."
28. Level 3: "No generative AI tools may be used to create, draft, or refine your submission for this assignment. You may not consult AI during closed-book or proctored assessments. You may not submit AI-generated answers as your own. You may not enter PHI or identifiable peer/faculty content into any AI tool. Violations will be addressed under Academic Integrity and Professionalism policies."
29. "Faculty may request a 'demonstration of understanding' for any AI-assisted assignment, which may include a process artifact (outline, notes, revisions, etc), or a short oral defense of understanding, comprehension, or reasoning."

Privacy, Security, and Professionalism
30. "Do not input real patient identifiers or sensitive educational content into AI tools unless PHI-approved."
31. "Do not impersonate others or fabricate data/citations using AI."
32. "Uploading peer or internal materials to AI tools is a professionalism violation."

Verification and Compliance
33. "Verification required when AI adds or changes factual, quantitative, or clinical content."
34. "Not required for grammar, tone, formatting, or ungraded personal study aids."
35. "Violations should be reported to the Office of Medical Education or Office of Student Affairs, and will be addressed under existing Academic Integrity and Professionalism policies."
36. "Of note, students should be aware of and comply with relevant AI policies if they are completing coursework, clerkships, or electives at outside institutions."

Methods Note
37. "Tool used, what AI generated, one verification step."
38. Example: "Tool: Copilot; AI drafted slides; verified dosing vs ADA 2024."

Faculty Use of AI
39. Level 1 (Faculty): "Draft rubrics/checklists; polish slide grammar/formatting; reorganize content for accessibility; convert text to plain language."
40. Level 2 (Faculty): "Generate first-pass teaching cases, slide bullets, practice or exam questions, or study guides; synthesize themes from non-identifiable learner misconceptions."
41. Required checks before use: "(1) Accuracy vs current guidelines/texts; (2) Bias review (avoid stereotypes; inclusive language); (3) Sensitivity review (avoid near-PHI; remove identifiable or distressing details); (4) Clarity rewrite in instructor's voice. These checks apply when AI-generated content conveys facts, numbers, or clinical reasoning used directly in instruction. If AI output is used only as scaffolding/formatting and is fully rewritten by the instructor, the checks may be abbreviated."
42. Faculty disclosure: "Add one line on slides/LMS: 'AI-assisted materials reviewed and finalized by [Instructor]. AI Tier for this course: [Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3].'"
43. "If Level 2, include a 2–3 line methods note with tool name, what AI generated, and one verification step when factual content is added."
44. Level 3 (Faculty): "Uploading OSCE cases, exam items, internal faculty slides not public, or identifiable student work to non-approved tools; releasing AI-generated materials without human review; using AI-generated content that reveals or mirrors assessment items."

Grading and Narrative Feedback
45. Permitted (Level 2): "Drafting narrative feedback (comment banks personalized by instructor), clustering themes, suggesting rubric-aligned comments."
46. Required: "Instructor reviews/edits/approves; ensure fairness; do not upload student work to non-approved tools."
47. Prohibited (Level 3): "Autonomous grading; AI-determined pass/fail; releasing feedback without human review; uploading grades or student artifacts to non-approved tools."
48. Syllabus line: "AI may assist instructors in drafting narrative feedback; final grades and comments are determined by the instructor after human review. Student work will not be uploaded to non-approved tools."

Approved Tools and Access

50. "Courses may restrict to specific approved tools for fairness."
51. "Until PHI-approved tools exist, assume no PHI entry is permitted."

## Sophistication Markers
- The clearest three-tier (traffic-light) system in the corpus, with ready-to-paste syllabus language per tier and a declared-tier mandate on every graded assignment (clauses 25, 55).
- Proportionality principle: disclosure requirements scale with stakes; no disclosure needed for Level 1 polish — avoids over-disclosure noise.
- "Methods Note" instrument: minimal three-element disclosure (tool / what AI generated / one verification step) with a worked example — the most usable disclosure artifact in cohort B.
- Equity of access as an explicit principle with a free-tool floor (Copilot) and a non-AI-alternatives duty (clauses 15, 16) — near-unique in the corpus.
- Full faculty-side tiering including grading/feedback governance: autonomous grading banned, human review required, artifact-upload bans.
- "Near-PHI" as a defined term; outside-institution policy-compliance duty for away rotations (clause 36); emergency amendment procedure; expiration date on the policy itself.
- Verification rule keyed to whether AI "added or changed factual, quantitative, or clinical content" — a functional trigger rather than a tool list.

## Distinctives
- Only policy in cohort B that extends identical tiering to faculty conduct, including narrative-feedback drafting and exam-item generation.
- "Demonstration of understanding" (oral defense / process artifact) as an alternative to surveillance — assessment-based integrity rather than detection-based.
- Former policy's Green/Yellow/Red disclaimer shows documented version evolution (v1.1) — evidence of live governance.
- Compact (SOP-style) but extremely dense: nearly every clause is operational rather than hortatory.

## Provisional Classification: pioneer
Justification: the tiered model is executed end-to-end — universal tier labels on all graded work, per-tier syllabus text, minimal-burden Methods Note, equity-of-access guarantees, non-AI alternatives, faculty parity including grading governance, verification triggers keyed to factual contribution, oral-defense integrity mechanism, and a genuine maintenance regime (36-month review, emergency updates, expiration). It is the reference implementation of the "tiered" policy model that most other schools gesture toward; nothing in cohort B matches its completeness except GW's disclosure apparatus (which lacks tiers) and Buffalo's clinical-supervision depth (which lacks tiering and equity).
