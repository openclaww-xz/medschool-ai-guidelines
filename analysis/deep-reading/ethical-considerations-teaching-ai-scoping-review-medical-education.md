# Ethical Considerations for Teaching with AI: Scoping Review in Medical Education Settings

## Research question
(1) How have publications on ethical considerations for teaching with AI in medical education developed over time? (2) What are the key ethical considerations highlighted?

## Methods
PRISMA-ScR scoping review; PubMed, Embase, Web of Science; 2018–Oct 2023; English; inductive codebook, dual coding to 100% consensus; chi-square test of focus-area × ethical-consideration independence.

## Sample
82 included articles (from 193 search hits; 76 duplicates removed, 35 excluded at screening). 50 journals; JMIR Med Ed (10), Academic Medicine (5), BMC Med Ed (4), Cureus (4) lead. 27/82 (33%) US first authors; Canada 7, UK 7, Germany/Oman/Turkey 4 each.

## Key findings
- Article types: reviews 37%, empirical 29%, editorials 28%, theoretical 6% — field still mostly synthesis, not data.
- ~87% of articles published 2021–2023; rate doubled 2022→2023.
- Focus areas: incorporating AI in med ed 37%; AI ethical principles 26%; ChatGPT in healthcare ed 12%; AI readiness evaluation 9%; challenges/opportunities 7%; remainder competency/training/student perspectives/case-based.
- Ethical considerations: bias 74%, privacy 65%, transparency 44%, informed consent 37%, responsibility 40%, accountability 30%, access 34%, fairness 27%, discrimination 22%, surveillance 12%, non-maleficence 15%.
- No significant association between focus area and ethical considerations, χ²(16)=14.37, p=0.98.
- Authors repeatedly note "absence of standardized ethical frameworks" and insufficient institutional policy support as implementation barriers.

## What it says about who leads policy
National bodies are named as anchor points (AMA 2023 AI principles; AAMC 2025 principles on transparency, equity, accountability; AMEE Guide 158; GDPR/HIPAA as compliance floor), but the review found no systematic inventory of institutional policies — governance is called for, not described.

## Limitations
Three databases, English-only; subjective qualitative coding; literature cutoff Oct 2023 (pre-dates most institutional policy issuance); no quality appraisal (scoping review).

## Relation to corpus findings
Directly corroborates the Clin Teach policy-gap study: this review independently concludes the med-ed AI ethics literature is dominated by reviews/editorials (65%) with little empirical work, and that standardized frameworks are missing. Our 378-source corpus answers precisely the question this review flags as open: what policies institutions actually issued, when, and what they contain — content this literature can only cite anecdotally.
