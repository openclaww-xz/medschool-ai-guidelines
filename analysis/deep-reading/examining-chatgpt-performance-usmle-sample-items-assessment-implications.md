# ChatGPT Performance on USMLE Sample Items and Implications for Assessment (Academic Medicine, 2023, NBME authors)

## Research question
How does ChatGPT 3.5 perform on USMLE sample items, and what does that imply for assessment and licensing?

## Methods
377 publicly released USMLE sample items (120 Step 1, 120 Step 2 CK, 137 Step 3) presented verbatim 3x each to ChatGPT 3.5 (Feb–Mar 2023); dual-rater scoring; correlation with examinee item-difficulty P-values; permutation tests by physician-task competency with Holm–Bonferroni correction. NBME-internal study.

## Sample
377 items; text-only subset 325; 52 items with nontext components.

## Key findings (with counts)
- Step 1: 60.8–64.2% correct; Step 2 CK: 67.5–71.7%; Step 3: 58.4–63.5% (sample easier than operational; Step 3 would likely fall below 60% operational).
- Text-only performance uniformly higher; items with nontext components still answered far above chance (ChatGPT "imagined" graphs).
- Inconsistent across replications on 76/377 (20%) items.
- Item difficulty correlation with human examinees weak (mean r: 0.22, 0.23, 0.03).
- Significantly worse on practice-based learning items (32% vs 67% correct, p<0.001).
- ChatGPT "performed well below a typical USMLE examinee" (LCME first-timers ~2 SD above passing standard).

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Explicit: testing organizations (NBME/USMLE) have a public-protection duty to re-evaluate assessment validity as AI changes learning — "testing organizations must reevaluate the alignment between the form and content of their tests and the function of those tests." Assessment bodies are an identified policy leader. Caution against promoting ChatGPT as learning aid without expert review.

## Limitations
Sample items not operational forms; GPT-3.5 only (pre-multimodal); no simulation component; approximate passing comparisons.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
Matches our national-framework corpus (NBME "AI in Assessment" executive summary; NBME-USMLE ChatGPT discussion): NBME itself leading the assessment-integrity response. Supports school-policy emphasis on assessment restrictions as the most common and strictest provision in our school corpus. Note the 2023 vintage — later model comparisons in corpus show passing became trivial; this is the baseline datum the field cites.
