# Exploring Applications of AI Tools in Clinical Care and HPE: An Online Module for Students (MedEdPORTAL, 2025)

## Research question
Can a 30-minute asynchronous online module improve first-year health professions students' foundational AI knowledge?

## Methods
Curricular innovation report (MedEdPORTAL): multidisciplinary working group; Articulate 360 module with pre/post 10-item knowledge test and feedback survey; IRB-exempt; distributed via LMS as required assignment.

## Sample
164 first-year medical students completing both tests (of 200 distributed); 144 feedback respondents. Piloted at U Miami Miller.

## Key findings (with counts)
- Knowledge: 74% → 87% mean correct (p<0.001).
- Quality: 129/135 (96%) rated good–excellent; 92 (69%) completed in 15–30 min; 108/119 (91%) saw career applicability.
- Hardest items: VR/AR distinctions, billing/reimbursement applications; easiest: EHR/administration.
- Notes no freely available equivalent module existed on MedEdPORTAL; AMA modules require membership; NAM recommended "core curricula" for AI/data science.
- Authors deployed it as prelearning for a 4-hour in-person AI workshop with reflective narrative components.

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Cites NAM recommendation as the policy anchor ("health professions schools should incorporate core curricula") and notes AMA's member-only modules as a barrier — i.e., national bodies recommend but provide gated resources; individual schools must build/borrow curriculum. Curriculum leadership is institutional, seeded by national recommendation.

## Limitations
Single institution; first-year med students only (nursing/PT not yet included in pilot); knowledge test is author-written 10 items; rapidly dating content; no long-term retention data.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
Corroborates our national-framework corpus: AMA produces educator resources but access-gated; NAM/AAMC issue recommendations without enforcement. Also matches our school-corpus finding that most schools lack structured AI curriculum (policies dominate; curriculum lags). Useful datapoint for the "effectiveness" section of synthesis: single 30-min module yields +13pp knowledge — low bar, modest effect.
