# FSMB — Press release: Recommendations on Responsible and Ethical Incorporation of AI into Clinical Practice (May 2, 2024)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: Federation of State Medical Boards (FSMB). The underlying report ("Navigating the Responsible and Ethical Incorporation of Artificial Intelligence into Clinical Practice") was **drafted by FSMB's Ethics and Professionalism Committee, chaired by Mark Woodland, MS, MD**, and **adopted by FSMB's House of Delegates** (members from each of the nation's 70 state medical and osteopathic boards) at the 112th Annual Meeting, Nashville, TN.
- Powers claimed: FSMB is "the voice of state medical boards"; the report "recommends best practices for state medical boards in governing the use of AI in clinical care." This is the strongest regulatory pathway in the corpus — recommendations addressed to the bodies that license and discipline physicians. It binds state boards only insofar as they choose to adopt; it binds physicians via future board action.
- CEO Humayun Chaudhry, DO, MACP anchors the release.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **risk-first-then-adoption / accountability-centered**. Framing quotes:
  - Chaudhry: "These guidelines are some of the first that clearly outline steps physicians can take to **meet their ethical and professional duties when using AI** to assist in the delivery of care."
  - "...we hope that this policy will **reduce the risk of harm to patients** and guide physicians by providing recommendations for state medical boards on how to promote safe and effective incorporation of AI into medical practice in a manner that **prioritizes patient wellbeing**."
- The default frame is professional duty and patient safety, with adoption encouraged once liability and accountability are resolved ("mitigate liability concerns that may hinder adoption of beneficial technologies").

## (c) Complete clause inventory
Normative/directional statements in the release (the full clauses live in the companion report; these are the release-level claims):
- Scope: report "recommending best practices for state medical boards in governing the use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in clinical care."
- Accountability theme: "The policy focuses on **providing accountability for the use of AI tools in different clinical settings in alignment with fundamental principles of professionalism**."
- Multi-stakeholder guidance: "The policy also provides guidance for **physicians, health systems, and developers** to mitigate liability concerns that may hinder adoption of beneficial technologies."
- Education mandate: "recommends **requiring continuing medical education focused on AI in healthcare**" — one of the strongest "require" formulations in the corpus (CME as a regulatory requirement, addressed to boards).
- Collaboration: "calls for **greater collaboration between federal and state policymakers, as well as between regulators and developers** of the technology."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Technology understanding: not displayed in the release itself (no hallucination/bias/drift vocabulary) — but the governance architecture it implies (physician accountability + health system + developer triad) shows structural sophistication.
- Evidence: none cited in the release.
- Mandates: gestures toward a mandate (required CME) but the actual mandating power sits with state boards. The release is a claims document; substance is in the full report (companion source in this corpus).

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- **Only body in the corpus with direct regulatory reach** (licensure/discipline via 70 state boards). AMA/ACP/AAP write ethics; FSMB writes state-enforceable guidance.
- Earliest mover (May 2024) among the clinical-practice regulators here; Chaudhry's "some of the first" claim is itself a leadership assertion.
- Names its drafting committee and chair — authorship transparency unusual in this corpus.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** — adoption-friendly but procedurally serious: accountability-first, liability-mitigating, CME-requiring. Not pioneer because the release-level document defers substance to the full report; the regulatory machinery it activates, however, makes FSMB the most consequential follower of the ethicist lead.
