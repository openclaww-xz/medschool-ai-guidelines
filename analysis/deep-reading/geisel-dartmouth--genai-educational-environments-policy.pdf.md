# Geisel School of Medicine at Dartmouth — GenAI in Educational Environments Policy (UME-CNTRL-0019)

## Authority / Authorship
- Numbered policy UME-CNTRL-0019; Effective August 1, 2025; Last Date Approved August 20, 2025.
- Authorized by Associate Dean for Medical Education + Associate Dean for Health Sciences Education; Approved by Dean, Educational Affairs (Sonia N. Chimienti, MD — named contact with email).
- Rich documented lineage: drafted 4/23/25–6/15/25 with input from Geisel deans/faculty/staff; MEC review 6/18/25; Dean's Academic Board 6/19/25; Faculty Council 8/7/25.
- **Disclosed AI drafting**: "GPT 4.1 mini 2025-04-14. Response to prompt: 'create a medical school policy on the use of generative artificial intelligence in the educational environment' (first draft generated April 23, 2025, https://chat.dartmouth.edu/)" — first draft generated by GPT-4.1-mini on Dartmouth's institutional chat gateway.
- Grounded in AAMC "Principles for the Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence in and for Medical Education" (Jan 3, 2025), all seven principles reproduced verbatim.

## Policy Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- Purposes: "1) to support the development of course materials, including learning objectives, 2) to maintain academic integrity with assignments and assessments, and 3) to respect privacy and confidentiality."
- "Ensure that generative AI tools are used to enhance learning and not to replace critical thinking and clinical judgment."
- Instructors "are encouraged to integrate generative AI tools into their delivery of the curriculum in a way that encourages critical thinking and enhances learning outcomes, to support but not replace critical thinking and clinical judgement."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory by Theme

### Scope
- "This policy applies to all Geisel students and instructors, across medical and health sciences degree programs, who use generative AI technologies in their educational activities... including... the development, delivery, and completion of coursework and clinical training."
- "Geisel students and instructors are responsible for critically assessing the accuracy, authenticity, and quality of their materials and assignments and for understanding the limitations of AI tools which could potentially misinformation, inaccuracies, or fabrications."

### Definitions
- "Generative AI (GenAI): A subset of AI technologies that can create new content, such as text, images, audio, or data, based on patterns learned from existing information... Specific examples include - but are not limited to - OpenAI, ChatGPT, chatbots, or any other Large Language Model or Generative Pre-Trained Transformer." (Note: lists "OpenAI" as an example tool — imprecise.)

### Instructor-side / curricular development (encouragement)
- "Generative AI tools may be used by instructors to assist in the creation of educational content, to develop supplemental educational materials, and to provide personalized learning experiences."
- "Instructors are encouraged to take advantage of guidance, support, and training on the ethical use of generative AI in medical and health sciences education."
- IP objective: "Protect the intellectual property (IP) of all instructors."

### Academic integrity (prohibition-default)
- "Unless explicitly stated by the instructor, students are not permitted to utilize generative AI for the completion of graded/assessed coursework, in the classroom or clinical settings."
- "The use of generative AI to draft or complete assignments, exams, or other assessments without proper attribution is considered misrepresentation of scholarly work, plagiarism, academic misconduct, and a violation of Geisel's Student Honor Code."

### Disclosure / citation
- "If/when the use of generative AI is permitted by the instructor, students must disclose and appropriately cite the use of generative AI tools in all of their academic work."
- "Students should be aware that courses differ in expectations regarding the use of AI."
- "If/when allowed by the instructor, students must disclose their use of AI as directed in a course syllabus or assignment instructions."
- "Just as they would when citing or referencing any other source, students may not use entire sentences or paragraphs suggested by an AI app without providing quotation marks and a citation."
- Citation format given: "OpenAI, ChatGPT. Response to prompt: 'Explain what is meant by the term "Triple Bottom Line"' (February 15, 2023, https://chat.openai.com/)."

### Privacy and confidentiality
- "GenAI-generated data must be managed with the same responsibility, security, and integrity as any other education, research, and patient data."
- "Unless explicitly allowed by the instructor, uploading Geisel course materials to local or cloud-based generative AI systems such as ChatGPT by medical students and health sciences students is forbidden."
- "Generative AI tools must be used in compliance with privacy laws and regulations (HIPPA and FERPA), including the protection of intellectual property (IP)." [sic — "HIPPA" misspelled in source]
- "Users must ensure that any data input into generative AI tools is de-identified and does not contain sensitive or confidential information pertaining to patients, students or any member of the Geisel community."

### Monitoring and compliance
- "Geisel has standing curriculum and academic progress committees that will monitor the use of generative AI and ensure compliance with this policy."
- "Violations of this policy will be addressed through the school's existing academic misconduct procedures."

### Review
- "This policy will be reviewed at least annually and updated as necessary to reflect rapid advancements in generative AI technologies, as well as any changes in legal and ethical standards."
- Portfolio alignment: "This policy is considered in the context of a portfolio of relevant policies across the Dartmouth community... and aligns with other institutional policies referencing the use of generative AI."
