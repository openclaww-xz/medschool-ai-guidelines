# GenAI in Medical Training: Utilization Patterns Across Knowledge, Patient Care, Systems Reasoning, Innovation (Medical Science Educator, 2026)

## Research question
How do medical trainees at different phases (preclinical, clinical, residency) utilize generative AI, and which platforms?

## Methods
Cross-sectional 18-item novel survey (Feb–May 2025), Qualtrics, anonymous; Kruskal–Wallis H tests across training levels; IRB approved.

## Sample
49/347 eligible trainees (36 students, 13 residents) at Carle Illinois COM (engineering-integrated curriculum) and Carle Health; 76% aged 20–29.

## Key findings (with counts)
- Policy awareness: 92% unaware/unsure of med school AI policy; 94% unaware of health system policy.
- Platforms: ChatGPT 85.7%, Open Evidence 38.8%, Gemini 36.7%, Anki Hub AI 8.2% (0 residents).
- 100% of AI users use it for medical-knowledge tasks (presentations 20%, review questions 17%, exam prep ~32% combined); 74% for patient care tasks (dx 19%, management 18%, interactions 15%); 52% for health-systems reasoning; 88% for research/innovation.
- Residents rank AI more useful for patient care than students (H=9.48, p=0.009).
- Verification: 67% cross-reference with validated sources most/always; 60% of residents vs 45% of students check bias; 72% of M3/M4+residents verify inputs.
- Free vs paid tiers unknown — equity concern flagged.

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Striking datum: near-total policy unawareness (92–94%) despite (presumably) existing institutional policy — a policy-communication/leadership failure, not just absence. Authors call for institutional onboarding and policy education.

## Limitations
Single institution (atypical engineering-focused school); 14% response rate; uneven training-level distribution; self-report; voluntary.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
Partially contradicts the simple "policy gap" framing of the Clin Teach study: even where policies exist (our corpus documents many), trainees don't know them — implementation gap vs existence gap. Supports our corpus's emphasis on disclosure/verification norms being policy centrepieces. Small-N caveat is serious; our corpus can't fix low survey N but can quantify how many institutional policies actually reach students (handbook vs LMS vs orientation).
