# GMC — Artificial intelligence and innovative technologies (Q&A learning resource)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: General Medical Council (UK statutory regulator of doctors, physician associates, anaesthesia associates). Genre: learning-materials Q&A explaining how existing professional standards apply to AI.
- Jurisdictional humility clause verbatim: "**We do not regulate or approve new technologies or medical devices. That is the role of the Medicine and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA).** Instead, we set the values, knowledge, skills, and behaviours expected of all doctors... And we help them to meet and understand these professional standards by providing guidance and advice."
- Process transparency: "We supported our public consultation with a **specialist policy workshop on digital technologies** with key experts from within the sector" when revising Good medical practice.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: the **professional-judgment model** — no AI-specific rules; existing standards stretch to cover AI. Key framing verbatim: "As with other medical devices, we expect doctors... to **use their professional judgment to apply the principles in our guidance** to the use of innovative technologies or AI tools."
- Responsibility allocation: "We recognise that not all doctors... are involved in the development, procurement and ongoing updating of these new technologies. We expect **those who have been involved in the creation, testing and updating of these technologies, whether developers, employers or doctors, to remain responsible for these aspects once they are in use**." And: "Doctors... are **responsible for the decisions they take** when using new technologies like AI, and the principles in our professional standards continue to apply."
- Meta-framing: "Our guidance provides a **high-level framework** which doctors... can apply in different settings. This will apply both now and in the future, and as innovative technologies continue to develop." — designed for technological churn.

## (c) Complete clause inventory
1. **Impact clause**: "It's hard to anticipate the full extent... their impact will be significant." Examples: wearables; AR/VR for surgery; virtual/remote care; "AI can now also support doctors... when reading a variety of different imaging types, including X-rays, CT, and MRI scans, including mammograms"; virtual wards with remote monitoring.
2. **Support clause**: "We want to support doctors, physician associates and anaesthesia associates through these changes, so they can continue to provide a high standard of care."
3. **Division-of-labor clause** (verbatim in (a)): MHRA regulates devices; GMC sets professional standards.
4. **GMP-2024 amendment clause**: "Good medical practice 2024 includes reference to **software, diagnostic tests, and digital tools as examples of medical devices for the purpose of adverse incident reporting**. This would also include software, diagnostic tests, and digital tools that use AI technologies."
5. **Paragraph 73 verbatim**: "'To help keep patients safe you must: **report adverse incidents involving medical devices (including software, diagnostic tests, and digital tools) that put the safety of a patient or another person at risk, or have the potential to do so**'" — a duty with "must" force, extended to AI tools.
6. **Rationale clause**: consultation feedback said guidance "could be clearer" on adverse-incident reporting for software/apps/AI; specialist policy workshop informed the revision.
7. **Professional-judgment clause** (verbatim in (b)).
8. **Developer-responsibility clause** (verbatim in (b)).
9. **Consent/patient-conversation clause**: "it's important to **discuss the use of innovative technologies with patients, what other options may be available and any uncertainties and limitations, so they can make informed decisions**. This is in line with... Decision making and consent."
10. **Escalation clause**: "If a doctor... is concerned that the technology or system they are being asked to use may put patients at risk of harm, they should follow our advice in **Raising and acting on concerns about patient safety**. We can't tell doctors... what to do in individual situations, but they should **put their patients at the heart of their decision making**."
11. **Competence clause**: "expected to **recognise and work within the limits of their competence**" (GMP paragraphs 3 and 13); "keep up to date... take steps to develop and improve their performance... likely to be expected to take part in education and training to understand how to use them in practice."
12. **Partnership clause**: "We will continue to work with other partners, including the MHRA, our registrants and the other health and care regulators... while maintaining patient safety."
13. Resources: NHS Digital Transformation intro; HEE AI & Digital Healthcare Technologies framework; NHS AI Lab Ethics Initiative; NHS Transformation Directorate AI pages.

## (d) Sophistication markers
- The regulatory-division clarity (GMC = conduct; MHRA = product) is the cleanest jurisdictional statement in the corpus — most documents conflate the two.
- Amending GMP paragraph 73 to enumerate "software, diagnostic tests, and digital tools" inside a pre-existing mandatory reporting duty = rule-extension by enumeration, not new rule creation — minimal-drafting regulatory technique.
- Explicit refusal of case-by-case directives ("We can't tell doctors... what to do in individual situations") while retaining principle-based accountability — deliberate regulator restraint.
- Limits-of-competence hook (paras 3, 13) reused for AI skills + anticipated CPD duty.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- This is the archetype of the **UK professional-judgment model** the synthesis tests: technology-neutral standards + mandatory incident reporting + consent conversations, versus Singapore's product-lifecycle regulation and Canada's task-force advocacy. GMC writes almost nothing new for AI — and says so.
- Companion pieces: joint regulators' education statement (education side of the same model) and GMP 2024 (the standards text itself, separate note).

## (f) Provisional classification
**cautious-informed** — deliberately minimal, jurisdiction-aware, professionally coherent; not technically deep (no failure-mode taxonomy — that lives with MHRA/NHS England), but fully aware of where its authority ends. A masterclass in regulator lane discipline.
