# GW SMHS — Guidelines for the Use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) for the MD Program

## Authority / Authorship
- Publisher: GW School of Medicine & Health Sciences; MD-program policy page. "Established in April 2026, these AI use guidelines were created by a multidisciplinary task force of MD program faculty, staff, and students, chaired by Ioannis Koutroulis, MD, PhD, MBA."
- Approval: "These guidelines take effect upon approval by the GW SMHS Committee on Undergraduate Medical Education (CUMEC)."
- Unusual transparency: a closing footnote discloses the task force's own AI use — "the task force used Microsoft Copilot to (1) brainstorm and generate initial ideas, (2) help structure an outline and generate examples… all conceptualization, interpretation, and argumentation were developed by the taskforce… They take full responsibility for the final content."

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- Destigmatizing disclosure: "To address the concern that disclosure may feel dangerous or stigmatizing, the task force encourages using standardized, neutral disclosure language so that students, faculty, and staff are not asked to 'confess' AI use, but simply to document it in the same way they might document other learning supports or productivity tools."
- Professionalizing transparency: "Faculty development will emphasize that appropriate, disclosed AI use when allowed should not be interpreted as laziness or lack of preparedness. Instead, transparency should be understood as a marker of professionalism."
- Disclosure-safety rule: "The guidelines will also be explicit that disclosure of permitted AI use, by itself, should not result in disciplinary or professionalism actions. Consequences apply to inappropriate or undisclosed misuse, particularly where patient safety, confidentiality, or academic integrity is at risk by the lack of compliance with other existing policies or course rules."
- "Equity/safety principle: AI outputs must be critically evaluated for bias, inequity, inaccuracy, or potential educational harm." Accessibility principle requires AI tools be accessible and inclusive, accommodations maintained, and GWU-approved tools recommended "to minimize financial burdens."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory
Purpose / framing
1. "The purpose of these guidelines is to establish clear expectations for the responsible, ethical, and professional use of Artificial Intelligence (AI), including Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) tools, within the MD Program."
2. "We also aim to draw very clear distinctions between permitted, limited, and prohibited uses of AI in each course or clerkship. When expectations are explicit, disclosure becomes safer and more routine."
3. "Equally important, we recognize that faculty interpretation plays a critical role."

Scope
4. "These guidelines apply exclusively to Undergraduate Medical Education (UME), including all pre-clerkship, clerkship, and advanced clinical phases of the MD Program."
5. "These guidelines do not apply to Graduate Medical Education (GME), residency or fellowship training, admissions or selection processes, faculty employment decisions, or clinical operations, all of which are governed by separate institutional, hospital, or accrediting-body policies."

Definitions
6. "Artificial Intelligence (AI): Computational systems capable of performing tasks that typically require human intelligence, including reasoning, pattern recognition, language generation, and evaluation."
7. "Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI): A subset of AI tools capable of generating text, images, code, questions, feedback, or other content based on user prompts (e.g., large language models)."

Guiding Principles
8. "Professional Accountability: AI tools may support educational activities, but accountability for accuracy, judgment, and ethical conduct always rests with the human user."
9. "Academic Integrity and Intellectual Ownership: All work submitted for evaluation or delivered instructionally must reflect the user's own understanding, reasoning, and scholarly contribution."
10. "Transparency and Disclosure: Use of AI must be disclosed clearly and appropriately."
11. "Bias Awareness, and Learner Safety: AI outputs must be critically evaluated for bias, inequity, inaccuracy, or potential educational harm."
12. "Educational Primacy: AI should augment, not replace, the development of foundational knowledge and skills, clinical reasoning, professionalism, and ethical judgment."
13. "Security: All AI use must comply with GW data classification, privacy, FERPA, HIPAA, and information security requirements."
14. "Accessibility: Faculty and instructional staff must ensure that any AI tools used for teaching, learning, assessment, or educational support are accessible and inclusive… AI-enabled educational materials should not create barriers for learners with disabilities, and reasonable accommodations must be maintained regardless of the use of AI technologies. Faculty should also consider recommending GWU Approved AI Tools to minimize financial burdens associated with students using AI tools in courses where possible. Responsibility for accessibility remains with the faculty member or course director utilizing the AI tool."

Relationship to other policies
15. "Use of AI within the MD Program is governed by, and explicitly linked to, the MD Program Code of Conduct in the Learning Environment policy, medical student professionalism standards, and applicable university-wide (e.g., FERPA) or healthcare organization-wide policies (e.g., HIPAA)… Violations… may result in remediation, grading consequences, course failure, or referral to appropriate accountability offices. HIPAA violations are a serious offense, and patient data should be protected in all cases."

Appropriate Uses (students, "as authorized by faculty/course director")
16. "Brainstorming ideas or outlining approaches; Clarifying complex concepts for personal learning; Editing for grammar, clarity, or organization; Literature search and summarization of articles/research; Generating practice questions or explanations for self-study (not submission)."
17. Faculty/staff appropriate uses: "Drafting lecture outlines or educational materials, with independent verification; Supporting curriculum design, instructional planning, or course administration; Generating exam questions; Assisting with evaluative educational workflows."
18. "AI-generated content can be inaccurate, misleading, or entirely fabricated and may contain copyrighted material. In all cases, users are responsible for verifying accuracy, relevance, and alignment with learning objectives."
19. "The use of AI tools should occur at an appropriate point in the learning process and should not prematurely replace critical cognitive tasks such as independent analysis, synthesis, or clinical reasoning. Faculty are responsible when necessary for clearly communicating when AI use is permitted or restricted, within specific phases of instruction, assessment preparation, or clinical education."
20. "Clinical training sites may maintain additional policies, procedures, or restrictions governing the use of AI tools. Medical students, faculty, and staff are required to comply with all site-specific guidelines or policies when participating in educational activities at affiliated hospitals, clinics, or other clinical learning environments."

Inappropriate Uses ("Unless explicitly authorized in writing")
21. "Submitting AI-generated content as one's own intellectual work or without disclosure (will be considered plagiarism) - see MD Program Code of Conduct in the Learning Environment policy."
22. "Using AI tools during examinations, quizzes, or graded assessments unless otherwise specified."
23. "Using fabricated citations, references, data, or sources."
24. "Using AI to mask lack of understanding or substitute for required learning activities or goals."
25. "Entering protected health information, student records, or confidential institutional data into AI platforms without prior approval. Information provided in prompts to AI tools is often stored by the software vendor to be used to train AI models."
26. "Public or non-approved AI tools may not be used to upload educational materials."
27. Faculty/staff guidelines: "Faculty and staff retain responsibility for curriculum, assessment, grading, and feedback. AI may be used to enhance efficiency and educational quality but not to replace human judgment. Faculty and staff AI use must comply with FERPA, HIPAA, and GW IT security requirements. Courses and clerkships should publish clear parameters and expectations for students regarding AI use or refer back to these guidelines as needed. Public or non-approved AI tools may not be used to upload student work or generate grades. AI may assist with drafting formative feedback or learning resources only within GW-approved tools and with human review. Fully automated grading or summative assessment by AI is not appropriate. Faculty and staff should be transparent with students when AI is used in teaching, assessment, or feedback."

Disclosure Requirements
28. "When AI meaningfully contributes to work submitted for evaluation, instruction, or dissemination, disclosure must include: The name and version of the AI tool used; The purpose and nature of use; The user's intellectual role in reviewing, revising, and finalizing the material."
29. "For formal citations, follow the applicable guidelines for citing AI (e.g., APA, AMA)."
30. "Disclosure alone does not make AI use acceptable; the work must still demonstrate intellectual ownership and professional judgment. Disclosure alone should also not make individuals subject to disciplinary action. Course directors/faculty may request in advance that AI users save a log of relevant prompts and output so that they could be provided in the event of further review of AI use."

Faculty/Staff Disclosure
31. "Course directors are encouraged to link these guidelines in their course syllabus. Course directors/instructors should determine and inform students in which parts of the course they are allowed/not allowed to use AI. Depending on the needs and goals/objectives of each course, the instructional team may be more restrictive on AI use but should not issue blanket bans on AI use without permission by the Office of Medical Education." (second anti-ban clause in cohort B, with Buffalo)
32. "Faculty should include in their course syllabus a reference to the MD program AI guidelines with specific instructions… (Appendix 2)."
33. Faculty disclosure triggers: "Lecture slides, presentations, or recorded instructional materials; Educational content delivered to students; Assessment item development (e.g., exam questions, cases, rubrics); Automated or semi-automated scoring, feedback, compilation of narrative comments or learning analytics."
34. "Disclosure should be proportionate and transparent (e.g., syllabus statements, slide footnotes, or assessment notes). Faculty remain fully responsible for the accuracy of content, mitigation of bias, fairness of assessments, and integrity of grading decisions, regardless of AI assistance."

Data Privacy and Security
35. "Only institutionally approved AI tools may be used with sensitive or regulated educational data. Public AI platforms must not be used for student records, assessment materials prior to administration, or confidential institutional information. All users must comply with university IT, cybersecurity, and data governance policies. Activity conducted through institutionally provided AI tools may be logged, retained, and reviewed by the university and its vendors to ensure compliance with institutional policies. Users should not assume confidentiality."
36. Oversight: "These MD Program-specific guidelines will be reviewed as needed and as determined by the Office of Medical Education and relevant educational governance or accrediting bodies. Questions, exceptions, or ambiguities regarding AI use should be referred to the Office of Medical Education to ensure consistent, principled decision-making."
37. Reinforcement: "course syllabi, clerkship orientation materials, faculty development sessions, and learner professionalism communications."

Appendix 1 — Disclosure framework (adapted from Cleland, Driessen, Masters, Lingard, Maggio, AMEE Guide No. 192, Medical Teacher 2025), verbatim elements:
38. "Specify the AI model used…; Describe the specific activities for which AI was used…; Declare consent if participant data were uploaded…; Describe data-protection measures taken…; State when AI was NOT used (when relevant)…; Reference relevant journal, publisher, or institutional AI policies…; Include a final statement of responsibility…; Maintain a record of prompts and interactions…"
39. Fill-in-the-blank disclosure template with eight fields (tool, activities, consent, data protection, what AI was NOT used for, compliance statement, responsibility statement, location of prompts/records).

Appendix 2 — Model syllabus language
40. Permissive model: "you are permitted to use such tools to generate content submitted for evaluation in this course, including [papers; take-home examinations; specified other assignments]… You remain responsible for all content you submit for evaluation."
41. Pitfall model: "Beware that use may also stifle your own independent thinking and creativity."
42. Citation model with example: "text generated using ChatGPT-4 should include a citation such as: 'ChatGPT-4. (YYYY, Month DD of query). "Text of your query." Generated using OpenAI. https://chat.openai.com/.' … Failure to do so in this course constitutes failure to attribute under the GW MD Program Code of Conduct in the Learning Environment policy."

Appendix 3 — Student professionalism scenario table (six rows: undisclosed AI assistance; AI-generated responses for graded assessments; entering patient information into public AI tools; unapproved AI use during clinical rotations; fabricated/AI-hallucinated citations; repeated or escalating AI misuse — each with typical policy language, example behavior, rationale, common institutional response, educational emphasis).
43. Representative entries: "Permitted AI use must be disclosed when allowed; failure to disclose constitutes a professionalism lapse… Professionalism review; documented warning if repeated." / "AI may not be used for graded work unless explicitly authorized… Academic integrity process and professionalism remediation." / "PHI* or identifiable patient data may not be entered into public or non-approved AI tools… Professionalism and privacy review; required HIPAA remediation." / "AI use in clinical settings must be explicitly approved and not replace supervision… Clerkship counseling and professionalism evaluation impact." / "Patterns of misuse may indicate professionalism deficiencies… Referral to professionalism or promotions committee."

Appendix 4 — Faculty poor-modeling table (undisclosed AI teaching materials; uploading prior year's exam+key; entering student grade info into public AI; unapproved AI scribe/ambient dictation in patient care = HIPAA violation; unverified AI-generated images).

Appendix 5 — Data classification (Regulated / Restricted / Public) with six worked scenarios:
44. Key takeaway (verbatim): "Regulated data (PHI, FERPA records) must never be entered into public AI tools. Restricted data (non-public GW materials) may only be used in approved, protected AI environments. Public data may be used with AI tools, subject to course and assessment rules."
45. PHI scenario hard line: "This use is never permitted unless there is approval by institutional officials or regulatory bodies even if the AI tool developers claim that it is HIPAA compliant."
46. FERPA scenario: student grades/evaluations into AI — "never permitted in public or non-approved AI tools"; alternatives: "Fully de-identified student data before use; Institutionally approved AI tools (e.g., Box)."
47. Restricted-data scenarios (lecture slides/exam review/OSCE prompts; curriculum committee notes): use allowed "only in GW-licensed AI environments with contractual data protections, or Future GW local tools that do not retain or train on uploaded content."

## Sophistication Markers
- The most developed disclosure infrastructure in the corpus: principles, mandatory disclosure elements, AMEE-Guide-192-derived best-practice framework, fill-in-the-blank template, syllabus citation exemplar, and prompt-log retention expectation (clause 30).
- Disclosure-safety guarantee (clause 30: disclosed permitted use cannot itself trigger discipline) + faculty-development commitment against "confession" framing — a genuine cultural-intervention design, not just rules.
- Data-classification appendix with worked scenarios (PHI/FERPA/restricted/public) — the only policy in cohort B teaching data taxonomy rather than just asserting "no PHI."
- Anti-HIPAA-washing clause (45): vendor claims of HIPAA compliance do not suffice — unusually sharp.
- Anti-ban safeguard (clause 31) mirroring Buffalo's.
- Accessibility + financial-burden principle (clause 14) — equity of access articulated at guideline level.
- Professionalism tables map behaviors to responses and educational emphases — operational bridge from policy to UVME professionalism systems.
- Self-disclosure of task-force AI use with responsibility statement — models its own disclosure norms.
- Cites scholarly source (AMEE Guide 192) for disclosure practice.

## Distinctives
- Longest and most apparatus-rich document in cohort B (606 lines incl. appendices); genre = "guidelines" deliberately, softer than policy, task-force authored.
- No tier labels — "permitted, limited, prohibited" distinctions exist (clause 2) but are not systematized into levels as at ECU.
- Fully automated grading banned for faculty; faculty disclosure duties specified for slides/assessment/feedback.
- Clinical-site deference clause (20); GME exclusion explicit.
- Uses "confess" language critique — rhetorically self-aware policy design.

## Provisional Classification: pioneer
Justification: matches ECU's completeness on a different axis. Where ECU perfects tiering, GW perfects disclosure culture and data classification: mandatory disclosure elements + template + AMEE-derived framework, a no-punishment-for-disclosure guarantee, faculty development strategy, scenario-based professionalism tables, data taxonomy with hard-lines (including against vendor HIPAA claims), accessibility/financial-equity provisions, anti-ban safeguard, and self-transparent authorship. Classed pioneer with a caveat: absent universal tier labels and syllabus mandates, instructors must assemble their own per-course posture, so implementation consistency depends on faculty uptake — the one dimension where ECU's design is stronger.
