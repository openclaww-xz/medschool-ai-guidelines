# HEE — AI & Digital Healthcare Technologies Capability Framework published (news + launch webinar transcript, 21 Feb; media reviewed 3 May 2023)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: NHS Health Education England. Named actors in the launch webinar: **Adrian Brooke** (Medical Director, HEE; paediatrician, self-described "interested dinosaur from the pre digital age"); **Hatim Abdulhussein** (GP; **HEE national lead for AI and digital workforce**); **Dr Alan Davies** (senior lecturer, University of Manchester; nursing + computer science background — framework co-author); **Professor Sonia Kumar** (GP, professor of medical education, Associate Dean University of Leeds).
- Instrument: framework published 21 February (2023) on NHS Digital Academy; commissioned from University of Manchester (learning needs assessment → capability framework). Lineage: builds on the **2019 Topol review** ("preparing the healthcare workforce to deliver the digital future") and extends the earlier "health and care digital capabilities framework."
- Maintenance: "Media last reviewed: 3 May 2023; Next review due: 3 May 2024."

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **workforce-capability adaptationism**. Framing verbatim: "The new AI capability framework... addresses the need for our health and care workforce to **continually adapt their skills and capabilities**, ensuring they can work in a digitally and data enhanced environment."
- Urgency clause (Brooke): "clinical teams in near future will be required to use AI... effectively and equitably... **that's starting now actually. This is not something for the distant future. This is occurring as we speak**."
- Technology-agnosticism commitment (Abdulhussein): "technology is fast adapting and in our framework, we've done our best to make sure we're **technology agnostic**, but we need to make sure that we continue to keep live to advancements... a mechanism in place to continue to review and refresh the capabilities."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
**News-page clauses:**
1. Publication statement (21 Feb) + adaptation rationale (verbatim in (b)).
2. Commissioning clause: HEE commissioned University of Manchester for learning needs assessment + capability framework "to aid the learning and development of the healthcare workforce of England."
3. Purpose clause: "help healthcare workers **identify gaps in their current knowledge and areas for preparatory activities** to support the digital transformation of the workforce."
**Webinar — framework structure (Brooke):** builds on Topol review recommendations ("world leaders in utilising digital technologies"); extends foundational digital literacy with "capabilities around the use of health data and the technologies that make use of this data, for example applications on your mobile. Phone or computer wearable technologies, software and programmes"; "further extended with more advanced capabilities like artificial intelligence and of course the advent of robotics"; capabilities "range across the whole spectrum from initial awareness through implementing these technologies in a healthcare environment and supporting digital transformation projects."
**Webinar — methodology (Davies):** "iterative mixed methods approach... involved code design"; systematic literature review by HEE knowledge and management team (academic + grey literature, international frameworks, policies) → concept map → online workshops (Miro boards, breakout rooms) targeting stakeholder groups (Topol fellows, NHS clinical entrepreneurs, industry: **Babylon Health, Google Health, Barclays, Bupa**) → digital survey.
**Webinar — implementation clauses (Abdulhussein):** sustainability = refresh mechanism for capabilities; **embed into HEE learning platforms** (Learning Hub) so "individuals can really measure their own learning"; work with educational bodies (HEIs, royal colleges, professional regulators) "to support the educational reform that we need"; example partnership: **British Institute of Radiology** — webinar series + learning materials launching at AI Annual Congress (radiology identified as most-affected workforce group in HEE's AI/data-driven technologies work).
**Webinar — implementation discussion:**
- Kumar's assessment-gap critique: "the **medical licencing exam, which comes in just for medical students in 2024, doesn't really mention digital health**... I was putting in words like technology, digital, remote consulting... it just isn't reflected"; "digital health at best is sometimes mentioned. It isn't a strong theme"; "that disconnect between what society is moving ahead with... but yet how educational bodies... are sort of somehow lagging behind."
- Kumar's pedagogy: "the evidence around how you teach digital health, how you actually embed this in curricula is **quite sparse**"; "Clearly, PowerPoints not gonna do it"; proposal — community-based learning: students do QI projects "upskilling communities in digital literacy... you sort of have a double win" (addresses patient digital gaps too).
- Brooke's knowledge-velocity observation: "the doubling time of medical knowledge... used to be 50 years and then 25 years, and then ten years and I think it's currently at about **70 days and shrinking**"; for technology "often released in a commercial setting first... our public are way ahead of us."
- Brooke on stroke AI adoption: imaging AI for intervention-amenable stroke "grown from about 10% uptake a couple of years ago and it's about 70% of units now using it."
- Workforce-planning Q&A (Jane Daily's question on contracts/rewards): Abdulhussein — CPD population beyond trainees; "culture of an organisation, top down leadership"; build digital literacy into **annual appraisals**; **digital self-assessment tool** being developed at HEE, piloted in the north of England.
- Davies — data literacy: "as the technologies get more advanced, they're often closely associated with data... if you're not putting the right data into it... what you get out of it can be affected"; academia's data-access problem → **synthetic data**, fake EHR data for training.
- Trainer-upskilling Q&A (Catherine Worley): Kumar — "none of us really knew as much as the students... I do wonder whether it is the new generation. It is our students that will be upskilling us... co-creating any curricular changes with our students"; module "digital health futures" at Imperial/Leeds as the worked example; Brooke — educators can use the same capability framework to plot their own journey.

## (d) Sophistication markers
- The transcript's honesty is its marker: regulators and educators publicly admitting the **licensing-exam gap** (UKMLA 2024 silent on digital health) and **trainer-knowledge inversion** (students upskilling faculty) — candor rare in official documents.
- Methodological transparency: mixed-methods, co-design, industry workshop participants named (Babylon, Google Health, Barclays, Bupa).
- Systems view: capability framework → learning platforms → appraisals → royal-college partnerships (BIR) → assessment reform; synthetic-data workaround for training-data access.
- Pre-LLM vintage (2023): AI framed as imaging/predictive tools; generative AI absent — the framework's technology-agnosticism was designed for exactly this blind spot.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- The implementation narrative is what the static structure page (separate note) lacks: who maintains it, how it reaches learners (Learning Hub), how it hooks appraisals, and which professions move first (radiology).
- Only document in the corpus showing the **assessment-driver theory** ("assessment drives learning... if you're going to be asked about it in the exam, that's quite a powerful driver") applied to national licensing exams.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** — methodologically built, refresh-mechanized, implementation-minded national capability infrastructure; pre-generative-AI in technical content but self-aware about obsolescence ("technology agnostic" + review cycle).
