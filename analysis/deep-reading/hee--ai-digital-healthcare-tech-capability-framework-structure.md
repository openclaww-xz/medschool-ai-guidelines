# HEE — AI & Digital Healthcare Technologies Capability Framework: framework structure

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: NHS Health Education England; content authored by University of Manchester under HEE commission. Page "last reviewed: 14 February 2023" — a dated, maintained artifact of the national workforce body.
- Non-binding capability taxonomy; authority is pedagogical and organizational (it structures training curricula and role profiles across the English health system).

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **embedding, tiered by depth and role**. Framing verbatim: "The framework is built on the foundation of **basic digital literacy**." and "This literacy also extends through the framework concerning literacy with more advanced concepts such as **Artificial Intelligence (AI) and robotics**."
- Philosophy in one line: everyone needs digital literacy; AI literacy is a graded extension of it, differentiated by role archetype and knowledge level — not a binary expert/lay split.

## (c) Complete clause inventory — the framework architecture
1. Foundation clause: framework "built on the foundation of basic digital literacy" (verbatim above).
2. Scale clause: "The framework consists of **6 primary domains consisting of 11 sub-domains and 195 individual capability statements**."
3. The 6 primary domains and sub-domains, complete:
   - **1. Digital implementation** (no sub-domains)
   - **2. Digital health for patients and the public** — 2.1 Providing direct care with digital technologies; 2.2 Remote consultation and monitoring
   - **3. Ethical, legal and regulatory considerations** — 3.1 Ethics; 3.2 Legislation and regulation
   - **4. Human factors** — 4.1 Management, leadership, and planning → 4.1.1 Management; 4.1.2 Leadership
   - **5. Health data management** — 5.1 Data management and processing → 5.1.1 Data collection and context; 5.1.2 Data storage; 5.1.3 Data visualisation and reporting; 5.1.4 Data processing and analytics; 5.2 Data/cyber security → 5.2.1 Data privacy and confidentiality
   - **6. Artificial Intelligence (AI)** — 6.1 Machine learning and natural language processing; 6.2 Using and implementing AI systems; 6.3 Evaluating AI systems; 6.4 Robotics
4. Leveling clause: capability statements "are further grouped into **4 knowledge levels that increase in complexity and depth from 1 to 4**." (Table headers: Level / Capability statements / Archetype(s).)
5. Archetype clause: "Each of the capabilities are badged with the archetypes introduced previously that are most relevant for a particular capability statement. Therefore you can review capabilities by topic and matching archetype to which you identify against." Archetypes: **Shapers = S, Drivers = D, Creators = C, Embedders = E, Users = U**.
- No prohibitions, permissions, or sanctions — the "clauses" are taxonomy units, not rules.

## (d) Sophistication markers
- The AI domain (6) is itself split into ML/NLP, use/implementation, **evaluation**, and robotics — evaluation of AI systems is a first-class capability domain, not an afterthought. That anticipates post-deployment scrutiny (drift, monitoring) at the workforce-training layer.
- Role-archetype badging (S/D/C/E/U) × 4 knowledge levels × 195 statements = a machine-usable competency matrix; this is curricular infrastructure, far more granular than any policy document in the corpus.
- Placing AI inside "Ethical, legal and regulatory considerations" (domain 3) as a parallel domain shows ethics/law is not folded into AI — both are siblings under digital capability.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- Only framework in the corpus defining an explicit **robotics** capability strand (6.4).
- The 4-level depth model and 5-archetype role model give it a maturity ladder no other international document has; most documents distinguish only users vs developers.
- Contrast with GMC/Singapore: those regulate conduct or product lifecycle; HEE regulates **competence** — it never tells you what you may do, only what you should be able to do.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** — methodologically constructed, adoption-assuming, non-punitive, and granular to the point of being an implementable curriculum standard. Its naive risk is omission, not error: no safety or failure-mode language anywhere in the structure.
