# Howard University — Artificial Intelligence Policy 100-022 (Academics and Research)

## Authority / Authorship
- Publisher: Howard University, Office of the Secretary (policy library PDF). Policy Number 100-022; Responsible Officer: Provost & Chief Academic Officer; Effective Date April 24, 2026.
- "This policy will be reviewed annually and updated as necessary by the Responsible Officer."
- University-wide scope — not a medical-school document; applies to the entire community "including faculty, staff, students, contractors, affiliates."
- Note: Howard also has a separate "Initial Howard University Guidelines for Use of Generative AI Tools" and an AI Advisory Council (AIAC), both cross-referenced.

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- Balanced-institutional: "AI technologies are increasingly used across the University and can enhance teaching and learning, support research and scholarship, improve administrative efficiency, and foster innovation when used responsibly. However, AI also presents risks related to bias, accuracy, accessibility, privacy, data security, intellectual property, and contractual obligations."
- Purpose: "establishes a University-wide framework for the responsible use of AI that promotes ethical conduct, protects individual and institutional interests, upholds academic integrity, and ensures alignment with applicable laws and University policies, while maintaining accountability for individuals and units using AI."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory
I. Policy Statement
1. "It is the policy of Howard University ('the University') to ensure the responsible use of artificial intelligence (AI) in support of the University's academic, research, and operational activities."
2. "This policy will be reviewed annually and updated as necessary by the Responsible Officer."

II. Rationale
3. (Balanced opportunities/risks passage quoted above.)
4. "Accordingly, this policy establishes a University-wide framework for the responsible use of AI…"

III. Entities Affected
5. "This policy applies to all members of the Howard University community, including faculty, staff, students, contractors, affiliates, and any other individuals or entities using artificial intelligence systems in connection with University activities."

IV. Definitions
6. "Artificial Intelligence (AI) – …computer-based systems or software that perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence, including learning from data, recognizing patterns, making predictions or decisions, generating content, and automating processes."
7. "Generative Artificial Intelligence (Generative AI) – …AI systems designed to generate new content, including text, images, audio, video, code, or other outputs, in response to user inputs or prompts."
8. "AI Tools – …software applications, platforms, or services that utilize artificial intelligence, including generative AI, to support or perform tasks in academic, research, administrative, or operational contexts."
9. "Confidential Information – any non-public or proprietary information of the University, where the unauthorized access, use, disclosure, or loss of which could result in a violation of University obligations or policies, or could reasonably cause harm to the university's reputation, programs and services, safety, operations, finances, or community members."
10. "Intellectual Property (IP) – …a concrete or abstract product of the human intellect protected by a category of intangible rights such as those granted by patents, copyrights, trademarks, and trade Secret protections, as defined in the Howard University Intellectual Property Policy (100-006)."
11. "Personally Identifiable Information (PII) – …information that can be used to distinguish or trace an individual's identity… shall be protected and handled in accordance with applicable University information and data protection policies, as defined in the… Acceptable Use… Policy 700-002."
12. "Protected Health Information (PHI) – individually identifiable health information that is created, received, maintained, or transmitted by a HIPAA-covered entity or its business associate, in any form or medium."
13. "Responsible Use of AI – …the design, development, deployment, and use of AI systems in a manner that is ethical, transparent, lawful, and aligned with University policies, including appropriate safeguards to mitigate bias, protect privacy, and ensure human accountability."
14. "University Data – data created, collected, maintained, or processed by or on behalf of the University in connection with academic, research, administrative, or operational activities…"

V.A Administration and Oversight
15. "The Provost and Chief Academic Officer is the Responsible Officer for this policy and is authorized to oversee its implementation, interpretation, and enforcement. The Responsible Officer may delegate administrative responsibilities as appropriate…"
16. "This policy shall be reviewed periodically and updated as necessary to reflect changes in applicable law, University priorities, and evolving artificial intelligence technologies."

V.B General Requirements
17. "Use of AI systems shall comply with all applicable University policies, legal and regulatory requirements, and contractual obligations."
18. "Individuals and units using AI systems shall remain accountable for verifying the accuracy and appropriateness of AI-generated outputs before using them."
19. "Use of AI shall be disclosed when required by the academic, research, administrative, or operational context in which it is employed, in a manner appropriate to that context."
20. "Confidential Information, Personally Identifiable Information (PII), University Data, or other protected information shall not be entered into AI systems unless expressly authorized in writing and subject to appropriate institutional review and approval."
21. "University Data and Confidential Information shall not be used to train AI systems or models."
22. "The University does not authorize third-party use of its data or intellectual property (IP) for marketing, training, or model development purposes without prior review, approval, and agreement by appropriate University authorities, including the use of anonymized or aggregated data in exchange for AI services, regardless of cost or consideration. Any new contract containing provisions for the use of University Data or IP must be reviewed and approved by the Office of the General Counsel to ensure compliance with this policy."

V.C Teaching and Learning
23. "Use of AI in teaching and learning activities shall be consistent with the University's commitment to academic integrity, honesty, and the pursuit of knowledge, as set forth in the Code of Ethical Conduct and the Academic Code of Student Conduct."
24. "Faculty shall establish and communicate, through course syllabi and other official course materials, course requirements regarding the permissible and impermissible use of AI tools, including whether and how AI may be used in coursework."
25. "Student use of AI shall comply with course requirements, instructor requirements, and all applicable University academic conduct policies, and in the absence of explicit course-level guidance regarding AI use, students shall seek clarification from the instructor prior to using AI tools for coursework or assessments." (default-to-ask rule)
26. "Use of AI in instructional activities shall not undermine learning objectives, assessment integrity, or the evaluation of student work, and shall remain subject to applicable academic integrity standards."
27. "Confidential Information, PII, or student education records shall not be entered into AI systems in connection with teaching and learning activities."
28. "Use of AI in instructional contexts shall comply with applicable accessibility, disability accommodation, and non-discrimination obligations under University policy and applicable law."

V.D Research and Scholarship
29. "Use of artificial intelligence (AI) in research and scholarly activities shall comply with all applicable University research integrity policies, sponsor requirements, publication and authorship policies, intellectual property obligations, and contractual obligations."
30. "Researchers shall comply with all applicable policies and requirements imposed by funding agencies, journals, professional societies, and other relevant external entities related to the use of AI… as well as in accordance with applicable policies and procedures of the Office of Regulatory Research Compliance (ORRC)."
31. "Confidential Information, Personally Identifiable Information (PII) that is sensitive information, or other protected University Data shall not be entered into AI systems in connection with research activities unless expressly authorized and subject to appropriate institutional review and approval."
32. "Use of AI in research shall be documented and disclosed as required by applicable funding agencies, publishers, or other binding external requirements."
33. "Researchers shall remain responsible for the accuracy, integrity, and validity of research outputs produced using AI at any stage of the research process, including responsibility for any errors, omissions, or misrepresentations resulting from such use."
34. "Use of AI shall not compromise the security, ownership, or value of the University's intellectual property or University Data or violate applicable intellectual property, copyright, or authorship policies."

V.E External Engagements and Partnerships
35. "Engagements, including partnerships, in which AI is involved in any way (i.e. 'AI-related') with external entities shall be conducted in a manner that protects the University's intellectual property, Confidential Information, University Data, and institutional interests… Any such proposed partnerships or engagements must be reviewed by the Office of the General Counsel and, if applicable, the Office of University Communications, before they are finalized."
36. "Employees, students, contractors, and affiliates shall not enter into any agreement or arrangement, whether formal or informal, with external entities involving the development, use, digitization, licensing, or sharing of University Data or intellectual property without prior legal review by the Office of the General Counsel and approval by authorized University officials."
37. "AI-related agreements shall be subject to appropriate institutional review prior to execution to assess legal, financial, data security, privacy, and intellectual property risks."
38. "University Data and intellectual property shall not be licensed, transferred, or made available to external entities for AI-related purposes in exchange for digitization, access, or other consideration without appropriate authorization and compensation and without legal review by the Office of the General Counsel."
39. "Use of AI in external engagements and partnerships shall not compromise the security, ownership, control, or value of the University's intellectual property, Confidential Information, and institutional interests."

V.F Work-Related Use
40. "Use of artificial intelligence (AI) in University work-related activities, including administrative and operational functions, shall comply with all applicable University policies, legal requirements, and data governance standards…"
41. "Faculty, staff, student employees, and other University personnel shall not enter Confidential Information or PII into AI systems in connection with work-related activities unless expressly authorized and subject to appropriate institutional review and approval."
42. "Use of AI in work-related activities shall comply with all applicable University policies governing data privacy, information security, and records management, including requirements related to student education records (FERPA) and protected health information (HIPAA)."
43. "AI tools or services used in the University operations shall comply with institutional privacy, security, and data stewardship standards and shall be subject to appropriate institutional review and approval prior to use."
44. "Use of AI in work-related activities shall be disclosed and documented when required by applicable University policies, legal obligations, or requirements established by an administrative unit overseeing the work."

VI–VII. Interim / Sanctions
45. "There are no interim policies."
46. "Failure to follow this policy may result in disciplinary action in accordance with applicable University policies and procedures."

## Sophistication Markers
- Enterprise-grade scope: single framework covering teaching, research, work operations, AND external partnerships/vendor contracts — the only document in cohort B with a full procurement/contracting clause set (V.E), including a bar on trading University data/IP for AI services "regardless of cost or consideration" (clause 22).
- No-training rule: "University Data and Confidential Information shall not be used to train AI systems or models" — explicit model-training prohibition beyond entry bans.
- Default-to-ask student rule (clause 25): absent course-level guidance, students must seek clarification before using AI — a clean ambiguity rule most policies lack.
- Accessibility and non-discrimination obligations integrated (clause 28).
- Numbered policy-library governance (100-022) with named Responsible Officer, annual review, and a dense cross-reference web to other numbered policies — real institutional-integration maturity.

## Distinctives
- Zero medical-school or clinical specificity: no PHI-in-clinical-education rules beyond generic HIPAA cross-reference, no H&P/note guidance, no professionalism-system routing, no permitted/prohibited study-use menus. The medical college presumably inherits this as floor policy.
- Everything is authorization-gated: protected data entry "unless expressly authorized in writing"; AI tools in operations subject to "institutional review and approval prior to use."
- Sanctions clause is one line — all discipline deferred to other policies.
- HBCU institutional context: strong emphasis on institutional IP sovereignty and protection against data extraction by vendors — reads as a data-sovereignty stance as much as an academic-integrity one.

## Provisional Classification: cautious-informed
Justification: highly sophisticated university governance (contract review, no-training rule, default-to-ask, accessibility duties) but deliberately minimal and authorization-gated in the teaching-and-learning domain: no permitted uses, no disclosure mechanism, no faculty obligations beyond syllabus statements, no clinical-learning content. As a medical-education AI policy it functions as a cautious, well-built floor. It is neither naive (the construction is too careful) nor permissive; cautious-informed with the caveat that its sophistication is enterprise/legal rather than pedagogical.
