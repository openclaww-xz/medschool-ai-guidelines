# Impact of an Ambient AI Scribe Among Clinicians and Patients: Time-Motion Study (JMIR Med Inform, 2026)

## Research question
What is the real-world effectiveness and acceptability of an ambient AI scribe vs standard documentation in a multilingual (non-US) outpatient setting, using direct observation rather than EHR time stamps?

## Methods
Prospective within-clinician observational time-motion study (SQUIRE reporting), Singapore General Hospital, Dec 27 2024–May 30 2025. In-house tool "Note Buddy" (ASR + GPT-4o; English/Mandarin/Malay/Tamil/Cantonese; no audio storage). 9 experienced users (≥30 prior notes), each with matched scribe/non-scribe sessions ≤2 weeks apart; 5 trained observers, in-room; linear mixed-effects models with clinician random intercepts; post-visit patient surveys by independent surveyors. Determined QI (no IRB needed per SingHealth policy).

## Sample
9 clinicians (mean 20.8 yrs practice; 7 specialties), 169 consultations (78 scribe, 91 standard; 63 English, 14 Mandarin, 1 Malay); 39/45 eligible patients surveyed (86.7%; 41% aged ≥65).

## Key findings
- Documentation time: −0.8 min/consultation (5.3→4.5 min, −15.0%; adjusted P=.04; significant only in mixed model, crude P=.07).
- Eye contact: +7.4 percentage points (69.6%→77.1%, +10.6%; P=.009).
- Consultation duration (−6.1%, P=.42) and total cycle time (P=.57) unchanged — no throughput gain.
- Heterogeneity: 7/9 reduced documentation time (7.9–34.6%); 6/9 shorter consultations; 8/9 more eye contact.
- Patients: 69.2% said physician focused more; 61.5% would recommend; none expressed discomfort; 51.3% neutral.
- Benchmarking vs US systems: Sutter 6.2→5.3 min; Permanente −0.4 min; Stanford −0.57 min — consistent modest savings; RCTs point to burnout reduction as primary value. Authors' conclusion: ambient scribes are quality-of-care/patient-engagement interventions, not productivity tools.

## What it says about who leads policy
Institution-led: in-house development at a health system (SingHealth), governed under internal institutional policy (QI determination, no-recording design, no patient consent required per institutional guidance). Illustrates health systems as policy actors deciding consent, privacy, and deployment rules themselves — no national framework invoked.

## Limitations (authors')
9 senior experienced users (optimal not average outcomes); no EHR integration (manual transfer underestimates savings); self-selected session allocation (Hawthorne, no blinding); no inter-rater reliability; social desirability in surveys; institutional (in-house tool) bias; note accuracy not reassessed; English-dominant sample.

## Relation to corpus findings
Supplies the effectiveness baseline our corpus's GME/health-system policies presuppose: scribes deliver engagement gains but not throughput. Contradicts any productivity rationale appearing in institutional AI policies; supports well-being framing. Our corpus can test whether health-system policies actually encode "draft-not-final-record" human-review requirements this literature demands — a direct policy-content question no single-site study can answer.
