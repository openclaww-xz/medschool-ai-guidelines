# Integrating GenAI into Physiotherapy Education: Use, Perceptions, Literacy (BMC Med Educ, 2026)

## Research question
(1) How did physiotherapy students' GAI use and perceptions change during implementation of GAI-related curricular adaptations (Jan 2024, Linköping University, Sweden)? (2) Did structured GAI learning activities (semesters 1–3) improve GAI literacy?

## Methods
Repeated cross-sectional programme evaluation (GREET), non-equivalent unlinked samples. Two-part: (a) use/perception surveys spring 2024 (n=123/224, 55%) and spring 2025 (n=112/217, 51%); (b) GAILS-10 literacy scale (5 dimensions × 2 items, 7-point) — semester 1 pre-training (n=95/98, 97%) vs semester 3 post-activities (n=80/84, 95%), aut 2025–spr 2026. χ²/Fisher with Cramér's V; t-tests/Mann-Whitney with Cohen's d. AMO (Ability-Motivation-Opportunity) framework guided intervention design; PBL context.

## Sample
Swedish physiotherapy programme (~30–50 students/semester); curriculum adaptations (constructive alignment review, standardized permissibility text in all courses) from Jan 2024.

## Key findings
- Daily GAI use rose 13% (2024) → 57% (2025) (χ²=62.40, p<.001, V=0.52 — large); never-use fell 24.4% → 4.5%.
- Uses diversified: brainstorming, question generation, text refinement all up (p<.001); generating new assignment text remained uncommon (both years).
- Learning contribution: "high/very high" 42.3% (2024) → 62.5% (2025) (p<.001, V=0.33); trust moderate (62.7% "neither" in 2025).
- Perceived sufficiency of institutional guidance on permissible use: similar across years (no significant change).
- GAILS-10: semester 3 (post) higher on all 5 dimensions — overall 5.8 vs 4.9 (p<.001, d=1.03); largest gain prompt engineering (d=1.25); smallest ethical/regulatory awareness (d=0.40).
- Rule-clarification requests fell 2024 (n=5) → 2025 (n=2); teacher-led lecture requests fell 9 → 0.

## What it says about who leads policy
Institution-led curriculum governance: programme-level adaptations with "standardised text on permissible GAI use" across all courses — a deliberate faculty-authored policy instrument. Cites UNESCO GAI guidance as the international anchor. Students' perceived guidance clarity did NOT improve despite policy implementation — an implementation-fidelity signal.

## Limitations (authors')
Repeated cross-sectional unlinked cohorts (no causal inference); possible cross-wave duplicates; semester-composition shift; purpose-developed perception survey without psychometric validation; GAILS-10 Swedish translation not culturally adapted/piloted; self-report with social desirability (possible under-reporting of misuse); researcher-instructor dual role.

## Relation to corpus findings
A rare prospective look at what happens AFTER a school adopts GenAI policy/curriculum: use rises sharply and literacy improves with structured teaching, but guidance clarity stagnates — corroborating the Clin Teach gap from the implementation side (policy exists ≠ policy communicated). Our corpus can measure how many schools pair policy with curriculum and communication mechanisms, which this single-programme study cannot.
