# Deep Read — JABSOM Guidelines on Generative AI for Learners

## Authority / Authorship
- JABSOM Education Technology Committee (per authorship disclosure); May 2025, v1. Complements University of Hawai'i system guidance (EP2.210/214/215/217, EP7.205/208, EP12.214) and Hawai'i statutes (Ch. 84-10 ethics; Ch. 708-890 computer crime); "subject to change to ensure alignment with UH, JABSOM, Hawai'i Residency Programs (HRP), and health system or clinical learning site policies."
- Covers UME + GME: "learner" = medical students, ʻImi Hoʻōla students, plus "residents and fellows of UH residency and fellowship programs."
- Misconduct routing: UME → Evaluation Remediation and Review Committee (ERRC) at course director discretion; GME → program director/faculty/CCC as professionalism breach, escalating to the Designated Institutional Official.
- **AI-authorship disclosure**: "OpenAI's ChatGPT was used in the information gathering, initial brainstorming, and early first draft outline... Subsequent revisions and additions were made manually by the JABSOM Education Technology Committee."

## Philosophy (verbatim framing quotes)
- "Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) represents a significant technological advancement, offering transformative possibilities in medical education, research, and administration. However, its use also raises critical considerations regarding academic integrity, data privacy, ethical standards, and professional conduct."
- Use role: "medical students may utilize generative AI as a tool, resource, or consultant and not as a substitute for their own knowledge acquisition, analysis, and self-reflection."
- Consent axiom: "Consent is always needed before an AI model incorporates another person's personal information, data, visual likeness, photographs, videos, voice, creative content, social media content, and any other outputs associated with that individual."
- Environmental/values framing: "Particularly given JABSOM's aspiration to uphold the University of Hawaii's identity as a Native Hawaiian place of learning, we recommend... values of aloha 'aina and malama honua by also considering the environmental impacts of our AI use." (unique in corpus)

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory

### Scope taxonomy (most complete in cohort)
Standalone and integrated generative AI including: LLMs "(such as Gemini or ChatGPT)... and future direct derivatives (i.e., Large World Models)"; Text-to-Image/Video (DALL-E); "Artificially Generated Audio, including voice synthesis and deepfake technologies, such as Suno AI"; "AI-driven Virtual Patients and simulation tools"; Text-to-Speech/Speech-to-Text; "AI-driven Personalized Learning Systems"; **"Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)"**; enterprise-embedded AI (Microsoft, Adobe, Google).

### General Guidelines
1. **Default policy + instructor flexibility**: uniform default applies "unless instructors provide specific alternative guidelines"; modifications must be "explicitly stated in the course syllabi or communicated directly to students"; may not conflict with JABSOM/UH/state/federal policy.
2. **Andragogy section**: faculty "encouraged to explore the pedagogical potential"; suggested strategies: "assignments that critically evaluate AI-generated content, scenarios that require students to compare AI outputs with human-created materials, and discussions on the ethical implications of AI in medicine."
3. **Professional conduct**: confidentiality, no academic dishonesty; consent rule (above); "It is a breach of professionalism for any learner who, without explicit consent, falsifies representations of another person, using AI or otherwise" (deepfake protection for any "student, colleague, faculty, staff, patient, or other real human being"); harm/violence/discrimination/explicit-content AI use prohibited on UH resources.
4. Training: "strongly recommended," advisory materials and workshops provided.

### Academic Integrity
1. Assignments: use permitted "only as described in Appendix A, or otherwise if explicitly allowed by the instructor"; full disclosure + critical assessment; accuracy/relevance/citation; "supplement to, rather than a replacement for."
2. Disclosure: "a clear disclosure statement indicating how and to what extent AI was utilized," placed per instructor; **worked example** ("I used ChatGPT to generate an initial draft of the literature review section... sources cited were independently verified"); nondisclosure → academic integrity violation.
3. Faculty responsibilities: model integrity by disclosing own AI use "for generating lecture content, exam questions, or any other educational resources"; **clerkship-comment summarization clause**: faculty may use GenAI "to collate and summarize qualitative comments on assessments and evaluations" if FERPA-compliant (de-identification and/or UH-licensed LLMs) and disclosed, "with a need for disclosure in subsequent documentation utilizing these summations, such as the MSPE."
4. Academic misconduct: deception, falsely-original work, unverified content → academic and professional misconduct; ERRC referral (failing grade → severe actions); GME CCC/DIO path.
5. **IP**: AI-generated products "generally not able to be copyrighted as they were not considered to have been created by a human being"; creators must be aware; **"JABSOM advises against and discourages the 'laundering' of previously copyrighted material through its re-generation via artificial intelligence."**
6. **Monitoring**:
   - AI detection: submissions "may be subject to appraisal with AI detection tools. While JABSOM does not encourage using such tools at this time, they are not forbidden"; only with UH-approved tools upholding privacy/security/professionalism; faculty must strip PII per FERPA incl. "indirectly identifiable information."
   - **Activity logging**: version-history proof of manual authorship (e.g., Google Docs) as mitigating strategy; opt-in default; if logging wasn't required in advance, "it is unreasonable to require the learner to procure such logs retroactively" and "Decisions about whether inappropriate use occurred cannot be predicated on the existence or absence of such a log"; anticipates future ubiquity.

### Data Privacy and Security
1. Prohibited use: no processing/storing/transmitting HIPAA/FERPA/PII data via GenAI; **no training/fine-tuning on sensitive or copyrighted data "without all appropriate and applicable forms of consent"**; exceptions for compliant platforms (business agreement with university; research agreements); route via course directors → Associate Dean for Academic Affairs.
   - Worked bad example: transcribing a patient interview with name/DOB/conditions into "an open AI platform like ChatGPT... constitutes a breach of HIPAA."
2. De-identification: highest standards; IRB approval for research; "consent and authorization forms... should note the potential use of their data with AI technologies"; user verifies AI outputs.
   - Worked good example: QI project deemed NHSR by IRB, identifiers removed, "processed using an AI tool hosted on a secure, UH-approved platform."
   - Worked bad example (extended): med-ed research on five underrepresented students — name-redaction alone is "inadequate precautions" given "indirectly identifiable, sensitive aspects of the interview participants' stories... through transcribed speech patterns alone"; remedial menu: consent, manual review for indirect identifiers, "identification of a lower-risk LLM, such as an institution-specific, approved LLM, which will not save or train on the inputted student data."
3. Secure platforms: "sensitive or potentially identifiable data is never input into open or unsecured AI platforms"; students trained on secure platforms.
4. **Proactive consent forms**: research consent forms disclose AI use; educational consent forms recommended "upon matriculation... and/or at the start of specific courses."
5. Consequences: course director/mentor review; ERRC case-by-case; "retraining, loss of access to AI tools, or more severe disciplinary actions"; GME path to DIO.

### Sustainability
- AI "significantly increase[s] the use of natural resources, including land, water, and energy... increases in carbon dioxide emissions"; aloha 'aina/malama honua framing; no quantitative rules yet but endorses "advocacy for sustainable practices in data management."

### Appendix A — Default Permissible Use (11 items)
1. Grammar/syntax/idea generation/organization **permitted**; "Generative AI is not allowed to produce differential diagnoses, treatment plans, or other clinical reasoning outputs."
2. "AI tools should not be used to generate substantive content for assignments, such as writing sections of essays or reports"; disclosure for allowed uses.
3. Critical analysis/verification required of any included AI content.
4. Data privacy strictly prohibited for sensitive/identifiable info; consult faculty first.
5. **Curricular materials/IP**: learners may upload their own assignment responses to third-party LLMs; rubrics/grading criteria upload "permitted with limitations" (assessment prep/feedback); "Strictly Prohibited: Sharing of instructional materials, such as syllabi, problem-based learning (PBL) case materials, simulation materials, and lecture slides... unless with the written permission of all educators or creators involved."
6. Final submissions: AI non-textual content (images/art/video/music) permitted with citation; **"the use of AI to generate textual content in final submissions is not permitted."**
7. Group work permitted under same rules.
8. Presentation/multimedia visual aids permitted with disclosure/citation.
9. "Exams and Quizzes: Strictly Prohibited" (incl. take-home).
10. Simulations/virtual patients not permitted unless explicitly allowed.
11. Faculty instructional use permitted with disclosure (multimedia, gathered info), cited.

## Sophistication Markers
- Richest modality taxonomy in the corpus (LLM→Large World Models, deepfakes/Suno, virtual patients, RAG, personalized learning systems).
- Case-based ethics: three worked examples (good QI; HIPAA breach; indirect-identification narrative risk) — pedagogically unmatched.
- Logging-evidence jurisprudence: no retroactive log demands; non-log-based adjudication — the most procedurally fair detection regime seen.
- Consent-first likeness/data axiom; deepfake-falsification professionalism clause.
- Faculty AI summarization of clerkship comments with MSPE disclosure cascade — the only explicit MSPE-AI linkage in the corpus.
- Environmental/sustainability section grounded in Native Hawaiian values.
- AI-assisted authorship disclosure (like LSUHSC-NO).
- IP: anti-laundering clause; copyright-human-authorship note.

## Distinctives
- Single unified UME+GME learner policy (unique — most schools split).
- Appendicular default rules replace course-by-course vacuum: fine-grained medium distinctions (text banned vs multimedia permitted in finals).
- Clinical-reasoning firewall: no AI differentials/treatment plans for students (parallel to OHSU PA's discouragement).
