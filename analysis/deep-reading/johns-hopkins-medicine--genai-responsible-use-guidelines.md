# Johns Hopkins — New Guidelines for Responsible Use of Generative AI (GenAI)

## Authority / authorship
- "JHU and IT@JH leadership recently released a set of guidelines for the responsible use of GenAI" — **IT-leadership-authored guidelines**, university-wide (not JHM-clinical-specific). Announcement-article format.

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "These guidelines were established to ensure that GenAI technologies are used safely and in compliance with current policies..."

## Complete verbatim clause inventory
1. "Start with our approved GenAI tools and connect with IT@JH before procuring new ones"
2. "Do not enter proprietary data—including clinical, financial, or business records—into third-party, unapproved GenAI tools"
3. "You are responsible for any AI-generated content that you produce or publish"
4. "Adhere to current university policies"
5. "Over the next few months IT@JH will be deploying new tools and resources — including our own AI platform, the 'Hopkins AI Lab.' Invitations to join the Hopkins AI Lab will be rolling out over the next few months, but the support site is available now. Our AI resources are also expanding."

## Sophistication markers
- **Clinical deployment governance:** none — no committee, review pipeline, or risk tiers. Procurement gate is "connect with IT@JH."
- **Monitoring:** none.
- **Model-failure handling:** none beyond the responsibility clause.
- **Ambient-scribe consent:** absent.
- **Clinician liability positioning:** clause 3 — "You are responsible for any AI-generated content that you produce or publish" — a clean, blunt individual-accountability stance; the system assumes none.
- Approved-tool-first posture + enterprise platform buildout (Hopkins AI Lab→HopGPT).

## Distinctives
- Four-bullet minimalism; the entire policy surface is one screen.
- Explicit "connect before procuring" procurement centralization.

## Provisional classification
**Cautious-informed (guidelines tier).** Correct but skeletal: safe defaults (approved tools, no proprietary data, personal responsibility, policy compliance) with no clinical governance depth. The enterprise platform (HopGPT) shows deployment ambition, but governance elaboration is absent from this artifact.
