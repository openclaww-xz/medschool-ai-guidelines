# JHU — Student Guidance on Responsible GenAI Use (Engineering for Professionals)

## Authority / Authorship
- JHU Engineering for Professionals (EP) academic-policy page; notes "Until Johns Hopkins University establishes formal terminology, EP uses 'Generative AI' and 'GenAI' as working terms."

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) can support learning, research, and productivity when used appropriately."
- HopGPT recommended: "Provides secure access to multiple Large Language Models (LLMs); Ensures equitable access for all students; Complies with university data-privacy standards."

## Complete clause inventory
1. Traffic-light course categories: **Red** ("GenAI use is not permitted... in any form... constitutes a violation of academic integrity"), **Yellow** ("Moderate GenAI use is permitted, with restrictions... must be disclosed"), **Green** ("Liberal GenAI use is permitted, with expectations for transparency and accountability").
2. Instructors may use "instructional, procedural, or technical methods to support academic integrity" (incl. proctoring).
3. Yellow examples: brainstorming then independent writing; grammar refinement after drafting.
4. Green examples: research/drafting/analysis/coding support; initial drafts later revised; data analysis/debugging.
5. Disclosure menu (5 forms): full transcripts; citations of model/website; short description; single citation per assignment; visual highlighting of AI content.
6. "Failure to disclose GenAI use when required may constitute academic misconduct."
7. Best practices: ask instructor first; "Treat GenAI as a learning partner, not an authority"; verify; save outputs "if disclosure or verification is requested."

## Sophistication markers
- Named-category scheme (Red/Yellow/Green) identical in structure to formal pioneer policies; equity rationale for the provisioned tool; evidence-preservation advice.

## Classification
- **progressive-structured** — a full tiered disclosure system delivered as *guidance* from a professional-school division, not main-campus policy; exemplifies JHU's decentralized model.
