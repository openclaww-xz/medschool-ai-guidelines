# Karolinska Institutet — Guidelines for students on the use of generative AI (Programme Committee, Biomedicine programmes), v1 rev 1, Sept 2024

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: Karolinska Institutet, **Programme Committee for the Study Programmes in Biomedicine** — a programme-level committee, not central university administration. Explicitly versioned: "Guidelines for the use of generative AI **Version 1, rev 1 - September 2024**."
- Scope clause: covers KI courses in the **Bachelor's Programme in Biomedicine; Master's in Biomedicine; Master's in Biostatistics and Data Science; Master's in Molecular Techniques in Life Science**. (Not the medical programme proper — biomedicine family.)
- Provenance transparency: the committee states its guideline "is guided by information from **https://www.ucl.ac.uk/teaching-learning/generative-ai/using-ai-tools-assessment**" — cross-national borrowing from UCL disclosed in the text; educator advice cross-referenced at staff.ki.se.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **default-permit with mandatory transparency, epistemic-caution list, and a disclosure template**. Framing: "Students of the Study Programmes in Biomedicine... **must** take the following into account if using generative AI for studies and assignments."
- Default-permit clause verbatim: "In the absence of other information, such as instructions from the Course Director or Examiner of a specific course, **students are allowed to employ AI tools to support their learning and improve communication and writing skills**. However, it is **mandatory** for students to be transparent and describe in detail how they used AI tools."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
1. **Pre-use duties ("Before using generative AI, you must ensure that:")**
   a. "You **know whether the use of generative AI is permitted** for your assignment/research in the course concerned. Please see Canvas or ask your Course Director."
   b. "You **understand the limitations and risks** of using generative AI."
   c. "Your work and submitted assignments are **based on your own ideas**, unless otherwise indicated (e.g., via referencing). Inappropriately taking credit for ideas or work that have been generated using AI is **considered cheating and action will be taken in accordance with KI disciplinary procedures**." — the sanctions hook.
2. **Awareness list ("When using generative AI, you should be aware that:")**
   a. "Generative AI produces information that may be **inaccurate, biased, or outdated**."
   b. "Generative AI is **not an original source of information: it reproduces information from unidentified sources**."
   c. "Generative AI **may fabricate quotations and citations**."
   d. "You should always, as far as possible, **refer to original and credible sources**."
3. **In-use duties ("If you do choose to use generative AI tools, you must always:")**
   a. "**Critically evaluate** any output it produces."
   b. "**Carefully check** any quotations or citations it creates."
   c. "**Correctly document your use** of the tools so that it can be appropriately acknowledged."
4. **Acknowledgment requirement**: use "must be acknowledged in an **'Acknowledgements' section** of any piece of academic work in which it has been used as a functional tool." Mandatory template text: "During the preparation of this work, the author(s) used **[NAME TOOL/SERVICE and VERSION, PUBLISHER and URL]** to **[REASON]**. After using this tool/service, the author(s) **reviewed and edited the content and take(s) full responsibility for the content of the work**."
   - Worked example discloses ChatGPT 3.5 (OpenAI, URL) "to summarise my initial notes and to improve the spelling, grammar, and language of my final draft, including the restructuring of sentences and paragraphs without substantially changing the underlying meaning."
5. **Boundary definition — what is NOT generative AI**: "you do not need to disclose the use of tools that **automate time-consuming tasks where the end result essentially remains the same**" — permitted without disclosure: reference managers (Endnote, Zotero, Mendeley); basic grammar/style tools "based on grammatical rules and stylistic principles, and these tools will not re-write your text" (Word grammar functions, Grammarly, Instatext). "However, **using generative AI parts of such programs must be acknowledged**." Even for basic functions: "you remain responsible for the output and must check it carefully."
6. **Default-permit clause** (verbatim in (b)).
7. **Information security clause** (adapted from KI educator advice): "The AI learns from the data fed to it, and therefore you risk that your data ends up in the hands of companies who are free to do as they please with the information. Therefore, you should **not submit protected material such as patient data, personal data or research data** when you use generative AI. It is **not appropriate to submit others' work (incl. group reports)** to such AI services **without explicit consent from everyone involved**."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- The tool/type boundary clause (Grammarly basic vs Grammarly GenAI) is the sharpest functional-boundary line in any student policy in the corpus — a rule students can actually apply.
- The acknowledgment **template with fill-in fields** (tool+version+publisher+URL, reason, responsibility statement) operationalizes disclosure to journal-standard; the example even picks a disclosure-safe use case.
- Epistemic awareness list is technically accurate and compact: inaccuracy/bias/staleness, non-originality ("reproduces information from unidentified sources"), fabricated citations.
- Third-party consent for group reports extends confidentiality beyond patient data to peers' work — rare.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- Only student guideline with a **verbatim compliance template** — transcription-ready. Contrast Melbourne ("must be acknowledged," no format), Sydney two-lane (lane definitions, no template).
- Disclosed cross-national lineage (UCL assessment guidance) is a provenance-honesty marker no sibling matches.
- Default-permit-unless-prohibited inverts the classic "prohibited unless permitted" integrity stance.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** — programme-level, versioned, default-permit, template-operationalized, with accurate technical epistemics; sanction-backed (KI disciplinary procedures) but proportionate. A model student-facing artifact.
