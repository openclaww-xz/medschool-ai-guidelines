# LCME — Strategic Re-visioning of the LCME Accreditation Standards (Learn Serve Lead session, Oct 8, 2025)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: LCME, the US medical school accreditor (AAMC + AMA sponsored). A **conference slide deck** (Learn Serve Lead 2025, "Take Two") presented by the LCME Secretariat; advisory groups named: Council of Deans group (facilitators Jerry Youkey, Robert Simari; ~17 deans) and Medical Education Senior Leaders group (facilitators David Lambert, LuAnn Wilkerson; ~20 members incl. Eva Aagaard, Karen Hauer). Not yet standards — a re-visioning process report with proposed Standard 7 language under public comment (to Jan 7, 2026).
- Powers claimed: **the strongest in the corpus — accreditation**. Whatever enters Functions & Structure binds all ~160 LCME-accredited schools. This deck shows that machinery in motion.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **incrementalist standards modernization under external political pressure**. AI appears as Challenge #2 of ten: "**Impact of technology (AI) on medical education, assessment, and healthcare delivery**." The framework philosophy is linkage-mapping — showing where new topics attach to existing elements rather than creating standalone AI standards.
- Political context embedded: the deck quotes the **Aug 27, 2025 letter from Secretaries Kennedy and McMahon** ("urgent demand for immediate policy reforms, concrete curricula overhauls, and binding assessment requirements" re: nutrition) and LCME's Sept 4, 2025 guiding-principles response (agreement + alignment with Standard 7 revision). Federal political interference is named a top-10 challenge ("Political interference with accreditation").

## (c) Complete clause inventory
**Process facts**
1. Top Ten Challenges (June 2024) — #2: "Impact of technology (AI) on medical education, assessment, and healthcare delivery." (Others: financing; clerkship competition; emerging curriculum content; UME/GME continuum; political interference; burnout; workforce/diversity; faculty wellbeing; unionization implications.)
2. Timeline: "February 2025: Revisions to specific elements of Standards 2, 4, 5, and 9"; advisory groups formed May–July 2025, first meetings Oct 2025; "The structure of new Standard 7 was approved in principle by the LCME in February 2025."
3. Advisory-group purpose: "Provide input into and feedback on proposed revisions to challenge-associated elements and standards as well as potential revisions to LCME processes."
**The AI-bearing clause (proposed Standard 7.2, Patient Care) — the operative text**
4. "The medical education curriculum includes content and clinical experiences related to the diagnosis and treatment of disease. This content and these clinical experiences include education and experiential learning in the areas of acute and chronic care, end-of-life care, continuity of care, and rehabilitative care; **in the appropriate use of artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies in diagnosis and patient management**; and in the development and effective application of the skills of evidence-based critical judgment to solving clinical problems."
5. Crosswalk disclosure: "Topics Not Explicitly Included in 2025-26 F&S: **Artificial intelligence in diagnosis and patient management**" — i.e., AI is NEW required content, not carried from the current standards.
**Other proposed Standard 7 elements (context, no further AI content)**
- 7.1 Foundational Medical Knowledge (biomedical/behavioral/socioeconomic sciences; research principles).
- 7.3 Health Promotion: "This content includes **the role of nutrition in preventing and managing chronic disease**" — nutrition element added explicitly "to address the explicit requirement for content related to 'nutrition'" (Kennedy/McMahon response).
- 7.4 Communication and Interprofessional Collaborative Skills.
- 7.5 Professionalism: "instruction and experiential learning in professionalism, human values, and medical ethics."
- 7.6 Practice-based Learning and Improvement: self-directed learning, "self-identify critical gaps in knowledge," CQI.
- 7.7 Systems-based Practice: social/structural determinants, healthcare delivery systems.
**Next steps**
6. "Public comment period and public hearing on the revisions to Standard 7 (comment period opened October 7th, with a public hearing on January 7th)."
7. "Challenge #3 (competition for clinical clerkship placements). **Standards 8 and 9 (related to Challenges #2, 4, and 5)**" — i.e., AI-related standard revisions continue into Standards 8–9 (faculty/resources).
8. More advisory groups: Student Affairs/Admissions, Faculty, Business Affairs, Health System Leadership, Former LCME Student Members; "Opportunities for public comment on substantive changes."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Technically shallow by design: the only AI clause is a single content requirement ("appropriate use of artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies in diagnosis and patient management") — a curricular coverage mandate, not a governance regime. No hallucination/bias/assessment-security vocabulary; "assessment" appears only in the challenge listing.
- Evidence: none; process artifacts only.
- Mandates: real — this is the only corpus document that will make AI education an **accreditation requirement** (proposed, pending comment). But the mandate is thin: teach it; no specified depth, no governance duty, no assessment-integrity rule.
- Political sophistication is high: the Kennedy/McMahon exchange shows the accreditor navigating federal pressure, folding nutrition demands into the same re-visioning that carries AI.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- The only accreditation-level text: everyone else persuades; LCME compels. Its single AI clause outweighs others' frameworks in binding force.
- AI enters through Patient Care (7.2), not through a tech/informatics element — framed as clinical competency, matching AMA literacy advocacy.
- Nutrition + AI in the same revision cycle (federal politics meets curriculum) is a unique window into standards-setter capture dynamics.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** — not because of depth but because of mechanism: it converts the field's AI-literacy consensus into a proposed binding curricular requirement through a deliberate, commented, staged process. Intellectually derivative (one clause), institutionally decisive.
