# LCME — Strategic Re-visioning of the LCME Accreditation Standards, "Take Two" (Learn Serve Lead 2025 session deck, Oct 8 2025)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: Liaison Committee on Medical Education (LCME) — the UME accreditor (joint AAMC/AMA). This is a slide deck from an AAMC Learn Serve Lead 2025 session (Oct 8, 2025) presenting the LCME's strategic re-visioning process, not a standards document itself.
- Process authorship is explicit: "Top Ten Challenges" adopted June 2024; two advisory groups formed May–July 2025 — Council of Deans (COD) group (facilitators Jerry Youkey, Robert Simari; ~15 named deans) and Medical Education Senior Leaders (MESL) group (facilitators David Lambert, LuAnn Wilkerson; ~15 named senior leaders) — first meetings October 2025. "Purpose of the advisory groups: Provide input into and feedback on proposed revisions to challenge-associated elements and standards as well as potential revisions to LCME processes."
- Power: the LCME is the ONLY body in this corpus with binding authority over US MD schools — revisions to Standards/Elements are accreditation requirements. The deck reports that "The structure of new Standard 7 was approved in principle by the LCME in February 2025" and is now in public comment ("Now – January 7, 2026", hearing Jan 7).

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: incremental standards modernization, not an AI policy. AI enters as ranked challenge: the LCME's "Top Ten Challenges (June 2024)" list item #2 = "Impact of technology (AI) on medical education, assessment, and healthcare delivery" (behind financing/health-system models). Response architecture: revise standards element-by-element, advisory-group input, public comment.
- The deck also shows external political pressure: "Letter from Secretaries Kennedy and McMahon / Sent August 27, 2025 … an urgent demand for immediate policy reforms, concrete curricula overhauls, and binding assessment requirements" (nutrition). LCME's Sept 4, 2025 guiding principles respond by aligning the response with "revisions currently being considered and undertaken by the LCME (specifically, Standard 7)" and committing to measurable outcomes via Part II survey and curriculum-database data.

## (c) Complete clause inventory (verbatim normative statements)
The AI-relevant normative text is one insertion in revised Element 7.2 (Patient Care):
- "The medical education curriculum includes content and clinical experiences related to the diagnosis and treatment of disease. This content and these clinical experiences include education and experiential learning in the areas of acute and chronic care, end-of-life care, continuity of care, and rehabilitative care; **in the appropriate use of artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies in diagnosis and patient management**; and in the development and effective application of the skills of evidence-based critical judgment to solving clinical problems."
- The deck flags as NEW relative to AY 2025-26 Functions & Structure: "Topics Not Explicitly Included in 2025-26 F&S: • Artificial intelligence in diagnosis and patient management" — i.e., AI use in diagnosis/management becomes explicit curricular content requirement for the first time.

Other revised Standard 7 elements (approved in principle Feb 2025; AI-adjacent only):
- 7.1 Foundational Medical Knowledge: curriculum "includes content from the biomedical, behavioral, and socioeconomic sciences … and includes instruction in the scientific principles of basic biomedical, clinical, and translational research and how such research is conducted, evaluated, and applied to patient care."
- 7.3 Health Promotion: "This content includes the role of nutrition in preventing and managing chronic disease…" (added "to address the explicit requirement for content related to 'nutrition'" per footnote).
- 7.6 Practice-based Learning and Improvement: curriculum "ensures that medical students learn, practice, and receive feedback on their progressive acquisition of the skills of self-directed learning, including the ability to self-identify critical gaps in knowledge … and to find, analyze, synthesize, and appraise the credibility of relevant information" — relevant indirectly to AI-era information literacy.
- 7.7 Systems-based Practice: instruction in "social and structural determinants of health, the structure and functioning of healthcare delivery systems…"
- Process commitments: public comment + hearing; next "Standards 8 and 9 (related to Challenges #2, 4, and 5)" — i.e., the AI-challenge (#2) work continues into Standards 8/9 (medical education community/resources).

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Understands that AI threatens assessment ("Impact of technology (AI) on medical education, **assessment**, and healthcare delivery") — names assessment in the challenge framing, but the only drafted AI text so far is a single clinical-use content clause in 7.2. No hallucination/drift/agentic/ambient-scribe language; no equity, oversight, or governance content on AI yet.
- Evidence: none cited — it is a process deck. Measurability emphasized (Part II survey data, curriculum database search).
- Mandates: yes in principle — Standard 7 revisions, once final, are binding accreditation requirements on all ~160 LCME-accredited programs. But as of this deck it is draft-in-comment (Jan 7, 2026 close), so the mandate is prospective.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- Only corpus body that converts AI language into compulsory accreditation standards (prospective). Everyone else publishes guidance; LCME amends the rulebook.
- Uniquely exposed to federal political pressure (Kennedy/McMahon letter shown verbatim in deck) — documents the political environment all US frameworks now operate in.
- Timing lag: challenge identified June 2024, structural approval Feb 2025, public comment to Jan 2026, Standards 8/9 still to come — a 2+ year cadence vs weeks-to-months for professional-society statements.

## (f) Provisional classification: cautious-informed
Justification: correctly diagnoses the problem (AI's impact on education AND assessment AND delivery as the #2 strategic challenge) and possesses real regulatory power, but its drafted normative output so far is a single clause — "appropriate use of artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies in diagnosis and patient management" — with the harder assessment-validity questions deferred to Standards 8/9. Informed caution moving at accreditation speed: neither naive (it names assessment explicitly) nor progressive-structured (no governance framework, no timeline certainty, mandate still in comment period).
