# Josiah Macy Jr. Foundation — AI in Medical Education Grants Program page

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: Josiah Macy Jr. Foundation, the med-ed-dedicated philanthropy. Authored as institutional program text (no named author). Follows the 2024 Macy conference on AI in Medical Education; page announces the grants initiative and describes three 2025 awards.
- Powers claimed: funding power only — "support for three demonstration projects, each receiving up to $200,000 over two years." Binds no one; steers the field by funding and by conference outputs (which AAMC webinars disseminate — the page cross-links AAMC-hosted webinars, showing the Macy→AAMC pipeline).

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **research-first enablement** — the field should learn what "responsible, effective, and ethical use" actually is via demonstration projects, not pronouncements. Framing quote: the initiative funds projects "that advance our understanding of what **responsible, effective, and ethical use of AI in medical education** will look like in the immediate future."
- Embedded skepticism inside the ARiSE description — the sharpest evidence claim on any page this short: "The stakes are high - **multiple studies now show that high-performing artificial intelligence technologies don't always improve doctor performance**."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
No formal must/should clauses; directional/normative-adjacent statements:
1. Purpose: "fund demonstration projects that advance our understanding of what responsible, effective, and ethical use of AI in medical education will look like in the immediate future."
2. Award structure: "provides support for three demonstration projects, each receiving up to $200,000 over two years."
3. Project 1 (Brat, BIDMC/Harvard): "leverage computer vision and AI methods to provide automated assessments of surgical skill in open procedures for surgical residents... The overall goal is to refine AI-based assessment for open procedures to augment feedback from educators and encourage targeted practice for surgical trainees."
4. Project 2 (NYU Grossman, "Communication Compass"): "developing Communication Compass, an AI-powered system designed to offer personalized, near real-time feedback on learners' communication skills by analyzing ambient audio from patient-physician interactions... will test this system's impact on both educational outcomes and patient care through a randomized trial. Ultimately, Communication Compass aims to create an ethical, scalable framework for improving communication skills."
5. Project 3 (ARiSE Network, BIDMC/Harvard + Stanford): "will use gold standard social science methodologies to understand how doctors are currently using AI in their clinical care. They will then run **national randomized trials** to determine which educational strategies truly boost doctor-AI teamwork in important clinical tasks like diagnosing illnesses, explaining care plans to patients and coordinating treatment. The curricula and assessments from this project will be **released openly** so medical schools and hospitals around the world can train future doctors to use AI confidently while keeping patients safe."
6. Normative benchmark embedded in #5: "medical educators will need to figure out how to better train doctors to work with AI."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- High for its size. Recognizes: **ambient audio / smart glasses** capture (projects 1–2), computer-vision skill assessment, **human-AI teaming** as a trainable competency ("doctor-AI teamwork"), and — uniquely blunt — the evidence problem ("high-performing AI technologies don't always improve doctor performance" — an automation-bias/team-performance insight).
- Evidence culture: mandates randomized trials in two of three projects ("gold standard social science methodologies," "randomized trial"); requires open release of curricula/assessments.
- Ethics appear as adjectives ("responsible," "ethical") rather than mechanisms.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- Only funder in the corpus; converts conference language into an empirical research agenda. It operationalizes what AAMC/AMEE write about: AI-driven assessment, ambient feedback systems, teaming curricula.
- "Teaming with AI" framing (ARiSE) prefigures competency language later bodies adopt.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** (as an actor rather than a policy): it does not write norms, it builds the evidence base that norms will cite, with structural safeguards (RCTs, open licensing) baked into awards. The "AI doesn't always improve doctor performance" framing shows a risk-literate embrace.
