# AI-Use Disclosure Statements in Medical Education Journals: Bibliometric Study (medRxiv preprint, 2025)

## Research question
What is the rate and nature of AI-use disclosure in empirical research papers published in leading medical education journals?

## Methods
Bibliometric study; all articles Jan 1–Jun 30 2025 in the 24 journals of the Medical Education Journals List (2,762 articles); Covidence screening removed 716 non-empirical/non-English; full-text search for AI-use disclosure statements in the remaining 2,046; structured extraction (journal, author metrics via WoS/Scopus, tools, purpose, location, word count, responsibility attestation); descriptive stats + content analysis; dual extraction with consensus.

## Sample
2,046 empirical articles; 51 with disclosures; 50 unique first authors from 46 institutions across 24 countries.

## Key findings
- Disclosure rate: 2.5% (51/2,046) — despite 12/13 journals with disclosures providing author guidance; no journal exceeded 7%.
- BMC Medical Education carried 24/51 (47.1%); Medical Teacher 7; Journal of Surgical Education 4.
- Geography: majority from non-native-English-speaking regions (Western Pacific 15, Europe 11, Americas 11, Mediterranean 9); USA/Germany/Iran/Australia most represented by country.
- First authors: median 11.5 publications, 66% began publishing within the past decade (junior-heavy).
- Tools: 18 unique; ChatGPT 19 (37.3%), Otter.ai 11 (21.6%), Editage 3; 5 articles multiple tools; 4 disclosed without naming a tool.
- Purposes: editing 54.9%, transcription 23.5%, thematic/data analysis 9.8%, drafting 5.9%, screening 3.9%; only 8 articles (15.7%) disclosed substantive uses.
- Disclosures averaged 43 words; only 2 exceeded 100 words; only 29% included responsibility attestation; attestations appeared in acknowledgements/dedicated sections but were ABSENT from methods-located disclosures.
- 4 negative disclosures ("no AI used") — all from non-native-English regions (China ×2, Hungary, Qatar).
- Context: Nature 2025 survey — 17% of mid-career researchers admitted undisclosed AI writing; Dimensions analysis — 468% rise in ChatGPT-style vocabulary with only 0.1% disclosure; orthopaedics: 1/28 AI-text articles disclosed.
- Interpretation: "safe disclosure" practice (performative, not informative); "transparency paradox"; risk of inequity — non-native English authors' disclosure may invite stigma and GPT-detector misclassification.

## What it says about who leads policy
Journals/publishers (ICMJE, JAMA Network guidance) lead via mandatory disclosure policies, but the policies demonstrably underperform — 2.5% compliance vs near-certain higher use. Proposals: journal dropdown menus of sanctioned uses, tiered disclosure frameworks, shifting from "disclosure" to routine methods reporting.

## Limitations (authors')
6-month window; publication lag (~188 days) may delay reflected disclosures; no disclosed-vs-undisclosed comparison; journal word limits constrain disclosure detail; preprint (not yet peer-reviewed).

## Relation to corpus findings
Documents the same policy-practice gap as Clin Teach but for journals: policy mandates exist, behavior lags drastically. For our corpus: shows that even when guidance exists (12/13 journals), stated-policy compliance cannot be assumed — a caution for interpreting institutional AI policies as evidence of practice. Our corpus measures the policy supply side; this shows demand-side behavior needs independent measurement.
