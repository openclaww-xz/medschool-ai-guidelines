# Michigan Medicine — Using AI Tools at MM (KB0023639, v9.0)

## Authority / authorship
- Internal IT knowledge-base article. **AUTHOR(s): Erin Dietrich, Deputy CIO for UMMS and HITS Senior Director for Academic IT. ENDORSEMENT(s): Jack Kufahl, Michigan Medicine Chief Information Security Officer.** Named deputy-CIO authorship + CISO endorsement.
- Points to two deeper instruments: "Guidelines for Acceptable AI Use at the University of Michigan Medical School" and the "**Michigan Medicine Appropriate Clinical Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence Tools Policy**," plus a governance body: "**Michigan Medicine Clinical Intelligence Committee, chaired by Dr. Michael Burns, Associate CMIO for Artificial Intelligence**."

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "Key Takeaway: When embracing AI, as with any technology, align with established information security standards and protocols."
- "While AI tools are new, the information security standards and protocols that are in place for technology already exist. As with any technology, you must follow established processes on how to buy or incorporate new technology."

## Sophistication markers
- **Clinical deployment governance:** exists but is elsewhere — the named Clinical Intelligence Committee (chaired by an Associate CMIO for AI) is a real clinical-governance body; this KB is the signpost layer. Notable that clinical AI governance is chaired clinically (Associate CMIO), not by compliance/IT.
- **Monitoring:** none here (data-sensitivity tiers maintained in the Safe Computing Guide).
- **Model-failure handling:** none here.
- **Ambient-scribe consent:** absent.
- **Clinician liability:** absent (deferred to the unreferenced-here clinical-use policy).
- Channel discipline: only HITS/TSPs may develop or manage applications and vendors — strong centralization.

## Distinctives
- Explicit "existing security policy already covers AI" stance — governance-by-incorporation rather than new AI-specific rules at this layer.
- U-M ecosystem tools (genai.umich.edu approved services, Safe Computing data-sensitivity guide) referenced as live infrastructure.
