# Morehouse School of Medicine — Student Use of Artificial Intelligence (Handbook)

## Authority / Authorship
- Publisher: Morehouse School of Medicine; 2026–2027 Student Handbook, "Policies and Procedures > Student Use of Artificial Intelligence" (SmartCatalog page).
- No named author, approval body, effective date, or review cycle. Shortest formal-policy document in cohort B (~315 words including page furniture).

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- Permissive-with-citation stance on assignments: "Use of AI and chat GPT and similar content tools in writing assignments is allowable only if specifically allowed by the program and course and its use duly noted in the references to the document."
- Tempered endorsement: "The use of generative AI tools in study, learning, and assignments where this is permitted can be an important aspect of learning."
- Critical-literacy framing: "It is noted that AI can generate undocumented information that may not be valid as well as omit important information and every student should develop and use data verification skills to avoid errors due to AI 'hallucinations.'"

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory
1. Use in assignments: "Use of AI and chat GPT and similar content tools in writing assignments is allowable only if specifically allowed by the program and course and its use duly noted in the references to the document."
2. Exams default rule: "Unless otherwise specified in the specific in-class/closed resource examination/assessment, use of generative AI content tools for examinations/assessments that are specifically not 'open book/open resource' constitutes academic dishonesty and will be grounds for SAPP referral."
3. Online assignments: "Students inappropriately using such resources will be subject to dismissal. This also applies to assignments requiring online postings, comments, and other online assignments unless specifically permitted."
4. Named tools: "Tools that may not be used for these assignments include chat GPT, GPT-4, Copilot, Google Bard, and other similar tools."
5. Correct citation of AI resources: "Use of generative AI resources in any assignment must be referenced appropriately in that assignment. Such reference would include the tool used, version, and url. APA and other standards and the assignment should provide more detailed and up-to-date information."
6. General use: "The use of generative AI tools in study, learning, and assignments where this is permitted can be an important aspect of learning."
7. Verification duty: "It is noted that AI can generate undocumented information that may not be valid as well as omit important information and every student should develop and use data verification skills to avoid errors due to AI 'hallucinations.'"

## Sophistication Markers
- Citation elements specified minimally (tool, version, URL) with deference to APA — a workable rule in seven lines.
- "Hallucination" literacy explicitly named; data-verification skills framed as a student competency.
- Escalation path named (SAPP referral) with dismissal on the table — proportionate seriousness for exam misuse.
- Open-book/closed-resource framing ties the exam rule to exam format rather than to AI per se — conceptually the right anchor.

## Distinctives
- Dated tool list ("chat GPT, GPT-4, Copilot, Google Bard") ages quickly and is defined by consumer chatbots, not clinical tools.
- No PHI/HIPAA/clinical-environment content whatsoever; no faculty duties; no institutional-tool/whitelist concept; no definitions; no review process; no permitted-use examples beyond assignments.
- Grammar/phrasing inconsistencies ("chat GPT", "duly noted in the references") and catalog ellipses indicate a lightly edited handbook entry rather than a drafted policy.
- Notably permissive default on assignments (allowed when course permits, citation-gated) vs. harsh exam default (academic dishonesty → SAPP → possible dismissal).
