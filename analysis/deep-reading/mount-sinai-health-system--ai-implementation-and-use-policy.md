# Mount Sinai Health System — AI Implementation and Use Policy (MSHS 223)

## Authority / authorship
- Formal system Policy & Procedure covering all 8 MSHS hospitals + Faculty Practices. **Policy Owner: DTP** (Digital and Technology Partners) — IT-led, not CMO-led, though clinical review is delegated to the AI Review Board and consent determinations to PPHS (Program for the Protection of Human Subjects).
- Governance bodies named: **AI Executive Committee** (oversees all), **AI Risks, Ethics, and Policy Committee**, **AI Review Board** (clinical/operational), **AI Teaching, Learning, and Discovery (TLD) Committee** (research/education tools >200 ISMMS users), PPHS (IRB office), Compliance Department, Privacy Officer, DTP.

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "The purpose of this Policy is to establish guidelines for the responsible, ethical, and effective implementation and use of artificial intelligence ... while minimizing risks; complying with applicable laws and regulations; conforming with standards for privacy, confidentiality, cybersecurity, and data security; upholding ethical principles; and ensuring patient safety, care quality, and equitable outcomes."
- "AI is a rapidly evolving area. This Policy may be amended or supplemented on an ongoing basis. Please review this Policy regularly."

## Complete verbatim clause inventory
**Scope/definitions:**
1. Applies to "all MSHS employees, voluntary physicians, students, trainees, volunteers, contractors, and third-party vendors who interact with, develop, or implement any AI Tools ... (collectively, 'AI Users'), without regard to the location of the AI Users ... or whether the AI Users operate the AI Tools on MSHS equipment ..., on the AI Users' personal devices, or on third-party electronic devices."
2. Covers "all AI technologies, including, but not limited to, AI Tools incorporating generative AI, machine learning, natural language processing, robotic process automation, and computer vision."
3. "AI Tool" = any app/software/system "that can independently change its own analytical methods and utilizes AI (including Generative and Algorithmic AI), machine learning, or other advanced algorithms to perform tasks, analyze data, or make (or assist in making) decisions." AAI vs GAI distinguished; "Sensitive Information" defined broadly.

**General Principles (a–j):**
4. "Relevance: AI Users should only use AI Tools in circumstances when those tools enhance or assist job-, training-, and education-related tasks ... by enhancing patient care, productivity, efficiency, and/or decision-making."
5. "Limited scope: AI Users should use AI Tools only for specifically identified and approved uses."
6. "Transparency: To the extent possible, AI Users should inform employees and patients about the particular uses of AI Tools."
7. "Explainability: The sponsors and users of an AI Tool should be able to describe in simple terms, and document in writing, what the model does, how it works, and its expected benefits. Sponsors should maintain written benchmarks, evaluation metrics, validation tests, or other methods to track if an AI Tool is functioning as intended."
8. "Education: AI Users and colleagues whose work involves outputs from AI Tools should be taught what each AI Tool does and how to use it and/or its outputs properly, and how to explain the AI Tool to anyone else who interacts with the AI Tool or its outputs, including patients."
9. "Fairness: AI Users must ensure that AI Tools are used in a fair and unbiased manner, and the appropriate personnel must regularly evaluate and audit AI Tools to identify and mitigate any biases or any inequitable outcomes."
10. "Human oversight: AI Users must have the ability to override any decisions made by AI Tools, ensuring that human oversight is always present in the decision-making process. In some cases, AI Tools will generate outputs that must be validated before they are utilized or relied upon."
11. "Privacy: AI Users must respect the privacy of MSHS patients, employees, students, and trainees, and ensure that any data collected is used only for legitimate and legally compliant purposes, including by obtaining the proper consents for such collection."
12. "Accountability and risk awareness: ... AI Users must recognize the limitations of these technologies; carefully review models, inputs, and outputs for errors; and remain vigilant to identify potential risks" — enumerated as: Input risks ("failure to obtain permission/consent to use input data; use of incorrect/incomplete/outdated data; use of biased or unrepresentative data; failure to anonymize or aggregate Sensitive Information"); Output risks ("biased or unrepresentative outputs; misleading, false, or hallucinatory outputs; outputs that include improper disclosures of Sensitive Information; outputs that plagiarize, fail to attribute, or infringe ...; misinterpretation of outputs leading to incorrect decisions; unpermitted or infringing use of outputs by non-MSHS parties"); AI model risks ("unreliability based on model's design, training materials, or maintenance; lack of transparency or explainability"); Oversight risks ("failure to secure and protect Sensitive Information; unauthorized access to model or training/output data; inadequate testing or calibration").
13. "Reporting: AI Users who have concerns ... should follow the Reporting Procedures set forth below."

**Use of Approved AI Tools:**
14. "Any AI Tool utilizing Sensitive Information that is used within MSHS ... must be approved and implemented in accordance with this Policy ..., other relevant MSHS policies, and any laws and regulations applicable to the AI Tool."
15. Compliance with MSHS policies "regarding data usage, HIPAA compliance, AI use in specific applications (e.g., teleconferencing software), intellectual property secrets, and records retention."
16. Third-party licensed information: user "must be familiar with and comply with any restrictions on the use of the information ... including the output data."
17. **Employment AI ban without consent:** "No AI User may use an AI Tool for personnel/employment decision-making purposes about applicants or employees (including decisions regarding screening, profiling, recruiting, hiring, retaining, promoting, transferring, monitoring the performance of, disciplining, demoting, or terminating) without the express written consent of MSHS' AI Executive Committee."
18. **Publication disclosure:** "Prior to publishing or distributing content generated by an AI Tool (in whole or in part), an AI User must receive approval from an appropriate supervisor or manager. When publishing or distributing ... AI Users must make known – through disclaimer, citation, or otherwise – that the content has been generated by AI."
19. "AI Users who violate this Policy may be subject to appropriate disciplinary action, up to and including immediate termination of employment or termination of contract."

**Reporting Procedures:**
20. Duty to "contact their supervisor or manager immediately" upon awareness of: policy violation; data-privacy/security breach ("In the case of a significant breach affecting protected patient information, MSHS' Privacy Officer, Director of Risk Management and/or CIO/HIPAA Security Officer must be informed immediately. In the case of a breach relating to human subject research, PPHS must be informed immediately"); or "known usage of and reliance on an AI Tool that violates other MSHS policies, including uses for insensitive, harassing, harmful, discriminatory, or biased purposes or outcomes, or that involve fraudulent or deceptive communications."
21. "Reports ... will be investigated by either the AI committee that approved the relevant AI Tool's intended use or by the AI Risks, Ethics, and Policy Committee. AI Users must cooperate."
22. **Suspension authority:** "An appropriate MSHS AI committee may, in its sole discretion, decide to suspend use of the AI Tool during any such investigation."
23. Corrective-measure cooperation duty.

**Approval procedures:**
24. "Prior to the purchase, implementation, or use of any AI Tool, the proposed sponsor must present the AI Tool to the relevant committee (e.g., the AI Review Board) for evaluation for its intended use."
25. **Use-by-use approval:** "The approval of an AI Tool for one intended use does not mean that the AI Tool is approved for different uses; that is, each intended use must be separately evaluated and approved."
26. Clinical/operational uses (incl. care delivery or billing/coding): "MSHS' AI Review Board must review and approve the AI Tool for its intended use ... to help ensure the technology will not compromise patient safety or quality of care."
27. Research uses: PPHS review/approval; students/faculty must comply with "the Appropriate Use of Technology Policy and the guidelines for Ethical Usage of AI (e.g., prohibiting the input of Sensitive Information)."
28. Employment uses: AI Executive Committee review "including by consulting with the General Counsel's Office," then HR/Labor Relations compliance check.
29. Education uses: comply with MSHS best practices/tool-specific guidelines; "For AI educational and research tools evaluated for use by over 200 anticipated ISMMS users, the AI Teaching, Learning, and Discovery Committee ('AI TLD Committee') will assist in evaluating and providing guidance ... to enhance pedagogical practices, address plagiarism issues, and improve learner outcomes and research discovery."
30. All other uses: "applicable Department leader(s) must review and approve."
31. Bias analysis requirement in evaluation: committees "will consider, as part of its analysis, how the AI Tool addresses and attempts to limit or avoid the potential for bias, risks associated with potential bias, and how those risks will be appropriately mitigated by MSHS."
32. **Material-change re-review:** "If, after approval, either the AI Tool or its intended use within MSHS materially changes, the revised technology and/or intended use should be re-evaluated in accordance with this Section."
33. **Approved-tool registry:** "DTP will maintain a list of all AI Tools approved for use within MSHS, noting the current approved scope of use."

**Implementation procedures (a–i):**
34. Identified protocols: documented protocols per tool; escalation if "infeasible or insufficient."
35. Applicable policies: implementation must comply with acceptable-use policies (IT-10); department leader responsible for "monitoring the use thereafter to ensure continued compliance."
36. Contract review per "MSHS enterprise contract policy ..., as supplemented by any applicable AI Tool intake checklists."
37. **Informed consent and/or notice:** "Prior to utilizing any AI Tool relating to the care of patients, PPHS will determine if the patient's consent is required and if so, will modify existing consent form(s) to include reference to the AI Tool or create a new consent form to address the technology. ... If informed consent is not required ..., the appropriate department or committee will evaluate whether notice to patients is required or advisable. If so, the applicable department leader will work collaboratively to develop an appropriate notification process, which may include written notice, verbal notice documented in the patient's medical record, or general posts and signage within MSHS or on MSHS' website."
38. **Medical record documentation:** "In no event may a provider or clinician utilize an AI Tool in the creation of patient medical record documentation without reviewing the record for accuracy and completeness and ultimately signing the record within the period required. MSHS will update its policies relating to medical record documentation as needed to incorporate use of AI Tools."
39. PHI protocols: notify Privacy Officer on approval; reference the tool "in MSHS' HIPAA Security Risk Assessment"; maintain signed BAA in contract repository; determine and document additional user training.
40. Billing/coding/revenue-cycle: CFO or designee consulted; relevant policies "reviewed, and updated, if needed."
41. Compliance integration: on approval, Compliance Department notified; tool included "in MSHS' compliance risk assessment"; risk-mitigation measures added "to the developed work plan."
42. Training: department leaders ensure users trained on "protocols for its use, the limitations and any specific risks of the technology, any specific privacy and/or compliance steps ..., and how to report concerns"; "Material changes to an AI Tool may require additional training."

**Review & Assessment (post-deployment monitoring):**
43. "After implementation of an AI Tool, the sponsor and the applicable Department leader(s) will periodically review and assess the performance of the AI Tool. Such review shall be no less frequent than annual, provided, however, that any AI Tool used in patient care settings, in the billing and coding of services, or in the employment context must be reviewed at least quarterly for the first 24 months of the use of the AI Tool. Thereafter, the relevant committee (e.g., the AI Review Board) may approve less frequent (but no less frequent than annual) reviews."
44. "Material changes to an AI Tool will require additional review to ensure that the AI Tool's originally intended benefits are being met or exceeded and that the AI Tool remains in compliance with this Policy."
45. "The review and assessment ... will be documented and reported to the Committee(s) that approved the use ... Any concerns identified ... will be reviewed by the applicable Committee and/or the Compliance Department, which will implement appropriate measures or an action plan."
46. **Decommissioning:** "Decommissioning and safely discontinuing an AI Tool once it has fulfilled its intended purpose or becomes outdated must be done in an ethical, legal, and privacy-compliant manner in line with any relevant regulatory requirements."

**Outside use by employees:**
47. "Employees should only use AI Tools in the performance of their job duties if the AI Tool is supplied or approved for use by MSHS ..., or if the AI Tool does not involve the use of any Sensitive Information. An employee who wishes to utilize such AI Tools ... must (i) first inform and obtain permission from their supervisor; (ii) must comply with this and all other applicable MSHS policies ...; and (iii) must not input or otherwise disclose to the AI Tool any Sensitive Information, unless otherwise specifically approved to do so."

**Disclaimer:** "Nothing in this policy is designed or intended to interfere with, restrain, or prevent employee communications regarding wages, hours, or other terms and conditions of employment or any other rights protected by the National Labor Relations Act or other applicable labor laws." (NLRA carve-out.)

## Sophistication markers
- **Clinical deployment governance (approval pipeline):** full lifecycle — committee routing by use type (clinical→AI Review Board; research→PPHS; employment→AI Executive Committee+GC; education→TLD; other→department leaders), use-by-use approval, material-change re-review, DTP-maintained approved-tool registry, contract/BAA gates.
- **Monitoring (post-deployment):** the strongest monitoring language of any policy document in this run — mandatory quarterly reviews for the first 24 months for patient-care/billing/employment tools, then annual minimum; documented reporting to approving committee; action plans for concerns; decommissioning requirements.
- **Model-failure handling:** reporting procedures with committee investigation, discretionary suspension of tools mid-investigation, corrective-measure duty; four-category risk taxonomy incl. "hallucinatory outputs."
- **Ambient-scribe consent rules:** generic but real — PPHS determines whether patient consent is required for patient-care AI; consent-form modification or new forms; notice hierarchy (written / verbal documented in record / signage/website). Not scribe-specific but applicable to scribes; falls short of per-encounter consent mandates (contrast UChicago residency policy).
- **Clinician liability positioning:** attestation duty — clinician must review and sign AI-assisted documentation "within the period required"; responsibility stays with the provider; discipline up to termination for policy violation. No indemnification language; enterprise takes no liability.

## Distinctives
- Quarterly × 24-month post-deployment review cadence — the most specific monitoring schedule seen in a health-system policy.
- NLRA carve-out and union-aware drafting.
- Employment-AI use requires AI Executive Committee express written consent — unusually strict.
- AI-publication disclaimer/citation duty for externally distributed AI content.
- Full-lifecycle coverage including decommissioning.

## Provisional classification
**Pioneer (policy-instrument tier).** IT-owned but committee-dense governance with use-routed approval, registry, re-review on material change, mandatory staged post-deployment monitoring, suspension authority, and consent/notice determination built into implementation. Whether the committees perform in practice is not provable from the document, but as written governance it is second only to UW COMP.308 in completeness and exceeds it on monitoring cadence.
