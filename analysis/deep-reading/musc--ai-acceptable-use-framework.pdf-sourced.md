# Deep Read — MUSC AI Acceptable Use Framework

## Authority / Authorship
- Produced by the **Center for the Advancement of Teaching & Learning (CATL)** with 13 named contributors (instructional designers, CATL leadership, College of Health Professions, Writing Center); feedback from Associate Provost. Approved by **Education Advisory Council (EAC), Student Affairs Advisory Council (SAAC), and Provost Council**.
- Not a conduct policy but a pedagogical classification framework; students remain "responsible for... complying with the MUSC Plagiarism and Artificial Intelligence Guidelines" (separate instrument).
- **Scholarly lineage**: adapted from Perkins, Furze, Roe & MacVaugh (2024), *The AI Assessment Scale* (AIAS), J. University Teaching & Learning Practice 21(06) — published, citable, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 licensed with required attribution. Hosted copy is a public-sharing PDF (mirrored at Marshall University CTL).

## Philosophy (verbatim framing quotes)
- "The framework isn't about restriction—it's about purposefully considering when AI enhances academic work versus when it might impede skill development or competency assessment."
- "AI tools should complement—never replace—the human connections and expertise that are foundational to the MUSC educational experience. We encourage the entire MUSC community to prioritize engaging with our wealth of human resources—including professors, peers, and specialists in the various academic support offices."
- Design notes: "We shifted from a numerical scale to a descriptive framework to avoid implying a hierarchy of value"; "progressive student agency."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory — the five categories

| # | Category | Description | Example tasks | Documentation requirements |
|---|----------|-------------|---------------|---------------------------|
| 1 | (No AI) | "AI may not be used at any point during the task." | "Exams and quizzes; Professional or clinical skills evaluations" | N/A |
| 2 | AI Planning | "AI may be used for planning during pre-task completion activities only." | "Initial research and brainstorming; Outlining and strategy development" | "A log of AI interactions used during planning (including prompts and responses); Reflection on which AI suggestions were helpful and why" |
| 3 | AI Limited | "AI may be used in a limited way to complete only specific aspects of the task as directed by the course instructor." | "Poster presentations (e.g., using AI to help with layout design while creating content independently); Revision and refinement based on AI Feedback" | "A log of AI interactions used for specified components (including prompts and responses); Brief explanation of how AI output was evaluated and used for specified components" |
| 4 | AI Extensive | "AI may be used extensively to complete any element of the task as directed by the course instructor. The instructor may allow students to determine the extent of AI use at their discretion." | "Complex data and evidence analysis; Practice Clinical Decision-Making; Exploring interdisciplinary approaches to complex healthcare challenges" | "Reflection on decision-making process for when and how AI was used; Explanation of how AI output was refined, evaluated, and integrated into the final work" |
| 5 | AI Exploration | "AI use is required to complete the task. AI serves as both a subject of inquiry and an innovation catalyst to enhance problem-solving or generate novel insights." | "Exploring AI usage in healthcare practice, education and research; Designing future-focused healthcare education models using AI (interactive chatbots, study skills application development)" | "Critical analysis of the strengths and limitations of AI for specified applications; Reflection on how AI transformed thinking and problem-solving processes" |

### Decision-making design notes (verbatim key items)
- Numerical→descriptive shift "to avoid implying a hierarchy of value."
- "'task' instead of 'assessment' terminology to recognize that AI use guidelines apply to a range of academic work beyond formal evaluations."
- Documentation examples "align with the level of AI integration, shifting focus from logging interactions to reflecting on decision-making processes."
- Renamings: "AI Collaboration"→"AI Limited"; "Full AI"→"AI Extensive" (feedback that "all permitted AI use could be viewed as collaboration").
- "AI Planning restricts use to ideation phases only, while both AI Limited and AI Extensive permit AI in final deliverables, establishing a clear progression."
- Limited vs Extensive: instructor-designated components vs broader application.
- Extensive vs Exploration: AI "primarily as a tool" vs "both tool and subject of inquiry."
- "We maintained instructor direction requirements across all categories."
- "AI tools are a complement to the support services we have and not a replacement."

## Sophistication Markers
- Grounded in the published literature (AIAS/Perkins et al. 2024) with DOI and formal adaptation credit — the only literature-anchored, openly licensed instrument in the corpus.
- Development transparency: the design-notes section documents category-construction rationale and renaming decisions from stakeholder feedback.
- Documentation requirements scale with permitted use (log → log+explanation → reflection+integration → critical analysis), i.e., assessment-aligned disclosure.
- Notably places "Practice Clinical Decision-Making" in AI Extensive — a permissive clinical-reasoning stance contrasting JABSOM/OHSU-PA firewalls.

## Distinctives
- A classification rubric, not a rulebook: no PHI/HIPAA content, no sanctions — purely the pedagogical lane, designed for instructors to label tasks.
- Creative Commons licensing invites cross-institutional adoption (hosted copy on Marshall University's CTL site demonstrates actual reuse).
- Category 5 (AI Exploration — mandatory AI use) exists nowhere else in the cohort.

## Provisional Classification: **Progressive-structured**
Justification: structurally elegant, literature-derived, governance-approved, with scaled documentation — but deliberately narrow (no privacy, clinical, enforcement, or governance content). A pioneer *rubric* inside a cautious-informed policy environment; as a standalone document it is progressive-structured.
