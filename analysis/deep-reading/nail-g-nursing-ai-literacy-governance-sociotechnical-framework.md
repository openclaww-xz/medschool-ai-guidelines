# NAIL-G: Nursing AI Literacy and Governance Framework (BMC Nursing, 2026)

## Research question
What would a nursing-specific AI literacy framework look like if it couples competency with governance and is anchored in socio-technical safety science (SEIPS, Sittig & Singh)?

## Methods
Best-fit framework synthesis + concept development (Walker & Avant/Rodgers); purposive iterative search (PubMed, CINAHL, Scopus, WoS, ERIC + grey literature from WHO, ANA, AACN, ICMJE, WAME, COPE), Jan 2010–Dec 2025; 14 core frameworks coded deductively to 2 a priori anchors (AACN Essentials domains; socio-technical work-system dimensions) with inductive residual codes; single-reviewer screening with co-author face-validity review; audit trail, traceability checks.

## Sample
Not empirical — 14 included frameworks/guidance documents (WHO ethics 2021 + LMM guidance 2025, ANA position statement 2022, Ng et al. and Long & Magerko AI literacy, DigComp 2.2, Jisc, AACN Essentials, SEIPS 2.0, Sittig & Singh, ICMJE/WAME/COPE).

## Key findings
- NAIL-G = 5 domains: (1) Foundations & Tools; (2) Quality, Safety & Risk; (3) Professionalism, Ethics & Law; (4) Systems, Equity & Social Impact; (5) Scholarship, Communication & Governance.
- Each domain has competencies plus MEASURABLE indicators, e.g., % artefacts with documented prompts/verification notes; % AI recommendations independently verified; AI-related incident/near-miss reports per quarter and time-to-mitigation; % assignments with complete AI-use disclosure; # tools with documented equity impact assessment; # curriculum/policy governance actions per year.
- Core reframe: AI literacy = "competency-plus-governance," not technical skill alone; approved-tool lists, pre/post-use safety checks, disclosure statements, artifact audits, committee oversight with nursing representation.
- Three illustrative teaching cases (virtual patients with verification; evidence briefs with provenance; equity impact analysis).

## What it says about who leads policy
Multi-level: WHO/international bodies supply principles; professional associations/regulators (ANA, AACN, nursing councils) should set position statements, competency standards, accreditation requirements; ministries of health, health systems, and educational institutions implement governance committees with nursing voices. Journal policies (ICMJE/WAME/COPE) govern scholarship.

## Limitations (authors')
Conceptual only — no empirical evaluation; purposive (not systematic) search; single-reviewer screening; HIC-dominated source frameworks; untested feasibility/reliability.

## Relation to corpus findings
Another 2026 framework paper confirming the governance vacuum: NAIL-G exists precisely because "AI diffusion has outpaced discipline-specific literacy, governance, and safety guidance." Like the ASPPH and implementation papers, it prescribes governance artifacts (approved-tool lists, disclosure norms, incident reporting) whose real-world adoption our corpus can measure directly across medical schools and health systems.
