# NBME — AI in Assessment: Ethics, Innovation and Research (Executive Summary, Mar 2024)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: NBME. Executive summary of an internal panel: moderator Dr. Andrea Anderson, MD (GWU Family Medicine); presenters **Victoria Yaneva, PhD (Manager, NLP Research, NBME)** and **Kimberly Swygert, PhD (Director, Test Development Innovations, NBME)**. Authored by NBME staff researchers — the assessment body speaking in its own technical voice.
- Powers claimed: over NBME's research and test-development practices; "as our work to develop a set of ethical guidelines moves forward" — guidelines promised, not yet issued here. Binds NBME process; influences the assessment field.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **evidence-first innovation with validity as the fixed constraint**. Framing quotes:
  - "The integration of these innovative technologies with medical education assessment has significant transformative potential, **as well as ethical guidelines that must be considered**."
  - "**The responsibility for the ethical use of these applications rests entirely on humans.**"
  - "AI is a tool that **will be integrated into NBME's existing foundational measurement work; it will not replace it**."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
**Impact areas (opportunity claims)**
1. Automated scoring: "pretrained transformers introduce a notable shift by enabling adaptation even with relatively small datasets through a process called **'fine-tuning'**... Evidence shows that this strategy can yield highly accurate automated scoring results."
2. Test development: "AI models can help obtain **preliminary estimates of item difficulty and expected response times prior to conducting pretests**, a capability that **enhances fairness in testing**. AI-predicted response times can help in **equitable assignment of newly developed items to test forms**."
3. Item development: "Using generative AI to assist human experts in creating test items is an area that has generated particular excitement... one important difference between reading passages and clinical test items is that **the latter cannot be fictional**. Instead, items in medical education must maintain **clinical correctness and relevance to current medical practices**."
**Hallucination clause (key)**
4. "the use of generative AI in content creation raises concerns about the **potential generation of factually inaccurate information, leading to 'hallucinations' in the generated content. Addressing this issue is imperative** to ensuring the reliability and relevance of AI-assisted content development in medical education. Numerous approaches to limiting hallucinations are being investigated by the AI community." — the most explicit hallucination treatment in the corpus so far.
**NBME research program**
5. Complex constructs: "To evaluate complex constructs like communication skills and clinical reasoning, medical educators require innovative assessments that go beyond the multiple-choice format... if 1,000 examinees see 20 open-ended items... 20,000 responses to score." AI scoring "can manage this variability at scale, **reserving human review for a smaller fraction of the more challenging responses**."
6. Method: SMEs mark responses correct/incorrect → fine-tune transformers → compare predictions to SMEs → "**F score**... a measure of the extent to which the scores assigned by the model overlap with those assigned by human raters."
7. Personalized feedback: "If this feedback is not accurate or prompt, learners may not benefit... can help deliver accurate and personalized feedback almost immediately."
8. Item generation: "NBME has begun an exploration of AI-assisted item development; however, it is still in its early stages. A key requirement... is to **ensure the items developed through this innovative, efficient process meet the same rigorous quality standards as current items**. Achieving this necessitates **placing human experts at the center of this process**, enabling them to steer development and uphold our quality benchmarks."
**Ethics framework (six categories, quoted core)**
9. Data Privacy: "Incorporate clear rules and assurances of how data will be collected, how personal data will be protected, how long data will be retained, and whether it's acceptable to share or repurpose data."
10. Accountability: "Consider regulatory requirements regarding the development and deployment of AI technologies to ensure compliance. **Be prepared to explain the aims, motivations and reasons for using AI.**"
11. Fairness: "**Begin with the assumption that the use of AI is never neutral or impartial**, and acknowledge that the use of AI, especially for tasks like automated decision-making, **could lead to discriminatory outcomes**."
12. Transparency: "Prepare to share information and an explanation about how AI is used through methods appropriate to your audience."
13. Human Control and Oversight: "**Maintain human oversight** to ensure that an organization's use of AI systems is transparent, explainable and trustworthy."
14. Promotion of Human Values: "**Support the principle of non-maleficence, or 'do not harm,' applying to both foreseeable and negative outcomes**" [sic].
**Trust-building commitments**
15. "Creating multidisciplinary teams to collect robust evidence and present it to the public through **peer-reviewed research**."
16. "Releasing datasets for the purpose of competitions or collaborations... **Through transparency, we build trust and ensure accountability.**" (Yaneva epigraph.)
17. Validity commitment: "NBME is working to identify item-writing tasks and process that **must continue to rely on human expertise**, and those can be made more efficient through the use of AI... We will design our future ethical guidelines to **merge with existing guidelines for test development, test scoring, and, in particular, the gathering of evidence for test validity. We will address potential AI bias with the same careful, methodical approach we use to tackle other potential threats to validity.**" — AI bias treated as a validity threat, folded into psychometric tradition.
**Further reading canon**: NBME Kaggle competition; NBME Research Library; NBME NLP proceedings; BEA'24 shared task on item difficulty/response-time prediction; **European Commission Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI**; **Biden Executive Order on Safe, Secure, Trustworthy AI**; "The Ethics of AI Ethics – An Evaluation of Guidelines."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Highest technical specificity in the corpus to this point: pretrained transformers, fine-tuning, F-score human-agreement metrics, item-difficulty prediction, free-text scoring at scale. Names **hallucination** explicitly and calls addressing it "imperative."
- Assessment-validity threat: central, not incidental — bias as "threats to validity," equity via item-time allocation.
- Evidence: peer-review norm, dataset release, Kaggle competitions — an open-science posture no other body matches.
- Mandates: internal commitments ("will," "must continue to rely on human expertise"); no external rules. Guidelines explicitly deferred ("moves forward").

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- Only document that operationalizes AI **inside** the assessment pipeline (scoring, item generation, difficulty prediction) rather than guarding against it — inverts the defensive posture of the USMLE ChatGPT post.
- Cites EU and Biden EO — international/federal anchor references absent from AMA/AAMC texts.
- "AI is never neutral or impartial" is the strongest fairness premise in the corpus.

## (f) Provisional classification
**pioneer** (within the assessment niche) — first-mover technical depth, explicit hallucination handling, validity-integrated ethics, open-data trust mechanics. Its restraint (human experts at center, same quality standards) is enacted, not gestured. As a national framework voice: technically leading, ethically derivative-but-disciplined.
