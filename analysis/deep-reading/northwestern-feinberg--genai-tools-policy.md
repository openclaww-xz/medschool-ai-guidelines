# Deep Read — Northwestern Feinberg GenAI Tools Policy

## Authority / Authorship
- Official FSM **Medical Student Policy**, filed under Professionalism/Learning Environment; **Relevant LCME Element 3.5 (Learning Environment/Professionalism)** — one of the few policies that maps itself to an LCME element.
- **Approving body: Curriculum Committee.** Revision history: 9/6/2023 original (per history: clarified grammar-checker acceptability, PHI prohibition, "removed requirement that generative AI is always attributed"); 12/4/2024 (added Copilot-in-Bing curricular upload permission). SHA256 recorded in front-matter — the repo's only hash-verified PDF.

## Philosophy (verbatim framing quotes)
- "Many assignments in medical school are designed such that the process of completing the work is as important as the final product, if not more so. This is particularly true of writing assignments, which can clarify thinking, promote the development of reflective practice, and foster critical reasoning and analysis skills that are foundational to the practice of medicine."
- Core rule: "Medical students may not utilize generative AI and other language models as a substitute for their own knowledge acquisition, analysis, and self-reflection."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory

### Scope carve-out
- "This policy addresses the use of generative AI and large language models to author original work products required by Feinberg. It does not address the use of common tools that utilize AI such as spelling and grammar checkers or reference managers."

### Persons affected
- "Medical students and Faculty."

### Operative clauses (III)
1. **Default authorship rule**: "Required assignments for submission must be authored by students directly unless specific permission is given to utilize generative AI. To do so represents plagiarism or the misrepresentation of the source of academic work, which is against the Enforcement of University and Feinberg Behavioral Standards Policy."
2. **Clinical documentation**: "Students may not create H&P or patient care notes using artificial intelligence applications outside of those supported by the EHR (e.g., dot phrases, Smart Phrases, other system-generated text). As described in each clerkship orientation, this may be grounds for failing a clerkship. Protected health information should never otherwise be used within a generative AI tool."
3. **Curricular materials**: "Students may only upload Feinberg curricular materials (including but not limited to lecture slides, learning guides, and written feedback) into systems protected by Northwestern University IT, which requires logging in with Northwestern credentials. Protected systems include Microsoft's large language model chatbot, Copilot in Bing. Students may not input Feinberg curricular material into publicly available generative AI tools without the approval of Feinberg leadership or content authors in advance. Students must also ensure that the use of these tools does not violate copyright or intellectual property laws."
4. **Assigned AI use**: "Students may be asked to use certain AI technologies for specific assignments... must carefully follow the instructional guidance of faculty... Students are responsible for any inaccuracies or misinformation resulting from the use of these tools."
5. **Scholarly work**: "students must adhere to generative AI policies set forth by journals and organizations and disclose when and how these tools have been used."

### Revision history (IV)
- "12/4/2024: Added that curricular materials may be uploaded into Copilot in Bing using NetID and password."
- "9/6/2023: Clarified that the use of spelling and grammar checkers and reference managers is acceptable; specified that using PHI in a generative AI tool outside of the EHR is prohibited; removed requirement that generative AI is always attributed." (**documented deregulation** — dropping the universal-attribution requirement, a rare instance of a policy getting less stringent as it matured)

## Sophistication Markers
- EHR-native nuance: prohibits AI note-writing outside EHR-supported mechanisms while naming legitimate EHR text tools (dot phrases, Smart Phrases) — clinically literate boundary drawing.
- Named approved platform (Copilot in Bing) with credential-gated curricular upload.
- Revision history shows iterative tuning in both directions (add platform; drop attribution requirement) — evidence of deliberate maintenance, and of a 2023 start.
- Clerkship-failure consequence attached directly to documentation violations.
- IP/copyright rider in the curricular-materials clause.

## Distinctives
- The only cohort-C policy with a visible **deregulatory** edit (attribution requirement removed 9/2023).
- Being cited as a reference by LSUHSC-NO (2023 vintage) — an early-mover whose text diffused to state schools.
- Concision as a design choice: 5 clauses, zero definitions section, yet each clause carries specific operational content.

## Provisional Classification: **Progressive-structured**
Justification: early (Sept 2023), LCME-mapped, committee-approved, iteratively revised with documented rationale, clinically and technically specific (EHR text tooling, credential-gated Copilot), and honest about scope limits. Lacks the governance/bias/definitions apparatus of the pioneer tier, but shows the highest sophistication-per-word ratio in the cohort.
