# NYU Grossman — Guidelines for Educational Use of GenAI (rev. 8/13/24)

## Authority / Authorship
- NYU Grossman Institute for Innovations in Medical Education (IIME); contact Dr. Marc Triola. Self-labeled twice: "This is a set of guidelines – not an official policy of NYU Langone or NYU Grossman School of Medicine." Complements official honor-code policies.

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "These guidelines are designed to encourage safe use of AI tools by NYU Grossman School of Medicine medical students, residents, and fellows."
- "Treat using AI like collaborating with a classmate."

## Complete clause inventory
1. "Unless explicitly instructed otherwise, AI tools should not be used on exams, quizzes, or other assessments."
2. Coursework use OK under two conditions: instructor approves AND honor code not violated; if used, "cite the specific tool used and ideally include the prompt."
3. Course directors free to set course/module policies in syllabi.
4. Never put personal/confidential info in public AI — enumerates PHI, research data, course materials, lecture slides, assessment questions, NYU IP, manuscripts/grants/essays.
5. Embedded-AI warning: Word/PowerPoint/Outlook/Google Docs/Grammarly "now have integrated AI. When you use the AI features of those tools, you are using public AI."
6. Lecture materials are faculty/NYU IP; no non-MCIT AI may record or upload lectures.
7. For confidential info: HIPAA-compliant ChatGPT provided by MCIT (nyugpt.nyumc.org).
8. Vendor-license clause: some content providers (journals, UpToDate, Amboss) prohibit ANY AI use with their material.
9. Accountability for accuracy; "AI makes mistakes and can include fabricated information. Always double check."
10. "You should not use any generative AI application in connection with or to generate your clinical documentation, such as patient notes included in the medical record."
11. Public-vs-private decision matrix (~14 worked scenarios: virtual OSCE, Anki generation, differential on real patient, Epic note, UpToDate summarization...) mapping each to public tools / private MCIT ChatGPT / neither.

## Sophistication markers
- The decision matrix is the standout: real med-ed use cases × public/private tool routing.
- Embedded-AI clause; vendor-license clause (UpToDate/Amboss); named secure instance; IP-recording clause.
- Honest self-labeling as guidance-not-policy.

## Distinctives
- The clearest exemplar in the corpus of the *guidance layer doing what policy won't*: concrete, tool-aware, scenario-based, written by an education-innovation unit rather than a governance body.

## Classification
- **pioneer (guidance-tier)** — permissive-with-guardrails posture, secure provisioned tool, scenario matrix; lacks only formal enactment.
