# OHSU DMICE (W. Hersh) — Course Policy for ChatGPT & GenAI

## Authority / Authorship
- William Hersh, MD, Professor, Department of Medical Informatics & Clinical Epidemiology — single-instructor course policy for BMI 510/610, AMIA 10x10, MINF 705B/709A (updated 9/30/2024).

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "These tools should not, however, be used to substitute one's own knowledge."
- Evidence-based: "This policy is based in part on the results of this study" — his peer-reviewed paper showing LLMs "can 'pass' the knowledge-assessment portions of this course."

## Complete clause inventory
1. Discussion forums: converse with AI for ideas OK; "what is actually posted in the forum by students should represent their own ideas, language, and thought processes."
2. Homework self-assessment: may ask AI about topics, "but are expected to answer the questions based on their own knowledge... not use generative AI with the questions themselves until after they have submitted their answers."
3. Term paper: AI brainstorming OK; warned "their output tends to focus on generalities and may be prone to confabulation, especially in generating references."
4. Final exam: "students must not access generative AI during the final exam, just as they may not consult other humans."
5. Guiding principle: "students should ask themselves: Will the tool's output be something I will be turning in directly?"
6. Derived from OHSU academic-integrity policy; program-wide GenAI policy "in development."

## Sophistication markers
- Only policy in the corpus grounded in the instructor's own published performance study of LLMs on the course's assessments.

## Classification
- **cautious-informed (course-level)** — scholarly, per-activity granular, containment-default; a model of instructor-level policy where school policy is absent.
