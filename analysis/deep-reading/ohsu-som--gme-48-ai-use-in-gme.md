# Deep Read — OHSU GME-48 AI Use in GME

## Authority / Authorship
- School of Medicine Policy, **Policy Number GME 48**, effective 2/12/2026. Policy Owner: **Graduate Medical Education**; Amendment/Approving Committee: **GMEC**.
- Version control: v1 12/18/2025 (GMEC, original); v2 2/12/2026 — "Addition of language as recommended by AI Governance Committee." Ties into ISP-25 (OHSU AI Information Security Policy), AI Governance Committee, Office of Research Integrity.
- Applies to all ACGME-accredited GME: PDs/APDs, supervising clinicians, coordinators, GME office staff, and "Trainees, i.e. residents and fellows." At non-OHSU sites, that institution's policy governs.
- Resources table credits **"UT Health San Antonio GME AI Policy — GME AI policy used as resource in development of OHSU GME AI policy."** (explicit cross-institutional lineage; also cites CAILHS center.)

## Philosophy (verbatim framing quotes)
- Purpose: "Enable innovation while safeguarding professionalism and academic integrity; Promote ethical, equitable, and secure AI use across education, research, and clinical care."
- "AI tools are intended to supplement—not replace—human judgment and expertise. Clinicians and trainees remain ultimately responsible for the accuracy, integrity, and ethical application of AI-assisted work."
- Skill-acquisition rationale: "Development of clinical skills requires trainees to independently acquire appropriate knowledge and skills, both while utilizing AI tools as an adjunct, as well as without AI-facilitated assistance."
- "Without guidance, trainees and faculty may be unclear on the safest, ethical, and professional ways to utilize AI tools in their learning and professional development."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory

### Definitions (III)
- AI; GenAI with named examples "(e.g., ChatGPT, DALL·E, Copilot, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, etc.)".
- **Automation Bias**: "The human tendency to over-rely on AI systems or decision-support tools, even when the output may be incorrect."
- **Confabulation**: "Generation of plausible but factually inaccurate information by AI systems."
- **Attribution**: "Proper acknowledgment and documentation of AI assistance in academic, clinical, or research work. AI utilizes data from various sources including copyrighted material, owned intellectual property, or other content requiring license for use, which must be recognizable when reviewing AI-generated content."
- **Algorithmic Bias**: "Systematic and repeatable errors that create and perpetuate unfair outcomes due to biased data or algorithm design."
- Consent vs disclosure distinction; PII; PHI; Educational Records; Clinical Learning Environment (OHSU network definition; non-OHSU site deference).

### Guiding Principles (IV)
1. Professionalism and Ethics: "Physicians and trainees are stewards of the ethical use of technology."
2. Responsible Use: "AI tools may only be used when approved or authorized by OHSU."
3. Attribution: "Regardless of the AI tool used, attribution of how the tool is used is required" (outside OHSU approved/embedded tools).
4. Transparency and Disclosure: disclose "tool, uses, their purpose, and the application in the work."
5. Accountability: shared responsibility; "it is imperative to include a 'human in the loop' to maintain oversight of the AI-assisted process."
6. Data Protection: "PHI or identifiable data (PII) may only be entered into OHSU prior-approved AI tools." Violations handled under ISP-23 sanctions policy.
7. Oversight: supervising-clinician oversight of AI use in duties and academic endeavors.

### Acceptable and Prohibited Uses (V) — three-tier structure
1. **Prohibited (strictly; disciplinary action)**:
   - "Inputting PHI, PII, or identifiable data or education records (including evaluations, milestones, data for CCC, or applicant data) into non-approved tools."
   - "Submitting AI-generated work as original without disclosure."
   - "Using AI to evaluate, rank or score residency or fellowship applicants unless the process is approved by institutional leadership and uses OHSU-approved methods."
   - "Using AI to bypass required academic or administrative work without appropriate engagement, editing, and verifying such work."
2. **Permitted with Attribution** (disclosure + verification): learning/teaching aid with critical faculty/trainee review; "clinical decision support tools approved by the institution and integrated into secure, HIPAA compliant systems."
3. **Encouraged**: "Using institutionally approved AI for education, workflow efficiency, or patient communication enhancement."

### Application by Setting (VI)
A. **Clinical**: only OHSU-approved tools (per AI Governance Committee); **"Individual programs may restrict or prohibit the use of certain OHSU-approved AI tools by trainees until the program has determined the trainee has reached pre-determined educational milestones"** (milestone-gated AI access — rare); "Patient consent and disclosure are required prior to use of AI scribes or documentation tools including documentation of the consent"; clinicians responsible for note accuracy — "examination for confabulations, hallucinations"; "Faculty oversight and timely review of AI-assisted documentation are mandatory."
B. **Educational**: assignments under one's name must be own original work; AI use requires transparency/acknowledgement; programs include AI expectations in syllabi and "model transparent, appropriate use"; "Unauthorized or undisclosed use of AI tools in coursework for the receiving audience constitutes a violation of the academic integrity policy"; verbal attribution example given: "(e.g., 'the differential I am presenting was generated by AI')"; educational data (evaluations, interview scores, milestones) only into OHSU-approved tools.
C. **Research**: comply with Research Integrity and Data Governance; disclose AI in "manuscripts, abstracts, or presentations" and verify accuracy; confidential data needs Office of Research Integrity approval; "Authors must accept responsibility for the accuracy and veracity of the work submitted in their name(s)."
D. **Professionalism**: physicians as "stewards in the ethical use of technology"; "we acknowledge AI based contributions to our work, reflect and act accordingly on the potential health impacts of AI, model responsible AI use"; "We aim to mitigate bias introduced and inherent in AI algorithms and will cross-check AI contributed work with validated academic sources."

### Resources / Governance / Enforcement (VII–IX)
- Registry of approved/prohibited AI tools via IT Security portal; pilot requests through AI Governance Committee; "Accessibility and assistive AI use will comply with all applicable laws and regulations."
- AI Governance Committee coordinates review/implementation/updates; GME office owns/disseminates; "reviewed annually or more frequently as AI technologies evolve"; aligns with Academic Integrity Policy, HIPAA/FERPA, Information Security Policy.
- Violations → corrective/disciplinary action; report to Program Director, GME, and/or OHSU Integrity Office.

## Sophistication Markers
- Named-tool examples in GenAI definition; definitions of **automation bias**, **confabulation**, **algorithmic bias** (cognitive-bias-aware vocabulary).
- Three-tier permitted/attributed/encouraged use structure.
- **Milestone-gated program-level restriction** of trainee AI use (educational-sequence thinking).
- Explicit **AI scribe patient consent** with documentation requirement.
- **AI in residency applicant screening** explicitly controlled (must be institutionally approved) — matches the era's GME flashpoint.
- Verbal-attribution exemplar for clinical team communication.
- Version control table + credited source policy (UT Health San Antonio) — documented policy lineage and iterative amendment via governance committee.

## Distinctives
- One of the few true GME-dedicated policies (with UKY, UTH-SA lineage). Covers residents/fellows, CCC data, applicant scoring, AI scribes, milestone gating — the resident-specific risk surface no UME policy reaches.
- Attribution definition embeds copyright/IP awareness.

## Provisional Classification: **Pioneer**
Justification: GME-specific depth (milestone gating, scribe consent, applicant-screening control, CCC-data protection), governance-backed versioning, bias/confabulation vocabulary, and a tiered use structure. It is a pioneer *within the GME niche* even though its institutional scope is narrower than Utah's enterprise instrument.
