# Deep Read — OHSU PA Program GenAI Policy

## Authority / Authorship
- OHSU School of Medicine PA Program policy ("Artificial Intelligence (AI) Use in the Physician Associate Program"), published 2026-06.
- Policy Ownership: the PA Program; annual-or-more-frequent review; aligned to OHSU Academic Integrity Policy, HIPAA/FERPA, Institutional Information Security Policy; violations to Director of Academic Education, Director of Clinical Education, or Program Director; "Some violations must also be reported to the OHSU Integrity Office."
- Structurally a sibling adaptation of OHSU GME-48 (same I–IX skeleton, same definitions and three-tier use scheme) — evidence of an institutional template propagating across programs (GME → PA).

## Philosophy (verbatim framing quotes)
- Enablement stance: "The OHSU PA Program encourages the ethical and responsible use of generative AI tools to assist students during their training. These tools can simplify tasks, offer creative inspiration, and help personalize learning and study strategies, making generative AI a valuable learning partner for students."
- Process-of-learning caveat: "many assignments during PA training emphasize the process of completing the work, fostering the development of critical reasoning, analysis skills, and all foundational skills in the field of medicine. Therefore, students must not use generative AI tools as replacements for their own knowledge acquisition, analysis, or self-reflection."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory

### Definitions (II)
AI; GenAI (named: "ChatGPT, DALL·E, Copilot, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, etc."); **LLM** ("An AI text prediction system trained on vast amounts of text that can mimic understanding by generating coherent, context-aware human-like language"); Automation Bias; "Hallucination or confabulation"; Attribution (with copyright/IP note); Algorithmic Bias; Consent vs disclosure; PII; PHI; Educational Records/FERPA.

### Guiding Principles (III)
1. Professionalism/Ethics — "Trainees are stewards of the ethical use of technology."
2. Responsible Use — "AI tools may only be used when approved or authorized by OHSU"; non-OHSU site deference.
3. Attribution required regardless of tool (outside OHSU approved/embedded tools).
4. Transparency and Disclosure — with **suggested disclosure language**: "This work was prepared with assistance from [AI tool name] to support [purpose] based on [source material]. [Your name] has reviewed all content for accuracy."
5. Accountability — shared responsibility; "'human in the loop'".
6. Data Protection — "PHI, identifiable data (PII), and FERPA-protected student information may only be entered into OHSU prior-approved AI tools"; ISP-23 sanctions.
7. Oversight — by students, faculty, supervising clinicians or designated supervisors.

### Acceptable and Prohibited Uses (V) — three tiers
1. **Prohibited**: inputting PHI/PII/education records into non-approved tools; submitting AI work as original without disclosure; "Using AI to evaluate, rank or score applicants unless the process is approved by institutional leadership and uses OHSU-approved methods"; bypassing required work without engagement/editing/verification.
2. **Permitted with Attribution**: learning/teaching/assessment aid with critical review; approved HIPAA-compliant clinical decision support.
3. **Encouraged**: institutionally approved AI for education, workflow efficiency, patient communication.

### Application by Setting (VI)
A. **Clinical**:
- "Use of AI tools including scribes are permitted for the purpose of transcription only. Patient consent must be obtained and use of AI must be disclosed and documented. Students must verify the accuracy of any transcription used in documentation."
- **"Generative AI tools may not be used by physician associate students for clinical documentation; including generation of summary, assessment and treatment plans."**
- "Use of generative AI as a clinical decision-making tool is discouraged; students are encouraged to use high-quality resources such as peer-reviewed publications, society guidelines, and expert opinion reviews."
B. **Educational**: original work under own name; "Use of AI is prohibited on all exams unless otherwise stated in the course syllabus" (default-off for exams); syllabus inclusion and modeling by programs; "Unauthorized or undisclosed use of AI tools in coursework constitutes a violation of the academic integrity policy"; educational data only in approved tools; **"Students are permitted to input curricular material into OHSU-approved generative AI tools for the purposes of studying. Use of other generative AI tools is at the discretion of the course director."**
C. **Research**: Research Integrity/Data Governance compliance; disclosure in manuscripts/abstracts/presentations + accuracy verification; confidential data needs ORI approval; author responsibility.
D. **Professionalism**: stewardship, modeling, bias mitigation, cross-checking "with validated academic sources."

### Resources/Governance (VII–VIII)
Approved/prohibited tool registry via IT Security portal; AI Governance Committee for pilots; accessibility/assistive AI compliance.

## Sophistication Markers
- LLM definition and hallucination/confabulation pairing; automation-bias and algorithmic-bias definitions.
- Ready-made disclosure sentence template (rare — most policies demand disclosure without providing language).
- Granular clinical taxonomy: transcription-only scribes permitted vs generative documentation banned for students; clinical decision-making "discouraged" with named preferred alternatives.
- Study-use carve-out: curricular material into approved tools for studying — an unusually concrete learning-affordance clause.

## Distinctives
- PA-specific conservatism on clinical documentation (stricter than OHSU GME-48, which allows approved AI documentation with mandatory faculty review): students here cannot generate A&P/plans at all.
- Demonstrates intra-institutional template diffusion (GME-48 skeleton reused with program-level deltas) — an OHSU house style.

## Provisional Classification: **Progressive-structured**
Justification: tiered use structure, disclosure template, defined vocabulary, and learning-partner framing; but shorter governance tail than GME-48 (no version table, no milestone gating) and deliberately more restrictive clinically, reflecting student (not resident) supervision realities.
