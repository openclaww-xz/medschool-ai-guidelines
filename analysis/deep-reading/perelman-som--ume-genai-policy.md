# Perelman School of Medicine (Penn) — UME Policy on Use of Generative AI (UME.AC.119)

## Authority / Authorship
- Formal numbered policy: UME.AC.119; Effective Date 07/21/2026; approved by the Undergraduate Medical Education Committee (UMEC) "in collaboration with PSOM leadership, Penn Medicine IT, the PSOM Medical Education Technology Committee (METC) and institutional data governance bodies."
- Sixteen named individual contributors (students/faculty: Stacey Kassutto, Flint Wang, Nathaniel Srikureja, Daniel Lai, Zoe Barinaga, Suzana Tsao, Daniel DiPietro, Rosalyn Schorr, Vivian Lee, Charles Bae, Nadia Bennett, Tessa Cook, Neha Patel, Andrew King, William Vervilles, Leah Carr) — unusually transparent authorship, apparently student-inclusive.
- Transparency about AI-assisted drafting: "This policy was developed with the assistance of OpenAI's ChatGPT Edu and Microsoft CoPilot (September-October 2025 and July 2026), which provided examples and frameworks drawn from publicly available institutional AI policies."
- References AAMC AI Policy Development Checklist for Medical Education.
- Scope: all UME students + instructional faculty; explicitly excludes BGS/Master's programs and non-UME operations.

## Policy Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "It aims to support innovation while preserving independent critical thinking, academic integrity, and data privacy."
- "AI tools can enhance learning and efficiency but also pose risks related to accuracy, bias, overreliance, and inappropriate data use."
- "AI tools are intended to support, not replace, the development of independent clinical reasoning, critical thinking, and professional judgment. This policy also provides guardrails to ensure responsible, ethical, and secure use."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory by Theme

### Definitions
- "'Artificial intelligence (AI)' refers to the ability and development of computer machines to perform tasks that previously would require human intelligence such as recognizing patterns, synthesizing data, problem solving, and adapting to new information."
- "'Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) such as large language models (LLMs)' refers to any technology or application that can generate text, images, audio, videos, and other media based on a user-submitted prompt."
- "'Public AI tools' refers to any service not licensed/contracted by Penn/PSOM/UPHS, while 'approved tools' refers to those services specifically listed on the PMACS Website."

### Classroom / coursework (prohibition-default)
- "Use of GenAI is not permitted in assignments, examinations, or other academic work unless explicitly authorized by course faculty."
- "When GenAI use is permitted, students must follow faculty instructions. Students remain responsible for the accuracy of all AI-assisted work."

### Curricular-materials IP
- "PSOM curricular materials (including but not limited to slides, small group handouts, study guides, practice questions, course syllabi, etc.) may only be uploaded into PSOM approved AI tools."
- "Faculty, students, and staff are not permitted to input PSOM curricular material into publicly available GenAI applications without the advance written approval of content authors or PSOM leadership. These guidelines are in place to protect intellectual property and copyright laws and will be enforced as described in the 'Violations of Conduct' section below."

### Clinical learning environment
- "AI use in clinical settings requires supervising faculty notification and approval and must align with site-specific policies. Unauthorized use of AI applications to create clinical documentation will be grounds for failing an assignment, course, or clerkship, and may initiate an academic review of professionalism."
- "AI tools may only be used for clinical documentation if they are approved and integrated within Penn Medicine and/or Children's Hospital of Philadelphia (CHOP) clinical systems. Use of non-approved AI tools for clinical documentation is prohibited. Violations may result in failure of an assignment, course or clerkship and may trigger an academic review of professionalism."
- "Use of AI tools such as Open Evidence for clinical decision support may be permitted in the clinical learning environment depending on the educational context, and only at the discretion of and with permission from individual course directors. Users of AI tools should verify all AI-generated content and remain responsible for accuracy."
- "Students must follow AI policies at all clinical training sites. While most align with PSOM/Penn Medicine policies, some external sites, including but not limited to, the Philadelphia Veterans Affairs (VA) hospital and CHOP may have additional requirements."

### Scholarly work
- "AI use should be cited, and users remain responsible for accuracy, integrity, and compliance with applicable policies and laws."
- "Students must not enter their own or others' unpublished or experimental data, grant proposals, proprietary or copyright-protected information, or confidential material into public AI platforms without the expressed permission of their faculty supervisor."
- "Use of AI tools with sensitive research data including HIPAA and FERPA-protected information should be discussed with supervising faculty."
- "Students and faculty must follow AI policies required by journals, professional organizations, or conferences when producing scholarly work."
- "Students and faculty should disclose the use of AI tools in the development of manuscripts, presentations, posters, and abstracts per journal and meeting guidelines."

### Data security / approved tools
- "Users must follow Penn Data Risk Classifications and use only institutionally approved AI tools for any sensitive or protected data."
- PMACS AI Tools described as providing "a quick visual overview and additional guidance on: Which AI tools are approved for use at PSOM; What types of data may and may not be used in each tool; Key reminders related to privacy, security, and compliance (HIPAA/FERPA)."
- "Confidential, restricted, proprietary, or legally protected information ('Protected Data' ex: PHI, learner data protected under FERPA, internal PSOM documents, etc.) may not be entered into any public AI tools."
- "Use of public AI tools with nonconfidential, nonrestricted, and nonproprietary data is generally permissible, provided users comply with applicable intellectual property, attribution, and academic integrity standards."
- "AI tools must be used in a way that honors copyrights, patents, and trademarks. Avoid any unauthorized copying or sharing of protected materials."

### Disclosure and accountability
- "Use of AI must be appropriately cited within academic work. Please see CETLI's Guidance on Citing AI Contributions for additional guidance."
- "Unauthorized use of AI within coursework, including failure to disclose use, may be considered a violation of academic integrity and may result in disciplinary action consistent with PSOM policies."
- "Users of AI must accept responsibility for generated content, including potential inaccuracies, misinformation, biases, or violations of privacy, professionalism, intellectual property, or codes of conduct, among other potential faults."

### Faculty responsibilities (notably including anti-detection stance and equity)
- "Faculty are responsible for ensuring appropriate supervision and oversight of AI use in educational, research, and clinical contexts."
- "Faculty must clearly communicate expectations for AI use in their courses and clinical rotations (e.g., in syllabi, course websites, or verbal guidance). Course syllabi should include a statement of acceptable use of AI in the course, including a hyperlink to this policy."
- "When AI use is permitted for coursework, faculty must clearly define acceptable and prohibited uses, and provide guidance on appropriate documentation, attribution, and validation of AI-assisted work."
- "Faculty should disclose to students when AI was used in the creation of course materials and assignments... Faculty retain responsibility for accuracy and alignment of AI generated content with course objectives."
- Equity clause: "PSOM acknowledges that access to AI tools may vary. Faculty should design assignments that do not disadvantage learners without access to premium tools."
- AI-detector skepticism: "Use of AI detection software is generally discouraged as its accuracy is imperfect and typically insufficient as evidence that a student has used AI. Uploading student work to an AI detector may also violate FERPA."

### Violations / reporting machinery
- "Students, faculty, and staff should report suspected misuse, data security and privacy concerns in the use of AI tools. Reports made in good faith are protected from retaliation."
- "Allegations of misconduct related to AI use may be reported through the following mechanisms. Consequences of misconduct will be presented to the Professionalism Sub-Committee of the Student Standards Committee (SSC), and outcomes will be determined on a case-by-case basis."
- Named channels: Professionalism at PSOM Portal (PennKey), Academic Programs Office administrators, Associate/Assistant Deans, Vice Dean for UME; course-director escalation to Associate Dean for Student Affairs and Wellness; evaluator professionalism flags via Registrar.

### Ongoing review
- "This policy will be reviewed at least annually, with interim updates as needed based on emerging technologies, regulatory changes, or institutional priorities and policies."
- Feedback channel: Medical Education Technology Committee (METC).

## Sophistication Markers
- **Tech understanding**: public-vs-approved tool taxonomy tied to a maintained PMACS tool matrix with per-tool data-type guidance; names Open Evidence as clinical decision support; EHR-integration requirement for documentation AI.
- **Evidence citations**: AAMC AI Policy Development Checklist referenced; alignment with university Acceptable Use Policy, CETLI citing guidance.
- **Assessment redesign**: limited — no classification taxonomy; prohibition-default with faculty opt-in. But has the **equity** clause (assignment design not disadvantaging students without premium tools) — a genuinely sophisticated pedagogical insight rare in this batch.
- **AI-detector skepticism**: explicitly discourages AI detection software on accuracy and FERPA grounds — evidence-informed, matches published detector-unreliability literature.
- **Tool investment**: PMACS/UPHS approved-tools ecosystem; policy itself drafted with ChatGPT Edu/CoPilot and says so.
- **Governance**: named contributors, multi-body oversight, annual review, case-by-case adjudication, anti-retaliation reporting.

## Distinctives
- The most administratively complete policy in the batch: numbered, scoped, authored, referenced, reviewed, with reporting channels and equity provisions.
- Radical transparency: discloses its own AI-assisted drafting with dates and tools.
- AI-detector discouragement + FERPA concern is a distinctive evidence-based stance.
- Stiffest documentation-AI penalty framing: unauthorized clinical documentation AI = grounds for failing assignment/course/clerkship + professionalism review.

## Provisional Classification: **progressive-structured** (upper end, near pioneer)
Justification: exceptionally structured governance, evidence-informed stances (detector skepticism, equity-aware assignment design), strong tool matrix — but its *student-use default* is prohibition ("not permitted... unless explicitly authorized"), it lacks a permissive learning-use statement or assessment taxonomy, and it invests no new secure deployment of its own. Pioneer-grade scaffolding around a cautious core.
