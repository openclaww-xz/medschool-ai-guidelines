# Performance of 5 AI Models on USMLE Step 1 Questions: Comparative Study (JMIR AI, 2026)

## Research question
How do 5 publicly accessible AI models (Grok 2, ChatGPT-4, Copilot, Gemini 2.0 Flash, DeepSeek v3) compare on the 2024 NBME Free 120 (USMLE Step 1 proxy) across accuracy, consistency, question format (text/image; case/information-based), and 14 subject areas?

## Methods
Cross-sectional observational comparison, Feb 10–Mar 5 2025; free tiers only (realistic student conditions); 119 questions (1 audio item excluded); standardized prompt cycle (intro phrase → question → "Are you sure?" ×2), new chat per question; manual scoring vs official key; χ²/Fisher with Bonferroni-adjusted post hoc.

## Sample
119 MCQs (96 text-only, 23 visual; 78 case-based, 41 information-based) across 14 subjects; 5 models × 3 responses each.

## Key findings
- Overall first-attempt accuracy: Grok 91.6% (109/119) > Copilot 84.9% > Gemini 84.0% > ChatGPT-4 79.8% > DeepSeek 72.3% (P=.002; only Grok>DeepSeek significant pairwise).
- DeepSeek's low rank driven by 0/23 on image items (no multimodal in v3); text-only it tied Copilot at 89.6% (all models 81–92%, P=.13, NS).
- Image-based: Grok 91.3%, ChatGPT 73.9%, Copilot 65.2%, Gemini 52.2% (P<.001).
- Consistency: Grok 100% (never changed), ChatGPT 99.2%, Gemini 98.3%, DeepSeek 96.6%, Copilot 94.1%; Copilot self-corrected 84.9%→89.9% (6 incorrect→correct, 0 reversions).
- Case-based: Grok 89.7% vs DeepSeek 67.9% (P=.01); information-based NS (P=.23).
- Subjects (exploratory, small n): best biostatistics/epidemiology 96.7%, ethics 88.9%; worst musculoskeletal 62.9%, cardiovascular 75.5%, pharmacology 76.6%.
- ChatGPT-4 ranking 4th of 5 reverses prior literature where ChatGPT led — model landscape shifts fast; benchmarking must be continuous.

## What it says about who leads policy
Indirect only: calls for supervised use, explicit training on AI limitations, faculty oversight, and continuous evaluation — institutional responsibility; no governance analysis.

## Limitations (authors')
Free-tier models only; single 119-item form; no expert validation of reasoning; possible data contamination (public questions in training data); static prompt; single reviewer scoring; fast-moving model versions.

## Relation to corpus findings
Capability evidence: multiple free models now at or above passing level on Step 1 content, including images — the technical substrate for student use documented in the survey studies in this corpus. Relevant to corpus assessment-integrity policies: any school relying on take-home MCQ assessments faces near-pass-level public tools, strengthening the assessment-redesign rationale found in policy-gap literature.
