# Enhancing Physician Resilience to Generative AI: Multilevel Framework (JMIR, 2026)

## Research question
Normative: what cognitive, professional, and organizational governance conditions preserve physician judgment ("resilience") in genAI-supported care?

## Methods
Viewpoint; interdisciplinary literature-informed synthesis across 5 research areas (resilience engineering, clinical reasoning/metacognition, CDS stewardship, AI governance/regulation, automation-bias/deskilling evidence); no empirical collection or consensus procedure.

## Sample
N/A (framework); ~44 references.

## Key findings (with counts)
- Three-pillar framework: (1) cognitive workload shaping (verification-trigger matrix on 4 dimensions: harm severity, irreversibility/time-criticality, uncertainty/conflict, equity sensitivity; tiered attestation), (2) clinical authority governance (decisional responsibility allocation physician/institution/developer; 4-tier Automation Scope of Practice rubric — Tier 0 nondelegable dx/dosing through Tier 3 conditionally delegated scheduling), (3) organizational safety governance (interprofessional safety review, protected safety investment, nonpunitive error taxonomy, skill-retention training).
- Patient-disclosure standard: 4 core points for consequential AI-supported recommendations; brief disclosure sufficient for documentation support.
- Explicit extension beyond rule-based CDS governance: genAI's fluent-but-false outputs require bounded verification, calibrated trust, bounded delegation.

## What it says about WHO leads policy
Institutions lead (governance committees, ASoP tables, monitoring, incentive alignment); physicians apply; developers manage known limitations; accountability distributed by operational control. Multidisciplinary AI governance committee specified (clinical leadership, informatics, quality/safety, legal, specialties).

## Limitations
Normative/untested; workflow-friction risk; institutional capacity varies; no outcome evidence.

## Corroboration/contradiction with our corpus
The institutional machinery it proposes (governance committee, tiered use approval, attestation) closely matches what our run-30 health-system corpus documents as already operating (Duke ABCDs, UCSF AI governance, Mount Sinai) — again literature converging on practice leaders. For medical schools, the ASoP tiering concept parallels the traffic-light permitted-use tiers in our school corpus but applied to clinical delegation; no school policy in our corpus yet adopts an explicit ASoP-style delegation rubric — a gap our corpus can document.
