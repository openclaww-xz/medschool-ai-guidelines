# Prevalence and Content of GenAI Use Policies in Iranian Medical Science Journals (Med J Islam Repub Iran, 2026)

## Research question
(1) What is the prevalence of generative-AI use policies among Iranian medical science journals, and how does adoption vary by language, indexing, COPE membership? (2) What is the content of those policies?

## Methods
Cross-sectional descriptive website audit of all Iranian Research Information System (IRIS) medical journals (429 identified → 411 after exclusions), Aug–Oct 2025; structured 25-variable checklist (built from prior policy studies + publisher guidance, expert-reviewed, piloted on 10 journals); 2 trained reviewers; χ²/Fisher tests in SPSS; no inter-rater reliability stats (single assessment per journal with random cross-checks).

## Sample
411 Iranian medical science journals; 117 with AI policies.

## Key findings
- 28.5% (117/411) had a public AI policy. Adoption higher among English-language journals (35.3% vs Farsi 13.5%, P<.001), internationally indexed (36.1% vs 22.5%, P=.002), and COPE-affiliated (34.4% vs 1.4%, P<.001).
- Content (of 117): mandatory disclosure 99.1%; permission type specified 98.3%; writing/text use addressed 91.5%; author responsibility 88.0%; disclosure location 81.2%; image/table generation 78.6% (mostly prohibited); AI authorship ban 70.1%; sample disclosure wording 60.7%; international guideline references 53.0%; coding/data analysis 51.3%; peer-review/editorial AI use 38.5% (nearly all prohibiting); consequences of violation only 11.1%; translation guidance 11.1%; 23.9% flagged policy as provisional/under revision.
- Comparative benchmarks cited: PM&R journals ~50%; STM publishers ~1/3; bioethics/health humanities 16%; top journals 87%.

## What it says about who leads policy
Journal publishers + international editorial bodies (COPE, ICMJE, EASE, WAME) are the policy anchors; COPE membership is the strongest adoption correlate — external standards bodies effectively lead, with international visibility pressure driving local adoption. National coordination absent.

## Limitations (authors')
Public websites only (internal guidance missed); existence ≠ enforcement; Iran-specific context; rapid policy change post-collection; no inter-rater reliability.

## Relation to corpus findings
Methodologically identical to our corpus approach (census website audit + structured checklist + content analysis) but for journals, not institutions. Corroborates the Clin Teach pattern in a different sector: under one-third adoption, broad principles over operational detail, enforcement rarely specified. Supports our central claim: census-style document audits are the right tool and have NOT been done for US medical schools at corpus scale.
