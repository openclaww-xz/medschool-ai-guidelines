# Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada — Task Force Report on Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Digital Technologies (February 2020)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: Royal College of Physicians and Surgeons of Canada — "sets the standards for the training, evaluation and certification of specialist physicians, and the accreditation of specialty postgraduate medical education programs" across 17 Canadian medical schools; 67 recognized disciplines; CPD via Maintenance of Certification (MOC).
- Task force chaired by **Dr. Richard Reznick** (Dean, Faculty of Health Sciences, Queen's University); prepared with Ken Harris (Deputy CEO), Tanya Horsley, Mohsen Sheikh Hassani. Membership of 13+chair mixing clinicians and AI scientists ("the latter predominating" per Terms of Reference): Colleen Flood (health law), Jaron Chong (radiology), Anna Goldenberg (computer science/SickKids), Khaled El Emam (privacy analytics), Daniel Hashimoto (MGH surgical AI), Brian Hodges (UHN), Frank Rudzicz (Vector/Winterlight), Joon Lee (Calgary), Osmar Zaïane (Amii), Alison Paprica (Vector). Term: 18 months (Oct 2018–Mar 2020).
- Methods fully disclosed: literature review (20+ reports, ongoing monitoring); 5 in-person + 7 teleconference meetings; **Delphi consensus** on recommendations; **22 key informant interviews** (Topol, Bengio, Meyerson (IBM), Harvey, Tarassenko (Oxford), Meskó, Bourassa (CIHR Indigenous Peoples' Health), Pineau (McGill/Facebook AI), etc.) — transcribed, double-coded in NVivo; **surveys of Fellows (3,754 completes of 50,835; 4,297 opened) and Resident Affiliates (222 completes of 2,011)**, English/French, thrice-piloted; an AI Roundtable (22 early adopters, Dec 18 2019); and a planned **scoping review** (Arksey & O'Malley/Levac methodology, InsightScope crowdsourced screening).

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **anticipatory competency redesign by the specialty accreditor** — AI as profound but manageable change to training and practice, not a crisis. Signature findings verbatim: "AI and emerging digital technologies **will not replace a physician's presence, intuition, creativity, judgment and compassion**"; AI "**could liberate physicians from repetitive tasks, allowing time for more patient care, including compassionate care**."
- Capability framing: "AI and digital technologies will become **fundamental to the practice of medicine**. To continue to meet patient needs, physicians will need a basic understanding of the available technologies, a stronger background in **mathematics and statistics**, and the ability to find and understand health information from electronic sources."
- Technological realism: the "It's just math" chapter — "Artificial intelligence is only artificial intelligence until some critical mass understands how it works. Then it's just a computer program" (Daniel Silver); AI systems "cannot create knowledge; they can only implement the rules they have been programmed to execute"; current AI is **narrow intelligence** (sepsis detector cannot detect arrhythmia).

## (c) Complete clause inventory
**The 12 recommendations (complete; grouped by the report's own headings):**
1. **Anticipated impacts**: "That the Committee on Specialties... continue to regularly monitor the health of individual disciplines, assessing the impact of AI and emerging digital technologies. **At this time there are no recommendations to alter the number and complement of specialist physicians being trained in Canada.**"
2. **A new fundamental competency**: "**Digital health literacy competencies should be integrated into the CanMEDS Framework**" — task force suggests "possibly as the eighth component of CanMEDS"; content minimums: "a common understanding of how the technologies and AI systems work, including knowledge on the input to algorithms, how an AI-based decision is derived and the ability to meaningfully interpret outputs; common taxonomy and language; and fundamental statistical knowledge such as linear regression."
3. **Changes to physician practice**: "**As a matter of urgency**, develop processes to address the current challenges faced by all specialties in developing new competencies or making career changes that may be needed as a result of the impact of AI" — career-transition thesis: "unlikely that physicians will remain in the same area of practice for their whole careers."
4. **Emerging careers** (two): "Consider introducing a new discipline in the area of **Clinical Informatics**"; "Partner with Canadian medical schools to promote AI and MD/PhD programs and clinician investigator programs focused on digital technology... encourage a cadre of **'clinical innovators'**."
5. **Team science**: "Play an active role in supporting Fellows and Resident Affiliates to **co-develop, refine, validate and spread AI-enabled technologies**."
6. **New products**: "Working with individual disciplines and the Committee on Specialties, **develop guidelines and principles for integrating the teaching of AI and emerging digital technologies across all residency training programs**, and incorporate these teachings as a component of **CPD for current Fellows**."
7. **Complementary addition**: "Promote... opportunities that support Fellows and Resident Affiliates in working with innovators in the public, not-for-profit and private sectors to co-develop... **taking into account considerations for conflict of interest**."
8. **Democratizer**: "Implement mechanisms to **include patients' perspectives in all facets of AI-related decision-making**."
9. **Ethical/legal**: "Collaborate with partner organizations to develop, tailor, curate and distribute educational offerings related to **privacy, discrimination, safety and other ethical and legal concerns** arising from adopting AI."
10. **Social accountability**: "Promote, enable and extend scholarship, education and other forms of support to physicians that increase their knowledge of the **social justice implications** of AI-based technologies."
11. **Institutional**: "Establish a working group to review how best to apply AI to support elements of the Royal College's operations, particularly in relation to its role as an **examining body**... [and] data governance and ownership."
12. **Surveillance**: "Develop an ongoing monitoring and development strategy to address the need for further recommendations."
**Key supporting doctrine:**
- **Physician-roles taxonomy (Table 1, diffusion-of-innovations based — complete)**: Innovators (entrepreneurial opportunity, CS expertise, hand-off pathways); Early adopters (confidence tech is safe, best practices for testing e.g. silent trials, acknowledgement/compensation); Majority adopters (distinguish promising-but-unproven from validated; explain AI use to patients; "**enhanced metacognition skills to integrate the outputs of AI**"; channel priorities to avoid "toy tasks"); Late adopters (referral pathways; "the option of continuing to practise medicine in traditional ways").
- Human-in-charge doctrine: "the human health care professional **must always remain in charge**. Machines will serve as assistants... The task force **does not imagine a machine ever making a final decision about the care a patient receives without human supervision**"; "Physicians' core skills and competencies must remain robust so that they... can intervene as needed... **Physicians need to be prepared for situations in which they will have to take full control of an autonomous task**" (GPS analogy); deskilling warning quoted from IBM's Meyerson ("if you become too reliant on technology, you lose the basic common sense that comes with the skill you were taught").
- AI capability curriculum: supervised/unsupervised learning, train/validation/test splits, neural network structure (weights, hidden layers), "AI systems can: process datasets larger than the human brain can handle; find complex relationships; predict cause and event... cannot yet create intelligence, learn on their own without rules, or predict patterns without sufficient and valid data."
- Data-deficiency taxonomy: missing data; problematic de-identification (Washington State re-identification case); poor data quality; bias (socio-economic/race/gender); Indigenous under-representation + cultural-values caveat + infrastructure gap (Bourassa quote); ~20% of Canadians in remote communities with less data to offer.
- Ethics chapters: timing ("ethics must be considered in the design phase... many ethical decisions must be made early... such as in data selection and collection"); **explainability/black-box** (unexplainable decisions → consent and accountability "questionable"; queue-priority example; support research into transparent/explainable AI, standard evaluation and auditing, public evaluations); **bias** (COMPAS and Google-ad cases; continuous-learning bias refresh; opaque origins of bias); **data considerations** (consent scope vs reuse; anonymization standards; **no data ownership in health care in Canada** (cigionline); privacy-by-stage — collection consent clarity, minimization, secured storage, right to withdraw/mask, disposal, modelling without leaks, deployment monitoring + model recall strategy); **liability** (laws "do not clearly identify who will be held accountable"; liability should span "manufacturers, programmers and developers" as well as physicians; refusal-to-use liability question raised; sole physician liability → refusal to adopt).
- Evidence base woven through: FDA approvals (1 in 2014 → 23 in 2018 → 35 to date; FDA's proposed AI/ML-SaMD modification framework), CE marks incl. Apple Watch AFib; Gulshan diabetic retinopathy (96%+, India deployment); Esteva dermatology; Rajpurkar arrhythmia (64,121 ECGs, beats 6 cardiologists); IEEE database (AI matching/outperforming physicians in 13 of 17 clinical decision fields); CloudMedx mock exam (physicians 75%, AI 85%, combined 91%); Liang pediatrics (1.36M visits; AI beats junior, loses to senior pediatricians); Johns Hopkins sepsis predictor (44,000 patient-days, 7,852 patients); Princess Margaret radiation planning (hours → minutes); JAMA 2019 VR neurosurgery assessment.
- Workforce economics: Senate 2017 (42% of Canadian jobs at risk); ~17% of health jobs automatable by 2030 "does not necessarily mean a loss of jobs, but likely a change in workflow"; Hinton-vs-Harvey radiology debate recounted with the task force's verdict — "the task force **does not foresee fewer jobs in any given specialty in the foreseeable future**"; WHO 14.5M workforce shortfall by 2030; seniors 23-25% of population by 2036; health spending $264B/11.6% GDP.
- Survey findings (complete appendix data): Fellows — familiarity: 18.6% level-1, 22.3% level-2, 35.1% level-3, 20% level-4, 3.9% level-5; 41% no/very little familiarity; current roles: N/A 7.6%, late 18.7%, majority 46.1%, early 27.8%; desired future: late 7%, majority 14.9%, early 37.7%, innovator 36.7%; AI-education interest 48% high (4-5); Royal-College-should-be-involved 60.4% (4-5); MOC integration: 31.4% (4-5) vs 34.9% (1-2); opinion on specialty impact: positive disruption 49.2%, definitely positive 23%, negative disruption 12.6%, no effect 10.6%, worry specialty at risk 4.6%; topic rankings (Fellows): 1 Basics-technical, 2 Ethics/Legal, 3 Human Factors, 4 New Models of Care, 5 Data, 6 Basics-execs, 7 Educational Approaches, 8 Workforce. Residents — 42% no/little familiarity; ~70% no/very limited exposure to AI conversations; **85% believe they have not received adequate teaching**; 56.9% level-1 preparation, 0.9% fully prepared; statistics: 14.7% struggle with base concepts; computer science: 40.9% level-1, 6.5% able to code; roles now: majority 52.6%, late 24.3%; desired: innovator/early/majority 30.9/31.6/31.6%; interest 56.5% high; positive-disruption 48.7%, definitely-positive 28%, at-risk worry only 3.4%; topic rankings: Basics-technical, New Models of Care, Human Factors, Ethics/Legal...
- Roundtable + scoping review (InsightScope crowdsourcing, validated for systematic reviews); interview thematic map (competencies; specialties most affected — radiology/pathology first; policy needs; shift "to role of interpreter, guide, coach").

## (d) Sophistication markers
- **Earliest comprehensive national-specialty-body response in the corpus (Feb 2020, pre-LLM)** and methodologically the heaviest: Delphi + 22 coded interviews + ~4,000 survey responses + roundtable + commissioned scoping review.
- The four-role adopter taxonomy with per-role needs (incl. "metacognition" for majority adopters and "the option of continuing to practise medicine in traditional ways" for late adopters) is a corpus-unique workforce-planning instrument.
- Liability analysis is the deepest available here: multi-party liability model, the refusal-to-use counterfactual, data-ownership vacuum named explicitly.
- Deskilling and human-takeover duties stated in 2020 — anticipating Singapore AIHGle 2.0's contingency duty and MSC's automation-bias competency by years.
- Honest quantitative literacy: every claim tied to a cited study with numbers; survey denominators and non-response disclosed.
- Naive-in-retrospect markers: pre-generative-AI (GenAI absent — no hallucination of LLMs, no assessment-validity threat, no education-use policies); some cited evidence (CloudMedx mock exam, IEEE "13 of 17") is vendor/press-grade; "It's just math" underestimates the LLM turn.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- The archetype of the **Canada task-force model**: convene national expertise → evidence-gather at scale → recommendations into the competency framework (CanMEDS) and CPD apparatus, with monitoring responsibility retained. Compare AFMC (same country, opposite direction: outward lobbying on misinformation) and Singapore (statutory lifecycle rules): the Royal College changes the profession's *competency constitution* rather than products or conduct rules.
- Only document to formally propose a **new discipline (Clinical Informatics)** and to survey the entire national specialist body (n≈3,900+230 with published marginals).
- The "late adopters may continue practising traditionally" clause is the corpus's only explicit conscientious-laggard protection — a strikingly pluralist touch.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** — evidence-saturated, competency-forward, adoption-assuming with human-primacy guarantees; technically pre-generative (2020) but not naive (validation, bias, deskilling, liability all present). Its recommendations structure (CanMEDS integration, Clinical Informatics, CPD) remains the template other national colleges followed.
