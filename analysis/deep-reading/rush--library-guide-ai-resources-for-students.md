# Rush Library — AI Resources for Students (excerpts Rush UAC0039 policy)

## Authority / Authorship
- Rush University Library guide; quotes the official (login-gated) UAC0039 Generative AI Use policy verbatim in part.

## Philosophy (verbatim framing — quoted policy)
- "While this is an understandable concern, the ability to reliably detect AI-generated text is still developing. Currently, AI detection tools are prone to producing false positives, have been shown to be biased against non-native English speakers, and can be confounded by minor textual modifications."

## Clause inventory
Library guidance: 1. "Your work should be your own, always." 2. Check each professor's guidelines. 3. Fact-checking always needed; ChatGPT fabricates references. 4. "Take a screen shot of your query and the answer provided" (evidence-hygiene advice). 5. Good-for/bad-for lists (brainstorming, narrowing topics, translation, code vs. library research, "information that would have dire consequences if it was incorrect"). Credits U. Arizona and U. Adelaide libraries.
Quoted policy UAC0039: 6. Anti-detection rationale (quoted above). 7. "students must be informed of AI course policies and under what circumstances they might responsibly use generative AI." 8. Syllabus-statement compliance enforced via Student Code of Conduct (UAC0030) and Academic Honesty (UAC0031). 9. Student accountability for "limitations, potential inaccuracies, and possible biases"; compliance with laws/ethics/"clinical practice on site." 10. Clinical settings: "Please refer to the location's policy on AI use for clinical work."

## Sophistication markers
- Strongest anti-AI-detection language in the guidance layer, coming from the *policy* itself (false positives, bias against non-native English speakers, defeat by paraphrase).
- Screenshot-your-query advice — practical due-process foresight.

## Classification
- **progressive-structured (library layer quoting a progressive policy)** — the library guide is the delivery vehicle; the underlying UAC0039 stance (disclosure over detection, site-level clinical deference) is evidence-informed.
