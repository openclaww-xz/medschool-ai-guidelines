# Scoping Review of GenAI Tools in Health Profession Education (BMC Med Educ, 2026)

## Research question
How is GenAI used across health profession education programs globally, and what opportunities, challenges, facilitators, and barriers are associated with integration?

## Methods
JBI scoping-review methodology (OSF-registered 10.17605/OSF.IO/RHDJ5), PRISMA-ScR; 7 databases (PubMed, ERIC, CINAHL, Embase, Scopus, Cochrane, ProQuest) + reference screening, through Jan 31 2025; any language; dual independent screening (κ=0.78) and extraction in Covidence; interventional vs perception classification; no critical appraisal (scoping design).

## Sample
14,208 records → 8,606 screened → 1,224 full-text → 241 included studies. Asia 43.1%, North America 29.0%, Europe 14.1%; 72.1% published 2024; medicine 43%, nursing 36%; undergraduate 79%; designs: cross-sectional 22%, mixed 12%, qualitative 11%, RCT 6%, 39% unreported.

## Key findings
- Applications: content creation 26.2%, assessment 18.0%, curriculum design only 2.7%, GenAI-literacy education 0.6%. ChatGPT dominant tool.
- Interventional findings: GenAI-inclusive blended learning improved satisfaction/skills/exam performance vs traditional; textbook group OUTPERFORMED ChatGPT group on complex ethical cases (critical thinking, EBP application); GPT-4 case generation 58% medically accurate (Japanese); DALL-E congenital-heart-disease images only 0.4% usable; MCQ generation 5–15 vs 30–60 min (all needing edits); scenario drafting cost £652 vs £1,530; ChatGPT-3.5 note-grading error 1.0% vs standardized patients 7.2%, but another study found only 26–83% agreement; GPT-4 grading stricter than humans (−3.51 avg).
- Perception findings: student use 20%–40%+ daily-to-weekly; medicine/pharmacy students avoid clinical-note/information-source use in clinical settings (contrast PharmD/midwifery heavy drug-info use); only 18% never verify outputs; only 5% educated about GenAI, 4.3% received ethics education; faculty ambiguity about GenAI commonly reported; PT program directors: 54% nervous, 42% optimistic, 75% believe it reshapes higher ed in 3–5 yrs, only 1/3 think benefits outweigh risks.
- Barriers: infrastructure/access (VPN restrictions, South African load-shedding, data costs), subscription cost for LMICs, language/cultural limits, output inaccuracy, image/math gaps; facilitators rare (institution-paid subscriptions, custom GPTs).
- Policy implication (authors'): institutions must "proactively develop policies" and GenAI literacy; comprehensive integration framework needed.

## What it says about who leads policy
Implicit: institutions are the responsible unit (policy + literacy + training); recommends institutional policies safeguarding integrity while evaluating detection tools — no national-level analysis.

## Limitations (authors')
Search cutoff Jan 2025; terminology inconsistency (GenAI/AI/chatbot conflated); no quality appraisal; heterogeneous self-reported outcomes; single-institution, single-discipline predominance; few theory-grounded studies.

## Relation to corpus findings
The broadest evidence map in our peer-reviewed set — confirms near-zero curricular policy/literacy content (0.6% of studies) against heavy use, and students "engaging without structured institutional guidance," echoing the Clin Teach gap from the demand side. Our corpus answers the supply side: which institutions issued the guidance this review says is missing.
