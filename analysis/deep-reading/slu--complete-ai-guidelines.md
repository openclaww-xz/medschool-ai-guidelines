# Deep Read — SLU Complete AI Guidelines

## Authority / Authorship
- **Office of the Provost**, university-wide guidelines page (not med-school-specific; SLU School of Medicine is encompassed but not named). Undated page.
- Actors named: Information Technology Services (ITS) approvals for contract language/security controls; **SLU Madrid Data Protection Officer** (GDPR jurisdiction — unique: a US guideline with an EU campus branch); Reinert Center for Teaching and Learning; University AI Committee; Office of General Counsel; Compliance; IRB/PolicyStat; SLU Libraries (licensed-content AI use).

## Philosophy (verbatim framing quotes)
- "AI must be used in ways that uphold academic integrity, fairness and human dignity."
- Six pillars: Ethical use; Transparency and disclosure ("transparent, prominent, and sufficient to allow others to understand the role AI played in the work product"); Privacy and data security (FERPA, HIPAA, **GDPR**); Human oversight ("AI should support, not replace, human decision-making in critical academic and administrative functions"); Non-discrimination and bias mitigation; plus data-input prohibitions.

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory

### Universal rules
1. **Restricted use data**: "must not input any restricted use data into generative AI tools."
2. **Confidential data**: prohibited "except when permitted by validated contract language and security controls (approved by Information Technology Services (ITS))"; Madrid campus also needs DPO approval.
3. **Personal information**: same prohibition/ITS exception/DPO rider for "personal information about SLU employees, students, faculty, or other stakeholders."
4. **IP/contract terms**: users must respect IP; "It is incumbent on individual users to ensure that the inputs and the outputs of their AI tools are properly protected for reasons such as copyright and patent laws, data protection regulations, and identity theft crimes"; **library-licensing clause**: "some publishers are asserting that using their content with AI tools is not allowed. Please contact SLU Libraries for assistance in defining acceptable uses for licensed content."
5. **Accuracy**: verify before relying; "It is possible for AI-generated content to be inaccurate, biased or entirely fabricated (sometimes called 'hallucinations')"; "You are responsible for any content that you publish that includes AI-generated material."
6. **Bias check**: consider "disparate impact... based on... protected classifications... race, ethnicity, gender, national origin, age, sexual orientation, or disability status. Do not rely on any output that is indicative of a potential bias."
7. **Disclosure**: must disclose materials "based on or derives from the use of generative AI."
8. **Third-party IP**: "must not hold out any output generated by generative AI tools as their own"; plagiarism risk in paraphrasing AI output.
9. **Malicious content**: prohibited — "malware, viruses, worms and trojan horses... to circumvent access control measures"; "prohibited from using AI tools with the intent to harm another person."
10. **Training**: expected before using AI "that could implicate ethical, legal or policy considerations."
11. **Opt-out of training**: "Where that option is available, SLU community members are expected to exercise it and opt out of training." (user-level opt-out duty — unique)

### Students
- Integrity: follow policy/syllabi/handbook "inclusive of all subtypes of AI such as generative AI, agentic AI, or other intelligent systems" (defines agentic AI).
- **Syllabus-permission default**: "Unless an instructor includes a clear statement in their syllabus granting permission, the use of generative AI tools to complete an assignment or exam is prohibited. Unauthorized use of AI will be treated similarly to unauthorized assistance and/or plagiarism."
- Disclosure/citation "encouraged" (not required) when permitted.
- Communicate with instructors about expectations.

### Instructors
- Clear expectations required via course syllabus policy; "Enhance Pedagogy: AI tools should be used to enhance pedagogy, not to replace instruction and engagement"; develop course AI statements; facilitate classroom AI discussion; use Reinert Center support.
- **AI Detection Tools**: "Be aware that AI detection tools carry risks of misidentification and have not been widely proven to detect AI use. Careful consideration should be given when deciding if the use of these tools is appropriate for assessing student work." (measured anti-detection caution)
- Responsible Employment: instructors should not "inappropriately employ GAI" in course design or "formative and summative assessment of student learning."

### Researchers
- Compliance with federal regs, agency guidance, sponsors, contracts, research misconduct policy, export controls, human subjects.
- Transparency/authorship: "AI-generated research output must be clearly identified, and authorship must reflect substantive human contributions."
- Accuracy: "AI has been known to generate non-existent citations or images for experiments that were never conducted."
- **Unpublished research data**: avoid uploading — "may lead to the disclosure of unpublished work, impede future intellectual property protection, or create privacy violations. This includes unpublished manuscripts or funding proposals that researchers may be asked to peer review, **as some funding agencies (e.g., NIH, NSF) prohibit using generative AI for peer review**." (peer-review prohibition awareness — unique)
- Confidential data; external journal/society policies; IRB approval for sensitive research areas.

### Administrative
- **Auditing for Fairness**: AI in "admissions, hiring, student services, and decision-making must be regularly audited for fairness and accuracy"; ITS contract review.
- **Human Review and Appeal**: "Automated decision-making processes must include a mechanism for human review and appeal."
- Responsible staff use; implementation safeguards per University AI Committee + ITS/OGC/Compliance.

## Sophistication Markers
- GDPR/DPO (Madrid campus) dual-jurisdiction data governance — unique in corpus.
- User duty to opt out of model training where available.
- Library licensing/publisher-AI-restriction clause; NIH/NSF peer-review AI prohibition; export controls.
- Mandatory fairness audits + human review/appeal for administrative automated decisions (admissions/hiring) — strongest admin-decision rights language in cohort C.
- Defines agentic AI in the student clause; measured detection-tool skepticism; IP/identity-theft framing.

## Distinctives
- Multi-domain comprehensiveness (universal + student + instructor + research + administrative) without being a medical school document — the med-school-relevant content is only the generic academic layer.
- Catholic-university human-dignity framing in the ethics principle.
- No clinical/PHI-specific operational detail beyond the general data clauses; no SOM-specific section.

## Provisional Classification: **Progressive-structured**
Justification: broad, current, and jurisdiction-aware (GDPR, funding-agency rules, fairness audits, appeal rights), with a coherent permission-default for coursework; but generic to the whole university — for medical-education purposes it lacks clinical, documentation, and trainee-specific depth, so it functions as solid upper-tier scaffolding rather than pioneer-level domain policy.
