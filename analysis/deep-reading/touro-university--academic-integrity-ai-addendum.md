# Touro University (System) — Policy for Appropriate Use of Artificial Intelligence in Academic Work (AI Addendum)

## Authority / Authorship
- Publisher: Touro University (System-wide, TUS); an addendum to the TUS Academic Integrity Policy, hosted on touro.edu/students/policies. University-wide across "all departments and campuses in the TUS" — covers Touro's medical/health-sciences programs but is not med-school-specific.
- Dated mechanisms: "Effective January 1, 2026, every course syllabus shall contain a clear and unambiguous statement of the course's policy on AI use." Updates: "the university will periodically review and update this addendum"; "Periodic updates on AI detection tools… will be posted on the TouroOne Portal by the Office of the Provost for Artificial Intelligence."
- Closing acknowledgement: "Portions of this document are based on AI summaries of best practices from leading universities and academic societies." (an AI-assisted policy, disclosed)

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- Encourage-with-limits: "Touro University encourages the responsible use of AI tools to enhance learning and research. We encourage all members of our university community to experiment creatively with AI, provided such activities are conducted ethically and responsibly. A student's academic work must reflect their own original ideas, organization, and expression. The use of AI tools should be limited to supporting—not replacing—the student's thinking."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory
Academic Integrity Policy definitions (as amended for AI)
1. Plagiarism: "the unauthorized use of the writings, ideas and/or computer-generated material of others without appropriate acknowledgement, and the representation of them as one's own original work. It also includes the unauthorized use of computer-generated material via AI and other emerging technologies without appropriate acknowledgement… Intentional plagiarism includes using computer-generated material via AI and other emerging technologies without attribution."
2. Cheating: "exceeding the restrictions put in place for 'take home' examinations… unauthorized use of computer-generated material via AI and other emerging technologies, or unauthorized collaboration on answers. Examples of unauthorized assistance include receiving assistance via AI and other emerging technologies without the authorization of the instructor."
3. "Misleading or fraudulent behavior includes taking credit for accomplishments achieved by another person or computer-generated material via AI and other emerging technologies."
4. "Students who are found to have violated the parameters of academic integrity will be subject to sanctions, following the procedures described in the Policy."

Default Policy (absent explicit instructor statement)
5. "Use of AI-generated text or output to write, complete, or substantially develop any portion of a graded assessment is not permitted."
6. "'Substantially' includes but is not limited to (a) generating full paragraphs, sections, or answers; (b) paraphrasing or rewriting significant portions of text; and (c) using AI to construct arguments, explanations, or problem solutions."
7. "Use of generative AI tools is permitted for pre-task support activities. These include such activities as: (a) brainstorming topics or ideas; (b) generating outlines; (c) identifying themes or directions for initial research; (d) finding keywords or search terms to guide exploration."
8. "Such use should be limited to supporting, not replacing, the student's own thinking and creative efforts."
9. "Any use of AI for these purposes must be acknowledged in accordance with the Acknowledgement of Use requirements outlined in Section 3 of this policy."
10. "Use of AI tools is permitted for grammar correction and language editing. This includes spelling, punctuation, word choice, and sentence structure. This does not include the use of AI tools to rewrite or enhance the substance of the student's writing."
11. Agentic-AI ban: "Use of agentic AI tools, which are AI systems that generate text or outcomes with minimal human direction or oversight, is prohibited for all graded assessments. This includes any AI agents that act autonomously to produce content, solve problems, or make decisions on behalf of the student." (the only explicit agentic-AI clause in cohort B)

Instructor Autonomy
12. "At their discretion, instructors may permit unrestricted AI usage, limited AI usage, or ban AI use for specified assignments and graded assessments. Instructors are responsible for making clear to students, in writing, how AI may and may not be used in a course, and students are responsible for ascertaining, understanding, and following each instructor's policy."
13. Mandatory Data Privacy Reminder in every instructor policy (verbatim): "Only AI tools approved by the university (which are available through the TouroOne Portal) are to be used when working with confidential or personally identifiable information (PII). PII includes, but is not limited to, full names, ID numbers, grades, health data, and any information that could be used to identify someone. Be advised that non-approved software can collect any information from your device without your knowledge or consent, which could result in unintended consequences. Use of non-approved AI platforms is prohibited on TUS machines, and may result in academic, legal, or disciplinary consequences."
14. Policy Transparency: "Effective January 1, 2026, every course syllabus shall contain a clear and unambiguous statement of the course's policy on AI use… The statement may refer to an easily available departmental or school policy or may be self-contained but must not permit actions otherwise prohibited by the TUS Academic Integrity Policy…"
15. Ethical Use: "Students are to use AI tools ethically, ensuring that they respect the privacy, autonomy, and safety of others. AI tools may not be used to deceive, manipulate, or harm others. Submitting AI-generated text, media, or code as one's own without acknowledgement is a form of plagiarism and subject to disciplinary proceedings and possible sanctions."

Acknowledgement of Use
16. "Any use of AI tools in student work must be acknowledged."
17. Unacknowledged use: "To avoid all doubt, if a student uses AI and does not acknowledge said use, regardless of any statements made or not made in the syllabus, it is considered plagiarism and subject to disciplinary proceedings and sanctions."
18. Citation form: "For written assignments, acknowledgement will generally be in the form of a footnote or citation (indicating what section(s) of the text were produced by/with the AI tool) with associated bibliographic reference; in other media, this may be a caption or other text included in the submission; and for code this will typically be a comment indicating which function or snippet was produced by an AI tool and in what manner. Citation style may vary by discipline… The exact prompt given to the AI tool should be specified."
19. Worked examples of acknowledgement: "This assignment was generated with support from Copilot (Microsoft, 2025) to assist with initial brainstorming and proofreading." / "This paragraph was drafted with assistance from Copilot (Microsoft, March 2025), prompt: 'Summarize the main arguments of X.'"
20. Reference-format examples in MLA, Chicago, and APA for AI outputs (Copilot).

Data Privacy and Security
21. "Per the Information Security Policy on Generative AI TUS faculty, staff, and students may only use AI tools that have been vetted and approved by the TUS when handling confidential or personally identifiable information (PII)… Confidential information includes any Touro-affiliated contact information… PII includes names, ID numbers, email addresses, grades, financial account numbers, employment history, school or department affiliation, device identifiers… health information, or patterns of information that could lead to identification, including but not limited to: zip code, age, job title, disability status, political affiliation, race, ethnicity, or religion."
22. "The university maintains vetted AI platforms that comply with institutional policies and relevant legal frameworks, including FERPA, COPPA, and GDPR."
23. "Open-source, public, and/or downloadable AI tools may collect such information from your device, biometric data, or other sensitive information without one's knowledge or consent…"
24. "Students, faculty, or staff who use AI platforms that are not approved by TUS assume full responsibility for any academic, legal, or ethical consequences resulting from such use. It is prohibited to use AI tools that are not approved by TUS on any TUS machine. Failure to comply… may result in disciplinary action, up to and including expulsion… It may also constitute a violation of federal law."
25. Intellectual Property: "Be mindful that some AI tools store user queries and outputs, which can affect future publication or patenting. In addition, the use of AI tools in the creation of text, images or code may impact the ownership of such intellectual property, as well as the creator's ability to register their rights…"

Suspected Violations / Enforcement
26. "Allegations of impermissible AI use are to be addressed with the same procedures and standards as any other suspected breach of the TUS Academic Integrity Policy."
27. "Periodic updates on AI detection tools, their company policies and disclaimers, and their effective use will be posted on the TouroOne Portal by the Office of the Provost for Artificial Intelligence."
28. "Violations of these AI guidelines fall under the broader TUS University System Academic Integrity Policy and may result in sanctions, up to and including expulsion."
29. "As AI technologies evolve, the university will periodically review and update this addendum."

Acknowledgement: "Portions of this document are based on AI summaries of best practices from leading universities and academic societies." + references to OpenAI ChatGPT and Microsoft Copilot citations.

## Sophistication Markers
- The corpus's only agentic-AI clause (11): autonomous agents banned for graded assessments regardless of instructor permission — anticipates the post-chatbot tool generation.
- Default policy with defined term "substantially" (6): enumerates what substantial development means — precision rare in integrity language.
- Prompt-level citation requirement (18): "The exact prompt given to the AI tool should be specified," with worked examples in three citation styles.
- Unambiguous-default rule (17): unacknowledged use is plagiarism regardless of syllabus silence — closes the loophole most policies leave open.
- Mandatory syllabus-statement requirement with a hard compliance date (Jan 1, 2026) and a required verbatim Data Privacy Reminder embedded in every instructor policy (13).
- Approved-tool regime tied to TouroOne portal; explicit FERPA/COPPA/GDPR compliance claim for vetted platforms; machine-level ban on unapproved AI ("prohibited on any TUS machine") with federal-law warning.
- Unusually broad PII enumeration (zip code, political affiliation, disability status, religion, biometric data).
- Standing infrastructure: Office of the Provost for Artificial Intelligence posting detection-tool updates — named responsible office for AI tooling intel.
- Self-disclosure that the document drew on AI summaries — practicing its own acknowledgement norm.

## Distinctives
- Genre: integrity-policy addendum, not a learning policy — no pedagogy/philosophy of medical education, no clinical-environment or EHR/PHI-in-education provisions (health data appears only inside the general PII list).
- Instructor autonomy is maximal (may permit unrestricted use or ban entirely) atop a restrictive system default — inverse of Buffalo/GW's anti-ban safeguards; here banning is a protected instructor choice.
- Code-specific citation guidance (comment indicating which function/snippet) reflects Touro's broader (non-medical) portfolio.
- Detection-tool posture: monitors and disseminates detector policy/disclaimer info rather than mandating or banning detector use.

## Provisional Classification: progressive-structured
Justification: institutionally engineered rather than aspirational — a defined default rule, an agentic-AI ban, prompt-level disclosure mechanics with examples, mandatory syllabus statements with verbatim privacy reminder, portal-gated approved tools, expulsion-backed enforcement, and a named provost office maintaining the regime. It stops short of pioneer because the framework is integrity-and-security-centric: no educational tiers, no clinical-learning rules, no equity/access dimension, and no faculty pedagogy obligations. Closest cohort-B comparator: GW (disclosure depth) and Howard (data governance), but Touro is the strongest on default-rule precision and enforcement clarity.
