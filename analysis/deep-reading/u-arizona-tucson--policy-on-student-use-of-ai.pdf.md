# University of Arizona College of Medicine – Tucson — Policy on Student Use of AI (Policy #4-108)

## Authority / Authorship
- Publisher: COM–Tucson; formal policy-library document: Policy #4-108, Category "4-Professionalism and Integrity"; Author/Authoring Body: Curricular Affairs; Responsible Unit: Curricular Affairs; Policy Contact: Curricular Affairs Dean Office.
- "Last Revision Date / Effective Date: November 13, 2024"; "Next Review Year: 2026."
- Approval: "2024/11/13: The Tucson Educational Policy Committee voted to approve the policy." Cross-references COM-T Policies 4-101 (Honor Code) and 4-107 (Student Use of University-Sponsored Educational Material) and the UA Code of Academic Integrity.

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "This policy outlines principles and guidelines for the appropriate use of artificial intelligence (AI) tools… It emphasizes ethical considerations, responsible usage, proper citation of AI tools, and HIPAA compliance in clinical documentation."
- Core principle: "AI tools should supplement learning and enhance educational outcomes, supporting—not replacing—the critical thinking and clinical reasoning skills essential to medical practice."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory
Summary
1. "Any use of AI for clinical documentation must adhere to the policies and guidelines of the clinical site and comply with HIPAA regulations to ensure the confidentiality and security of patient information."
2. "AI tools may not be used for writing History and Physicals (H&Ps) in the Doctor & Patient Course."

Applicability & Scope
3. "This policy applies to students in all phases of the medical education program leading to the MD degree."

Definitions
4. "AI, AI Tools – Artificial intelligence technologies, including but not limited to generative AI systems (e.g., large language models), machine learning platforms, and image recognition software."

Policy Statement — Principles
5. Educational Enhancement: "AI tools should supplement learning and enhance educational outcomes, supporting—not replacing—the critical thinking and clinical reasoning skills essential to medical practice."
6. Transparency and Accountability: "Students must be transparent about AI usage and accountable for their work. Any assistance from AI must be acknowledged according to institutional guidelines."
7. Patient Confidentiality and Data Security (a): "The use of AI must comply with HIPAA and other relevant laws regarding patient confidentiality and data security. AI tools used for clinical documentation must be certified as HIPAA-compliant."
8. (b): "Students must ensure that protected health information (PHI) is only used with approved AI systems and shared in a secure manner."
9. Ethical Standards: "Students are expected to adhere to the highest ethical standards in their use of AI. This includes avoiding plagiarism, ensuring the accuracy of information, and maintaining professional integrity."

Guidelines — Permitted Uses
10. Learning and Research: "AI may be used for educational purposes, such as studying, researching medical literature, and understanding complex topics."
11. Administrative Tasks: "AI may assist with non-clinical administrative tasks, such as scheduling and organization."
12. Clinical Documentation: "Any use of AI for clinical documentation must adhere to the policies and guidelines of the clinical site and comply with HIPAA regulations…" a) "Exception for Doctor & Patient Course: AI tools cannot be used for writing History and Physicals (H&Ps) in the Doctor & Patient Course. AI tools may, however, be used for other tasks, such as researching clinical questions."
13. AI Training Opt-Out: "Where available, students must opt out of having the data they submit to generative AI systems be used for the system's own training. This step helps protect data confidentiality and intellectual property." (mandatory opt-out — strongest form of this clause in cohort B)

Guidelines — Prohibited Uses
14. Assessments and Assignments: "All student contributions to any assessment or assignment must represent the student's own, original work. Any use of AI, unless explicitly permitted for a given assessment/assignment, is prohibited."
15. Non-HIPAA-Compliant Tools: "AI tools that do not meet HIPAA standards must not be used for processing or storing any form of PHI."
16. Doctor & Patient Course: "AI tools may not be used for writing History and Physicals (H&Ps) in the Doctor & Patient Course."
17. Unauthorized Patient Interaction: "AI should not be used in direct patient interactions unless authorized by supervising faculty and in compliance with institutional policies."
18. Intellectual Property and Compliance with Terms: "students must ensure that any content they input does not violate intellectual property rights or the terms and conditions governing the AI tool. This includes respecting copyright and any restrictions on licensed content from the institution. Also see the COM-T's Policy on Student Use of University-Sponsored Educational Material."

Citation
19. "Any use of AI tools in assignments, research, clinical documentation, or other academic work must be clearly cited according to institutional guidelines."
20. "Students must describe the nature and extent of AI assistance, ensuring transparency and ethical responsibility."

Compliance and Review
21. "Faculty and administrative staff will monitor compliance with this policy."
22. "Plagiarism detection tools may be used to verify originality in student work."
23. "Violations of the policy will be subject to disciplinary actions per the Honor Code Policy."
24. "This policy will be reviewed annually to reflect advancements in AI technology and educational practices."
25. Procedures: "None." / Resources: UA AI teaching/learning resources; "HIPAA-Compliant AI Tools: Freed… Heidi…" (ambient-scribe products listed as named resources).

## Sophistication Markers
- Mandatory training opt-out (13) — most policies encourage; Tucson requires where available.
- Course-specific carve-out anchored in pedagogy: H&P writing banned in the Doctor & Patient (doctoring) course but AI permitted there for "researching clinical questions" — targets the specific skill being trained.
- Named HIPAA-compliant ambient-scribe tools (Freed, Heidi) in resources — practically helpful and unusual.
- Policy-library maturity: policy number, category, authoring body, contact, EPC approval vote date, annual review, cross-referenced policies.
- Plagiarism-detection use acknowledged (22) — one of few cohort-B policies to state a detection posture at all.

## Distinctives
- Near-verbatim shared template with COM–Phoenix (same principles/guidelines skeleton; Phoenix is a 2026 revision of the 2024 Tucson text — see companion note for divergences).
- Default-deny on all assessments/assignments (14) coexisting with a broad permitted study/research channel (10) and clinical-site deference (1).
- "Procedures: None." — explicit admission that the policy has no operational procedures.
- No faculty obligations, no disclosure format, no approved-institutional-tool list (defers to clinical sites and UA guides).

## Provisional Classification: cautious-informed
Justification: well-formed policy-library document with real governance metadata and two genuinely sharp clauses (mandatory opt-out; doctoring-course H&P carve-out), but default-deny on assignments, detection-tool reliance, no disclosure mechanism, no faculty duties, and no educational structure beyond principles. Sound and informed, not progressive.
