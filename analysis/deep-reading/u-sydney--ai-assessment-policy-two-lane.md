# University of Sydney — AI assessment policy: "two-lane approach" (news, 27 Nov 2024)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: University of Sydney; named spokes-officers: **Professor Joanne Wright, Deputy Vice-Chancellor (Education)** and **Professor Adam Bridgeman, Pro Vice-Chancellor (Educational Innovation)**. Dated 27 November 2024.
- Regulatory interlock: "Two academics from the University of Sydney were recently asked by **TEQSA, the national quality assurance and regulatory agency**, to show the risks and opportunities of AI in assessment" — the policy was co-developed with the national regulator's visibility.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **default-allow, assessment-redesign as the integrity strategy**. Key framing verbatim: "From Semester 1 2025, the University will **change its default to allow the use of AI for assessments**, except for exams and in-semester tests or if teaching staff choose not to."
- Rationale (Bridgeman, verbatim): "It's no secret that **any take-home assessment can be completed to a high level by AI, and it's not practical, enforceable or desirable to implement a blanket ban** on these tools, or even to restrict their use."
- Graduates-and-workforce framing (Wright): "Generative AI has already had a profound impact on workplaces and our graduates are **expected to demonstrate skilled use of the relevant tools in job interviews**... we are preparing students for their careers, without compromising their learning or the integrity of our world-class education."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
1. **Default-allow clause** (S1 2025): AI use permitted for assessments except (a) exams and in-semester tests, (b) where teaching staff opt out.
2. **Two-lane definition** (from Semester 2): a "realistic and forward-looking 'two-lane approach'" comprising:
   - Lane 1: "**secure, in-person assessments** to ensure graduates can demonstrate the knowledge, skills and attributes detailed in the course learning outcomes **without external aids**";
   - Lane 2: "**appropriately designed 'open' assessments** that support the use of all available and relevant tools, to ensure students can learn and prosper in the contemporary world."
3. **Originality clause**: "Students will be required to **apply critical thinking and analysis to produce original work** aided by appropriate learning tools, **including acknowledging how they use AI**."
4. **Staff support clause**: "Training has been developed for staff to support the transition alongside workshops, resources and ongoing communications for students and staff."
5. **Equity/provisioning clause**: "a partnership with Microsoft to make the generative AI tool **Copilot for Web available to students and staff for free**, with regular training sessions" — use AI "equitably, effectively and ethically."
6. **Guidance artifact clause**: AI in Education Working Group (staff and students) "co-designed and launched a publicly available online guide AI in Education showing how generative AI can be used productively and responsibly in assessment and learning."
7. **Cogniti clause**: "an AI assistant for students called **Cogniti**, which allows teachers to create **customised AI agents that can be steered with instructions and subject-specific information**, answer students' questions about content and syllabus, and provide instant personalised feedback, guidance and support, 24 hours a day." Adoption: "**over 1000 Cogniti agents** have been developed... adopted by many other institutions in Australia and beyond." Awards noted.
8. **Recognition clause**: "named AI University of the Year in the 2024 Future Campus Awards."
9. Related content: student-authored guide "How to use AI to learn (without cheating)"; student-voice piece "Students on AI: 'Please do not assume the worst of us'" (Danny Liu, Adam Bridgeman, Cecilia Ka Yuk Chan).

## (d) Sophistication markers
- The two-lane construct is the corpus's most operationally honest response to assessment invalidity: stop pretending take-home work is unaided; secure the must-be-unaided outcomes in person, open everything else. "Not practical, enforceable or desirable" is an enforceability analysis, not sentiment.
- Cogniti = institutional steer-able agents (guardraided tutoring at scale, 1000+ agents) — moving from policy to infrastructure; no other university in the corpus builds and exports its own governed AI tooling.
- Free Copilot provisioning = equity by license; co-designed guidance with students = procedural legitimacy.
- Regulator engagement (TEQSA) while designing — policy made with the accreditor, not despite it.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- The definitive **two-lane assessment** model the synthesis compares against Melbourne (coordinator discretion + universal acknowledgment) and Karolinska (default-permit + template disclosure): Sydney alone restructures the assessment *portfolio*, not just the disclosure rule.
- Only institution in the corpus operating its own AI-agent platform (Cogniti) at 1000+ agent scale with external adoption.
- Australian regulatory pairing: AMC's 2019 ethics exploration (upstream) → TEQSA engagement → Sydney two-lane (downstream operationalization).

## (f) Provisional classification
**pioneer** — the most institutionally committed adoption-plus-redesign model in the international corpus: default-allow, structural two-lane assessment, equity provisioning, and home-built governed tooling. Risk it carries: integrity now depends wholly on the secure lane's quality and open-lane design discipline.
