# University of Sydney — FAQs about the two-lane approach to assessment in the age of AI (Adam Bridgeman & Danny Liu, Educational Innovation; living FAQ + all-staff webinar 27 Nov 2024)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: University of Sydney Educational Innovation (Teaching@Sydney); authors **Adam Bridgeman** (PVC Educational Innovation) and **Danny Liu**. A "living FAQ" compiling staff questions, with asterisked questions from the 27 Nov 2024 all-staff webinar.
- Anchoring: Coursework Policy + Assessment Procedures; **TEQSA** "Assessment reform for the age of artificial intelligence" (two principles cited); Group of Eight principles; Higher Education Standards Framework (Threshold Standards) 2021. TEQSA's Nov 28 2024 toolkit "Gen AI strategies for Australian higher education: Emerging practice" features Sydney's two-lane approach "as a key example."

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **structural realism about enforcement** → assessment redesign. The two-lane table (verbatim):
  - **Secure (Lane 1)**: role = "Assessment of learning"; level = "Mainly at program level"; security = "Secured, in person"; GenAI role = "May or may not be allowed by examiner"; TEQSA alignment = "Principle 2 – forming trustworthy judgements of student learning"; examples = "In person interactive oral assessments; viva voces; contemporaneous in-class assessments and skill development; tests and exams."
  - **Open (Lane 2)**: role = "Assessment for and as learning"; level = "Mainly at unit level"; security = "'Open' / unsecured"; GenAI role = "As relevant, use of AI scaffolded & supported"; TEQSA alignment = "Principle 1 – equip students to participate ethically and actively in a society pervaded with AI"; examples = "AI to provoke reflection, suggest structure, brainstorm ideas, summarise literature, make content, suggest counterarguments, improve clarity, provide formative feedback."
- Core validity doctrine (verbatim): "**Any unenforceable restriction damages assessment validity** so a scale or traffic light approach of telling students that they can only use AI for certain purposes, or use certain AI tools, is untenable. A clearer and more realistic approach is to consider the use of AI in lane 2 assessments **as a menu**, where students can pick anything and it is our role as educators to guide them which options are more delectable (better for learning)."
- No-amber rule: "In the two-lane approach, there is **no 'amber' traffic light and AI use can only be restricted when we can do so securely and reliably**. In Open (Lane 2) assessments, there is only a 'green' light."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
**Clarifications:**
1. Lane 2 ≠ merely "open/unsecured" — "full" lane 2 embraces TEQSA principle 1; mere non-secured = "'towards lane 2'."
2. Lane 2 is "primarily about learning the 'stuff, skills, and soul' of the unit, program, and discipline" — not learning AI per se; AI analogy to "written word, printing press, calculator, and internet."
3. Lane 1 ≠ "no AI": secure environments "may or may not involve the use of AI" — architecture example: industry already uses image-gen for ideation, so a secure 1:1 mock client-architect oral with AI could be valid.
4. Lane 2 dual purpose (prepare for lane 1 + develop AI skills): "It's both."
**Assessment and curriculum design:**
5. Program-level assessment (deliberately NOT "programmatic assessment," which is "associated with... medical education in particular"; for competency-based programs "such as dentistry, medicine and veterinary science, a fully programmatic approach may be warranted" — explicit medical-education recognition).
6. Coursework Policy Principle 5 ("Assessment practices must be integrated into program design") requires: integrated assessment/feedback across units; development support enrolment-to-graduation; "students' learning attainment can be **validated across their program at relevant progression points and before graduation**."
7. Principle 6: program design in a "trustworthy way" with "supervised assessments... designed to assure learning in a program."
8. Distribution: "secured assessments be distributed throughout a student's journey, not just at the end"; model = "a lane 1 hurdle task at the end of each year, progressing from an oral discussion to a final exam with proper support... practice, access to multiple attempts, adjustments."
9. Redesign timeline: alignment by Semester 2 2025; "more profound changes to the assessments themselves and the curriculum will occur over a longer timeframe of 1-2 years."
10. **Traffic-light/scale rejection** (verbatim in (b)); "unsecured assessment – restricted AI" option refused: "There is no way to reliably restrict or select which, if any, generative AI tools students should use... except in Secure (Lane 1) environments"; "unsecured assessment – no AI" drop-down "only a temporary measure, noting that it is actually not possible to enforce this."
11. Menu doctrine: educator "helping to guide but not control the choice"; language shift "from what uses of AI are 'allowed' or 'permitted', to what uses of AI are most **'helpful'**."
12. Lane-2 brain-use defense: tool selection, use, output evaluation "requires skill, disciplinary knowledge, and evaluative judgement"; "Crudely – if students choose to bypass learning in their assessment for learning, they will fail the assessments of that learning."
13. Not all-lane-1: "Generative AI tools are an intimate part of our productivity and communication tools and their use cannot be reliably detected. Generative AI is already being added to 'wearables' such as glasses, pins and hearing assistance aids... Genuinely securing assessment is already expensive, intrusive, and inconvenient" — mix per program: some units lane-1-only, some lane-2-only, some mixed.
14. Weighting: early discussions — "grading only should only apply to lane 1 assessments, which may become 'hurdle' (must pass) tasks, whilst lane 2 assessments are pass/fail"; heuristic: "you likely want to more heavily weight lane 2 assessments"; marks drive behaviour ("marks drive student behaviour and promote effort").
15. Lane-1 scope: "Lane 1 assessments assure the learning outcomes of the program... rather than of the individual units" (HESF Threshold Standards course-level emphasis); 2024 datum: "around 80% of our units (corresponding to just under 60% of unit enrolments) did not have exams."
16. Paired assessments permitted: Open essay + Secure oral hurdle; mark for essay dependent on oral discussion is possible; presentation model — slides/script AI-assisted (Lane 2) + "Q&A following presentation, submission or placement" as Secure Lane 1; even avatar-presented video allowed in lane 2.
**Assessment validity:**
17. Default flip (S1 2025): "the default academic integrity setting will move from generative AI being prohibited except when expressly allowed by a coordinator to being **allowed unless expressly prohibited**"; exams and in-semester tests keep prohibited default; "By January 2025... coordinators will not be able to prohibit its use" in open assessment.
18. Free Copilot for Web for all enrolled students; expectations conversations.
19. Online quizzes are de facto open: "Plugins exist for all of the common browsers which give students undetectable tools... Testing internationally suggests that these generative AI tools are capable of at least passing such assessments, often obtaining high marks and outscoring humans"; orals via web-conference also unsecured.
20. Why not allow collusion/plagiarism by same logic: "Because collusion or plagiarism are not graduate qualities we wish to develop" — AI use is.
21. Marking philosophy: mark product accordingly, but "the process they use to produce is likely to be more important"; rubrics combining product+process; keep marks on lane 2 for motivation; possibly pass/fail.
**Academic integrity, contract cheating, detection:**
22. Contract cheating remains "a serious integrity breach"; two-lane "ensures that the use of such approaches will not lead to students gaining degrees because they will not pass the Secure (Lane 1) assessments"; hope that free Copilot "lost[s]" the contract-cheating business model.
23. Detection stance (verbatim): "Research has consistently shown (e.g. Scarfe et al (2024) and Fleckenstein et al (2024)) that **humans cannot accurately detect good AI use**"; detection software "unreliable on its own"; "effectively an 'arms race' in which those who can afford the latest models will remain undetected"; detection "likely only to 'catch' those who use the tools poorly or who cannot afford the latest AI models" — an equity argument against detection. Similarity software survives for verbatim plagiarism.
24. Viva-on-suspicion: "evidence suggests that it is only poor use that is reliably detected" — securing the task, not suspect-testing, is the answer.
**Secure lane operations:**
25. Capacity/resource realism: 80%-no-exams baseline; program-level design reduces total exams; invigilation of wearables "potentially more intrusive"; remote proctoring "will become increasingly difficult to secure... accompanied by a risk assessment and... appropriate approval"; online courses use placements/intensives/exam centres.
26. Interactive oral assessments: "a 15 minute conversation allows them to explore student understanding in a much deeper way than reading a written report"; community of practice from 2025; scale answers.
27. Hurdle tasks: "natural for Secure (Lane 1) assessments to become hurdle tasks"; "milestone" and "stage gate" points; hurdle exam with "specified minimum pass threshold" recommended in Academic Integrity Procedures.
**Marking/feedback with AI:**
28. Reciprocity: "The use of generative AI by educators should be acknowledged just as we require students to do" (aligned with Federal AI Ethics Principles); "the final mark and feedback are the responsibility of the marker"; Cogniti agents enhance feedback; educator AI uses: drafting rubrics, MCQs with feedback, resources, analogies; "build 'doubles' of themselves."
29. Hallucination definition given verbatim ("not databases of knowledge... false or misleading information presented confidently"); marker's accuracy-judgment unchanged; scaffolded acknowledgment "expected to greatly reduce the number of integrity cases."
**Policy changes & support:**
30. S1 2025 changes minimal (default flip + "AI allowed" icon in unit outlines; Sydney Curriculum/Akari drop-down); S2 2025 new assessment categories/types ("In person – secured": practical/performance/creative task; written/creative task; Q&A following presentation/submission/placement; "Practice or application – open" etc.).
31. Adjustments/equity: universal design of lane 2 removes most adjustment needs; lane 1 adjustments personalised; special-consideration matrix redevelopment 2025; simple extensions & anonymous marking roles to be re-examined.
32. Support: Strategic Education Grants (2024 assessment focus, creative-writing project), Teaching Symposium, AI in Higher Education Symposium, 1:1 consultations, MPLF modules, Library/Learning Hub workshops, **Academic Honesty Education Module (AHEM)** updated with new integrity settings; embedded AI tutorial in first-semester transition units; AI in Education student-partnered site (bit.ly/students-ai); endorsed-tools list + guardrails intranet page.
33. Equity: paid-tool access gap + school-sector variance named; Copilot/Cogniti institutional provision as the floor.
**Ethics:**
34. Conscientious objectors: tool choice may include "none"; architecture-type outcomes may require AI; "in 3-5 year's time, generative AI will feel much less 'strange'... design assessments so that conscientious objectors are able to equitably achieve learning outcomes."
35. IP: approved tools "do not hand over IP for the training of generative AI models."
36. Sustainability: Federal AI Ethics Principles quoted ("AI systems should benefit individuals, society and the environment"); energy/water use acknowledged; AI literacy incl. environmental impact.
37. Hallucination responsibility: "AI hallucinations are not something that the University can address; major AI labs are working on this."
38. Purpose question: "the discourse has shifted towards a recognition that higher education needs to re-evaluate its value and purpose."

## (d) Sophistication markers
- The validity-first doctrine ("unenforceable restriction damages assessment validity") converts assessment theory into policy logic — no other corpus document derives its rules from a psychometric principle.
- Equity-based anti-detection argument (detection catches only the poor or the poor-in-pocket) — unique framing; empirically cited (Scarfe 2024, Fleckenstein 2024).
- Graduate-quality criterion ("collusion... are not graduate qualities we wish to develop") provides a principled boundary between banned and permitted conduct.
- Operational depth: systems (Akari drop-downs, AI-allowed icons), hurdle/stage-gate architecture, special-consideration matrix redevelopment, wearables/implant foresight, remote-proctoring risk assessments.
- Medical-education literacy: explicit acknowledgment that "programmatic assessment" originates in medical education and suits competency-based dentistry/medicine/veterinary programs.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- The definitive articulation of the Australia two-lane assessment model — the deepest single assessment-policy text in the corpus (implementation FAQ with ~50 answered objections).
- Contrast UvA (licence-gated, detection-skeptical but compulsion-limited) and Karolinska (template disclosure): Sydney removes the possibility of unenforceable prohibition entirely rather than regulating around it.
- Its risk: total dependence on lane-1 quality and resourcing (capacity, invigilation, wearables) — which the FAQ itself documents.

## (f) Provisional classification
**pioneer** — full structural redesign of assessment around an enforcement-realism axiom, with regulator co-development (TEQSA), equity provision, and institutional tooling; the most intellectually coherent assessment regime in the corpus.
