# Deep Read — UAMS AI Generative Tool Use Policy 2.1.6

## Authority / Authorship
- Division of Academic Affairs; **approved by Council of Deans and Provost 2/9/2024**; revised 11/7/2025; **semi-annual review commitment** ("This policy will be updated as needed, but at minimum semi-annually").
- Applies to all UAMS departments and colleges across education, clinical care, research, administration; to employees, faculty, academic visitors, and trainees "including students, residents, post-docs" for all "work-related content."
- Central governance actor: **Chancellor's AI Governance Committee** (approves tools, receives 24-hour violation reports, must approve grading-adjacent AI uses); also Director of the Institute on Digital Health and Innovation (clinical CDS/pilots), HIPAA Office, FERPA officer, IRB. References policy stack 2.1.23 (PHI), 4.4.01 (Code of Conduct), 2.2.14 (Student Code), 16.1.04 (RCR), UA Board 210.1/210.2 (IP).

## Philosophy (verbatim framing quotes)
- "UAMS considers the presentation of Al-generated work as one's own work without appropriate attribution/citation to be plagiarism."
- Human-authorship doctrine: "The written document or other scholarly work must be authored by a human, although GAI information may be incorporated and cited. As such, that document is considered [to] be cognitive work of the faculty, employee, student, or academic visitor."
- Anti-detection stance embedded: "it is important to note that AI detection software currently available is inaccurate and unreliable."
- "No GAI tool is a substitute for human judgement" (repeated verbatim across research/education/clinical sections — a refrain structure).

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory

### Definitions
- AI: algorithms performing "tasks that typically require human intelligence"; mechanical types (punctuation, grammar, formatting, translation, visual scanning, voice recognition) vs generative.
- Generative AI: "a subset of AI that leverages computers to mimic the problem-solving and decision-making capabilities of the human mind"; some synthesize/recommend (Clinical Decision Tools), some "generate entire written, audio, synthetic data, or visual works from a prompt."
- Work: UAMS-related content "presented for peer review, grading as an assignment, or for public and/or scholarly dissemination."

### I. Contextual
- GAI/LLM uses "to help write clinical notes, manuscripts, student essays, presentations, and research articles."
- "Using Al and GAI tools is becoming an important skill for diverse health professions."
- Plagiarism framing tied to Basic Code of Conduct, RCR policy, Student Code, Core Values, and each College's Academic Misconduct/Integrity policies (decentralized enforcement).
- Planned AI incorporation "should be submitted to the appropriate review process" for HIPAA/FERPA/IT compliance; "AI or GAI tools that are unsafe or inappropriate will be blocked on UAMS computers and networks"; "A list of approved AI detection tools and GAI tools... can be obtained from the UAMS AI Governance Committee."

### II. Legal and Ethical Considerations
- Six enumerated bias mechanisms: (i) prompt quality — "master 'prompt engineering'... make sure prompts do not unintentionally introduce bias"; (ii) bias in training data — models "reproduce, rather than challenge, errors in the sources"; (iii) lack of diversity in training data; (iv) "Lack of critical thinking... they lack abductive reasoning to make judgments with incomplete information"; (v) lack of transparency; (vi) lack of accountability.
- "these biases can be unconscious, unintended, and hard to detect, but they can have serious consequences."
- "Use good judgement to determine if/where/when to use these tools. They do not always make products easier and/or better."
- **Copyright**: "the texts and images produced by GAI prompts are currently not protected by U.S. copyright law. This means that products created from the use of GAI are publicly available to anyone." Must not violate copyright/IP laws (cited board policies).
- **Indemnification**: "Users are responsible for ensuring GAI outputs do not infringe third-party rights; indemnification risk lies with the requester absent a vendor contractual defense."

### III. Citation of Work Utilizing GAI
- Full disclosure of "how and where" GAI was used; placement guidance ("methods or acknowledgements section"; "note in slide footers or syllabi").
- Disclosure content requirements: "what GAI program was used, what queries or instructions were given, how potential harms were avoided, how the reliability of the GAI program was evaluated, and how individuals performed quality checks."
- Style-guide citation (AMA/APA); journal-specific Methods paragraph.
- **Exemptions from disclosure**: "Chancellor's AI Governance Committee-approved tools, dictation software, language translation, spelling/grammar/punctuation, formatting, alphabetizing items, and formatting of citations (e.g., converting an APA citation to an AMA citation)."
- Uncited GAI use violates Student Code "because the information derived from these tools is not the product of an individual's unaided mind."
- Accuracy/bias check required before any use; human authorship doctrine (above).

### IV. Research
- Human judgment supremacy; **consent-form disclosure**: "Investigators must clearly disclose in consent forms any use of generative AI tools when the outputs could potentially re-identify research participants."
- "Any use of external generative AI tools involving study data requires prior Institutional Review Board (IRB) review and approval."
- Bias assessment duty, especially human-subjects datasets.
- **Research-file provenance**: "Investigators must maintain a record of AI tool versions, prompts, outputs, and decision rationales in the research file."
- IRB "should establish procedures for periodic review of AI use in active studies."
- "Inputting identifiable participant data into public or non-secure AI tools is prohibited"; all AI use "must be reviewed and approved by the Chancellor's AI Governance Committee."

### V. Education/Teaching
- Student use to satisfy course requirements "must be authorized by the course faculty, trainer, or education supervisor."
- **Three-mode faculty labeling mandate**: faculty must state whether GAI is "prohibited (i.e., not allowed in any capacity), restricted (i.e., allowed for specific projects/activities with appropriate citation), or unrestricted (i.e., allowed in any course activity with appropriate citation)."
- Helpful uses listed: "analysis, rephrasing, essentializing, synthesizing, and/or gathering information about the typical understanding of a topic"; "it must be the trainee who guides, verifies, and crafts the final answers."
- Sanctions up to dismissal.
- **FERPA/detection clause block**: "instructors may not input any student-identifiable information or student work into GAI, plagiarism checking tools, or Al-detection software without explicit written permission from the student unless approved for use by UAMS"; AI detection software "is inaccurate and unreliable"; approved tools/rules from AI Governance Committee; "Instructors are prohibited from asking Students for permission to input their work into GAI without express permission from the AI Governance Committee"; approval required for "centralized tracking of licensure and any potential integration into other systems like Blackboard"; **"any use of GAI by instructors to assist or review grading or evaluating student class assignments or work requires prior approval by the AI Governance Committee"**; GAI analysis of grades/records needs Dean + Committee approval.

### VI. Clinical
- AI-CDS tools: diagnostic/treatment/prognostic recommendations; "AI-CDS tools cannot be used to automatically interpret clinical results, integrate findings, or conceptualize cases."
- Automation exception only "when the literature... suggests that it is safe to do so with approval from the Director of the Institute on Digital Health and Innovation and the Chancellor's AI Governance Committee" (visual scanning, chart summarization).
- Mechanical GAI functions allowed (dictation, translation, formatting, EHR tools).
- **Outside-EHR queries**: "PHI may only be inputted and processed by an AI or GAI tool if fully approved by UAMS AI Governance Committee and only when a fully executed BAA exists" (Policy 2.1.23).
- "Clinicians must disclose whether patient consent was obtained before the use [of] AI or GAI technology (if applicable)."
- Pilot projects need Director + Committee approval.

### VII–VIII. Compliance and Future
- "Al-related disclosures or violations of this Policy must be reported within 24 hours to the UAMS Chancellor's AI Governance Committee and one or more of the following... HIPAA Office, the Office of Research Misconduct, Teaching and Learning Support Committee, and/or FERPA officer."
- **Mandatory annual AI training for all faculty, staff, and students** ("will be required").
- Semi-annual policy review.

## Sophistication Markers
- Six-mechanism bias taxonomy incl. abductive-reasoning limitation; prompt-engineering duty.
- Copyright status of GAI outputs + indemnification allocation to the requester — the only cohort-C policy touching tort/contract risk allocation.
- Research provenance record (tool versions, prompts, outputs, decision rationales) — audit-trail thinking.
- IRB review for external-GAI-on-study-data; consent-form re-identification disclosure.
- The most restrictive instructor-side regime in the corpus: grading-adjacent GAI requires Governance Committee pre-approval; instructor solicitation of student permission itself restricted; detection software declared unreliable while still listing "approved AI detection tools."
- 24-hour incident reporting clock; mandatory annual training; semi-annual review cadence.
