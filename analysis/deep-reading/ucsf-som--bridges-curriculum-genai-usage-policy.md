# UCSF Bridges Curriculum Generative AI Usage Policy

## Authority / Authorship
- Publisher: UCSF Medical Education; formal curriculum policy page under Policies & Procedures.
- "Accountable Dean or Director: Associate Dean for Assessment, Improvement, and Accreditation and Director of AI" — a named **Director of AI** accountable officer, a notable structural marker.
- "Approval and Governing Body: 12/10/2024, Committee on Curriculum and Educational Policy (CCEP)." "Last Procedural Update: August 11, 2026" — actively maintained (updated ~2 years after approval).

## Policy Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "This policy provides guidance for the ethical and secure use of generative artificial intelligence (AI) tools in undergraduate medical education."
- "As the use of generative AI tools continues to expand, students, GNE trainees, staff and faculty must understand how to use these tools safely and appropriately." [text reads "GME trainees" in source: "students, GME trainees, staff and faculty"]
- Four principles (verbatim): users must (1) "understand and adhere to UCSF and UC system-wide policies and expectations on AI use," (2) "have sufficient proficiency in the subject area to verify the accuracy of the output," (3) "understand and acknowledge potential biases and limitations of information produced by generative AI tools," and (4) "follow processes for documentation and appropriate attribution of AI use."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory by Theme

### Definitions
- "Generative AI refers to a category of artificial intelligence (AI) algorithms that generate new outputs including text, images, sounds, animation, 3D models, and other types of data using a chatbot (prompt) interface."
- "Many generative AI tools have been trained on massive amounts of text data and can produce human-like text and generate responses; these are referred to as large language models [LLMs]."

### Institutional platform / secure deployment
- "At UCSF, in collaboration with our Privacy, Procurement, and Legal teams, we now have generative AI technology through UCSF's Microsoft Azure cloud environment using the Azure OpenAI service called ChatGPT Enterprise."
- "ChatGPT Enterprise (UCSF platform) generative AI tool is allowed for students, faculty, and staff including patient care activity at UCSF Health sites because it is HIPAA compliant and approved for UCSF data including patient information."
- "While use of ChatGPT Enterprise is allowed at all sites, use with patient data is currently permitted only at UCSF Health sites."

### Compliance / site-level policy
- "Use of any AI tools must comply with the policies and procedures outlined by the applicable campus, department, and health system or clinical setting."

### Prohibited commercial platforms for sensitive data
- "Use of any AI tools for sensitive UCSF data, including identified or de-identified patient, personnel, confidential, faculty-owned/copyrighted and other proprietary materials or otherwise sensitive UCSF data, is prohibited on commercial platforms (e.g., ChatGPT (not Enterprise), Claude, Gemini, etc.)."

### Assessments
- "The use of AI tools is prohibited for activities in which students are evaluated as representing their own knowledge or skills unless explicitly granted permission by the faculty."
- "Students are responsible for verifying whether the use of generative AI tools are permissible from the faculty."

### Accountability
- "The student is responsible for reviewing all AI generated texts for accuracy and appropriateness."

### Enforcement
- "Violations of this policy will be considered academic misconduct, underscoring the seriousness of adherence to these guidelines."

### LCME anchoring
- "Related LCME Standards: 5.9 Information Technology Resources/Staff; 7.4 Critical Judgment/Problem-Solving Skills; 7.7 Medical Ethics."

## Sophistication Markers
- **Tool investment**: institutionally negotiated ChatGPT Enterprise deployment on UCSF's Azure tenant, explicitly HIPAA-approved for patient information within UCSF Health — one of the strongest concrete deployments in this batch; cross-functional governance named (Privacy, Procurement, Legal).
- **Tech understanding**: distinguishes Enterprise vs. consumer ChatGPT; distinguishes de-identified vs. identified data; site-scoped permissions (patient data only at UCSF Health sites).
- **Governance**: accountable named officers including a "Director of AI"; curriculum committee approval; dated update trail.
- **Evidence citations**: LCME standards cited explicitly (5.9, 7.4, 7.7).
- **Assessment redesign**: absent — prohibition-default on evaluated activities, no redesign program.

## Distinctives
- Shortest full policy of the pioneer candidates yet among the most operationally precise: it *allows* patient-care AI use on the approved enterprise platform — far beyond most schools' education-only scope.
- Named "Director of AI" as accountable official.
- Explicit de-identified data still banned from commercial platforms.

## Provisional Classification: **pioneer**
Justification: HIPAA-compliant enterprise deployment explicitly authorized for patient-care activity; named AI directorship; legal/procurement/privacy co-governance; LCME-anchored; current (2026 procedural update). It is thinner on pedagogy/citation than Stanford or UCSF PharmD, but its operational deployment reality is frontier-level.
