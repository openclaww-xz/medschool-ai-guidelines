# UCSF School of Pharmacy — Policy on Use of AI in Assessments and Deliverables

## Authority / Authorship
- Publisher: UCSF School of Pharmacy (pharmd.ucsf.edu/policies/ai) — formal school-level policy.
- "Policy approved by CEPC October 18, 2023" (Committee on Educational Policy, presumably) — committee-approved, dated.

## Policy Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "AI is likely to transform many fields and becoming familiar with its strengths and limitations will be as important for health care providers as learning to use the internet to search."
- "Learning is not simply memorization of facts—it is building flexible knowledge structures that can be called upon to solve problems and evaluate possible solutions. AI systems represent one source of possible solutions, but to evaluate the value of an AI's proposed solution, it is necessary to have adequate proficiency in that domain."
- Three-pillar principles: **Proficiency**, **Verification** ("Students must take full responsibility for AI-generated materials as if they had produced them themselves"), **Documentation** ("All ideas that are not originally one's own have a source, and that source generally must be attributed").

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory by Theme

### Definitions
- "Generative AI (gAI), or computer systems that can produce content (e.g., ChatGPT, Bard, Bing) in response to natural language queries, is now widely available to produce text, images, and other media."
- Assessments defined: "Demonstrations of knowledge or skill, whether proctored or unproctored, such as summative examinations, formative examinations, objective structured clinical examinations, quizzes, and the like."
- Deliverables defined: "Written, oral, or audiovisual assignments or presentations such as reflection assignments, journal club presentations, Discovery Project manuscripts, etc."

### The four-tier classification system (the policy's core)
- "The following classification scheme will be used for all course assessments and deliverables required for course completion."
- "Instructors and preceptors may provide a blanket classification for all assessments or deliverables for a learning experience or provide separate classification for individual assessments or deliverables."
- **Default rule**: "If an assessment or deliverable does not have a classification provided, it is assumed to be classified AI-1." (AI-1 = disallowed.)
- **AI-1: Disallowed** — "Generative AI tools cannot be used in this assessment or deliverable... Use of AI will be considered an academic integrity violation and will trigger the Policy on Student Misconduct in Academic Studies. Examples... Summative assessments. Formative assessments and quizzes. Reflections."
- **AI-2: Restricted** — "Generative AI tools are restricted for this assessment or deliverable and require documentation... students are restricted in either the types of AI tools that may be used, or on which aspects of the assignment AI may be employed. All use of AI must be appropriately acknowledged... Examples: Journal article assignments where summarization is performed by AI, but assessment of strengths and limitations is generated by the student. Production of summaries of topics that provide a basis for further non-AI-assisted inquiry."
- **AI-3: Documented** — "Generative AI tools may be used in any manner for this assessment or deliverable but require documentation... Examples: Background summaries of topics where the main point of the assignment is generation of a new idea or proposal. Assignments whose goal is to develop skills in using AI-based services."
- **AI-4: Unregulated** — "Generative AI tools are not restricted for this assessment or deliverable and documentation of use is not required... Note that AI products are increasingly integrated into standard software packages (e.g., Microsoft) to provide grammar and spellchecking, and these capabilities will likely increase. Current versions of such products do not require citation."

### Submission attestation (implied-contract clauses)
- "By submitting an assessment or deliverable for evaluation: Students assert that they have respected all specific requirements of the assigned work, in particular requirements for transparency and documentation of process, or have explained themselves where this was not possible."
- "When use of AI is allowed, students assert that it accurately reflects the facts and that they have verified the facts, especially if they originate from generative AI resources."
- "When use of AI is allowed and documentation is required, students assert that all sources that go beyond common knowledge are suitably documented. Common knowledge is what a knowledgeable reader can assess without requiring confirmation from a separate source."

### Privacy / care and research boundary
- "This policy is designed to apply to assessments and deliverables used in the pedagogical process, not as part of direct patient care or human subjects research."
- "Most commercially available AI systems are not compliant with HIPAA or FERPA protections and entry of patient or student information (identified or de-identified) into such systems is a violation of UCSF policies and potentially a crime."
- "Use of AI systems in patient care or research requires direct, positive confirmation from a preceptor or research director that such use is allowed and that the system is authorized to work with such information."

### Citation appendix
- "When assessments or deliverables are classified as AI-2 or AI-3, documentation as to the manner of gAI use (if used) must be provided. Documentation may take the form of primary source citations, summary statements, or both."
- "While gAI content may include references to primary sources, the gAI's output is not reviewed by experts and the source references may not be correct. When primary source citations are specified, all factual statements in the work product that are not common knowledge must have the original source cited per standard citation styles."
- Summary statement template (verbatim): "I acknowledge the use of [insert AI system(s) and link] to [specific use of generative artificial intelligence]. The prompts used include [list of prompts]. The output from these prompts was used to [explain use]."
- Full worked example included (ChatGPT, Monash University background-summary example, dated 4 January 2023).

### References
- "Sentient Syllabus Project; UNESCO ChatGPT and artificial intelligence in higher education: quick start guide; Monash University: Policy and practice guidance around acceptable and responsible use of AI technologies; UCSF Data Protection and Large Language Models (requires MyAccess login)."

## Sophistication Markers
- **Assessment redesign**: the strongest in the batch — a complete four-level classification taxonomy (AI-1..AI-4) applied to every assessment and deliverable, with instructor discretion, blanket classification option, and default-to-restricted rule. This is a genuine redesign of the assessment-contract machinery, not a bolt-on prohibition.
- **Tech understanding**: recognizes ambient AI integration ("AI products are increasingly integrated into standard software packages... grammar and spellchecking") and exempts it — a 2023 policy anticipating the tool-embedding problem.
- **Evidence citations**: external scholarly/policy references (Sentient Syllabus, UNESCO, Monash) — rare and sophisticated.
- **Legal precision**: "entry of patient or student information (identified or de-identified) into such systems is a violation of UCSF policies and potentially a crime."
- **Tool investment**: no dedicated secure deployment named in this policy itself (cross-references UCSF LLM data-protection guidance).

## Distinctives
- The AI-1→AI-4 taxonomy with per-assignment classification is the most operationalized permission architecture in this batch.
- Positive framing: AI literacy compared to "learning to use the internet to search."
- Submission-as-attestation construction ("By submitting... students assert...") — legal-contract framing of integrity.
- PharmD-specific (School of Pharmacy), demonstrating the pattern extends beyond MD programs.

## Provisional Classification: **pioneer**
Justification: the classification taxonomy constitutes real assessment redesign; external evidence citations; anticipatory handling of embedded AI; attestation-based integrity model; pedagogically articulate rationale (flexible knowledge structures). Frontier of policy design even though it lacks its own named secure deployment.
