# UK joint statement from statutory regulators of health and care professionals — Using AI in health and care professional education

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: a coalition of **UK statutory regulators** (the accreditation/licensing bodies with legal authority over health professions education). Signatories listed at the foot (the file lists): General Chiropractic Council, General Optical Council, General Osteopathic Council, General Pharmaceutical Council, Health and Care Professions Council, Royal College of Veterinary Surgeons. (Note: GMC is a sibling regulator captured separately; the signatory list in this capture may be partial relative to the full statement.)
- Authority basis stated verbatim: "As regulators, we **set the knowledge, skills, understanding and professional behaviours** expected of health and care professionals. Education providers are **required** to meet our education and training standards and professionals our professional standards." Statutory, not hortatory.
- Ecological positioning: "The aim of this statement is **not to supersede existing guidance, but to complement** and provide clarity around regulator expectations" — acknowledging OfS's "principles based approach to regulation" and QAA's curated generative-AI resources.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **risk-benefit-balanced regulator expectations, principles not rules**. Framing verbatim: "if used appropriately, AI can be a **positive tool for learners** as they develop the skills and knowledge required for future practice"; risks: "an **overreliance on AI and the loss of core skills**, as well as the potential for **biased or misleading outputs**, which can all impact on patient safety."
- Non-negotiable core: "Ensuring that our **standards are not compromised** through the increasing use of AI is highly important to service users and the professions that we regulate."
- Check-in mechanism: "We would welcome the opportunity through **engagement activities to see how these principles have been considered**" — regulator review without new rulemaking.

## (c) Complete clause inventory
**Introduction clauses:**
1. Regulatory-authority clause (verbatim in (a)).
2. Landscape clause: "The education landscape is in a state of change. We know that learners are using AI (in particular, generative AI) in many different ways to support their learning journeys" — footnoted to **Student generative AI survey 2025, Higher Education Policy Institute** (the corpus's only HEPI evidence citation).
3. Benefit clause: "improved efficiencies, the creation of more realistic simulations, and its use as a personalised learning tool."
4. Risk clause: overreliance/loss of core skills; biased or misleading outputs; "which can all impact on patient safety."
5. Learner-entitlement clause: learners "receive proper support and understand both the risks and benefits"; "understand how AI can be applied in their future practice and develop the skills necessary to use this technology ethically, safely and effectively."
6. Complement clause (not supersede; OfS and QAA acknowledged).
**Principles (complete, by heading):**
7. **Accountability**: (a) "Learners, education providers and staff appropriately **communicate where and how AI is being used**." (b) "Learners are **accountable for their use of AI** and understand and adhere to their institutions' AI policies."
8. **Academic integrity**: (a) "Education providers ensure that **assessment methods continue to remain reliable and valid**, with the increased accessibility of AI for learners." (b) "Even when using AI, learners **must still meet the requirements linked to each regulator's professional standards**."
9. **Development of AI literacy for staff and learners**: (a) staff teaching AI-linked content "have appropriate skills and knowledge and are supported by their institution"; (b) "Staff developing and managing assessments have **sufficient knowledge and skills in AI** to ensure assessments are in line with the 'academic integrity' area"; (c) "support for learners and staff... through a **positive learning culture, the right to challenge and access to adequate resources**"; (d) "Learners and staff develop skills to **identify biased, inaccurate or misleading content** in AI responses"; (e) learners understand ethical use "in line with their profession's practice standards, including... **data protection legislation... to maintain patient confidentiality**"; (f) "education providers ensure **equitable access to AI that does not amplify existing inequalities** between learners from different backgrounds or **discriminate with respect to protected characteristics**."
10. **Preparation for practice**: (a) providers prepare learners for appropriate use in future practice — "practical, legal and ethical use," plus "critical thinking skills required to become an **autonomous professional**"; (b) "**Learners demonstrate AI explainability.** This means that they understand how decisions are made and are equipped with the skills to **explain their use of AI to service users or caregivers** in a way that is clear and easy to understand, **including the outlining of any risks**"; (c) providers equip learners to keep developing understanding "given the rapid pace of change once in practice."
11. **Glossary clauses**: AI defined per NHS England ("use of digital technology to create systems capable of performing tasks commonly thought to require human intelligence"); Generative AI per Government Digital Service ("a subset of AI capable of generating text, images, video or other forms of output by using **probabilistic models trained across one or more domains**").
12. **Signatory list** (as captured): GCC, GOC, GOsC, GPhC, HCPC, RCVS.

## (d) Sophistication markers
- "**AI explainability**" as a *learner demonstrable* requirement — not a developer duty — is the corpus's clearest articulation of explain-to-the-patient as a registrable competence; paired with "outlining of any risks."
- Assessment-validity language ("continue to remain reliable and valid") used precisely — the regulators name the construct-validity problem, not just "cheating."
- Equity clause tied to **protected characteristics** (legal anti-discrimination hook) — sharper than AAMC's access principle.
- Borrowed definitions from NHS England and GDS show inter-locking state digital architecture; HEPI 2025 survey grounds empirics.
- "Loss of core skills" as a named risk is educationally precise (overreliance → deskilling).

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- The only **multi-profession statutory-regulator joint instrument** in the corpus: six regulators speaking with one voice across chiropractic, optics, osteopathy, pharmacy, allied health, veterinary. GMC parallels (separate file) for medicine.
- "Right to challenge" within learning culture is unique procedural language.
- Functions as the UK professional-judgment model's education anchor: regulators set outcome expectations; providers keep design freedom.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** — risk-literate, evidence-cited, principles-based statutory expectations without micro-rules; the balanced center of the international corpus. (Not pioneer: it enables rather than redesigns.)
