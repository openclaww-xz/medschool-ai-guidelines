# Deep Read — UKY COM Use of AI in GME Policy

## Authority / Authorship
- University of Kentucky College of Medicine, Graduate Medical Education. **"Approved by GMEC: 05-25-2026."** No named author; GMEC is the approving body.
- Complements (not replaces) UK clinical, IT, privacy, security, research, and professionalism policies; compliance with "accreditation requirements, and federal and state laws."
- Cross-references: "UKHC Guidelines and Recommendations for Generative AI in Clinical Care" and the university-wide **"Commonwealth AI Transdisciplinary Strategy (CATS AI) framework"** — nested policy stack (enterprise → health system → GME).

## Philosophy (verbatim framing quotes)
- "The purpose of this policy is to establish guidelines for the responsible, ethical, and transparent use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Graduate Medical Education (GME)."
- Scope carve-out showing tool-taxonomy awareness: "This policy governs the use of generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools that create text, images, code, or analytic outputs. It does not apply to limited-function tools such as spelling and grammar editing, reference managers, or routine clinical decision support systems already governed by existing clinical policies."
- Principle of proportionality: "Higher-stakes decisions require greater oversight and caution."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory

### Definitions
- AI: "Computer systems designed to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence, including content generation, summarization, pattern recognition, and decision support."
- Generative AI: "AI systems capable of producing new content based on user prompts, such as large language models."
- **Human-in-the-Loop**: "A required process in which a qualified individual reviews, verifies, and remains accountable for AI-assisted outputs."

### Guiding Principles
- **Human accountability**: "AI may support but must never replace professional judgment."
- **Transparency**: "AI use must be disclosed when it meaningfully contributes to educational, research, clinical, or recruitment-related activities." (materiality qualifier — rare precision)
- **Privacy and security**: "PHI, trainee educational records, and applicant data must be safeguarded in accordance with UK policies."
- **Fairness**: "AI use should be monitored to ensure consistent and unbiased application and to mitigate unintended differences in outcomes."
- **Proportionality**: higher stakes → greater oversight.
- Blanket: "All users remain accountable for outcomes resulting from AI-assisted work."

### Education and Training
- Use allowed for "learning, teaching preparation, curriculum development, and faculty development activities, provided that AI-generated content is reviewed and verified for accuracy and AI is not used to bypass required learning activities or assessments."
- **Decentralization mandate**: "Individual GME programs are expected to develop program-level guidance regarding appropriate AI use within their educational and clinical training environments, reflecting the unique needs, risks, and workflows of their specialty and clinical settings."

### Program Administration and Evaluation
- AI may support "drafting, organization, or synthesis" with safeguards.
- **"AI must not independently generate, determine, or finalize milestone assessments, promotion or graduation decisions, remediation plans, or disciplinary actions. All evaluative decisions require human review and accountability."**

### Clinical Care
- Must comply with UK clinical/privacy/IT policies; GME follows UKHC GenAI-in-Clinical-Care guidelines.
- "Residents and fellows may not enter Protected Health Information (PHI) into unapproved AI tools or use AI tools independently to generate clinical documentation, orders, or treatment decisions. AI may not replace clinical judgment or supervision."

### Research and Scholarly Activity
- Disclosure "at all relevant stages of the research process"; IRB/sponsor/publication compliance; "AI tools do not replace authorship responsibility or scholarly accountability."

### Recruitment and Selection
- "AI may be used in a limited, supportive role... such as organizing application materials or drafting communications."
- **"Only UK Approved AI tools for recruitment (e.g. Thalamus Cortex) may be used"** — a named approved product.
- **"AI must not independently screen, score, rank, or select applicants. All recruitment decisions must be made by human reviewers."**

### Ethics, Equity, and Professionalism
- "All AI use in GME must uphold ethical standards, professionalism, and respect for trainees, applicants, and patients. Inappropriate use may be addressed through existing institutional policies."

### Approved Tools, Privacy, and Security
- "Only University-approved AI tools that meet institutional privacy, security, and information technology standards may be used for activities involving trainee data, applicant information, clinical data, or research materials."
- Forward-link: "Users are encouraged to remain informed of University of Kentucky AI initiatives and guidance that are concurrently in development... updated in the Commonwealth AI Transdisciplinary Strategy (CATS AI) framework."

## Sophistication Markers
- Explicit out-of-scope taxonomy (grammar tools, reference managers, existing CDS excluded).
- Materiality-based disclosure trigger ("when it meaningfully contributes").
- Named recruitment AI product (Thalamus Cortex) — concretely tool-specific.
- Proportionality principle; HITL defined as a "required process."
- Program-level subsidiarity mandate (each GME program must write its own guidance).

## Distinctives
- Cleanest separation-of-powers architecture in the cohort: an umbrella GME policy that deliberately defers to a health-system clinical guideline (UKHC) and an enterprise strategy (CATS AI), and pushes specialty-specific detail down to programs.
- Recruitmentscreening clause is firmer than OHSU's (UK: never independently screen/score/rank/select; OHSU: allowed if institutionally approved).

## Provisional Classification: **Progressive-structured**
Justification: concise but structurally mature — definitions, principles with proportionality, evaluative-decision firewall, named tools, nested governance — without the operational depth (scribe consent mechanics, versioning) of OHSU GME-48 or Utah's enterprise instrument. Not pioneer only because it delegates rather than specifies most operational detail.
