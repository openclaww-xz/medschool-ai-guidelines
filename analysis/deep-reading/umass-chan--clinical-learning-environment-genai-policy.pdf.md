# UMass Chan Medical School — Usage Policy for Generative AI-Powered Tools in the Clinical Learning Environment

## Authority / Authorship
- Publisher: UMass Chan Medical School, T.H. Chan School of Medicine, Office of Undergraduate Medical Education; PDF filename "clinical-genai-policy-v2-draft".
- "Authored by: Nancy Skehan, MD, Allen Chang, MD, Anindita Deb, MD" — named physician authors, rare in this corpus.
- Status: v2 DRAFT — "Submission Date / Date Approved / Approved by: list relevant institutional leaders and/or committees / Updated / Effective Date" all blank. Not yet enacted.
- Explicitly "a supplement to the SOM Usage Policy for GenAI-Powered Tools" — a two-layer policy architecture (general + clinical).
- Closing disclosure: "This policy was reviewed in ChatGPT 5.2 to enhance clarity of language."

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- Developmental-sequence framing: GenAI tools "hold the promise of increasing efficiency, allowing rapid and targeted access to knowledge, and improving diagnostic accuracy but also hold the possibility of disrupting development of clinical skills if they are introduced to a learner without guidance prior to initial skill acquisition."
- Governing principle (verbatim): "GenAI cannot replace core cognitive work, but rather can support, augment, or reinforce learning."
- "It is important for students to be able to leverage the assistance and support that GenAI technology can offer in a way that augments learning and efficiency, while maintaining independent development of foundational skills such as clinical reasoning, clinical decision-making, and critical appraisal."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory
Scope
1. "This policy applies to all students enrolled in the SOM."
2. "This policy is in effect whenever students are participating in learning activities in a clinical environment."
3. "The GenAI policy of the clinical system in which a student is practicing is applicable and enforceable via sanctions from the relevant clinical system."
4. "Additional specific guidance around GenAI use within any given course should be reviewed in the course syllabus."
5. AI Commons: "The official suite of GenAI tools available to the UMassChan community is offered through UMass Chan AI Commons. UMass Chan AI Commons provides GenAI guidance, policies, and training as well as private GenAI tools that allow for input of some materials (such as internal course materials) that are not allowed in public GenAI tools."

IP gate
6. "To protect intellectual property, licensing agreements, and academic ownership, educational materials that may be copyrighted or proprietary should be used only within institutionally approved LLM platforms, such as those offered through UMass Chan AI Commons. Users should refrain from submitting such materials to external GenAI systems that are not authorized by UMass Chan Medical School."

Allowed Uses
7. "Students are permitted to use GenAI tools in the clinical learning environment to support their academic work, provided that their use aligns with the following guidelines…"
8. "Outlining: Using GenAI to help brainstorm or organize ideas into a structured outline for written assignments."
9. "Summarizing Documents: Using GenAI to create summaries of articles, research papers, or other readings, provided the summary is treated as a preliminary resource and not a substitute for a full reading. GenAI (such as AI scribes) should not be used to create summaries of meetings as this can contain PHI/PII."
10. "Editing and Proofreading: Leveraging tools for grammar checks, clarity improvements, or stylistic refinements."
11. "Supplementary Learning: Using GenAI for explanations of course concepts or generating study questions. Students may use GenAI-powered natural language consultation tools for evidence-based clinical queries (OpenEvidence, UpToDate) with citation of its use"
12. Embedded-GAI carve-out: "Students may use GenAI features that are embedded within institutionally provided platforms or workflows when such functionality is not optional. Examples include GenAI features embedded into radiographic image reconstruction, billing and coding, and chart summarization."
13. Catch-all: "If there is GenAI usage not described here that the student believes aligns with the principle stated above, they should review its use with the course director for permission for usage"

Attribution Requirements
14. "Any use of GenAI tools must be clearly disclosed in the submitted work." Examples verbatim: "In an outline: 'This outline was partially generated using ChatGPT for brainstorming and organization.'"; "In a document summary: 'A summary of [Title] was initially generated using ChatGPT and revised based on my reading of the text.'"; "In an assignment: 'These citations were aggregated and summarized through the use of OpenEvidence.'"; "For editing: 'Grammarly was used to refine grammar and clarity in this paper.'"

Student Responsibilities
15. Verification: "Students are responsible for ensuring the accuracy, relevance, and reliability of any GenAI-generated content. GenAI outputs can contain errors, misinterpretations, and plagiarized text."
16. Understanding: "Summaries or outlines generated by GenAI do not replace the responsibility to engage with and understand assigned materials in depth. Relying solely on GenAI for comprehension will hinder learning and negatively impact academic development."
17. Supervision: "When GenAI output influences clinical judgment, students must review conclusions with their supervising clinician and should not act independently on GenAI-generated suggestions."

Prohibited Uses
18. "Submitting GenAI-generated work as original content without modification and attribution."
19. "Using GenAI to generate differential diagnoses or management plans de novo"
20. "Using GenAI to generate entire essays, responses, or projects."
21. "Use of ambient scribes (e.g. Nuance DAX/Microsoft Dragon Copilot, Doximity) is prohibited unless permission is granted from the course director. Students may use standard dictation software that is not powered by GenAI."
22. "Use of PHI/PII in publicly available GenAI tools is not permitted due to risk for data compromise; PHI/PII may only be used in GenAI tools that are approved by the medical school or clinical system for such use. Examples include: Any individually identifiable health information; Research data that contains patient records or health identifiers"
23. "Using GenAI outside those uses allowed by the policy of the clinical system in which a student is practicing with regards to electronic health record content or other aspects of the legal medical record."
24. "Using GenAI for completion of quizzes, exams, or any other graded assessment unless explicitly approved within allowed uses or by course instructor."
25. "Uploading copyrighted materials or any course materials to public GenAI tools."

Enforcement
26. "Failure to adhere to these guidelines may result in academic penalties under the T.H. Chan School of Medicine Honor Code, Technical Standards and/or Professionalism guidelines and policies, as well as sanctions from the relevant clinical system."

Meta
27. "This policy was reviewed in ChatGPT 5.2 to enhance clarity of language." + blank approval block (draft status).

## Sophistication Markers
- Ambient-scribe governance is the most granular in cohort B: scribes banned by default with course-director opt-in, non-GenAI dictation expressly allowed (21), AI meeting summarization banned for PHI risk (9) — names Nuance DAX/Dragon Copilot/Doximity.
- Embedded-AI carve-out (12): when GenAI is non-optional in institutional workflows (image reconstruction, billing/coding, chart summarization), student use is legitimate — the only clause in the corpus that regulates unavoidable AI rather than chosen AI.
- Principle-plus-catch-all structure (13): unlisted uses adjudicated against the stated principle by the course director — flexible without being open-ended.
- Supervision duty tied to clinical-judgment influence (17): trigger is whether output influenced judgment, not tool category.
- Two-layer architecture (base SOM policy + this clinical supplement) + clinical-system policy stacking (3) with dual sanctions (26).
- Named consultation tools (OpenEvidence, UpToDate) permitted with citation; disclosure examples given per use-type (14).
- Named authors and an AI-assistance disclosure for the policy text itself.

## Distinctives
- Clinical-environment-only scope — the only cohort-B policy that does not attempt to govern preclinical coursework (left to the parent SOM policy, which is not in this corpus set).
- Draft status: approval fields blank, so enforceability is prospective; dated "v2" implies an iterated prior version.
- "De novo" qualifier on differential-diagnosis generation (19) implies AI-assisted refinement of differentials may be negotiable — subtle and deliberate wording.
- Meeting-summary ban extends PHI protection to a usage pattern (ambient capture) most policies never mention.

## Provisional Classification: progressive-structured
Justification: within its clinical scope it is the most finely calibrated policy in the cohort — permitted uses enumerated at task granularity, disclosure examples per genre, ambient-scribe and embedded-AI regimes, principle-based catch-all with course-director adjudication, supervision trigger keyed to judgment influence, and stacked institutional enforcement. Not pioneer because it is unenacted (draft), depends on an unseen parent policy for the preclinical domain, and lacks equity/access and faculty-development dimensions. Pairs with Buffalo as the two strongest clinical-environment policies in cohort B; Buffalo is enacted and literature-grounded, UMass is more granular at the tool level.
