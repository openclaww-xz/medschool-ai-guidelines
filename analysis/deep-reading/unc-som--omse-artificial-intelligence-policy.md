# UNC School of Medicine — OMSE Artificial Intelligence Policy

## Authority / Authorship
- Offices of Medical Student Education (OMSE) policy; "Approved by Education Committee July 2026"; review cycle fixed: "The Education Committee will review and approve the policy in even-numbered years."
- LCME references: 7.2 (Patient Care) and 7.7 (Medical Ethics).
- No named author; formal policy template (purpose/scope, responsible party, definitions, policy, violations).

## Policy Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "may support learning when used responsibly, transparently, and in ways that strengthen, not replace, student thinking, clinical reasoning, and professional development."
- "AI should assist your learning, not think for you."
- "Limits on AI use are necessary to ensure students develop and demonstrate their own clinical reasoning, knowledge, and professional judgment. Because AI outputs may be inaccurate, biased, or incomplete, AI should support learning, not replace independent thinking, clinical decision-making, or accountability."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory by Theme

### Scope
- "applies to student use of AI in the medical education program across academic and clinical learning environments. It applies to coursework, independent study, formative learning activities, and assessments unless a course, clerkship, or assignment states otherwise. Course and clerkship directors may establish alternative expectations for specific learning activities or assessments."
- "UNC guidance recommends that instructors state their AI expectations clearly and align them with course learning goals."

### Definitions (glossary style)
- "Machine learning: systems that learn patterns from data, like spam filters."
- "Natural language processing (NLP): tools that understand or generate text, like chatbots."
- "Computer vision: systems that interpret images or video, like facial recognition."
- "Speech recognition: converts spoken language to text, like voice assistants."
- "Generative AI: creates new content, like text, images, or code."
- HIPAA-compliant, FERPA-protected records, PHI, PII all defined in plain language.

### Permitted learning uses (allow-list — distinctive)
- "Unless explicitly prohibited by the course director, students may use approved AI tools to support learning in limited ways, such as: reviewing and reinforcing concepts, differential diagnoses, and treatment guidelines; exploring clinical reasoning and decision-making frameworks; preparing for examinations and shelf exams; generating study aids or practice questions for personal use; and brainstorming ideas."
- "Students must independently verify AI-generated information before relying on it."
- NOTE the inverted default: permitted **unless explicitly prohibited** (for learning uses) — the reverse of most policies in this batch.

### Assessments and assignments (competency-mapped)
- "AI use is prohibited to complete assessments, assignments, and reflections intended to evaluate the student's individual knowledge, judgment, or clinical reasoning unless explicitly authorized." (Tagged to milestones: "MK 1–5; PC 5–9; PBL 3–5; IC 2–4; PR 2".)
- "AI use for routine spelling, grammar, punctuation, and formatting are permitted as long as it does not alter the content of the student's original work and does not require disclosure unless otherwise specified."
- "Use of citation-management assistance with AI should be disclosed."

### Clinical documentation (competency-mapped)
- "AI use for clinical documentation must follow clinical-site policies and privacy requirements. Unless supported by approved institutional systems or explicitly permitted by the course, clerkship, clinical site, or supervisor, students may not use AI to generate, substantially modify, or fabricate H&Ps, patient notes, assessment and plans, or case presentations. These activities demonstrate data gathering, synthesis, communication, and clinical reasoning, not merely written documentation." (Tagged "PC 1–9; IC 2–4; PR 1–2; SBP 4".)

### Patient confidentiality
- "Students may not enter protected health information (PHI) into any AI system unless they have been explicitly informed by the institution that the system is HIPAA-compliant and approved for the use of PHI."
- "PHI, FERPA-protected records, personally identifiable information (PII), and other sensitive institutional data may not be entered into public, non-approved, or otherwise unauthorized AI systems." (Tagged "PR 1–2; PC 8; SBP 4".)

### Faculty IP / approved platforms
- "Students may not upload proprietary faculty-generated teaching materials to any external AI platform. Microsoft CoPilot and LLMs accessed through UNC PromptLab are permitted for this purpose." — names UNC PromptLab, an institutional LLM gateway.

### AI meeting summaries (unique clause)
- "Students may not use AI-generated meeting summaries without the knowledge and consent of all meeting participants. This applies to in-person meetings and virtual meetings, including Zoom. Students should follow best practices for notifying meeting participants of the use of meeting summaries when they are in use."

### Clinical use / disclosure / violations
- "Students must not rely on AI-generated output as a substitute for clinical judgment. Institutional policies at clinical sites may impose additional restrictions or limitations on AI use, which must be followed."
- "AI should not be used in any assignment unless explicitly permitted. When AI use is allowed, students must disclose that use in the manner required by the course director and remain fully responsible for: the accuracy of content; appropriate citation or acknowledgment; compliance with course expectations; academic integrity and professionalism."
- "Improper AI use may constitute a violation of course policy, professionalism standards, and/or academic integrity expectations and may result in assignment failure, loss of professionalism points, course failure, referral to the Student Progress Committee, and/or referral through university student conduct processes."
- "Students should use only UNC-approved AI tools for any permitted academic or clinical purpose."

## Sophistication Markers
- **Assessment/curriculum mapping**: every restriction is tagged to UNC MD competency milestones (MK, PC, PBL, IC, PR, SBP with numeric ranges) — the only policy in this batch that binds AI rules to its competency framework.
- **Default-permissive learning uses** with explicit allow-list (differentials, study aids, exam prep, brainstorming) — pedagogically specific about legitimate use.
- **Tech understanding**: plain-language tech glossary; distinguishes approved systems (CoPilot, UNC PromptLab) from external platforms; grammar/spelling carve-out; citation-management disclosure nuance.
- **Tool investment**: UNC PromptLab institutional LLM access named.
- **Assessment redesign**: partial — allow-list thinking, but no tiered classification or syllabus mandate.
- **Novel risk coverage**: AI meeting summaries (Zoom) consent clause — unique in corpus; anticipates ambient-AI consent issues.

## Distinctives
- "AI should assist your learning, not think for you" — memorable framing shared lineage with UNC GMB guidance.
- Competency-milestone tagging throughout.
- Grammar/punctuation micro-use carve-out without disclosure; citation-manager use DOES require disclosure — fine-grained disclosure calibration.
- Meets both edges: default-allow for learning, hard prohibition for evaluation.

## Provisional Classification: **progressive-structured** (near pioneer)
Justification: default-permissive allow-listed learning uses, competency-mapped restrictions, named institutional tool gateway, and a novel ambient-AI consent clause show pioneer instincts; held back from pioneer by absence of secure-deployment detail, external evidence citations, or a formal assessment-classification scheme.
