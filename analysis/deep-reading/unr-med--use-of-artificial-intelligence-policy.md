# Deep Read — UNR Med Use of Artificial Intelligence Policy

## Authority / Authorship
- UNR Med policy, effective 1/8/2026. Applies to "UNR Med administrative faculty, academic faculty and staff" — notably **not students** (faculty/staff instrument; students presumably reached indirectly).
- Nested under NSHE Records Retention Policy and **State Policy on the Responsible Use of AI** (Nevada state-level AI policy — state-system linkage). Operational arm: Med IT, which administers the "Restricted Use of Commercial AI Tools" agreement and the **UNR Med AI Attestation** form.

## Philosophy (verbatim framing quotes)
- "The purpose of this policy is to provide a framework for the responsible use of Artificial Intelligence (AI) at UNR Med."
- Data-retention rationale: "AI creates content from user supplied data, which means the information you supply is often retained within the model, posing an unintended risk of sharing sensitive data."
- "This policy provides guidance on compliance, security and operations and is not intended to limit academic freedom."
- "The use of AI does not preclude the responsibility of the user in checking and editing the result of Al-generated content."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory

### Definitions
- Generative AI: "a type of artificial intelligence that can create new content, such as text, images, videos, audio, or software code, in response to user prompts. These systems use generative models, like large language models, to statistically sample new data based on the training data set used to create them."

### Checking AI Content — six failure-mode checklist (user duty)
1. "Al Hallucination: This occurs when an AI generates plausible but incorrect information. For example, an AI might create a false summary of a news story or generate incorrect details about an event."
2. "Bias: AI systems can exhibit biases based on the data they were trained on. This lead to unfair or discriminatory outcomes, such as favoring certain groups over others in decision-making processes."
3. "Misinterpretations: These errors happen when an AI system incorrectly understands or processes the input it receives."
4. "Entity Recognition Errors: These occur when an AI fails to correctly identify and categorize entities such as names, dates, or locations within the text."
5. "Context Handling Failures: AI systems sometimes struggle to maintain context over long conversations or complex tasks."
6. "Reliable Sourcing: AI systems may not differentiate between reliable and unreliable source materials."

### Open-Source Language Model Sharing
- "End users will not supply regulated, sensitive, or confidential information to any open-source generative AI model."
- "UNR Med's preferred generative-AI platform of Microsoft Co-Pilot for Office 365, which does not supply end user data to its large language model and can be used for regulated, sensitive, or confidential information."
- "Use of non-enterprise, commercially available generative AI platforms (e.g., ChatGPT Free or Plus) is discouraged due to their potential to retain and use inputted data."
- "Users purchasing a subscription to non-enterprise AI platforms must complete the 'Restricted Use of Commercial AI Tools' agreement available from Med IT."
- **"AI must not be used for the purpose of deidentifying protected health information, since datasets deidentified in this manner may be reverse engineered."** (unique clause — bans AI de-identification itself)

### Use of AI for Original Content
- "To the extent that the end-user relies substantially on the use of AI to produce original content, such use will be disclosed with the following statement: Al was used to generate this content, and the content was reviewed and edited for validity." (prescribed disclosure sentence)

### AI for Transcribing Meeting Minutes
- Transcription must comply with confidentiality requirements; "recordings or Al-generated transcripts must be deleted following conversion to official minutes, unless retention is otherwise required. **Meetings with legal counsel must not be transcribed.**" (privilege-protection clause — unique in corpus)

### Software Containing AI
- "Any software purchases that include the use of AI must be reviewed by UNR Med IT to ensure that sensitive information is not being shared with an external large language model."

### Training
- "All users intending to use generative AI for academic, clinical, or administrative tasks are encouraged to complete a brief institutional AI orientation module."

### Attestation (References section)
- "Microsoft Copilot is UNR Med's approved AI tool and is strongly encouraged for AI-assisted work. If you choose to use a non-Copilot AI tool (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini), completion of the UNR Med AI Attestation is required." (administrative attestation mechanism for off-platform use)

## Sophistication Markers
- Six-item AI failure-mode taxonomy (hallucination, bias, misinterpretation, entity recognition, context handling, sourcing) — the most granular operational error checklist in the cohort.
- Prescribed disclosure statement; attestation form as compliance mechanism; procurement review for AI-embedded software.
- Prohibition of AI-based PHI de-identification with re-identification rationale.
- Legal-counsel meeting transcription ban (attorney-client privilege awareness).

## Distinctives
- Operational/administrative orientation (minutes, software purchasing, attestations) rather than academic-integrity orientation; no graded-work/exam clauses because audience is faculty/staff.
- State-system dependency (NSHE + Nevada State Policy on Responsible Use of AI).
- Typos ("ATTESTION", "Al" for "AI") indicate light editorial pass.
