# University of Toronto — Toward an AI-ready University: guidelines hub

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: University of Toronto (tri-campus). Authority chain named: **Professor Susan McCahan, Provostial Advisor on Artificial Intelligence**, who "convened an AI Task Force" with "expertise across a range of academic, administrative, and technical areas."
- Deliverable: "In June 2025, the Task Force produced a final report... '**Toward an AI-ready university**'" — the university-level strategic instrument under which all local guidelines sit.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: institutional self-positioning + guideline aggregation. Framing verbatim: "With a rich and robust history of leadership in the study and development of Artificial Intelligence (AI), the University of Toronto is **uniquely positioned to harness and shape the transformative potential** of this rapidly evolving field."
- Aim of the report: "to ensure GenAI is used **safely, securely, and appropriately** by our community" — a safety/security/appropriateness triad.
- Living-registry clause: "U of T has been **continually assessing the technological landscape and updating relevant guidelines and protocols as needed**. We will be collecting and sharing the guidelines and protocols as they become available."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
1. Positioning clause (verbatim in (b)).
2. Governance clause: Provostial Advisor appointed; AI Task Force convened "to develop a vision and strategic plan for U of T's AI activities"; membership spans academic, administrative, technical expertise.
3. Task-force purpose clause: "leverage AI to enhance our research expertise, support student success... and improve operational efficiencies – **all within a framework of clear and consistent principles**."
4. Report clause: June 2025 final report "Toward an AI-ready university" to ensure safe, secure, appropriate GenAI use.
5. Registry clause: guidelines collected and shared; "Additional resources will be posted to this page as they emerge regarding GenAI usage by faculty, students, researchers, and staff across our three campuses."
6. The collected guidelines index (complete list):
   - Guideline on the Use of AI by University Administrative Staff (*New)
   - Academic Integrity: Using a GenAI tool on a marked assessment
   - Coursework and GenAI: A Practical Guide for Students
   - Information Security Council: Use AI intelligently
   - Guidance on the use of Generative AI for marketing and communications staff
   - Office of the Vice-Provost, Innovations in Undergraduate Education: FAQs on GenAI in the classroom
   - **School of Graduate Studies: Guidance on the appropriate use of GenAI in graduate theses** (captured separately in this corpus)
   - University of Toronto Libraries: Citing GenAI tools
   - University of Toronto Libraries: GenAI and copyright considerations
   - Using artificial intelligence to help manage your meeting minutes
- No binding rules on the hub page itself; it is an index of ten sub-guidelines spanning staff, students, security, marketing, libraries, meetings.

## (d) Sophistication markers
- Named single accountable advisor (Provostial Advisor on AI) + taskforce + dated final report = a complete governance signature rare at university level.
- The index's breadth (administrative staff, marketing, meeting minutes, copyright, citing) shows AI governance escaping the classroom-only frame — operational AI is governed alongside academic AI.
- "Continually assessing... updating as needed" — explicit living-document mechanism.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- U of T is the corpus's clearest case of **hub-and-spoke** governance: one strategic report, ten spoke guidelines by office. Contrast with Melbourne (taskforce + principles but fewer published spokes) and Sydney (single deep policy for assessment).
- Temerty MD (separate note) plugs into this hub rather than writing its own AI policy — evidence of deliberate structural deference to the central university.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** — strategic, versioned, multi-audience, with a named accountable advisor; not technically deep on this page (depth delegated to spokes).
