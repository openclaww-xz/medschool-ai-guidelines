# U of T School of Graduate Studies — Guidance on the Appropriate Use of Generative AI for Graduate Academic Milestones (last updated Sept 9, 2026)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: University of Toronto **School of Graduate Studies (SGS)**; named contact **Vina Goghari, Vice Dean, Research & Program Innovation**. Per-FAQ "Last Updated" dates (July 4, 2023 → September 5, 2025 → page Sept 9, 2026) show three years of tracked revision.
- Self-described as "**preliminary guidance**" in FAQ form covering "comprehensive exams, candidacy requirements, language requirements, the doctoral thesis, and the final oral examination." Enforcement hooks: **Code of Behaviour on Academic Matters** (academic offence), **Policy on Ethical Conduct in Research** and **Framework to Address Allegations of Research Misconduct** (research misconduct).
- Devolution clause: SGS "will continue to work with faculties and graduate units" which "may therefore issue additional guidance" — central principles, unit-level rules.

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **prior-approval + full-transparency regime for graduate milestones**. Framing verbatim: "the use of generative AI tools to support the achievement of graduate academic milestones **must always take place with full transparency**" — between students/supervisors/graduate units ("who must **agree in advance** how any generative AI tools will be used") and toward audiences ("a clear and complete description and citation of any use").
- Universal-advice aphorism: "Whether the decision-maker is the student, the supervisor, the graduate program leader or the university leader, the advice is largely the same: **be guided by foundational principles, recognize every context may be unique, understand your own changing landscape, set shared expectations early, disclose what you are doing, and be prepared to uphold the standards** of high quality research and scholarly activities."
- Balanced-risk framing: "Innovative and creative uses of generative AI may support scholarly activities... However, it may also hinder the quality of research produced."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
**Overview:**
1. Scope clause: FAQ guidance for graduate milestones (list above); "More detailed guidance... may be issued in future."
2. Transparency clause (verbatim in (b)) — advance agreement + audience citation.
3. Approval clause: "It is recommended that students **seek and document in writing unambiguous approval from their supervisor(s) and supervisory committee in advance** of the use of generative AI tools."
4. Sanctions clause: "**Unauthorized use** of generative AI tools for scholarly work at the University of Toronto **may be considered an offence** under the Code of Behaviour on Academic Matters, **and research misconduct** as defined in the research-integrity policies.
5. Citation clause: "careful attention must be paid to appropriate citation and description of the use of generative AI tools... in line with disciplinary norms" — enumerated uses: "searching, designing, outlining, drafting, writing, editing or producing audio or visual content... and may include other uses."
6. Devolution clause: graduate units may add field-specific guidance; examples listed — "reviewing literature, writing text, conducting analytical work, reporting results (e.g., tables or figures), writing computer code, demonstrating language competence, or completing comprehensive exams and / or the final oral examination."
7. Definition footnote: generative AI = "tools that use predictive technology to produce new text, charts, images, audio, or video."
**FAQs (complete):**
8. **Responsibility**: "Graduate students who make use of AI tools and include the output in their research and written work are **ultimately responsible for the content**" — including publishing/preprints; T&Cs "often hold the user responsible"; exposure list: "allegations of perpetuating false or misleading information, infringement of intellectual property rights, violating the conditions of research ethics approval, other research misconduct, infringement of privacy rights, or other issues that carry **academic, civil, or criminal penalties**."
9. **Doctoral thesis**: thesis must be "the work of the student"; Key Criteria + Ontario OCAV Doctoral Degree Expectations; **originality analysis**: "These originality requirements **may not be met** by work produced using generative AI tools, which rely on existing sources to generate content-based probabilistic or other predictive functions that **may not result in sufficiently original content** to meet the criteria."
10. Prior-approval-for-thesis clause: use "in any aspect of researching or writing of the thesis **must be done with the prior approval of the supervisor(s) and supervisory committee**" — consistent with other thesis decisions.
11. Description standard: "In the same way that analytical tools and specific analytical approaches are identified and described in the thesis, **generative AI tools and interactions with them must be equivalently described**"; "It must be clear to the reader which generative AI tools were used, as well as how and why."
12. Contribution-segregation clause: approvals must make clear "how the student's versus the AI tool's contributions will be identified"; student must "provide sufficient evidence that they themselves have met the Key Criteria"; assessed "at each supervisory committee meeting."
13. **Oral-defense clause**: "Students must be able to **describe and defend any use of generative AI**... during their final oral examination."
14. **Research≠writing clause**: approval for research use does NOT extend to writing; "Graduate students must seek and document approval... for the use of generative AI in writing **even if they already have approval**... in their research"; "(un)authorized" specific tools "should be explained."
15. **Editing clause**: same principles for figures/images/graphs/sound/video; notes journals (e.g., **Nature**) apply more stringent criteria to images, "in some cases completely prohibiting such content."
16. **Master's level**: SGS "does not regulate master's-level theses" but recommends doctoral principles; the Code and research-misconduct framework still apply; units should issue specific guidance.
17. **Research tool use**: "AI is now integrated into many of the everyday tools we use for research and writing, including word processor software, search engines"; "it must be clear to faculty and students which uses of AI are acceptable, and which are not"; supervisors unsure → "seek clarification from their graduate unit."
18. **Citation norms**: units should clarify discipline-specific description/reference norms — "is it adequate to include the prompts provided to a tool along with excerpts of responses? Should students save or include the full text of their interactions with AI tools in an appendix?"; cites APA Style Blog; U of T Library citation pages.
19. **Writing-skill development**: "Learning the practices of disciplinary scholarly writing is a key aspect of graduate education. The use of generative AI **could hamper the development of graduate writing skills because writing capacity is highly dependent on practice**... Using AI to lessen the burdens of writing could undermine the development of these invaluable skills... Most writers learn about their own thinking through the **iterative act of writing**; if AI is doing some part of that writing, writers may be missing a crucial opportunity to cement their own scholarly expertise."
20. **Accuracy**: "Generative AI may produce content that is **wholly inaccurate or biased**. AI tools can **reproduce biases** that already exist in the content they are trained on, include **outdated information**, and can **present untrue statements as facts**... have also been shown to **reference scholarly works that do not exist**, and to **generate offensive content**." Also predictive-tools-may-not-produce-novelty clause.
21. **Security pointer**: defers to U of T Information Security ("Use artificial intelligence intelligently"; "Information risk assessment").
22. **Publication**: journal policies "vary widely"; some allow research-process use with description, prohibit AI text; others allow text not images. **Authorship clause**: "Emerging consensus is that generative AI tools **do not meet the criteria for authorship**... because these tools **cannot take responsibility or be held accountable**" — cites COPE and WAME.
23. **Copyright/IP pointer**: defers to U of T Libraries copyright resources.
24. Related policies/reading list (12 items incl. OCGS "Artificial Intelligence: Considerations for Graduate Research").

## (d) Sophistication markers
- The **originality-vs-prediction analysis** (FAQ 9/20) is philosophically the deepest clause in the corpus: it grounds the thesis-ownership rule in what probabilistic generation can and cannot produce ("may not... arrange existing knowledge in such a way as to reveal the need for the novel contribution").
- Writing-as-thinking pedagogy ("writers learn about their own thinking through the iterative act of writing") — cognition-level argument, not integrity boilerplate.
- Contribution-segregation + per-committee-meeting evidence + oral-defense defenseability = an **evidentiary chain** for attribution, not just a disclosure rule.
- Hallucinated-references and offensive-content specifics; authorship-consensus (COPE/WAME) and Nature image-policy awareness; civil/criminal penalty exposure named.
- Three-year per-FAQ versioning.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- Strictest graduate regime in the corpus: **prior written supervisor approval per milestone** (vs Karolinska's default-permit; vs Sydney's default-allow). Research-use approval explicitly does not extend to writing — a two-key system.
- The oral-defense clause makes the human-accountability check *examinable* — unique mechanism.
- Functions as U of T's graduate spoke under the "AI-ready University" hub; Temerty MD's index (separate note) contains no MD-level AI policy, so MD/PhD students hit this SGS layer first.

## (f) Provisional classification
**cautious-informed** — deeply reasoned, evidence-aware, procedurally demanding; precautionary default (approval-gated) rather than progressive default (permission-gated). The strongest "informed" document among university-level policies in this corpus.
