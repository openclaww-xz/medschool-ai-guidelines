# USC Keck School of Medicine — Medical Student Use of Generative AI Tools Policy

## Authority / Authorship
- Keck MD Student Portal policy page, nested under "MD Student Policies" / Handbook and Policies.
- "Reviewed and Approved: December 17, 2025. Medical Education Curriculum Committee (MECC)" — curriculum-committee-approved, dated.
- No named author.

## Policy Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "As generative AI tools become more accessible, the Keck School supports the responsible use of such technologies to enhance learning and patient care."
- "Understanding when to use, and when not to use these technologies in both the clinical and non-clinical settings is essential to safeguarding patient care and confidentiality, protecting academic integrity, and ensuring an optimal educational experience."
- Enforcement hook: "Inappropriate or unauthorized use of generative AI tools is considered a violation of the Keck Code of Professional Behavior."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory by Theme

### Coursework / assessment (prohibition-default across all phases)
- "The use of generative AI tools is prohibited for assignments, activities, and other required coursework that is intended to evaluate a student's own knowledge, reasoning, skills or reflections unless explicitly permitted by the course director or supervising faculty." (Applies to "Pre-Clerkship, Clerkship and Post-Clerkship.")

### Permitted-use conditions
- "When use of generative AI is permitted, it should be used as a tool, and not as a replacement for student reasoning or reflection."
- "Students are responsible for verifying the accuracy, bias, and appropriateness of all submitted content."
- "When generative AI is used, it must be appropriately cited in the student's work."

### Clinical settings / documentation
- "The use of generative AI in clinical settings must comply with all policies and procedures as outlined by the applicable health system and clinical setting."
- "Students cannot use generative AI tools for documentation of patient care, and it is never permissible to enter patient protected health information into a generative AI tool unless explicitly approved and sanctioned by the applicable health system and clinical setting."

### Curricular materials / IP
- "The uploading of curricular materials to AI must comply with all policies, guidelines, and procedures in place as outlined by USC and Keck School IT."
- "Students cannot use generative AI tools for the upload of curricular materials unless explicitly approved by the instructor. When allowable, it is the student's responsibility to ensure that the use of these tools does not violate copyright or intellectual property laws."

### Scholarly work
- "When submitting scholarly work for publication or presentation, students must comply with the generative AI policies set forth by the relevant journals and organizations and must disclose how generative AI was used in their scholarship."

## Sophistication Markers
- **Tech understanding**: moderate — no tool tiers, no named tools, no secure deployment; recognizes site-level clinical policy variance.
- **Assessment redesign**: none.
- **Evidence citations**: none.
- **Tool investment**: none named (delegates to USC/Keck IT).
- Note: the captured page's bulk is portal navigation chrome (calendars, offices, committees); the operative policy text is ~250 words.

## Distinctives
- Compact, clean, uniformly structured across pre-clerkship/clerkship/post-clerkship phases.
- Ties violations to the Code of Professional Behavior rather than a standalone academic-integrity mechanism.
- Blanket student prohibition on AI clinical documentation (no EHR-integration exception, unlike Stanford/UVA/Penn).
