# Deep Read — McGovern (UTHealth) Policy on AI Generative Tools

## Authority / Authorship
- **Adopted by the McGovern Center for Humanities and Ethics** for "its educational programs and courses" — a center-level policy, not a whole-school or institutional mandate. Parent stance: "UTHealth Houston and McGovern Medical School do not expressly prohibit the use of AI tools like ChatGPT and others, leaving faculty and departments to determine how it may or may not used in classes."
- Last updated October 21, 2025; subject to change for alignment with McGovern/UTHealth policies.
- **Lineage significance**: McGovern's 2023 policy is cited by LSUHSC-NO's references, and OHSU GME-48 credits "UT Health San Antonio GME AI Policy" — UT-system schools were early texan reference points (note: San Antonio, not Houston, for the OHSU lineage).

## Philosophy (verbatim framing quotes)
- Balanced framing: "Concerns over plagiarism, ethics, and academic integrity are plentiful; however, AI tools can be used in responsible ways... explore their benefits rather than solely focusing on the drawbacks."
- Humanities-specific protection: generative tools "should not replace students' own critical thinking and reflection, both of which are essential in the humanities and ethics."
- Technical note: "this tool (ChatGPT)... is currently using GPT-5"; training data "primarily through 2024"; "Real-time data and more current information (post-2021) are not yet part of the language model's training" (internally inconsistent date claims; the post-2021 line is stale 2023 text).

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory

### Background
- Tool enumeration: "ChatGPT is but one example of a GPT (Generative Pretraining Transformer) language model, but there are many others out there (e.g., Bing Chat, Copilot, ChatSonic, Claude, Dall-E 2, Firefly, Gemini, Jasper.ai, etc.)."
- Limitations: hallucination "with citations to non-existent references or write paragraphs that are partially or wholly incorrect"; "Offensive and violent language has been reported"; journal policies "to prohibit or limit these tools' use."

### Appropriate Use
- Institutional default: no express prohibition; faculty/department discretion.
- **Permitted examples for students (enumerated)**: "to clarify definitions of terms or differentiate between terms (e.g., methods vs. methodology, health humanities vs. medical humanities), to synthesize information about ethical principles, to summarize key points from a supplementary text not primarily assigned in your program or course, or to serve as an editor to review your writing style for grammar, spelling, and organization."
- Assignment-integrated use: "There may also be opportunities to explore AI tools as part of an individual or group assignment. These instances will be clearly described in course or program materials."
- **Pre-clearance for creative/writing work**: "If a student plans to use AI generative tools for assignments involving artwork or writing, they are advised to contact the program or course director in advance to obtain permission and avoid inappropriate use."
- **Citation format prescribed**: "Tool Name. (Year, Month Date of query). 'Text of query.' Generated using Tool Name. Link to tool name."
- **Reflective-writing ban**: "students should not use these tools to generate reflections for writing assignments (e.g., journals, reflective essays)."
- Responsibility: "students... are responsible for any inaccurate, biased, offensive, or otherwise unethical content they submit, regardless of whether they personally authored it or used an AI tool to generate it."
- **AI detection**: "Faculty will use AI detection software to identify instances of AI-generated writing in student work. This will be automatic when Turnitin (which has AI detection software) is used as part of a Canvas assignment. If a student is found to have used AI tools without proper citation, they may be subject to McGovern Medical School's code of conduct and UTHealth Houston's policies on academic dishonesty or misconduct."

### Ground Rules
- "If you use an AI tool like ChatGPT or others, cite it."
- "Do not use these tools to generate references or citations as they may return incorrect information, such as citations to works that do not exist."
- "do not use them for reflective writing assignments. GPT language models may produce well-written text, but this does not substitute for your own independent thinking and creativity."

### Exceptions
- Syllabus-defined deviations; **editing-tool carve-out**: "there are other generative tools that serve as spelling or grammar checkers, such as Grammarly or Microsoft Word, or review one's writing style, as with the Hemingway Editor. Even ChatGPT, with the proper query, can serve as a valuable tool to help with grammar, spelling, or organization. Students are free to use these apps to help with rephrasing sentences, rearranging paragraphs, grammar, etc. that are otherwise self-written."

## Sophistication Markers
- Prescribed citation format (query-level provenance: date, query text, tool, link) — most specific citation spec in the cohort.
- Reflective-writing prohibition grounded in humanities pedagogy.
- Explicit pro-detection stance (Turnitin/Canvas auto) — opposite of Utah's enterprise guidance; useful corpus contrast point.
- Editing-tool carve-out taxonomy (Grammarly/Hemingway/self-written text).

## Distinctives
- Center-level (humanities/ethics) rather than school-level scope; the only cohort-C document focused on reflective/professional-identity writing protection.
- Stale factual claims (GPT-5 vs "post-2021" training cutoff) reveal accretive editing without reconciliation.
- Historically influential: cited as a source by LSUHSC-NO.
