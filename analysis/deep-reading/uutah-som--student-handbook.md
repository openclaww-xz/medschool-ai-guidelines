# Deep Read — Utah SFESOM Student Handbook (AI clause + context)

## Authority / Authorship
- Official repository of medical student policies for SFESOM; maintained by Office of Student Affairs; "All medical students must be familiar with its contents." Multiple sections dated/updated through 1/26/2026.
- Governance entities named: Curriculum Committee, EPPC, Advancement Review Committee (ARC), Academic Appeals Committee (under University Policy 6-410), GMEC. The University-wide AI Use Guidelines (OAI/Chief AI Officer, Sept 2026) sits above it as institutional guidance; the handbook contains the SOM-level academic-rule integration.

## The AI clause (verbatim, complete — lines 563-565, under ACADEMIC PERFORMANCE STANDARDS)
> "We recognize that there are a variety of artificial intelligence (AI) tools available, such as ChatGPT. Unless, specifically outlined in the course syllabus these tools are not to be used for any part of the course, including but not limited to exams, essays, assignments, presentations, etc . If the student is unsure when to use, or not use, AI they are to directly speak with the course instructor for clarification. Students must cite sources properly and ensure that their work is original to avoid any potential plagiarism issues."

That is the entire AI-specific content of the handbook. Clause inventory:
1. Recognition clause: "there are a variety of artificial intelligence (AI) tools available, such as ChatGPT" — one named tool, generic.
2. **Default prohibition unless syllabus permits**: "Unless, specifically outlined in the course syllabus these tools are not to be used for any part of the course, including but not limited to exams, essays, assignments, presentations, etc." — opt-in model; burden on course to authorize.
3. Clarification duty on student: "If the student is unsure when to use, or not use, AI they are to directly speak with the course instructor for clarification."
4. Citation/originality clause: "Students must cite sources properly and ensure that their work is original to avoid any potential plagiarism issues."

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- The clause opens with acknowledgment ("We recognize that there are a variety of...tools available") rather than risk framing — but the operative rule is prohibition-by-default.
- No institutional philosophy beyond the clause itself; the handbook's surrounding philosophy is professionalism-based: "academic dishonesty is considered both academic misconduct and a violation of professional and ethical standards."

## Context clauses (relevant adjacency, verbatim key items)
- Academic misconduct: "In the Spencer Fox Eccles School of Medicine, academic dishonesty is considered both academic misconduct and a violation of professional and ethical standards... may result in the student's dismissal from medical school." Links to https://regulations.utah.edu/academics/6-400.php.
- Integrity professionalism behaviors: "Students will neither give nor receive aid in examinations or assignments unless such cooperation is expressly permitted by the instructor" (line 167) — pre-AI-era parallel opt-in rule the AI clause mirrors.
- "Students will uphold the highest standards of academic honesty"; research: "report results truthfully, and appropriately credit ideas developed and work done by others."
- Social media guidelines (adjacent technology-conduct policy): "Do not disclose confidential information"; "Do not post identifiable patient information online."
- Medical student documentation: Epic Student Notes, pending orders, attending responsibility for billable documentation — the clinical-documentation regime into which ambient AI scribes would someday insert (no AI mention here).
- ARC automatic actions and MSPE notations define the enforcement backdrop; violation of the AI clause would route through this machinery.

## Sophistication Markers
- Minimal. One named tool (ChatGPT), no GenAI/LLM taxonomy, no PHI clause, no disclosure/attribution mechanics, no detection stance, no approved-tools list, no clinical/AI-scribe guidance. The clause predates and ignores the university's own Sept 2026 OAI guidelines (which permit non-approved tools for personal study and bar detection-only misconduct findings).
- Internal tension: University guidelines say "Faculty retain authority to determine whether and how AI tools may be used" and treat detection skeptically, while the SOM handbook's default is prohibition absent syllabus authorization — reconcilable (syllabus is the vehicle of faculty authority) but unstated.

## Distinctives
- Stark two-tier reality within one institution: an enterprise-grade pioneer guideline (uutah--ai-use-guidelines-2026) coexists with a one-paragraph, prohibition-default SOM handbook clause. Useful corpus evidence that institutional IT/governance maturity does not automatically propagate to UME academic policy.
- Opt-in (course-authorization) model is the more restrictive variant among peer schools; most cohort-C schools default to "unless prohibited" or tiered disclosure.

## Provisional Classification: **Conservative-naive**
Justification: single generic clause, prohibition-by-default, one named tool, no definitions, no PHI/clinical dimension, no appeal to any framework; drafts clearly predate the 2026 institutional guidance. The enforcement context (ARC, dismissal) is robust, but the AI-specific content is boilerplate circa-2023.
