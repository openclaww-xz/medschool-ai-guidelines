# University of Amsterdam (UvA) — Policy framework and guidelines on GenAI in education (4 June 2025)

## (a) Authority and authorship
- Publisher: University of Amsterdam Executive Board; policy framework dated 4 June 2025, evolving from a June 2023 "AI in Education Policy Memo." Governing program: **project plan "Navigeren op de oceaan van kunstmatige intelligentie"** (Jan 2025), two phases; phase 1 (2025) = education, with four connected subprojects (policy, **reliable/independent GenAI infrastructure** (UvA AI Chat), **AI literacy programme**, pilots), aligned with the **VU-UvA Task Force on AI in education** recommendations.
- Legal anchors: **Netherlands Code of Conduct for Research Integrity** (honesty, scrupulousness, transparency, independence, responsibility); **EU AI Act**; **WHW (Dutch Higher Education and Research Act) Article 7.3**; UvA Assessment Policy Framework (2022) + Addendum "Focus on GenAI in assessment" (May 2025); model Fraud and Plagiarism Regulations.
- Architecture: central framework → **decentralised elaboration** at faculty/graduate school/programme level; representative bodies consulted. Explicit revision clause: "subject to change, given the rapid pace of developments."

## (b) Policy philosophy
- Posture: **structured, tool-gated adoption** — embrace opportunities but through institution-controlled infrastructure. Framing verbatim: "The UvA recognises and embraces the opportunities GenAI brings to education... but this does not mean that GenAI should become a guiding principle of our education. The decision on whether or not to deploy GenAI should always be made on the basis of **substantive and didactic considerations**. Where it is indeed deployed, GenAI should be used to **enrich education, as a tool rather than an end in itself**."
- Licence-status clause: "Given the challenges posed by GenAI and the fact that **the UvA does not currently have a licence for a GenAI tool, the UvA does not currently allow unlimited use of GenAI**. Controlled pilot projects... through the use of **UvA AI Chat**... aim to scale up this UvA-managed tool as soon as possible."
- Programme-level responsibility: "Whether, how, where, and in what way GenAI should play a role in a degree programme is therefore **not the responsibility of individual lecturers, but of the programme as a whole**."

## (c) Complete clause inventory
**§3 General guidelines (five, verbatim headings):**
1. **Human in the lead — final responsibility**: "All staff and students remain responsible for the work they submit at all times." Assessment clause: "In line with the EU AI Act, students' work must be assessed by an **examiner appointed by the Examinations Board and cannot under any circumstances be outsourced to a GenAI tool**." Lecturers "ultimately responsible for the content and quality of their teaching, also if they use GenAI to prepare."
2. **Transparency regarding use**: "students must be transparent about how they have used GenAI"; lecturers transparent in advance "how and why GenAI may be applied and how students must report the use of GenAI (for example **specifying the prompts used**)." Consequence clause: "Unauthorised use of GenAI or inadequate reporting... make it impossible for lecturers, examiners and the Examinations Board to assess the work."
3. **A critical attitude towards output**: "GenAI results are **not reliable scientific sources**"; named risks: "factual inaccuracies, non-existent references and hallucinations"; "violation of copyright and privacy, security and storage of personal, corporate and research data"; "the output often contains **stereotypes and bias**... one-sided or monotonous output"; AI-literacy campaign.
4. **Without a UvA licence, usage cannot be compulsory**: "As long as the UvA does not have a licence for a GenAI tool, **its use cannot be compulsory in education**... students and lecturers cannot be required to create their own personal accounts or purchase a tool"; "it must also be possible to **pass units of study without using GenAI tools**." Analogy footnote: unlicensed AI tools "are similar to platforms such as Facebook, Twitter and Instagram... tools for which you create a personal account and agree to the terms of use." Tool-selection factors: "**digital sovereignty**" — "ability to switch providers at any time, lock-in and switching costs, data integrity and confidentiality, interoperability, open source, sustainability and the promotion of alternative infrastructures."
5. **Do not share privacy-sensitive data (GDPR)**: public/commercial tools carry "data processing risks (copyright and privacy violations, security and storage of personal, corporate and research data)"; providers "can store everything entered and use that input for other purposes (to sell or to further train their models)."
**§4 Specific guidelines:**
1. **GenAI for personal support** — brainstorming, practice questions, spell check, "sparring partner"; where UvA AI Chat access exists, "students and staff are expected to use this tool for education-related support."
2. **GenAI as part of educational practice** — three roles: (a) **exit qualification** of a programme; (b) **subject of education** (learning objectives); (c) **didactic tool** (lesson plan). Key design question verbatim: "**which exit qualifications and/or learning objectives must students achieve without GenAI tools** and for which... is the use of GenAI as a tool permitted (also taking into account the competencies required in the labour market)? For example: should a student first learn the basics of programming without GenAI tools to be able to assess the quality of code generated by AI tools later in the programme?"
   - Constructive-alignment requirement (quoted from Educational Quality Policy Framework): learning-outcome approach, alignment of "learning outcomes, learning pathways, learning objectives, teaching methods and forms of assessment."
   - Accreditation clause: "if **more than 50% of the exit qualifications have changed** between two programme accreditations, the programme may be classed as 'new' and may need to undergo an **Initial Accreditation**"; "Quality assessments... increasingly focus on how programmes deal with the risks associated with AI and GenAI."
   - Worked examples (all three roles, complete): exit qualifications — "analyse the reliability, ethical implications and application value of GenAI technologies within a discipline"; responsible use of programming tools "and demonstrate an understanding of the implications for efficiency, code quality and maintainability." Learning objectives — "name both positive and negative effects of GenAI for journalism"; "aware of GenAI tools and language models relevant to their own discipline"; "able to weigh up when the use of GenAI may be useful or desirable." Didactic — chatbot "to practice debate and argumentation skills"; "generate practice questions"; "**practise anamnesis interviews**" (the medical-relevant example).
3. **GenAI and assessment quality assurance**:
   - Threat clause: "A number of common assessment methods, such as **essay writing and take-home examinations, are prone to the unauthorised use of GenAI**"; footnote: "Ghostwriters and other more costly opportunities to commit fraud have been around for some time... Generative AI, however, is **simple and cheap to use and therefore accessible to a wider audience**. This increases the temptation."
   - **Detection rejection** (verbatim): "**Fraud detection using AI detection tools is not reliable as of yet: the use of such tools produces false positives as well as false negatives and bias. This is one of the reasons why the UvA does not view AI detection tools as a solution** to the challenge of assuring the quality of assessments. Instead, the UvA aims to adapt assessment formats that are at high risk."
   - Programme-level portfolio rule: "**None of the exit qualifications can be assessed exclusively using GenAI-sensitive assessment formats**"; assessment plan with variety; "a take-home assignment is not the best way to assess basic programming skills, which should preferably be assessed in a more controlled setting."
   - Fraud definition extension: model Fraud and Plagiarism Regulations Article 1(1) ("any act or omission... which makes an accurate assessment of their knowledge, insight and skills partially or wholly impossible") applied: "The unauthorised use of GenAI in assessments, or any such use that does not take place or is not reported in accordance with the agreements... **renders the proper assessment... partially or wholly impossible. Such a case constitutes fraud**."
**Appendix 1 — Tasks and responsibilities (complete role matrix):** Executive Board (vision; licensing "taking into account digital sovereignty, sustainability and ethical considerations"; risk alerts; awareness programme; resources); Dean (encourage AI literacy, courses; adjust educational content to labour market; risk alerts; resources); Programme/education management (investigate adjustments with students/lecturers/professional field; implement; alert risks; ensure awareness); Examinations Boards (advice to dean/programme; assurance task; adapt model Fraud Regulations; **"Determine, facilitate and promote the required level of AI literacy within the teams of lecturers (particularly examiners and Examinations Board members)"**; risk evaluations of programmes; support legal duties); Lecturers (AI literacy incl. copyright/bias/inequality; "**only permitted to use GenAI during the teaching and assessment of a course following consultation with the programme management and if the GenAI tool is legally offered by the UvA**"; "**Do not use GenAI when evaluating formative or summative assessments**"; experiment within controlled pilots); Students ("**Only use GenAI for assignments or essays that will be assessed... if this is explicitly permitted (and offered) by the unit of study and within the degree programme**"; responsible for submitted work; AI literacy); Central TLC + faculty TLCs/assessment experts/Library (expertise; training modules for lecturers/examiners/Boards/programme directors; GenAI-assessment workshop; **GenAI University Teaching Qualification addendum**; support assessment adaptation).
**Appendix 2 — Draft step-by-step plan (5 steps):** Step 1 adapt educational content & curriculum (exit-qualification review with work-field advisory council; update Teaching and Examination Regulations; translate to learning trajectories; determine which objectives can/cannot be achieved with GenAI; constructive alignment). Step 2 AI as didactic tool (controlled pilots via faculty TLC with UvA AI Chat; evaluate pilots "not only in terms of student satisfaction, but also specifically in terms of **learning effects**"). Step 3 quality review and assurance (risk analysis of assessment formats; adapt formats; varied assessment programme "at different times and using different assessment formats"). Step 4 policy and communication (clear frameworks; Course Catalogue/syllabus disclosure). Step 5 evaluation and monitoring (continuously update faculty policy; long-term employability via AI-literacy integration).

## (d) Sophistication markers
- **Licence-gated compulsion rule** is corpus-unique and equity-precise: no requirement to buy accounts or use personal-data-exposed tools; every unit passable without GenAI — an access floor stronger than "equitable access" rhetoric.
- **Digital sovereignty procurement criteria** (switchability, lock-in, interoperability, open source) — the only procurement-level AI clause in the corpus.
- Detection-tool rejection on stated empirical grounds (false positives, false negatives, bias, citing the Springer IJEI testing literature) — the most explicit anti-detection stance among university policies.
- EU AI Act integration: "students' work... cannot under any circumstances be outsourced to a GenAI tool" for assessment; assessment authority constitutionally reserved to Examinations Board examiners.
- Accreditation-awareness (>50% exit-qualification change → Initial Accreditation) shows policy drafted inside the accreditation machinery; the fraud definition is reinterpreted rather than rewritten — minimal-amendment technique.
- Lecturers barred from using GenAI **when evaluating** formative/summative assessments — a symmetric rule (staff, not just students) unique in the corpus.
- WHW Article 7.3 constructive-alignment grounding; learning-effects (not satisfaction) pilot evaluation.

## (e) Distinctives vs sibling frameworks
- The most **administratively complete** university framework here: role matrix across six governance layers, step-by-step programme plan, and legal integration (WHW + EU AI Act + national integrity code) — contrast Sydney's single-mechanism elegance or Karolinska's template simplicity.
- Amsterdam system: this framework (governance) + UvA AI Chat (infrastructure) parallels Amsterdam UMC's EPIC/playground/consumer tiers — the same city's two institutions both practice **environment-tiering**.
- The anamnesis-interview chatbot example is the corpus's clearest medical-education use case inside a general university policy.

## (f) Provisional classification
**progressive-structured** — adoption-permissive in principle but infrastructure-gated in practice (no licence → no compulsion → controlled scale-up); legally anchored, role-complete, detection-skeptical, equity-floored. The EU-regulatory density model at institutional scale.
