# UVA School of Medicine — MD Program Policy on Generative AI

## Authority / Authorship
- UVA SOM MD Program policy page; "Developed by the UVA SOM Generative AI Advisory Team and reviewed and approved by the Medical Education Management Committee. Updated July 2026."
- Subordinate to university-wide guidance: "Where the two policies overlap, the University-wide guidance takes precedence" (in.virginia.edu/genai-useguidelines).

## Policy Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "UVA SOM supports medical students to use generative AI and large language models to augment learning with appropriate educational use defined by faculty."
- "We acknowledge that there are many potential benefits of AI software as well as inherent risks (e.g., bias, limited contextual understanding, plagiarism, ethics and academic integrity, inappropriate language, errors in referencing, etc.)."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory by Theme

### Tool taxonomy (two tiers — distinctive)
- "Tools that are provisioned by UVA or UVA Health. With institutional data agreements these tools offer 'safe harbor' for content. As of July 2026 – CoPilot, Google AI (including Gemini Notebook), Dax Co Pilot, and a small-scale pilot of Claude."
- "Content-Restricted External Tools. These typically require individual accounts and do not have institutional data agreements behind them. Some, such as OpenEvidence, Consensus, and UpToDate Expert AI, are designed for clinical queries and draw on the medical literature."
- Scope list includes consumer tools (ChatGPT, CoPilot, Gemini, Claude, DeepSeek, Perplexity, Elicit AI, Dall-E) and medical tools (OpenEvidence, UpToDate Expert AI, Consensus) — the most current, specific tool inventory in the batch, including clinical-AI ambient documentation tools (Dax) and a Claude pilot.

### Faculty-set disclosure thresholds (distinctive construct)
- "Faculty will set expectations for the use of AI in their specific assignments including how the use of generative AI should be disclosed and cited."
- "A distinction applies between AI-assisted and AI-generated work. AI-assisted work—where a student uses generative AI to support their own thinking (e.g., brainstorming, summarizing source material, or checking their own drafts)—generally carries a lower disclosure threshold than AI-generated work, in which the tool substantially produces the content submitted. Faculty should indicate, for a given assignment, whether and at what level of involvement disclosure is required; where faculty have not specified a threshold, students should err toward disclosure and describe how the tool was used."
- Links MLA Style Center guidance for citation.

### Scholarly work
- "When submitting scholarly work for publication or presentation, students must adhere to generative AI policies set forth by journals and organizations and disclose when and how these tools have been used."

### Accountability
- "Students are responsible for any inaccurate, biased, offensive, or otherwise unethical content they submit regardless of whether they personally authored it or used AI software to generate the content."

### Clinical documentation
- "Students may not create clinical documentation of any kind (e.g., H&Ps, progress notes, discharge summaries) within the authentic clinical environment using artificial intelligence applications outside of those supported by the EHR (e.g., Dax or Abridge)."

### PHI / de-identification (most nuanced in batch)
- "Students may not enter patient protected health information into any Content-Restricted External Tool, for any purpose. This includes not only clinical documentation but also clinical reasoning, question-asking, and image interpretation."
- "Simply removing a name is not sufficient; the question must be framed generically and carry no identifiers such as name, medical record number, dates, images, or location. Reliable de-identification is harder than it looks—metadata, rare diagnoses, and identifiable features can still identify a patient."

### IP / third-party content
- "The use of generative AI tools must be consistent with copyright, intellectual property rights, and relevant legislation (FERPA, HIPAA, etc.)."
- "Licensed third-party content (e.g., UWorld questions or Anki decks built from copyrighted textbooks) may not be entered into any generative AI tool." — names UWorld and Anki specifically; unique in corpus.

### Enforcement
- "If a student is found to have inappropriately utilized generative AI, the student will be subject to the UVA SOM Code of Conduct and greater University Honor Code."

### Worked examples of acceptable use (six scenarios — pedagogical instrument)
1. **Reviewing recorded course content**: Copilot on Panopto transcripts — "Because this content is under UVA copyright, the student must use a UVA-provisioned tool with contractual data protection—no data leaves the institution, and no prompts are used for AI model training."
2. **Study aids from UVA-owned content**: immunology slides into Gemini Notebook for Anki cards — "may only be entered into a UVA-provisioned tool."
3. **Differentials from classroom case**: ChatGPT on a fictional FCM case — "Because the case is simulated and contains no real patient information or proprietary UVA content, none of those content restrictions apply."
4. **Clinical question on rounds**: OpenEvidence/UpToDate Expert AI — student phrases question generically: "What is the most likely differential for a geriatric man presenting with acute unilateral eye pain and blurry vision?"
5. **Public/self-authored content with external tools**: ChatGPT quiz generation from public resources and personal summaries — permitted.
6. **Learning objectives into Mapify** for concept maps — permitted because objectives are general and non-proprietary.

### Governance
- "This Student Policy on Generative AI is subject to change to ensure alignment with UVA SOM, UVAHealth, and University of Virginia policies."
- Oversight: Medical Education Management Committee.

## Sophistication Markers
- **Tech understanding**: top-tier — two-tier tool taxonomy ("safe harbor" vs "Content-Restricted External Tools"); named current tools including ambient-DI vendors (Dax, Abridge) and a Claude pilot; understanding that prompt data flows determine copyright compliance ("no data leaves the institution, and no prompts are used for AI model training").
- **De-identification epistemics**: "Reliable de-identification is harder than it looks—metadata, rare diagnoses, and identifiable features can still identify a patient" — matches re-identification literature; extends PHI ban to reasoning/question-asking/image interpretation, not just documentation.
- **Assessment redesign**: partial — the AI-assisted vs AI-generated distinction with calibrated disclosure thresholds is a genuine conceptual instrument; no formal tier system.
- **Tool investment**: provisioned tier with data agreements (CoPilot, Google AI/Gemini Notebook, Dax CoPilot, Claude pilot).
- **IP specificity**: UWorld/Anki clause addresses the actual study-tool ecosystem students use.
- **Worked examples**: six concrete scenarios are a teaching device no other policy in this batch matches.

## Distinctives
- Example-driven policy design (scenario → rule application).
- AI-assisted/AI-generated disclosure-threshold distinction.
- Explicit July 2026 tool inventory with named pilots.
- Consumer+clinical tool naming (DeepSeek, Perplexity, Elicit, Mapify, Consensus) shows live awareness of the student tool landscape.

## Provisional Classification: **pioneer**
Justification: permissive-supportive framing ("supports medical students to use... to augment learning"), the cleanest tool-tier taxonomy, the most sophisticated de-identification and IP clauses, disclosure-threshold calibration, worked examples, and current named deployments including a Claude pilot. No assessment-classification scheme and university-guidance precedence slightly cap it, but the pedagogical-operational fusion is frontier.
