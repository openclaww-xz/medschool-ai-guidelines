# Deep Read — UVM Larner Policy 645.00 Use of AI in Medical Education

## Authority / Authorship
- Larner Medical Student Handbook policy 645.00; adopted/affirmed **7/15/2025 by the Medical Curriculum Committee**; Policy Oversight: **Associate Dean for Students**. Persons affected: medical students, faculty, staff, patients.
- Technical authority delegated to **COMTS** (COM Technology Services) and UVM ETS for system approval. Related LCME standards: "Not Applicable."

## Philosophy (verbatim framing quotes)
- "This policy aims to ensure that AI tools are used ethically, transparently, and in ways that enhance the educational experience while maintaining academic integrity and protecting individual rights."
- "Human-Centered Focus: AI tools should support and enhance human learning and decision-making, not replace these. Human judgment must remain central in all applications of AI."
- Evidence-based-learning principle: "AI tools for education should facilitate or utilize evidence-based learning methods."

## Complete Verbatim Clause Inventory

### Definitions (1)
- AI: "The use of machine learning models and/or algorithms trained or developed with pre-existing known and unknown sources of data to perform tasks with some degree of autonomy from human input."
- Traditional AI: "A subset of AI used to analyze and interpret existing data to improve efficiency, accuracy, and decision-making within predefined boundaries."
- Generative AI: "A subset of AI that utilizes models for the creation of novel text, image, or audio-based content using various known and unknown sources of training data as input."
- **Open System**: "a system where input data is accessible outside of UVM and provides no meaningful privacy protections."
- **Closed System**: "A model or data storage system where any input data is not used in training or made accessible to any non-authorized user or agent."
- **Closed Compliant System**: "A Closed System that complies with privacy laws and regulations including but not limited to FERPA and HIPPA [sic]."
- **UVM Instance**: "the system has been reviewed, approved and its use sanctioned by COMTS or UVM ETS."

### General Principles (2)
Human-Centered Focus; Equity and Ethics ("avoiding biases and discrimination"); Transparency and Accountability; Data Privacy and Security ("If FERPA or HIPPA data will be input, only use Closed Compliant System"); Continuous Learning and Adaptation; Collaboration and Stakeholder Engagement ("Diverse stakeholders, including educators, students, and the community, must be involved in AI policy development"); Responsible Use ("Users of AI systems are ultimately responsible for assuring the system's security, effectiveness, fairness, and accuracy"); Evidence-based Learning.

### Student use
- "Students must author their own work and cannot use generative AI tools to complete graded items (including but not limited to readiness assurance tests and summative exams) unless explicitly permitted by faculty."
- "Use of AI in graded activities without permission and/or citation is against the Code of Academic Integrity..."
- **Universal disclosure duty**: "In any assignment, students must disclose their use of generative AI tools in completion of assignments including reflections and case reports, specifying tools used."
- "Use of AI in developing self-study and self-assessment tools distinct from graded assessments is permitted."
- "Student is accountable for the accuracy and integrity of AI generated information that they use for assignments, self-study, presentation."

### Faculty use
- "Faculty must disclose their use of generative AI tools when developing educational materials and student assignments, fostering a culture of transparency and setting an example for students by openly sharing information about the data used for training, the challenges encountered, and the evaluation methods employed."
- **"Faculty must disclose if AI products are used for analysis of student performance (e.g. OSCE or note review and feedback). Faculty are ultimately responsible for ensuring the accuracy of the results generated."**

### Clinical training environments
- "Allowable AI use in clinical environments is primarily dictated by policies of the hosting facility."
- "Protected patient health information may only be uploaded into HIPPAA [sic] compliant applications that are supported by the hosting facility."
- **"AI-generated clinical documentation outside of supported EHR systems is prohibited."**
- "When used, patient-specific health information must always be completely and rigorously de-identified prior to use as input in a generative AI tool."

### Curricular materials
- "Faculty-created educational materials may be uploaded to closed systems OR to third party sites only with explicit approval by the faculty"; university-commissioned content "requires institutional approval prior to uploading/posting to third party sites"; third-party content per data use agreements.

### Assignments and Publication
- Faculty guidance governs assignment use; for publication: AI use "must be reviewed and approved by the faculty advisor, adhere to requirements for individual publication or venue and must be disclosed. Disclosure must include the specific AI tool and how it was used."

### Use of student/learner data
- "Data generated from student activities including... student performance evaluations, student assessments, course evaluations, narrative assignments, demographic information may only be entered into secure COMTS approved closed systems."

### Bias Mitigation / Human Oversight
- "Users of AI systems should regularly assess output for evidence of bias."
- "Decisions influenced by AI must be subject to human review. Human reviewers must have the authority to override AI-generated outcomes if necessary."

### Data Privacy
- "Patient, student, or curricular data may only be input to Closed Compliant Systems."
- "Unless the AI system is a pre-approved, UVM instance, the determination on whether a system is closed must be made by COMTS. Prior authorization is required before using AI."
- **Burden of verification on user**: "Users are solely responsible for verifying whether the version of the AI system is open or closed and must never enter NPPD (PHI, PII, and other data as defined in UVM's Privacy Policy) into an Open System."
- "Inputting NPPD into an open system is considered a breach of data security laws, such as HIPAA and the Vermont Data Breach Notification law, both of which require notification to government oversight agencies and to the individual."
- "Personal data must be collected minimally and securely deleted when no longer needed and in accordance with UVM's Records Management and Retention Policy."

### Best practices (3) — AI literacy curriculum
- Training encouraged before implementation: basic knowledge of how AI/LLMs work ("a model's output might look truthful but not necessarily be so"); concepts users should explain "in their own words": Model; Types of models (deep learning, generative AI, LLMs); **Agent/Instance**; Dataset; Training; Input/output; Supervised/Unsupervised Learning; **Model evaluation metrics (accuracy, benchmarks, AUC, etc.)**; Hallucination.
- "AI models are fallible tools and... each model will have specific biases, caveats and pitfalls."
- Science-based learning alignment with named techniques: "Retrieval practice (recall); Interleaving; Spaced practice (spaced repetition); Elaborative interrogation; Concrete examples."

## Sophistication Markers
- **Open/Closed/Closed-Compliant/UVM-Instance systems taxonomy** — the corpus's cleanest data-flow classification, tied to state breach law (Vermont Data Breach Notification).
- **AI-literacy syllabus embedded in policy** (model types, AUC, agents, hallucination) — no other policy defines evaluation metrics.
- Faculty dual disclosure: materials development AND performance-analysis uses (OSCE/note review).
- Learning-science canon (retrieval practice, spaced repetition) as AI-tool selection criterion.
- EHR-boundary rule for clinical documentation; de-identification precondition.
- Recurrent typo "HIPPAA/HIPPA" suggests lighter editorial review.

## Distinctives
- Small public school producing one of the most conceptually complete UME policies: systems taxonomy + literacy curriculum + evidence-based-learning test.
- Explicit community stakeholder involvement principle.
- Override-authority requirement for human reviewers.
