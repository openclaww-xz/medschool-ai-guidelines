# UW Medicine — COMP.308 Use of Artificial Intelligence (policy)

## Authority / authorship
- Formal compliance policy: **UW Medicine Compliance** instrument, signed by **Beth DeLair, Chief Compliance Officer, UW Medicine; Associate Vice President for Medical Affairs, UW** (8/26/2026).
- Governance bodies named in the policy: **AI Use Case Review Council**, **AI Education Review Workgroup**, **AI Operations Subcommittee**, UW IRB / Human Subjects Division, UW Medicine Information Security Program, contracting teams incl. Attorney General's Office.
- Full policy apparatus: applicability statement, superseded policies, dates, procedures, addendum references, approval signature block. The most procedurally complete document in this run.

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "The adoption of Artificial Intelligence (AI), including Generative AI (GenAI), presents an opportunity to accelerate UW Medicine's strategic, financial and operational goals. ... However, the use of AI poses unique characteristics associated with possible elevated risk."
- "Out of abundance of clarity, this policy applies to UW School of Medicine." (sic — unusual drafting phrase)
- "This policy will evolve over time in response to rapid changes in this innovative technology, the legal and regulatory landscape..."

## Complete verbatim clause inventory
**Purpose/scope:**
1. Risks enumerated: "Privacy and security of data; Accuracy and integrity of Algorithm & Model; Bias; and potential impacts on the delivery of medical care and the patient experience."
2. Policy describes: "1) general requirements regarding the use of AI at UW Medicine; 2) when a proposed use of AI requires internal review for assessment and mitigation of potential risks; 3) when a proposed use of AI may be undertaken without review; and 4) additional requirements for Learners."
3. Applicability: UW Medicine (Airlift Northwest, Harborview MC, UW Medical Center, UW Medicine Primary Care, UW Physicians, UW SoM) and UW Medicine Affiliated Covered Entity; WWAMI program in scope. "This policy does not apply to Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center."
4. "AI, as referenced within this policy, can refer to an AI System or AI within a tool, application or use case."

**A. General Requirements:**
5. "This policy does not replace existing UW Medicine policies and/or processes."
6. AI use must comply with UW/UW Medicine policies "including UW Medicine Compliance Patient Information Privacy policies and Information Security Risk Management Standard."
7. "The proposed AI use at UW Medicine must follow the established process for internal review if it meets the criteria set forth in Section B."
8. Applies to all actual/proposed AI uses for: "a. Clinical Care, b. Education, c. UW Medicine Business Operations ... d. Research as defined in Section C."
9. **Verification clause:** "The use of AI has the potential for inaccurate or hallucinated outputs. Users are responsible for reviewing outputs prior to use (e.g., cross-verifying information, complying with Appropriate Use Policies as applicable)."

**B. Uses requiring internal review — trigger:** any of the characteristics below AND not within the Approved AI Tool/Application List scope (list published on Vitals intranet).
10. "a. Its output is patient-facing without validation by an individual with the expertise necessary to detect an error (e.g., hallucinations, missing information)."
11. "b. A patient will be directly interacting with it."
12. "c. It is trained on, has access to or individuals could input identifiable clinical data (including PHI), including Limited Data Sets."
13. "d. It is trained on, has access to or individuals could input UW Medicine De-Identified Health Information that could be accessed by a third party ... or could leave the Secure UW Medicine Environment and has not been certified as de-identified through the Honest Broker process."
14. "e. It is trained on, has access to or individuals could input UW Medicine Data (e.g., sensitive data such as legal, financial, IP, PII, confidential/proprietary, trade secret, subject to contractual sharing limitations, academic or Learner data protected under FERPA)."
15. "f. It affects and/or automates Clinical Care (e.g., diagnostics or decision-making) with or without human validation of output."
16. "g. It affects and/or automates clinical coding/billing with or without human validation of output, and/or is designed to impact clinical coding/billing."
17. **h. Labor impact:** "It fundamentally changes the work functions of represented (unionized) staff or trainees."
18. **i. Financial threshold:** "It requires significant financial investment, exceeding $100,000 (either one-time cost or annually, including internal labor)."
19. "j. It affects the selection of candidates for academic appointments, admission, scholarships, awards, or other forms of recognition."
20. **k. Equity trigger:** "It presents equity and/or accessibility impacts (e.g., language access, translation accuracy, digital-divide considerations and other impacts as determined when reviewing with the support of the Equity Impact Review Tool)."
21. "l. It involves a medium or high level of organizational change management to be successful."

**B.2 Internal review structure:**
22. Review leveraged "through the UW Medicine IT intake process"; "If appropriate, more than one review may be required."
23. "a. AI Use Case Review Council Review. A review will be conducted by the AI Use Case Review Council to ensure relevant risks are identified and approval may be granted with appropriate risk mitigation. The review may include a request for consultations with identified subject matter experts across UW Medicine (e.g., clinical risk, compliance, legal, technical architecture, equity, or contracting representatives)."
24. "b. AI Education Review Workgroup. An additional review will be conducted for use cases involving Learners or the Learning Environment ... For Learners, additional authorization may be required; see Section E."
25. "c. AI Operations Subcommittee Review. A review may be triggered by certain characteristics associated with possible elevated risk and/or if appropriate controls cannot be implemented to mitigate risks during the AI Use Case Review Council review." (escalation tier)

**C. Research:** UW IRB reviews research by a UW PI when (1) uses AI, (2) requires IRB review, (3) uses UW Medicine Data or targets UW Medicine patients/staff/faculty/Learners. HSD "has expanded the definition of human research to include the use of data that is at high risk of re-identification" plus "a required UW Medicine security review."

**D. Uses NOT requiring internal review ("low risk"):** approved-list tools used within scope; 8 enumerated examples:
26. "1. Any tool listed on the UW Medicine Approved AI Tool/Applications List (see Vitals for list) and used within the appropriate scope and parameters."
27. "2. Using a public chatbot (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) to synthesize a published, publicly available scholarly paper concisely for personal review."
28. "3. Using a public chatbot (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) to develop content, so long as neither the prompt nor any other content contains UW Medicine Data ... and the generated content is carefully vetted for Accuracy."
29. "4. Using a public image generator (e.g., DALL-E, NanoBanana) to create an image for a Microsoft PowerPoint presentation."
30. "5. Using web-based Microsoft CoPilot to create a schedule or agenda for a meeting."
31. "6. Using an open-source AI data summarization tool (e.g., LangChain using open-weight models) to analyze publicly available data sets."
32. "7. Extracting and summarizing information from published articles, and other information available publicly on the internet."
33. "8. Studying, self-tutoring, practice, or personal reflection, excluding any use involving UW Medicine Data."
34. Caveat: low-risk uses "must adhere to other existing policies and processes ... and users are still responsible for verifying that outputs are accurate."

**E. Learner authorization:**
35. "Use of AI tools by Learners for certain activities requires authorization by the Learner's program, supervisor, or instructor, consistent with UW's Student Conduct Code" — even for Approved or "low risk" tools — for: "1. Coursework ...; 2. Assessments. This includes, but is not limited to, clinical documentation review, performance ratings, multisource, narrative, oral patient presentation, peer, portfolio-based, practical (lab), project-based, standardized, and simulated; 3. Exams ... and degree milestones ... (licensure, preliminary exams, general exams, theses, dissertation, capstones, and meta-projects); and/or 4. Clinical Learning Environment. Any use of AI in the Clinical Learning Environment as specified under this Policy."

**Procedures:**
36. Intake: "IT Project Intake" ('New Project Idea' form) or "IRB Intake" (Human Subjects Research Determination Worksheet; IRB application via Zipline).
37. Contracts: vendor agreements "can only be executed with appropriate UW or UW Medicine delegation of authority after the proposed AI use undergoes internal review and approval. Existing agreements may need to be amended if AI functionality is incorporated after initial execution."
38. Agreement review by contracting teams and SMEs "(e.g., Attorney General's Office) to identify risks and ensure contractual safeguards"; may include "an IT Security Terms with AI technology and accessibility requirement exhibits"; BAAs/DUAs/DPAs as required by data type.
39. Security: "the UW Medicine Information Security Program must review any AI use under this policy to assess the level of information security risk" with "an initial and periodic assessment of the security controls in place."
40. Referenced instruments: COMP.103/106 (PHI), SS-06, SS-12, HSD AI research guidance, Student Conduct Code, PCISA, Equity Impact Review Tool, Honest Broker Policy.
41. Signature: Beth DeLair, Chief Compliance Officer, 8/26/2026.

## Sophistication markers
- **Clinical deployment governance (approval pipeline):** the strongest in this run — a defined intake → risk-characteristic screen → multi-body review (Use Case Review Council + Education Workgroup + Operations Subcommittee escalation) → contract/security gates, with an approved-tool allowlist as the low-risk bypass. Risk triggers include patient-facing output, patient interaction, clinical automation, billing impact, union labor impact, >$100k spend, and equity impacts.
- **Monitoring:** partial — Information Security "initial and periodic assessment" of security controls; policy-level review cycle (next review 2029). No clinical-performance monitoring program named in this instrument.
- **Model-failure handling:** user-level duty ("Users are responsible for reviewing outputs prior to use"); trigger B.1.a explicitly keys on unvalidated patient-facing output. No incident/suspension mechanism in this document.
- **Ambient-scribe consent:** not addressed here (no scribe-specific clauses).
- **Clinician liability positioning:** implicit — responsibility devolved to the user; no indemnification or enterprise-assumption language; silent on documentation attestation.

## Distinctives
- Union/labor impact as an explicit review trigger (rare).
- Dollar threshold ($100k) as a governance trigger — unusually concrete.
- Equity Impact Review Tool and digital-divide language embedded in the risk screen.
- Honest Broker de-identification certification pathway.
- HSD expanded human-subjects definition to cover re-identification-risk data used in AI research.
- Approved-tool allowlist with named example vendors (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, DALL-E, NanoBanana, LangChain, Copilot) — pragmatic and current.

## Provisional classification
**Pioneer (governance-instrument tier).** A signed, dated, procedurally complete compliance policy with a working risk-tiered approval pipeline, defined review bodies, contract/security gates, and an allowlist. Its gap is post-deployment clinical monitoring (only security re-assessment is periodic), which peers like Duke (ABCDS) and UCSF (IMPACC) address in dedicated programs — but as a governance artifact it is the most rigorous in this run.
