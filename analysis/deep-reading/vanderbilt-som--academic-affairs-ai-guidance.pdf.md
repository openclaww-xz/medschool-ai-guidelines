# Vanderbilt — Academic Affairs Guidance for AI Tools (Nov 29, 2023)

## Authority / Authorship
- Vanderbilt University Academic Affairs; "Updated November 29, 2023; Effective January 2024." University-level, applies to SOM.

## Philosophy (verbatim framing)
- "Artificialial Intelligence is a valuable, dynamic, and vital tool."
- "You are the author of content that you produce with AI and responsible for its accuracy, impact, and compliance with relevant laws and policies."

## Complete clause inventory (POLICY STATEMENT + PRINCIPLES)
1. Abide by laws/policies; use AI ethically; follow dean/instructor/supervisor guidelines (incl. prohibitions).
2. "You should disclose the use of AI in an appropriate way."
3. Exercise sound judgment; respect confidentiality/privacy policies.
4. Applies to research, service work, creative expression.
5. Authorship/responsibility clause (quoted above).
6. Faculty decide whether/how GenAI is used in courses.
7. **Default-permissive rule**: "If a statement on the use of generative AI tools is not provided by an instructor, the use of such tools is permitted but must be disclosed."
8. Faculty must communicate what constitutes academic dishonesty.
9. Students responsible for knowing rules per course.
10. Faculty+students must disclose AI use in research/service/review/data/creative work.
11. AI-respect for confidentiality/privacy; ethical and reasonable use.

## Sophistication markers
- One of the few default-PERMIT rules in the entire guidance layer (absent instructor statement → permitted-with-disclosure).
- Authorship framing puts output responsibility on the human regardless of tool.

## Distinctives
- 2023-vintage yet structurally ahead: the disclose-by-default inversion most schools adopted only in 2025-26.

## Classification
- **progressive-structured (near pioneer for its date)** — short, but the permission default + universal authorship clause make it a model guidance text.
