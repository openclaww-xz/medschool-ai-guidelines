# JABSOM (John A. Burns School of Medicine, University of Hawai'i) — Guidelines on Generative AI for Learners (v1, May 2025)

**Classification: pioneer** (run-29 cohort C; `analysis/deep-reading/29-cohortC-synthesis.md`)

## Who drove it

The **JABSOM Education Technology Committee**, which both authored the document and disclosed its own drafting provenance: "OpenAI's ChatGPT was used in the information gathering, initial brainstorming, and early first draft outline... Subsequent revisions and additions were made manually by the JABSOM Education Technology Committee" (`raw/us-med-schools/jabsom--guidelines-on-generative-ai-for-learners.md`; `analysis/deep-reading/jabsom--guidelines-on-generative-ai-for-learners.md`). Misconduct routing names real bodies: UME violations to the **Evaluation Remediation and Review Committee (ERRC)** at course-director discretion; GME violations through program director/CCC as professionalism breaches escalating to the **Designated Institutional Official (DIO)**. The policy sits inside a dense compliance stack: UH system executive policies (EP2.210/214/215/217, EP7.205/208, EP12.214) and Hawai'i statutes (Ch. 84-10 ethics; Ch. 708-890 computer crime).

## Institutional context

A small public state school whose enabling conditions are statutory and cultural rather than financial: Hawai'i's legal environment supplies the compliance plumbing, and the university's identity frames the ethics — "Particularly given JABSOM's aspiration to uphold the University of Hawaii's identity as a Native Hawaiian place of learning, we recommend... values of aloha 'aina and malama honua by also considering the environmental impacts of our AI use." JABSOM also uniquely hosts UME and GME under one health-system umbrella (Hawai'i Residency Programs), making a unified learner policy structurally possible.

## What they built and why it is pioneer-grade

1. **The only unified UME+GME learner policy in the corpus**: "learner" = medical students, ʻImi Hoʻōla students, plus residents and fellows, with differentiated enforcement paths. Everywhere else GME and UME are separate instruments (run 29, §4.4).
2. **The richest modality taxonomy anywhere**: LLMs "and future direct derivatives (i.e., Large World Models)," text-to-image/video, "Artificially Generated Audio, including voice synthesis and deepfake technologies, such as Suno AI," AI virtual patients, "Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)," personalized learning systems, enterprise-embedded AI — a scope definition that will still be current when tool-specific policies are obsolete.
3. **Procedural-fairness jurisprudence on evidence**: faculty may use AI detection "not encouraged... not forbidden," only with UH-approved tools and FERPA-stripped PII; and on version-history logging — if logging wasn't required in advance, "it is unreasonable to require the learner to procure such logs retroactively" and "Decisions about whether inappropriate use occurred cannot be predicated on the existence or absence of such a log." This is due-process drafting most law schools would envy.
4. **Three worked ethics cases** (a good QI project on a secure platform; a HIPAA breach transcript; a med-ed study where name-redaction alone fails because of "indirectly identifiable, sensitive aspects of the interview participants' stories... through transcribed speech patterns alone") — case-based policy teaching matched only by UVA's scenarios.
5. **The only explicit MSPE linkage**: faculty may use GenAI to collate clerkship comments if FERPA-compliant and disclosed, "with a need for disclosure in subsequent documentation utilizing these summations, such as the MSPE" — governing the residency-application transcript itself.
6. **Consent axioms and deepfake protection**: "Consent is always needed before an AI model incorporates another person's personal information, data, visual likeness..."; falsifying representations of any real person via AI is a professionalism breach. Plus an anti-laundering IP clause ("discourages the 'laundering' of previously copyrighted material through its re-generation").

## What they explicitly chose NOT to do

- No detection-first enforcement — detection is permitted-but-discouraged with FERPA constraints and evidentiary limits.
- No prohibition default: Appendix A's eleven-item default rules permit grammar/organization/idea-generation and multimedia content in finals (with citation) while banning AI *textual* content in final submissions and all AI differentials/treatment plans — a clinical-reasoning firewall drawn by medium, not blanket ban.
- No tool whitelist — behavior-level rules with UH-approved-platform carve-outs.
- It declines to require students to self-surveil: logging is opt-in and non-retroactive by design.

## Borrowed vs original

The statutory surface (Hawai'i chapters, UH EP policies) is inherited, not borrowed from peers. Original: the modality taxonomy, the logging jurisprudence, the MSPE cascade, the consent/likeness axiom, the environmental section. Run 29's structural finding applies: state-school self-scrutiny (ChatGPT-drafting disclosure) is a phenomenon the elite privates do not show (`analysis/deep-reading/29-cohortC-synthesis.md`, pattern 5).

## Weaknesses / what's missing

- No named provisioned platform of its own (relies on "UH-approved platforms" generically).
- The Appendix A text-ban-in-finals rule is stricter than its philosophy suggests and partially contradicts the permissive disclosure culture — a coherence cost of medium-based lines.
- No faculty-wide tiering or grading governance (faculty duties are disclosure and modeling only).
- No equity-of-access provisions despite the values framing.

## What to steal concretely

1. **The non-retroactive-logging rule** — adopt verbatim into any honor-code AI procedure: evidence rules that don't punish students for the institution's failure to state logging requirements in advance.
2. **The modality-scope definition** (RAG, deepfakes, virtual patients, LWMs) as your policy's scope clause — it future-proofs the instrument.
3. **The MSPE disclosure cascade** — if AI summarizes clerkship comments, the downstream document must say so.
4. **The three worked cases** (good QI / HIPAA breach / indirect-identification) as training material — the indirect-identification case is the best privacy teaching artifact in the corpus.
5. **Consent-before-likeness axiom + deepfake professionalism clause** — increasingly urgent as voice/video synthesis goes consumer.
6. **Unified UME+GME learner scope** where a single system hosts both — one set of definitions, two enforcement paths.
