# The Pioneer Playbook — the common institutional recipe

One page, distilled from the 19 close-up profiles in this directory. Sources: `../deep-reading/27-schoolsA-synthesis.md`, `28-cohortB-synthesis.md`, `29-cohortC-synthesis.md`, `30-health-systems-synthesis.md`, `32-international-synthesis.md`, and the raw policies cited in each profile.

## 1. A named body owns it — and outlives the drafting moment

Every pioneer routes the policy through a standing governance structure with a name, not a dean's memo: UVA's Generative AI Advisory Team + MEMC; ECU's EAC with Office of Medical Education + Compliance + IT Security tri-ownership; OHSU's GMEC with AI Governance Committee amendments; UAMS's Chancellor's AI Governance Committee; Duke's Implementation Subcommittee + independent Review Committees; MSHS's four-committee lattice; Sydney's DVC/PVC leadership with an AI in Education Working Group. **Recipe:** standing committee + named chair/owner + document control (version, approval date, review date — ECU adds an expiration date and emergency-amendment path; UAMS commits to semi-annual review).

## 2. The sophistication center of gravity is NOT the elite privates

Run 29's structural finding, confirmed across all five syntheses: the most complete instruments come from small/public state schools (JABSOM, UVM, ECU, UAMS) and state systems (Utah, OHSU, Buffalo), while several elite names produced cautious or thin documents. Diffusion runs **state-school-to-state-school** (OHSU credits UT Health San Antonio; LSUHSC-NO cites McGovern/Feinberg/UCSF/UVA; MUSC is mirrored by Marshall), and borrowing is **credited** (Icahn→University of Wales; GW→AMEE Guide 192; UCSF PharmD→Monash/UNESCO; Sydney→TEQSA). **Recipe:** do not wait for a national framework; copy the credited sources directly and credit your own.

## 3. Classify the assignment or the data flow — never the tool

The three strongest architectures classify by things that don't churn: UCSF PharmD and ECU classify **assignments** (AI-1..4; Level 1–3 with mandatory per-assignment declaration); UVA and UVM classify **data flow** (contract-backed "safe harbor" vs restricted tools; Open/Closed/Closed-Compliant systems); UW and Duke classify **risk** (12-item trigger screen; full/fast-track/registration-only pathways). Tool-list policies are already incoherent across the corpus (the grammar-tool divergence). **Recipe:** tiers on every graded assignment + a data-flow taxonomy with a named approval authority.

## 4. Provision the compliant path — tooling is the strongest pioneer separator

Every top-ranked school names at least one provisioned platform (run 27 batch pattern): UVA (CoPilot/Gemini/Dax/Claude pilot), Stanford (Secure GPT + AI Playground), Sinai (Gemini + ChatGPT Edu + Minerva), U-M (GPT/Maizey), UCSF Health (Versa), Utah (ai.utah.edu inventory), Sydney (free Copilot + 1000+ Cogniti agents). Equity closes the loop: ECU's free-tool floor and non-AI alternatives; Sydney's free Copilot as "equity by license." **Recipe:** if you don't fund a compliant tool, your policy is a ban by poverty.

## 5. Enforcement realism beats prohibition theater

Stanford concedes bans "cannot be strictly enforced" and shifts the burden to competency demonstration; Sydney derives an entire system from "any unenforceable restriction damages assessment validity"; ECU replaces surveillance with oral defense; UNC GMB's rule is "if you cannot verify it as factual, you should delete it." Detection is uniformly rejected or constrained by pioneers — on evidence (Penn State, Sydney's Scarfe/Fleckenstein citations), on FERPA grounds (JABSOM, Penn), or as due-process risk (Utah's "not conclusive evidence"; UAMS's "inaccurate and unreliable"). **Recipe:** state the non-enforceability honestly, secure what must be secured (orals, in-person), and verify the human, not the text.

## 6. Disclosure must be safe, minimal, and modeled

Pioneers lower disclosure friction and raise disclosure safety: GW's no-punishment-for-disclosure guarantee and "document, don't confess" framing; ECU's three-element Methods Note ("Tool: Copilot; AI drafted slides; verified dosing vs ADA 2024"); UCSF's submission-as-attestation; UNC's usage table with conversation links; Stanford's five-element citation (tool, model/version, date, query, output excerpt). And the policies disclose their own AI use (GW's task force, JABSOM's committee, UNC GMB, Geisel, Penn) — the 2026 genre marker. **Recipe:** proportional disclosure instruments + immunity for disclosed permitted use + publish your own drafting provenance.

## 7. Govern the governors — faculty and grading AI are the frontier

ECU tiers faculty conduct and bans autonomous grading; UAMS requires governance-committee pre-approval for any grading-adjacent AI and bars instructors from uploading student work without written permission; Utah mandates pilot-validation and drift spot-checks for grading tools plus notice-and-appeal rights; UVM requires faculty disclosure when AI analyzes student performance (OSCE/note review); JABSOM cascades disclosure into the MSPE. **Recipe:** the strictest AI rules in your institution should bind the people assigning grades.

## 8. Clinical deployment needs a pipeline with a clock — and consent at the point of risk

Health-system pioneers converge on registry + risk tiers + independent review + post-deployment monitoring: Duke (checkpoints through "monitoring, and maintenance"), MSHS (quarterly × 24 months, use-by-use approval, suspension authority, decommissioning duty), UW (trigger screen incl. union-labor and $100k thresholds; equity tool), UCSF Health (named IMPACC monitoring program). At the point of clinical risk, UChicago's program-level Abridge policy sets the consent standard: per-encounter verbal consent from patient and all third parties, charted via smartphrase, charted declination, PGY-gated eligibility, liability equivalence. **Recipe:** registry everything; review on a published cadence; consent per encounter, documented in the EHR.

## 9. Borrow with attribution; be borrowable

The pioneers' lineage is traceable and credited — that is how the network functions. UCSF PharmD's taxonomy, ECU's tiers, Sydney's lanes, GW's AMEE-192 template, UChicago's consent package, UW's trigger list: each is a ready-to-import artifact. **Recipe:** credit your source in the policy's resources table; publish stealable instruments (templates, tables, syllabus language) rather than principles.

## The one-sentence recipe

A standing, named committee with document control; assignment/data-flow/risk classification instead of tool lists; a funded compliant platform with an equity floor; honest non-enforceability with assessment-based integrity; safe proportional disclosure; faculty-and-grading AI governed harder than student AI; clinical AI in a registry with a review clock and per-encounter consent — and credit your sources so the next school can steal from you.
