# UAMS — Academic Affairs Policy 2.1.6, AI Generative Tool Use

**Classification: pioneer** (run-29 cohort C; `analysis/deep-reading/29-cohortC-synthesis.md`)

## Who drove it

**Division of Academic Affairs policy, approved by the Council of Deans and Provost on 2/9/2024**, revised 11/7/2025, with a committed cadence: "This policy will be updated as needed, but at minimum semi-annually" (`raw/us-med-schools/uams--ai-generative-tool-use-policy-2.1.6.md`; `analysis/deep-reading/uams--ai-generative-tool-use-policy-2.1.6.md`). The central governance actor is the **Chancellor's AI Governance Committee** — it approves tools, receives 24-hour violation reports, and must pre-approve grading-adjacent AI uses. Supporting named offices: the Director of the Institute on Digital Health and Innovation (clinical CDS/pilots), the HIPAA Office, FERPA officer, and IRB. The policy is anchored in a real policy stack: 2.1.23 (PHI), 4.4.01 (Code of Conduct), 2.2.14 (Student Code), 16.1.04 (RCR), UA Board policies 210.1/210.2 (IP).

## Institutional context

UAMS is a whole academic health center (all colleges, all missions — education, clinical care, research, administration) governed as one enterprise, which is why a single policy can reach from student essays to clinical decision support. Arkansas's single-institution AHSC structure means the Chancellor's committee actually has authority over every domain the policy names. The 2024 original date with 2025 revision shows a two-year live maintenance record.

## What they built and why it is pioneer-grade

1. **The strongest enforcement plumbing in the corpus**: 24-hour incident reporting clock ("AI-related disclosures or violations of this Policy must be reported within 24 hours to the UAMS Chancellor's AI Governance Committee" plus HIPAA Office / Research Misconduct / Teaching and Learning Support / FERPA officer); **mandatory annual AI training for all faculty, staff, and students**; semi-annual policy review; unsafe tools "blocked on UAMS computers and networks."
2. **The most student-protective instructor-side regime anywhere**: "instructors may not input any student-identifiable information or student work into GAI, plagiarism checking tools, or AI-detection software without explicit written permission from the student unless approved for use by UAMS"; detection software declared "inaccurate and unreliable"; instructors are even "prohibited from asking Students for permission to input their work into GAI without express permission from the AI Governance Committee"; and **any grading-adjacent GAI use requires prior Committee approval**. This inverts the usual asymmetry: the institution polices faculty AI use harder than student use.
3. **Legal-economic depth no peer shows**: "the texts and images produced by GAI prompts are currently not protected by U.S. copyright law. This means that products created from the use of GAI are publicly available to anyone"; and indemnification allocation — "indemnification risk lies with the requester absent a vendor contractual defense."
4. **A six-mechanism bias taxonomy** including "they lack abductive reasoning to make judgments with incomplete information," plus a prompt-engineering duty ("make sure prompts do not unintentionally introduce bias").
5. **Research-provenance audit trail**: "Investigators must maintain a record of AI tool versions, prompts, outputs, and decision rationales in the research file"; consent forms must "clearly disclose... any use of generative AI tools when the outputs could potentially re-identify research participants"; external-GAI-on-study-data requires prior IRB approval.
6. **Clinical guardrails with an escape valve**: AI-CDS "cannot be used to automatically interpret clinical results, integrate findings, or conceptualize cases"; automation exceptions only with dual approval (Institute Director + Committee); outside-EHR PHI only with Committee approval "and only when a fully executed BAA exists."

## What they explicitly chose NOT to do

- No detection reliance (declared unreliable) — while still maintaining a Committee-approved tool list, the policy refuses detection as an integrity mechanism.
- No decentralized discretion: faculty cannot unilaterally authorize grading AI, student-work uploads, or clinical pilots — everything routes through the Committee.
- No blanket prohibition on student GAI use: coursework use "must be authorized by the course faculty," with helpful uses enumerated (analysis, rephrasing, synthesizing) and "it must be the trainee who guides, verifies, and crafts the final answers."
- Mechanical functions (grammar, translation, formatting, citation conversion) are **exempted from disclosure** by enumerated list — a deliberate noise-reduction choice.

## Borrowed vs original

The three-mode faculty labeling (prohibited/restricted/unrestricted) parallels UCSF-style tiering — but UAMS's instructor-labeling duty emerged independently in 2024 (pre-dating most tier adoptions) and differs in who holds authority (committee vs instructor). The copyright/indemnification analysis and the research-file provenance record are original in this corpus. Run 29 places UAMS in the state-school governance-committee signature pattern (`analysis/deep-reading/29-cohortC-synthesis.md`, cross-cutting findings).

## Weaknesses / what's missing

- **OCR-garbled source** (image-PDF recovery; "GAL/GAT/GAIT" artifacts) — administrative document-quality issue that complicates canonical citation.
- **Internal tension**: declares detection "inaccurate and unreliable" while maintaining "a list of approved AI detection tools" — an unresolved contradiction the deep-read flags.
- No assessment-redesign architecture (no tiers on assignments, no attestation instruments).
- Committee pre-approval for every grading-adjacent use may bottleneck: no service-level commitments are stated.
- No equity-of-access provisions; heavy on control, light on enablement (no named provisioned innovation platforms for students beyond approved tool lists).

## What to steal concretely

1. **FERPA-hard instructor firewall**: the exact clause banning instructor uploads of student work to GAI/detection tools without written student permission — the strongest learner-data protection clause in the corpus.
2. **Pre-approval gate for grading-adjacent AI** — one sentence ("any use of GAI by instructors to assist or review grading... requires prior approval by [governance body]") that prevents silent automated grading creep.
3. **The 24-hour incident reporting clock with named offices** — AI incidents routed like safety events.
4. **Research-file provenance record** (tool versions, prompts, outputs, decision rationales) — audit-trail language ready for any IRB application.
5. **Copyright + indemnification disclosure pair** — students and faculty should read both sentences before submitting GAI-assisted work publicly.
6. **Semi-annual review + mandatory annual training** as a funding-backed cadence, not an aspiration.
