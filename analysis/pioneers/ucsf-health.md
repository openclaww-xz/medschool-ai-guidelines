# UCSF Health — AI Governance, Oversight, and IMPACC Monitoring

**Classification: pioneer by inference (all three legs referenced; thin artifacts)** (run-30 health systems Tier 1; `analysis/deep-reading/30-health-systems-synthesis.md`)

## Who drove it

The **AI@UCSF enterprise program** (ai.ucsf.edu) publishes the governance portal; the **UCSF Health AI Oversight Committee** — "a cross-disciplinary approach to reviewing AI deployed within the health system" — is the named review body (`raw/health-systems/ucsf-health--ai-governance-guidance-policy.md`; `analysis/deep-reading/ucsf-health--ai-governance-guidance-policy.md`). On the school side, UCSF's Bridges curriculum policy names an "Associate Dean for Assessment, Improvement, and Accreditation **and Director of AI**" as accountable officer (CCEP-approved; `raw/us-med-schools/ucsf-som--bridges-curriculum-genai-usage-policy.md`) — a titled AI leadership role inside medical education. No single named author; governance is institutionally attributed.

## Institutional context

UCSF's position as an AI-building institution: the portal links **"Guidance for Developing and Publishing GPTs"** — UCSF builds and publishes its own GPT tools rather than only consuming vendors' — and **IRB guidance for ChatGPT/LLMs**, plus a single authorized cloud platform, **Versa**: "Versa is currently the only cloud Generative AI platform authorized for use with UCSF protected data. Versa, by design, does not save any interactions" (`raw/health-systems/ucsf-health--genai-considerations.md`). The PharmD assessment taxonomy (separate profile) shows the same institution's educational layer innovating independently — UCSF is a pioneer at three altitudes simultaneously (assessment policy, MD curriculum policy, health-system governance).

## What they built and why it is pioneer-grade

Run 30's three-leg test, all referenced:

1. **Governance**: a standing, named Health AI Oversight Committee with cross-disciplinary composition reviewing "AI deployed within the health system" — not a proposal-stage body but a deployed-portfolio reviewer.
2. **Monitoring**: **IMPACC** — a *named* post-deployment monitoring program ("IMPACC Monitoring" heading on the governance portal). Run 30: monitoring is "the rarest leg"; only Duke (lifecycle checkpoints), Mount Sinai (quarterly × 24 months), UCSF (IMPACC), and Ochsner (one fairness sentence) evidence anything beyond one-time approval. UCSF is one of three systems in the corpus with a named monitoring program.
3. **Tooling**: Versa as the single authorized protected-data platform with a no-interaction-retention design claim; GPT-building guidance for internally developed tools; IRB integration for LLM research use.

The companion "Considerations" page adds conceptual depth unusual for guidance material: automation bias named for users ("we users of AI systems should exercise enhanced caution"); the context-relativity of bias (the look-right-vs-look-left street-crossing example); hallucination epistemology ("AI doesn't produce facts, but rather generates outputs based on patterns"); escalation rules ("If the AI output is critical, have it reviewed by a domain expert"); even **model temperature** as a controllable Versa parameter; and environmental cost acknowledgment.

## What they explicitly chose NOT to do

- The Considerations page is enablement-plus-education, not enforcement — no consent/liability/monitoring clauses live there; UCSF separates awareness content from governance machinery rather than blending them.
- Single-platform discipline: rather than approving many tools, one authorized platform (Versa) for protected data — concentration over breadth.
- The portal does not publish the committee's criteria, decisions, or pipeline — no transparency into oversight outcomes.

## Borrowed vs original

IMPACC (implementation/monitoring program) is UCSF's original contribution — the monitoring institution the rest of the corpus lacks. The registry/committee pattern parallels Duke and MSHS (convergent). Versa's single-platform approach parallels Stanford's Secure GPT and Sinai's Gemini/ChatGPT Edu — the enterprise-platform wave. Run 32's import logic names monitoring cadence as the Singapore-template element; UCSF is the US health-system instance (`analysis/deep-reading/32-international-synthesis.md`, §2).

## Weaknesses / what's missing

- **Thin artifacts**: "the captured pages are thin, so classification is pioneer by inference (all three legs referenced, none elaborated in this corpus slice)" (run 30). The Oversight Committee's criteria, IMPACC's methodology, and enforcement paths are all behind links not captured — the corpus cannot verify the machinery's depth.
- No ambient-scribe consent rules visible at the system level (the UChicago standard remains unmatched here).
- No equity triggers or labor-impact screens (contrast UW).
- The Considerations page's excellent content carries no governance weight — education and enforcement are not connected on the public record.

## What to steal concretely

1. **Name your monitoring program** — "IMPACC" as a branded, referenced post-deployment surveillance function signals that monitoring is an institution, not an intention. Most systems with approval pipelines have nothing equivalent.
2. **Single authorized platform with no-retention design** for protected data — one platform, one contract, one message ("Versa... does not save any interactions"), instead of a confusing multi-tool allowlist.
3. **GPT-development guidance** — if your faculty build internal GPTs, publish the rules for building and publishing them; UCSF treats tool-building as a governed activity.
4. **The teaching-quality Considerations page** — the street-crossing bias example, the domain-expert escalation rule, temperature-as-a-parameter; steal the pedagogy even if you keep your own governance instrument.
5. **A Director of AI inside medical education** (per the Bridges policy's accountable officer) — a titled role that connects the educational and enterprise governance layers.
6. **The caveat lesson**: publish your committee's criteria and monitoring methods, or the outside world can only infer your quality — UCSF's pioneer status is demonstrable but unverifiable from public artifacts.
