# UNC School of Medicine — GMB Program Student Generative AI Usage Guidance

**Classification: pioneer (2023 vintage caveat)** (run-27 batch A rank #5; `analysis/deep-reading/27-schoolsA-synthesis.md`)

## Who drove it

A July 2023 guidance letter from the **GMB (Genetics and Molecular Biology) PhD program**, adopting "recommended language" from the **UNC Generative AI Committee** — "created by the Provost and Deans... with representatives from every academic unit" (`raw/us-med-schools/unc-som--gmb-student-genai-usage-guidance.md`; `analysis/deep-reading/unc-som--gmb-student-genai-usage-guidance.md`). The lineage matters: a university-level committee wrote the philosophy; a degree program operationalized it. The document also footnotes its own provenance with unusual honesty: "ChatGPT was used in the development of these guidelines... to generate suggestions for student use policies and to rephrase and consolidate certain sections," crediting Sentient Syllabus as a source of ideas. (UNC's later OMSE policy — 2026, PromptLab gateway, competency-tagged restrictions — continues the lineage but is classed progressive-structured; `analysis/deep-reading/unc-som--omse-artificial-intelligence-policy.md`.)

## Institutional context

2023-era: no institutional AI platforms existed yet, so the policy operates purely on norms and disclosure instruments — proving pioneer status does not require tooling. The enabling context was governance (a standing university committee with every-unit representation) and culture (a PhD program willing to write "you may submit material that contains AI-generated content... if this use is properly documented" a few months after ChatGPT shipped.

## What they built and why it is pioneer-grade

1. **The most permissive coursework default in the corpus**, derived from an explicit inevitability philosophy: "society's acceptance and use of generative AI is inevitable. Organizations will expect our students to have generative AI skills" — operationalized as "In principle, you may submit material that contains AI-generated content, or is based on or derived from it, if this use is properly documented."
2. **Eight assignment-type-specific rules** (writing/presentation, multimedia, math/stats/coding, readings/discussions, research, simulations, group work, in-class, exams) — a de facto classification scheme that anticipates UCSF-style tiering by two years, keyed to *task type* rather than tool.
3. **An attestation + usage-table disclosure instrument**: choose between "I attest that this project did not use AI at any stage" or a usage table with rows (topic selection, brainstorming, research, outlining, drafting, media creation, peer review, revising, polishing) and columns **Tool Used / How you edited the output / Conversation Link** — the conversation-link field is a verification mechanism no other policy includes.
4. **The bluntest accountability language anywhere in the corpus**: "You are 100% responsible for your final product... If the AI makes a mistake, and you use it, it's your mistake... If you cannot verify it as factual, you should delete it."
5. **A prescient anti-fabrication clause**: "it is advised that AI not be used to generate images resembling scientific data due to concerns over data fabrication."

## What they explicitly chose NOT to do

- No prohibition default for coursework; prohibition reserved for written/oral exams absent explicit permission.
- No detection regime whatsoever.
- No tool whitelist/blacklist — rules attach to behaviors ("do not enter an assignment question into ChatGPT and copy & paste the response"), not products.
- It does not pretend non-determinism away: "AI output is original and cannot be precisely recreated; therefore, it is advised that you save AI output" — adapting evidence norms to the technology's actual properties.

## Borrowed vs original

Borrowed: Sentient Syllabus (credited); the university committee's framework. Original: the usage-table-with-conversation-links instrument, the eight-type task taxonomy, the delete-if-unverifiable rule, the scientific-image clause. Its influence flows outward: UCSF PharmD also cites Sentient Syllabus (shared international source pool), and UNC's own later OMSE policy inherits the philosophy (`analysis/deep-reading/27-schoolsA-synthesis.md`, §5; `analysis/deep-reading/29-cohortC-synthesis.md`).

## Weaknesses / what's missing

- **No tooling** — era-appropriate but real: nothing provisioned, no secure platform guidance beyond "don't enter confidential data."
- **PhD scope**: GMB is a doctoral program; the MD program needed its own (later) instrument — the guidance does not address clinical environments, PHI in clinical learning, or documentation. The MD-side gap was eventually filled by UNC OMSE's 2026 policy (PromptLab gateway, competency-milestone-tagged restrictions; classed progressive-structured, near-pioneer — `analysis/deep-reading/unc-som--omse-artificial-intelligence-policy.md`), so UNC's overall arc runs GMB-2023 pioneer → OMSE-2026 near-pioneer.
- No faculty-side obligations and no equity provisions.
- The permissive default relies entirely on documentation goodwill; without secure assessment, unverifiable take-home work remains unverifiable (the gap Sydney's two-lane model exists to close).
- No maintenance regime: unlike ECU (36-month review, expiration) or UAMS (semi-annual), the 2023 guidance carries no stated review cycle — a vintage instrument still circulating.

## What to steal concretely

1. The **usage-table attestation with Conversation Link column** — a disclosure instrument that actually permits after-the-fact verification; pair it with the save-your-outputs advice.
2. **"If you cannot verify it as factual, you should delete it"** — adopt this exact accountability sentence; nothing else in the corpus is as clear.
3. **Task-type rules instead of tool lists** — eight assignment categories age better than any named-tool policy.
4. The **anti-fabrication scientific-image clause** — one sentence addressing a real research-integrity risk almost nobody covers.
5. **Policy-provenance footnoting** — disclosing ChatGPT's role in drafting the policy; cheap credibility, increasingly a genre marker of 2026 documents (`analysis/deep-reading/28-cohortB-synthesis.md`, cross-cohort observations).
