PURPOSE
The adoption of Artificial Intelligence (AI), including Generative AI (GenAI), presents an opportunity to
accelerate UW Medicine’s strategic, financial and operational goals. The use of this emerging technology
has the potential to improve quality and efficiency across UW Medicine including within the healthcare
system and the UW School of Medicine including WWAMI4. However, the use of AI poses unique
characteristics associated with possible elevated risk. Such risks include, but are not limited to: Privacy

POLICY

    A. General Requirements
          1. This policy does not replace existing UW Medicine policies and/or processes.
          2. The use of any AI must comply with all relevant University of Washington (UW) and UW
              Medicine policies, including UW Medicine Compliance Patient Information Privacy
              policies and Information Security Risk Management Standard.
          3. The proposed AI use at UW Medicine must follow the established process for internal
              review if it meets the criteria set forth in Section B.
          4. This policy applies to all actual or proposed AI uses for:
                     a. Clinical Care,
                     b. Education,
                     c. UW Medicine Business Operations (see glossary for definition), and
                     d. Research as defined in Section C.
          5. The use of AI has the potential for inaccurate or hallucinated outputs. Users are
              responsible for reviewing outputs prior to use (e.g., cross-verifying information,
              complying with Appropriate Use Policies as applicable).


    B. AI Uses for Clinical Care, Education, and UW Medicine Business Operations Requiring Internal
       Review. When a proposed AI use for Clinical Care, Education, or UW Medicine Business
       Operations includes any of the characteristics described in Section B.1. and is not within the
       appropriate use scope and parameters outlined on the UW Medicine Approved AI
       Tool/Application List (see Vitals for list), then the use is considered to have possible elevated risk
       and must undergo internal review as described in Section B.2.

            1. AI Characteristics Associated with Possible Elevated Risk:
                   a. Its output is patient-facing without validation by an individual with the expertise
                       necessary to detect an error (e.g., hallucinations, missing information).

       b. A patient will be directly interacting with it.
       c. It is trained on, has access to or individuals could input identifiable clinical data
          (including PHI), including Limited Data Sets.
       d. It is trained on, has access to or individuals could input UW Medicine De-
          Identified Health Information that could be accessed by a third party (e.g.,
          vendor or otherwise) or could leave the Secure UW Medicine Environment and
          has not been certified as de-identified through the Honest Broker process.
       e. It is trained on, has access to or individuals could input UW Medicine Data (e.g.,
          sensitive data such as legal, financial, IP, PII, confidential/proprietary, trade
          secret, subject to contractual sharing limitations, academic or Learner data
          protected under FERPA). (See glossary for UW Medicine Data definition)
       f. It affects and/or automates Clinical Care (e.g., diagnostics or decision-making)
          with or without human validation of output.
       g. It affects and/or automates clinical coding/billing with or without human
          validation of output, and/or is designed to impact clinical coding/billing.
       h. It fundamentally changes the work functions of represented (unionized) staff or
          trainees.
       i. It requires significant financial investment, exceeding $100,000 (either one-time
          cost or annually, including internal labor).
       j. It affects the selection of candidates for academic appointments, admission,
          scholarships, awards, or other forms of recognition.
       k. It presents equity and/or accessibility impacts (e.g., language access, translation
          accuracy, digital-divide considerations and other impacts as determined when
          reviewing with the support of the Equity Impact Review Tool).
       l. It involves a medium or high level of organizational change management to be
          successful.

2. Internal Review. Proposed AI use that has characteristics associated with possible
   elevated risk described in Section B.1. will be subject to review by leveraging the
   UW Medicine IT intake process for Clinical Care, Education, and UW Medicine
   Business Operations, as described further in Procedures below. If appropriate,
   more than one review may be required.
         a. AI Use Case Review Council Review. A review will be conducted by the AI Use
             Case Review Council to ensure relevant risks are identified and approval may
             be granted with appropriate risk mitigation. The review may include a request
             for consultations with identified subject matter experts across UW Medicine
             (e.g., clinical risk, compliance, legal, technical architecture, equity, or
             contracting representatives).
         b. AI Education Review Workgroup. An additional review will be conducted for
             use cases involving Learners or the Learning Environment to consider
             additional potential risks. The review may include a request for consultations
             with identified education subject matter experts. For Learners, additional
             authorization may be required; see Section E.
         c. AI Operations Subcommittee Review. A review may be triggered by certain
             characteristics associated with possible elevated risk and/or if appropriate
             controls cannot be implemented to mitigate risks during the AI Use Case
             Review Council review.

C. AI Uses Requiring Internal Review for Research. The UW IRB will conduct a review when
   research is conducted by a UW principal investigator (PI) that involves:
        1.   The use of AI, and
        2. Requires UW Institutional Review Board (IRB) review, and
        3. Either UW Medicine Data or targeted enrollment of UW Medicine patients, staff,
           faculty, or Learners.

    The UW Human Subjects Division (HSD) has expanded the definition of human research to
    include the use of data that is at high risk of re-identification and developed guidance for
    research involving AI including a required UW Medicine security review. These added
    components will help the UW IRB ensure relevant AI risks are identified and if appropriate
    controls are implemented to mitigate the risks. (See UW HSD guidance on School of Medicine
    Research using Artificial Intelligence for additional information)

D. AI Uses Not Requiring Internal Review. Notwithstanding the above, proposed AI uses that do not
   contain any of the characteristics associated with possible elevated risk described above in this
   policy are considered ‘low risk’ and do not require internal review. Use of previously approved
   tools used consistent with their acceptable use scope and parameters do not require additional
   internal review. These AI uses must adhere to other existing policies and processes (e.g.,
   Information Security Risk Management Standard and Privacy, Confidentiality, & Information
   Security Agreement (PCISA)) and users are still responsible for verifying that outputs are
   accurate. For Learners, additional authorization may be required; see Section E. Examples of AI
   uses not requiring internal review include:

        1. Any tool listed on the UW Medicine Approved AI Tool/Applications List (see Vitals for
           list) and used within the appropriate scope and parameters.
        2. Using a public chatbot (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) to synthesize a published,
           publicly available scholarly paper concisely for personal review.
        3. Using a public chatbot (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) to develop content, so long as
           neither the prompt nor any other content contains UW Medicine Data (see glossary for
           definition) and the generated content is carefully vetted for Accuracy.
        4. Using a public image generator (e.g., DALL-E, NanoBanana) to create an image for a
           Microsoft PowerPoint presentation.
        5. Using web-based Microsoft CoPilot to create a schedule or agenda for a meeting.
        6. Using an open-source AI data summarization tool (e.g., LangChain using open-weight
           models) to analyze publicly available data sets.
        7. Extracting and summarizing information from published articles, and other information
           available publicly on the internet.
        8. Studying, self-tutoring, practice, or personal reflection, excluding any use involving
           UW Medicine Data (see glossary for definition).

  E. Additional Authorization for Learners. Use of AI tools by Learners for certain activities requires
     authorization by the Learner’s program, supervisor, or instructor, consistent with UW’s
     Student Conduct Code. Use of AI for the following activities requires authorization, even if
     using UW Medicine Approved Tools or tools considered “low risk” under this Policy:
         1. Coursework. This includes, but is not limited to, class assignments, projects, quizzes, and
             presentations;
         2. Assessments. This includes, but is not limited to, clinical documentation
             review, performance ratings, multisource, narrative, oral patient presentation, peer,
             portfolio-based, practical (lab), project-based, standardized, and simulated;
         3. Exams. This includes, but is not limited to, course or rotation written, computer-based,
             clinical performance, or oral; and degree milestones and culminating experiences for
             bachelors, master’s and doctoral programs (licensure, preliminary exams, general
             exams, theses, dissertation, capstones, and meta-projects); and/or
         4. Clinical Learning Environment. Any use of AI in the Clinical Learning Environment as
             specified under this Policy (see glossary for definition).

PROCEDURES

  A. Internal Review. For AI uses requiring internal review, review is initiated through one of two
     pathways: IT Project Intake or IRB Intake.
         1. IT Project Intake. For Clinical Care, Education, and UW Medicine Business Operations
             requests, submit the “New Project Idea” request form.
         2. IRB Intake. To determine if a research study involving AI requires internal review by the
             UW IRB, complete the Human Subjects Research Determination Worksheet.
                   a. If the worksheet indicates UW IRB review is required, submit a UW IRB
                       Application through Zipline.

  B. Contract Requirements.
        1. For Clinical Care, Education, and UW Medicine Business Operations arrangements
            involving a third party/vendor, a written agreement can only be executed with
            appropriate UW or UW Medicine delegation of authority after the proposed AI use
            undergoes internal review and approval. Existing agreements may need to be amended
            if AI functionality is incorporated after initial execution. For research meeting the
            criteria as defined in Section C, contracting review and approval processes may occur
            concurrently with internal review.
        2. The written agreement must be reviewed by the appropriate contracting team (e.g., UW
            Procurement, UW Medicine Supply Chain, UW School of Medicine Regulatory and
            Business Affairs, UW Office of Sponsored Programs, etc.) and additional subject matter
            experts (e.g., Attorney General’s Office) to identify risks and ensure contractual
            safeguards are in place before using the proposed AI product.
        3. If appropriate, the agreement may include an IT Security Terms with AI technology and
            accessibility requirement exhibits.
        4. Depending on type of UW Medicine Data (e.g., PHI, PII), additional agreements (e.g.,
            Business Associate Agreement (BAA), Data Use Agreement (DUA), and/or Data
            Processing Agreement (DPA)) and approvals may be required.

    C. Security Assessments.
       As part of the IT Project Intake or IRB Intake processes referenced in Procedures Section 1A and
       Section 1B, the UW Medicine Information Security Program must review any AI use under this
       policy to assess the level of information security risk to the organization. As appropriate, the UW
       Medicine Information Security Program will conduct an initial and periodic assessment of the
       security controls in place.
