Rationale

This policy establishes a framework for the responsible, ethical, and educationally sound use of generative AI by learners, faculty, and staff.

Goals:

– Support safe, transparent, and equitable use of AI.

– Protect academic integrity, privacy, and professionalism.

– Maintain consistency in expectations across courses and assessments.

Scope

Applies to all educational activities at Brody School of Medicine, including pre-clinical, clinical, and administrative work where AI might be used.

– Covers written, multimedia, and clinical documentation.

– Includes faculty- and staff-generated teaching materials.

– Excludes research uses governed by separate IRB and research integrity policies.

Key Definitions

Generative AI:
Tools that produce new content (e.g., Copilot, ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude).

Predictive AI:
Tools that classify, analyze, or predict from existing data.

PHI:
Protected Health Information under HIPAA.

Near-PHI:
Information that could indirectly identify patients.

Institutional Tool:
AI systems reviewed and approved by ECU IT and Compliance.

Non-Approved Tool:
Any unapproved AI system; use prohibited for academic or clinical work.

Methods Note:
A short disclosure describing AI use in Level 2-tier assignments.

AI Use Tier

The policy classification for permitted AI engagement within a course or activity:

Level 1:
Open use encouraged

Level 2:

Conditional use with disclosure

Level 3:
Restricted or prohibited use

Responsibilities

Faculty / Course Directors / Staff:

– Declare the AI tier for each assignment or assessment.

– Review AI-generated materials for accuracy, bias, and professionalism.

– Avoid uploading PHI or identifiable data to non-approved tools.

– Ensure equitable access to at least one approved AI tool.

– Provide equivalent non-AI learning options when AI use is encouraged.

Students / Trainees:

– Follow the declared AI tier.

– Submit a brief Methods Note for Level 2 assignments.

– Never use AI on Red-tier assignments or in proctored assessments.

– Never enter PHI or identifiable peer/faculty content into unapproved tools.

– Be prepared to explain submitted work verbally.

Procedures for Implementation and Review

1. Guiding Principles

1. Transparency – AI use must be visible to faculty to ensure authentic learning and assessment.

2. Privacy and Safety – No PHI, near-PHI, or identifiable data in non-approved tools.

3. Proportionality – Disclosure should match assignment stakes.

4. Equity of Access – Students must have free access to one approved tool.

5. Skill Development, Not Replacement – AI should aid, not replace reasoning.

Rationale:
Protects fairness, safety, and the learning mission.

2. Tiered AI Use Framework

Level 1
– AI supports clarity and structure; grammar, outlines, formatting. No disclosure required.

Level 2
– AI contributes to content or summaries; background text, tables, slides. Requires Methods Note.

Level 3
– AI prohibited for high-stakes, confidential, or PHI-related work.

Rationale:
Ensures transparency, fairness, and accountability.

3. Assignment-Level Expectations

Every graded assignment must list its AI tier (Level 1, Level 2, or Level 3) in syllabi, LMS, or verbally.

Sample Syllabus & Assignment Language

Level 1 (AI Support Allowed):
“You may use generative AI for brainstorming, outlining, clarity/grammar improvement, formatting to a provided structure, translation for understanding, drafting routine professional emails, and personal study aids (your own summaries, flashcards, practice questions). If AI does not add new facts or citations and the final wording is yours, no disclosure is required.”

Level 2 (AI Collaboration Allowed With Disclosure):
“You may use generative AI to help draft or transform substantive content that appears in your submission (e.g., literature summaries, background paragraphs, tables/figures, slide bullets you keep). Include a brief Methods Note. If the AI added or changed factual content or citations, name one verification step; otherwise write ‘N/A — reformative use only.’ Be prepared to explain your reasoning.”

Level 3 (No AI Assistance Permitted)
:  “No generative AI tools may be used to create, draft, or refine your submission for this assignment. You may not consult AI during closed-book or proctored assessments. You may not submit AI-generated answers as your own. You may not enter PHI or identifiable peer/faculty content into any AI tool. Violations will be addressed under Academic Integrity and Professionalism policies.”

Faculty may request a “demonstration of understanding” for any AI-assisted assignment, which may include a process artifact (outline, notes, revisions, etc), or a short oral defense of understanding, comprehension, or reasoning.

4. Privacy, Security, and Professionalism

Do not input real patient identifiers or sensitive educational content into AI tools unless PHI-approved.

Do not impersonate others or fabricate data/citations using AI.

Uploading peer or internal materials to AI tools is a professionalism violation.

Rationale:
Protects confidentiality and institutional integrity.

5. Verification and Compliance

Verification required when AI adds or changes factual, quantitative, or clinical content.

Not required for grammar, tone, formatting, or ungraded personal study aids.

Violations should be reported to the Office of Medical Education or Office of Student Affairs, and will be addressed under existing Academic Integrity and Professionalism policies.

Of note, students should be aware of and comply with relevant AI policies if they are completing coursework, clerkships, or electives at outside institutions.

Methods Note for Level 2-tier work:

Tool used, what AI generated, one verification step.

Example: ‘Tool: Copilot; AI drafted slides; verified dosing vs ADA 2024.’

Violations follow existing Academic Integrity and Professionalism pathways.

6. Faculty Use of AI

Level 1 (Faculty):
Draft rubrics/checklists; polish slide grammar/formatting; reorganize content for accessibility; convert text to plain language.

Level 2 (Faculty):
Generate first‑pass teaching cases, slide bullets, practice or exam questions, or study guides; synthesize themes from non‑identifiable learner misconceptions.

Required checks before use (when applicable):

(1) Accuracy vs current guidelines/texts;

(2) Bias review (avoid stereotypes; inclusive language);

(3) Sensitivity review (avoid near‑PHI; remove identifiable or distressing details);

(4) Clarity rewrite in instructor’s voice. These checks apply when AI‑generated content conveys facts, numbers, or clinical reasoning used directly in instruction. If AI output is used only as scaffolding/formatting and is fully rewritten by the instructor, the checks may be abbreviated.

Disclosure to learners:

Add one line on slides/LMS: “AI‑assisted materials reviewed and finalized by [Instructor]. AI Tier for this course: [Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3].”

If Level 2, include a 2–3 line methods note with tool name, what AI generated, and one verification step when factual content is added.

Level 3 (Faculty): Uploading OSCE cases, exam items, internal faculty slides not public, or identifiable student work to non‑approved tools; releasing AI‑generated materials without human review; using AI‑generated content that reveals or mirrors assessment items.

Grading and Narrative Feedback

Permitted (Level 2):
Drafting narrative feedback (comment banks personalized by instructor), clustering themes, suggesting rubric‑aligned comments.

Required: Instructor reviews/edits/approves; ensure fairness; do not upload student work to non‑approved tools.

Prohibited (Level 3):
Autonomous grading; AI‑determined pass/fail; releasing feedback without human review; uploading grades or student artifacts to non‑approved tools.

Syllabus/Policy line:
“AI may assist instructors in drafting narrative feedback; final grades and comments are determined by the instructor after human review. Student work will not be uploaded to non‑approved tools.”

7. Approved Tools and Access

Microsoft Copilot meets the baseline access requirement for non-sensitive educational use.

Courses may restrict to specific approved tools for fairness.

Until PHI-approved tools exist, assume no PHI entry is permitted.

8. Governance, Communication, and Review

Owner:
Office of Medical Education, with Compliance and IT Security.

Review:
every 36 months, or sooner if platform or regulatory changes occur.

Emergency updates:
OME issues interim updates, notifies students and faculty within 5 business days, and logs changes on the policy page.

Implementation:

Require a syllabus line on AI policy for every course.
