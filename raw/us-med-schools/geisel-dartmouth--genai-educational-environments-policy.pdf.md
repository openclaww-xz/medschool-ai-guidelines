Policy for the Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence
            (GenAI) in Educational Environments

Summary of Purpose

         This new policy outlines the guidelines for the responsible, ethical, and privacy-conscious use of
         generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) in medical and health sciences educational
         environments, for both teaching and learning. The purposes of this new policy are 1) to support
         the development of course materials, including learning objectives, 2) to maintain academic
         integrity with assignments and assessments, and 3) to respect privacy and confidentiality.


Scope and Applicability

         This policy applies to all Geisel students and instructors1, across medical and health sciences
         degree programs, who use generative AI technologies in their educational activities. These
         activities include but are not limited to the development, delivery, and completion of coursework
         and clinical training.

         Geisel students and instructors are responsible for critically assessing the accuracy,
         authenticity, and quality of their materials and assignments and for understanding the limitations
         of AI tools which could potentially provide misinformation, inaccuracies, or fabrications.


Definitions

         Generative AI (GenAI): A subset of AI technologies that can create new content, such as text,
         images, audio, or data, based on patterns learned from existing information. This includes tools
         such as language models and image generators that can assist with learning and teaching.
         Specific examples include - but are not limited to - OpenAI, ChatGPT, chatbots, or any other
         Large Language Model or Generative Pre-Trained Transformer.




1
 In this policy, the term “instructors” refers to all faculty and/or non-faculty educators who are engaged in teaching

Policy Statement

    Generative AI technologies, such as language models and image generation tools, have the
    potential to enhance teaching and learning across Geisel’s medical and health sciences
    educational environments and programs. The objectives of this new policy are to:

    •   Promote the responsible and ethical use of generative AI in educational activities.
    •   Ensure that generative AI tools are used to enhance learning and not to replace critical
        thinking and clinical judgment.
    •   Maintain academic integrity and prevent misuse of generative AI technologies.
    •   Protect the intellectual property (IP) of all instructors.
    •   Safeguard the privacy of individuals and the confidentiality of personal, educational, and
        institutional data that may be accessed or processed by generative AI tools.


Procedure/Guidelines for Use
    Educational Enhancement, Curricular Development
    • Generative AI tools may be used by instructors to assist in the creation of educational
       content, to develop supplemental educational materials, and to provide personalized
       learning experiences.
    • Instructors are encouraged to take advantage of guidance, support, and training on the
       ethical use of generative AI in medical and health sciences education.
    • Instructors are encouraged to integrate generative AI tools into their delivery of the
       curriculum in a way that encourages critical thinking and enhances learning outcomes, to
       support but not replace critical thinking and clinical judgement.

    Academic Integrity
    • Instructors are expected to educate students on the ethical use of generative AI and the
       importance of maintaining academic integrity.
    • Unless explicitly stated by the instructor, students are not permitted to utilize generative AI
       for the completion of graded/assessed coursework, in the classroom or clinical settings.
    • If/when the use of generative AI is permitted by the instructor, students must disclose and
       appropriately cite the use of generative AI tools in all of their academic work. Specifically:
            • Students should be aware that courses differ in expectations regarding the use of AI.
            • If/when allowed by the instructor, students must disclose their use of AI as directed in
               a course syllabus or assignment instructions.
            • Just as they would when citing or referencing any other source, students may not
               use entire sentences or paragraphs suggested by an AI app without providing
               quotation marks and a citation.
            • Citation format: OpenAI, ChatGPT. Response to prompt: “Explain what is meant by
               the term ‘Triple Bottom Line’” (February 15, 2023, https://chat.openai.com/).
            • The use of generative AI to draft or complete assignments, exams, or other
               assessments without proper attribution is considered misrepresentation of scholarly

       Privacy and Confidentiality
       • GenAI-generated data must be managed with the same responsibility, security, and integrity
           as any other education, research, and patient data.
       • Unless explicitly allowed by the instructor, uploading Geisel course materials to local or
           cloud-based generative AI systems such as ChatGPT by medical students and health
           sciences students is forbidden.
       • Generative AI tools must be used in compliance with privacy laws and regulations (HIPPA
           and FERPA), including the protection of intellectual property (IP).
       • Users must ensure that any data input into generative AI tools is de-identified and does not
           contain sensitive or confidential information pertaining to patients, students or any member
           of the Geisel community.

       Monitoring and Compliance
       • Geisel has standing curriculum and academic progress committees that will monitor the use
         of generative AI and ensure compliance with this policy.
       • Violations of this policy will be addressed through the school's existing academic misconduct
         procedures.

       Review and Updates
       • This policy will be reviewed at least annually and updated as necessary to reflect rapid
          advancements in generative AI technologies, as well as any changes in legal and ethical
          standards.
       • This policy is considered in the context of a portfolio of relevant policies across the
          Dartmouth community, including but not limited to Dartmouth College, and aligns with other
          institutional policies referencing the use of generative AI.


Accreditation Standards 2025
For the MD program, Liaison Committee for Medical Education (LCME): No standards specific to
generative AI. Relevant related standards include:

Element 7.4, Critical Judgment/Problem-Solving Skills, states the following:
      “The faculty of a medical school ensure that the medical curriculum incorporates the
      fundamental principles of medicine, provides opportunities for medical students to acquire skills
      of critical judgment based on evidence and experience, and develops medical students' ability
      to use those principles and skills effectively in solving problems of health and disease.”

Element 7.7, Medical Ethics, states the following:
      “The faculty of a medical school ensure that the medical curriculum includes instruction for
      medical students in medical ethics and human values both prior to and during their participation
