Purpose and Expectations

As in all scholarly work, students are expected to present their learning and ideas as their own. Graduate studies provide the opportunity to deepen field-specific understanding and apply critical thinking and knowledge in academic and professional settings.

Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) can support learning, research, and productivity when used appropriately. To support the development of your critical thinking and learning skills, standards for using GenAI in your coursework have been established.

Students are responsible for understanding and following GenAI-use expectations as defined by their instructor and course materials.

What Is Generative AI?

Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI)
refers to technologies that can produce content, such as text, images, code, data, or multimedia, based on prompts or user input (e.g., HopGPT).

Until Johns Hopkins University establishes formal terminology, Engineering for Professionals (EP) uses
“Generative AI”
and
“GenAI”
as working terms. These terms are intended to broadly encompass any artificially generated or machine-produced content created through algorithmic, machine-learning, or automated systems.

Generative AI Use Categories

Your instructor may assign one of the following GenAI-use categories to a course or course components, based on learning objectives and assessment goals. An instructor may use instructional, procedural, or technical methods to support academic integrity.

Red:
GenAI use is not permitted

GenAI may not be used in any form.

Students must complete all work independently without GenAI assistance, including drafting, editing, or revision, unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Use of GenAI in this category constitutes a violation of academic integrity.

Yellow:
Moderate GenAI use is permitted,
with restrictions

GenAI may be used in limited ways to support learning, but not to replace original thinking.

All GenAI use must be disclosed according to course expectations.

Green:
Liberal GenAI use is permitted,
with expectations for transparency and accountability

GenAI may be used throughout the workflow.

Disclosure of GenAI use may be required, depending on course or assignment guidelines.

Students remain fully responsible for verifying accuracy, identifying bias, and ensuring the quality of final submissions.

Instructors may use proctoring methods.

Examples

Yellow: Moderate GenAI use is permitted,
with restrictions

Using GenAI to generate brainstorming ideas, but writing the final response independently.

Using GenAI to refine grammar or clarity after drafting original work.

Green: Liberal GenAI use is permitted,
with expectations for transparency and accountability

Using GenAI to support research, drafting, analysis, coding, or design tasks.

Using GenAI to draft initial content that is later revised and evaluated by the student.

Using GenAI to support data analysis or code debugging.

Disclosure of Generative AI Use

Your instructor will outline specific standards regarding the level of GenAI-use disclosure required. Disclosure may include, but is not limited to:

Full transcripts of chats or chat history

Citations of a specific model or website used for particular tasks

A short description of how GenAI was used

A single citation per assignment, citing each model or website used

Visual identification (for example, highlighting) of GenAI-generated content

Failure to disclose GenAI use when required may constitute academic misconduct.

Academic Integrity and Misuse

Using GenAI in ways that violate course policies, such as submitting AI-generated work as one’s own, failing to disclose required use, or using GenAI when prohibited, may be considered academic misconduct.

Please consult course syllabi, assignment instructions, and applicable
Johns Hopkins University, Whiting School of Engineering academic integrity policies
.

Best Practices for Students

If you are unsure of course standards, ask your instructor before using GenAI.

Treat GenAI as a learning partner, not an authority.

Verify all GenAI-generated content for accuracy, bias, and relevance.

Disclose or cite all GenAI-supported content as required by your course.

Save GenAI outputs or interaction records if disclosure or verification is requested.
