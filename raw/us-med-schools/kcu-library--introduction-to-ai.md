What is AI

Artificial intelligence
(AI) has existed for decades. The
official definition
from the
Oxford English Dictionary
(OED):

"The capacity of computers or other machines to exhibit or simulate intelligent behaviour."

Most conversation today focuses on
generative artificial intelligence
(genAI), defined by
OED
:

"Type of AI designed to produce output (text or images), previously thought to require human intelligence, typically by using machine learning to extrapolate from large collections of data."

GenAI tools rely on
Large Language Model
(LLM) technology:

"A complex algorithm that learns patterns from vast amounts of text to generate human-sounding responses to user prompts."

This guide focuses on
genAI and LLM tools
; it is up to the user to determine which is best for their purposes.

This guide also provides current resources on the constantly evolving discussions of
AI in medicine
.

KCU Policies and Notes

KCU does not have a university-wide policy about the use of AI tools in coursework and other written documents such as theses or dissertations. Each college has empowered instructors and advisors to set their own standards and communicate them to students.

AI Library Resources

KCU Libraries are always investigating and evaluating options to improve resources for the KCU community. We currently have the following AI tools available through our databases:

Scopus AI
: Scopus AI is a search tool powered by genAI that provides summaries of articles or sets of articles, produces concept maps, and allows you to sort search results by various categories, such as most influential.

Link to more information from Scopus on the assistant

Web of Science Research Assistant
: A generative Al-powered tool that provides summaries of articles or sets of articles, produces concept maps and other graphical depictions of search results, and provides prompts to guides your next steps in the search process.

Link to more information from Clarivate on the assistant
