NYU Grossman School of Medicine Institute for Innovations in Medical Education 
Guidelines for the Educational use of Generative Artificial Intelligence Tools 
Last revised 8/13/24 
 
These guidelines are designed to encourage safe use of AI tools by NYU Grossman School of Medicine medical 
students, residents, and fellows.  These guidelines complement the relevant official policies for medical students 
and residents.  If you have any questions or comments on these guidelines, please contact Dr. Marc Triola.  
 
Generative artificial intelligence applications can create convincingly authentic images, videos and text on 
demand.  Many products are available from Open AI (ChatGPT), Google (Gemini), Anthropic (Claude), Microsoft 
(Copilot), and others.   
 
Guidelines on using Generative AI for your education: 
• Unless explicitly instructed otherwise, AI tools should not be used on exams, quizzes, or other 
assessments.  Treat using AI like collaborating with a classmate. 
• AI tools can be used in other coursework under two circumstances: the instructor approves, and the use 
does not violate NYU Grossman School of Medicine's Honor code. If you do use AI in coursework, cite the 
specific tool used and ideally include the prompt. 
• Individual course directors are free to set their own policies regulating the use of generative AI tools in 
their courses/modules. Faculty should set such policies in their course syllabi.  
• Never put personal or confidential information in a public AI tool.  Sensitive information includes protected 
health information (PHI) of your patients, research data, course materials, lecture slides, assessment 
questions, intellectual property of NYU (or your own), or other information you don't want others to see 
such as manuscripts, grants, essays, etc. Be mindful that tools like Word/PowerPoint/Outlook, Google 
Docs, and grammar/spellcheckers (e.g., Grammarly) now have integrated AI.  When you use the AI 
features of those tools, you are using public AI. 
• Lectures and lecture materials are the intellectual property of the faculty and NYU Grossman School of 
Medicine.  You should not permit any AI application other than those provided by MCIT to record, or 
upload to non-MCIT AI, recordings or lecture materials from lectures, labs, seminars, or other non-public 
NYU Grossman School of Medicine educational activities. 
• If you want to use AI with personal or confidential information, use the HIPAA compliant ChatGPT provided 
by MCIT:  https://nyugpt.nyumc.org.  
• Some commercial content providers do not permit the use of any AI (public or private) with their material.  
This includes medical journals, textbooks, licensed material such as UpToDate, Amboss, and others. 
• You are accountable for the accuracy of any AI-generated information.  AI makes mistakes and can include 
fabricated information.  Always double check AI output. 
• You should not use any generative AI application in connection with or to generate your clinical 
documentation, such as patient notes included in the medical record.
