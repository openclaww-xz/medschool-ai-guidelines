Accessibility Considerations for AI Tools

AI tools vary in their level of accessibility, which can create barriers for some users. When an AI tool is used by more than one person (such as in a class, program, or administrative office) an accessibility review is required before use to comply with
Policy AD69
.

Request an accessibility review if:

The AI tool will be used by multiple users, and

The tool supports instructional, administrative, or shared work.

How to Request an Accessibility Review

To initiate a review, email
accessibility@psu.edu
and include:

The name of the AI tool

Intended use (for example, a course activity or office workflow)

Estimated number of users

If accessibility gaps are identified, an
Enterprise‑wide Equally Effective Alternative Access Plan (EEAAP)
will be required.
Pre‑Reviewed Tools and EEAAP Templates

The Accessibility Office has reviewed the tools listed below and provides EEAAP templates to support group use. You must still submit an EEAAP for your specific use case, even if the tool is listed.

ChatGPT (free version) EEAAP template

Standard Copilot (free version) EEAAP template

M365 Copilot (paid version) EEAAP template

Use of AI Software to Assist with Grading Student Work

Instructors need to secure approval prior to using
courseware
. Software being used to assist with grading is classified as courseware. Before submitting a request for an AI-assisted grading software, approval is needed (email is sufficient) from both the department head and dean, or appropriate academic leadership at commonwealth campuses.

Is the courseware already on the
Reviewed Courseware List
?

If yes, send an email to
courseware@psu.edu
identifying the courseware to be used along with the approvals from the department head and dean, or appropriate commonwealth campus leadership, confirming that the intended courseware use for grading student work is approved.

If no, fill out the online Software Request Form. Email
courseware@psu.edu
with a copy of the completed
Software Request Form
, along with the approvals from the department head and dean, or appropriate commonwealth campus leadership, confirming that the intended courseware use to assist with grading student work is approved.

Copyright and AI-Generated Content

AI-generated content—including text and images—does not automatically carry unrestricted usage rights. Ownership and licensing of AI outputs vary depending on the platform and its terms of service, and in many cases you may not hold a copyright over AI-generated work at all.

An additional concern is training data. Many AI tools are built on datasets that include copyrighted material, which can complicate the commercial or public use of their outputs.

Using AI-Generated Images at Penn State

When creating images intended for distribution use Adobe Firefly, Penn State's approved image generation tool. Unlike many publicly available tools, Adobe Firefly is trained exclusively on licensed and public domain content, meaning its outputs are cleared for commercial and institutional use.

Images generated by other AI tools may carry licensing ambiguities and should not be used in distributed or published University materials without first consulting your unit's communications or legal staff.
