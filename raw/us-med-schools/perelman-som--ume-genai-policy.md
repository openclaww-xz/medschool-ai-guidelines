Policy on Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence
I.   PURPOSE and BACKGROUND
     This policy establishes the appropriate use of artificial intelligence (AI) within Undergraduate
     Medical Education (UME) at PSOM. It aims to support innovation while preserving independent
     critical thinking, academic integrity, and data privacy.

     AI tools can enhance learning and efficiency but also pose risks related to accuracy, bias,
     overreliance, and inappropriate data use. AI tools are intended to support, not replace, the
     development of independent clinical reasoning, critical thinking, and professional judgment. This
     policy also provides guardrails to ensure responsible, ethical, and secure use.

II. SCOPE
     This policy applies to all Perelman School of Medicine (PSOM) students in UME and faculty in
     their instructional roles within UME courses and clinical training environments. Students enrolled
     in Biomedical Graduate Studies (BGS), Master’s Degree programs, and other distinct PSOM
     programs and courses should reference their program specific policies and consult with their
     course directors for additional guidance.

     This policy does not govern faculty, staff, or clinical operations outside the UME educational
     context. Those groups should refer to relevant PSOM, Penn Medicine, Graduate Medical
     Education (GME), and other applicable institutional AI policies.

     This policy is designed to align with and complement existing University and Health System
     policies.

III. POLICY and GUIDELINES

   The use of AI tools at PSOM must align with the University of Pennsylvania's Acceptable Use
   Policy for Electronic Resources.
   Definitions
    • “Artificial intelligence (AI)” refers to the ability and development of computer machines to
        perform tasks that previously would require human intelligence such as recognizing patterns,
        synthesizing data, problem solving, and adapting to new information.
    • “Generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) such as large language models (LLMs)” refers to
        any technology or application that can generate text, images, audio, videos, and other media
        based on a user-submitted prompt.
    • “Public AI tools” refers to any service not licensed/contracted by Penn/PSOM/UPHS, while
        “approved tools” refers to those services specifically listed on the PMACS Website .


   Core Principles
    • All users are responsible for the accuracy, integrity, and appropriate use of AI-generated
        content. Outputs should be verified using trusted sources, and use must comply with
        institutional policies related to academic integrity, privacy, and data security.

   Classroom and Coursework
    • Use of GenAI is not permitted in assignments, examinations, or other academic work unless
        explicitly authorized by course faculty.
    • When GenAI use is permitted, students must follow faculty instructions. Students remain
        responsible for the accuracy of all AI-assisted work.
    • PSOM curricular materials (including but not limited to slides, small group handouts, study
        guides, practice questions, course syllabi, etc.) may only be uploaded into PSOM approved
        AI tools.
    • Faculty, students, and staff are not permitted to input PSOM curricular material into publicly
        available GenAI applications without the advance written approval of content authors or
        PSOM leadership. These guidelines are in place to protect intellectual property and copyright
        laws and will be enforced as described in the “Violations of Conduct” section below.

   Clinical Learning Environment
    • AI use in clinical settings requires supervising faculty notification and approval and must
        align with site-specific policies. Unauthorized use of AI applications to create clinical
        documentation will be grounds for failing an assignment, course, or clerkship, and may
        initiate an academic review of professionalism.
    • AI tools may only be used for clinical documentation if they are approved and integrated
        within Penn Medicine and/or Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia (CHOP) clinical systems.
        Use of non-approved AI tools for clinical documentation is prohibited. Violations may result
        in failure of an assignment, course, or clerkship and may trigger an academic review of
        professionalism.
    • Use of AI tools such as Open Evidence for clinical decision support may be permitted in the
        clinical learning environment depending on the educational context, and only at the
        discretion of and with permission from individual course directors. Users of AI tools should
        verify all AI-generated content and remain responsible for accuracy.

 •   Students must follow AI policies at all clinical training sites. While most align with
     PSOM/Penn Medicine policies, some external sites, including but not limited to, the
     Philadelphia Veterans Affairs (VA) hospital and CHOP may have additional requirements. If
     available, please refer to individual site-specific AI policies for guidance on AI tools that are
     acceptable for direct patient care.

Scholarly Work
 • AI use should be cited, and users remain responsible for accuracy, integrity, and compliance
     with applicable policies and laws.
 • Students must not enter their own or others’ unpublished or experimental data, grant
     proposals, proprietary or copyright-protected information, or confidential material into public
     AI platforms without the expressed permission of their faculty supervisor.
 • Use of AI tools with sensitive research data including HIPAA and FERPA-protected
     information should be discussed with supervising faculty.
 • Students and faculty must follow AI policies required by journals, professional organizations,
     or conferences when producing scholarly work.
 • Students and faculty should disclose the use of AI tools in the development of manuscripts,
     presentations, posters, and abstracts per journal and meeting guidelines.

Data Security and Approved Tools
 • Users must follow Penn Data Risk Classifications and use only institutionally approved AI
     tools for any sensitive or protected data.
 • Currently approved PSOM AI Tools can be found at PMACS AI Tools, which provides a
     quick visual overview and additional guidance on:
          o Which AI tools are approved for use at PSOM
          o What types of data may and may not be used in each tool
          o Key reminders related to privacy, security, and compliance (HIPAA/FERPA)
 • Confidential, restricted, proprietary, or legally protected information (“Protected Data” ex:
     PHI, learner data protected under FERPA, internal PSOM documents, etc.) may not be
     entered into any public AI tools.
 • Use of public AI tools with nonconfidential, nonrestricted, and nonproprietary data is
     generally permissible, provided users comply with applicable intellectual property,
     attribution, and academic integrity standards.
          o AI tools must be used in a way that honors copyrights, patents, and trademarks.
             Avoid any unauthorized copying or sharing of protected materials.
 • Additional detailed data-use guidance is maintained at the following resources:
          o UPHS AI Tools
          o Information Systems and Computing Generative AI Tools and Resources

Disclosure and Accountability
 • Use of AI must be appropriately cited within academic work. Please see CETLI's Guidance
     on Citing AI Contributions for additional guidance.
 • Unauthorized use of AI within coursework, including failure to disclose use, may be
     considered a violation of academic integrity and may result in disciplinary action consistent
     with PSOM policies.
 • Users of AI must accept responsibility for generated content, including potential
     inaccuracies, misinformation, biases, or violations of privacy, professionalism, intellectual
     property, or codes of conduct, among other potential faults.

Faculty Responsibilities
 • Faculty are responsible for ensuring appropriate supervision and oversight of AI use in
     educational, research, and clinical contexts.
 • Faculty must clearly communicate expectations for AI use in their courses and clinical
     rotations (e.g., in syllabi, course websites, or verbal guidance). Course syllabi should include
     a statement of acceptable use of AI in the course, including a hyperlink to this policy. Please
     reference CETLI Guidance on GenAI Resources for additional guidance.
 • When AI use is permitted for coursework, faculty must clearly define acceptable and
     prohibited uses, and provide guidance on appropriate documentation, attribution, and
     validation of AI-assisted work.
 • Faculty should disclose to students when AI was used in the creation of course materials and
     assignments. Please see the Statement on Guidance for the University of Pennsylvania
     Community on Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence. Faculty retain responsibility for
     accuracy and alignment of AI generated content with course objectives.
 • PSOM acknowledges that access to AI tools may vary. Faculty should design assignments
     that do not disadvantage learners without access to premium tools.
     Use of AI detection software is generally discouraged as its accuracy is imperfect and
     typically insufficient as evidence that a student has used AI. Uploading student work to an AI
     detector may also violate FERPA.

Violations of Conduct
 • Students, faculty, and staff should report suspected misuse, data security and privacy
     concerns in the use of AI tools. Reports made in good faith are protected from retaliation.
 • Allegations of misconduct related to AI use may be reported through the following
     mechanisms. Consequences of misconduct will be presented to the Professionalism Sub-
     Committee of the Student Standards Committee (SSC), and outcomes will be determined on
     a case-by-case basis.
            o Reports may be submitted confidentially online through the Professionalism at
               PSOM Portal by anyone with a PennKey, including students, housestaff, faculty,
               staff, and administration. Alternatively, reports can also be made to the Academic
               Programs Office administrators or any of the Associate and Assistant Deans, and
               the Vice Dean for Undergraduate Medical Education (VD-UME).
            o Any course director who determines that there is an immediate professionalism
               concern will report the issue directly to the Associate Dean for Student Affairs and
               Wellness.
            o Individual evaluators in courses or clerkships may submit an evaluation which
               documents a professionalism issue or concerns which are reviewed by the Office
               of the Registrar. This will alert the appropriate departmental administrator.

Ongoing Review:
 • This policy will be reviewed at least annually, with interim updates as needed based on
    emerging technologies, regulatory changes, or institutional priorities and policies. Students
    and faculty are responsible for staying informed of any changes to this policy.
 • Feedback on this policy will inform ongoing revisions and may be submitted to the Medical
    Education Technology Committee (METC@pennmedicine.upenn.edu).
