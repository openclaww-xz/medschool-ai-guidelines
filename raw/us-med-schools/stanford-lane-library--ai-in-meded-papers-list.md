Gordon, Morris, et al. “A Scoping Review of Artificial Intelligence in Medical Education: BEME Guide No. 84.” Medical Teacher, vol. 46, no. 4, Apr. 2024

This scoping review reveals a growth in AI-related publications in medical education since 2018. The studies are mainly from Anglo-North American countries, focusing on undergraduate medical education. A smaller percentage addresses graduate medical education and continuing professional development. The publications are categorized as articles/innovations or perspectives. AI applications are found across six basic science disciplines and 24 clinical specialties. The review also identifies a geographic distribution of studies across different regions.

Boscardin, Christy K., et al. “ChatGPT and Generative Artificial Intelligence for Medical Education: Potential Impact and Opportunity.” Academic Medicine, vol. 99, no. 1, Jan. 2024

The paper discusses the integration of AI in medical education, highlighting its potential to transform various educational facets. Key findings include the importance of AI literacy and core competencies for healthcare professionals, such as understanding AI ethics, data security, and accurate evaluation. While AI can generate application essays in medical school admissions, it may lack personalization. In research, AI aids in literature reviews and paper outlines but must adhere to scientific standards. ChatGPT benefits interactive learning and communication skills practice, though limitations include outdated information and potential over-reliance, necessitating human oversight for accuracy and credibility.

Lee, Peter, et al. “Benefits, Limits, and Risks of GPT-4 as an AI Chatbot for Medicine.” New England Journal of Medicine, vol. 388, no. 13, Mar. 2023

The article discusses the integration of AI, specifically GPT-4, in medical education, highlighting its potential benefits and risks. Key findings suggest its applications in medical note-taking, exam problem-solving, and providing consultative advice. GPT-4's ability to summarize, analyze, and engage in medical research emphasizes the need for curriculum updates. Medical educators and faculty are encouraged to incorporate AI competency, ensuring students can use AI tools to enhance clinical decision-making and research capabilities.

Mousavi Baigi, Seyyedeh Fatemeh, et al. “Attitudes, Knowledge, and Skills towards Artificial Intelligence among Healthcare Students: A Systematic Review.” Health Science Reports, vol. 6, no. 3, Mar. 2023

The paper reviews healthcare students' attitudes, knowledge, and skills regarding artificial intelligence (AI) in medicine. The systematic review included 38 high-quality studies, revealing that while students generally have a positive outlook on AI's role in healthcare, their knowledge and skills are limited. This gap is primarily due to inadequate exposure and lack of training courses specific to AI in the medical curriculum. The findings suggest enhanced educational efforts, including face-to-face instruction and detailed training manuals, to help students understand and effectively utilize AI technologies in their future medical practice.

Civaner, M. Murat, et al. “Artificial Intelligence in Medical Education: A Cross-Sectional Needs Assessment.” BMC Medical Education, vol. 22, no. 1, Nov. 2022

The paper explores the transformative role of AI in medical education, stressing the need for AI literacy and core competencies among healthcare professionals. It addresses the integration of AI in four key areas: selection into medical programs, learning, assessment, and research. AI offers interactive learning experiences and aids in literature review and paper drafting. However, it has limitations, including outdated information and the risk of over-reliance, highlighting the necessity of human oversight. The importance of training educators and collaborating with non-clinicians for effective AI integration is also emphasized, alongside the need to develop specific learning outcomes tailored to AI education.

Teng, Minnie, et al. “Health Care Students’ Perspectives on Artificial Intelligence: Countrywide Survey in Canada.” JMIR Medical Education, vol. 8, no. 1, Jan. 2022

Healthcare students hold a positive attitude towards AI in medicine; however, their knowledge and skills in this area are lacking due to insufficient exposure and inadequate AI-specific training in the curriculum. The review suggests incorporating face-to-face instruction, detailed training manuals, and interdisciplinary courses in digital health to bridge the educational gap. This approach aims to enhance students' understanding and effective utilization of AI technologies in their future medical practice.

Karaca, Ozan, et al. “Medical Artificial Intelligence Readiness Scale for Medical Students (MAIRS-MS) – Development, Validity and Reliability Study.” BMC Medical Education, vol. 21, no. 1, Dec. 2021

Excerpt from abstract: In this study, we have described the development of a valid and reliable psychometric measurement tool for the assessment of the perceived readiness of medical students on AI technologies and its applications in medicine. The newly developed Medical Artificial Intelligence Readiness Scale for Medical Students (MAIRS-MS) was found to be a valid and reliable tool for the evaluation and monitoring of perceived readiness levels of medical students on AI technologies and applications.

Artificial Intelligence in Medical School Curriculum

Car, Josip, et al. “The Digital Health Competencies in Medical Education Framework: An International Consensus Statement Based on a Delphi Study.” JAMA Network Open, vol. 8, no. 1, Jan. 2025

The article emphasizes the need to integrate digital health competencies into medical education by focusing on foundational concepts over technical skills to balance curriculum practicality. Suggestions include incorporating discretionary learning outcomes through continuing education, vertically integrating content into existing curricula, and gradually introducing material. Utilizing an integrated curriculum approach is recommended. The framework distinguishes between mandatory, elective, and supplementary learning outcomes, aiding curriculum differentiation and definition.

Singla, Rohit, et al. “Developing a Canadian Artificial Intelligence Medical Curriculum Using a Delphi Study.” Npj Digital Medicine, vol. 7, no. 1, Nov. 2024

The article advocates for integrating AI education into medical school curricula, emphasizing enhancing medical competency. Key findings include identifying 82 essential AI competencies, suggesting methods for integration, and underscoring the importance of AI literacy and application skills. Recommendations include incorporating AI into biostatistics, case-based learning, clinical rotations, and involving guest lecturers. The approach adapts to existing frameworks and emphasizes hands-on sessions and AI ethics, preparing students for future healthcare settings.

Tolentino, Raymond, et al. “Curriculum Frameworks and Educational Programs in AI for Medical Students, Residents, and Practicing Physicians: Scoping Review.” JMIR Medical Education, vol. 10, July 2024

This article emphasizes the need for medical education to evolve with the integration of artificial intelligence (AI). Recommendations include developing general AI curricula for undergraduate medical education and specific curricula for postgraduate and continuing medical education. Institutions should collaborate with curriculum designers, data scientists, and medical educators to implement and evaluate AI educational programs. These steps will equip current and future physicians with the necessary knowledge, skills, and confidence to utilize AI in healthcare effectively.

Varma, Jonny R., et al. “The Global Use of Artificial Intelligence in the Undergraduate Medical Curriculum: A Systematic Review.” Cureus, May 2023.

The study evaluates the role of AI in undergraduate medical education, highlighting its potential to revolutionize learning and assessment. Despite the current inflexibility in teaching pre-clinical medicine, AI can enhance integration with clinical curricula through virtual patient cases and personalized learning. It stresses that AI should complement existing methods, not replace real patient interactions. The study calls for standardized frameworks and addresses ethical, financial, and performance considerations for AI implementation in medical curricula.

Ngo, Brandon, et al. “The Cases for and against Artificial Intelligence in the Medical School Curriculum.” Radiology: Artificial Intelligence, vol. 4, no. 5, Sept. 2022

The article emphasizes the need to incorporate AI into medical school curricula, highlighting its future impact on medicine. Key insights include the benefits of introducing AI at both preclinical and clinical levels, developing AI literacy, and understanding ethical implications. It suggests creating AI modules, electives, and integrating AI discussions in clinical rotations. By doing so, medical institutions can prepare students to critically assess AI applications, improve patient care, and effectively communicate AI-related information.

Çalışkan, S. Ayhan, et al. “Artificial Intelligence in Medical Education Curriculum: An e-Delphi Study for Competencies.” PLOS ONE, edited by Kamran Sattar, vol. 17, no. 7, July 2022

The study emphasizes defining AI competencies essential for medical graduates, advocating curriculum integration to address evolving healthcare technologies. Key findings suggest incorporating AI principles, ethical considerations, and effective health service applications into medical education. It encourages curriculum developers to create AI-focused content, revise existing courses, and involve in further research to ensure comprehensive training. This approach aims to prepare future physicians to adeptly utilize AI tools, enhancing healthcare delivery and decision-making processes.

Responsible Use of AI in Medical Education

“Principles for the Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence in and for Medical Education.” AAMC, Jan. 2025

The AAMC framework for integrating AI into medical education emphasizes maintaining a human-centered approach, ensuring ethical use, providing equitable access, fostering continuous education, and developing curricula through interdisciplinary collaboration. It highlights protecting data privacy and constant evaluation of AI tools. These principles guide medical educators, faculty, and school directors in redesigning curricula, modifying courses, and defining AI competencies to prepare students for responsible AI use in patient care.

Masters, Ken. “Ethical Use of Artificial Intelligence in Health Professions Education: AMEE Guide No. 158.” Medical Teacher, vol. 45, no. 6, June 2023

The article emphasizes the need for medical schools to develop comprehensive guidelines and policies for using AI in education, including lesson creation, teaching, assessment-taking, learning, grading, citing, and referencing. It highlights the importance of incorporating ethical considerations and the responsible use of AI tools by students and faculty. The key takeaway is to create a curriculum that integrates AI literacy and competency while addressing ethical issues and preparing students for the evolving medical landscape.

Academic Integrity/ Assessment

Fried, Juliana Coraor, et al. “Using Generative Artificial Intelligence When Writing Letters of Recommendation.” Academic Medicine, Mar. 2025

Generative AI (genAI) offers faculty in academic medicine a practical way to streamline the time-intensive task of writing letters of recommendation (LORs). This article introduces an ethical, structured framework for GenAI to draft personalized, bias-aware LORs. It explores selecting appropriate genAI models, creating effective prompts, and safeguarding privacy while maintaining transparency. Faculty can enhance LOR quality by moving away from repetitive shortcuts like copying from CVs. The authors emphasize responsible use and call for future research to develop AI tools tailored for LOR writing—supporting faculty in a time of pass/fail grading and virtual interviews.

Elkhatat, Ahmed M., et al. “Evaluating the Efficacy of AI Content Detection Tools in Differentiating between Human and AI-Generated Text.” International Journal for Educational Integrity, vol. 19, no. 1, Sept. 2023

The article emphasizes the inconsistencies in AI content detection tools, suggesting a more holistic approach involving manual review for academic integrity. It advocates leveraging AI to enhance learning and assessment, including personalized feedback and complex tasks. Medical educators should integrate AI-enhanced methods, regularly evaluate AI tools, and foster academic honesty. Future research should focus on improving AI detectors' accuracy and adapting to evolving technologies. This approach supports robust AI competency in medical curricula.

“Detecting AI Content in Responses Generated by ChatGPT, YouChat, and Chatsonic: The Case of Five AI Content Detection Tools.” Journal of Applied Learning & Teaching, vol. 6, no. 2, July 2023

The article explores the limitations of current AI content detectors in accurately distinguishing between AI-generated and human-written texts, highlighting their reliance on probabilistic estimates and mechanical differentiators in premium versions. Medical educators are urged to improve AI detection through advanced machine learning algorithms. These findings can guide curriculum redesign to include courses on AI literacy, ethical considerations, and technological proficiency, ensuring that MD students are competent in navigating AI applications in medical practice.

Policy Document/ AI Policy/ Guidelines

Canada's Drug Agency. “2025 Watch List: Artificial Intelligence in Health Care.” Canadian Journal of Health Technologies, vol. 5, no. 3, Mar. 2025

The article emphasizes integrating artificial intelligence (AI) into medical education. Key recommendations include introducing AI training to enhance clinical practice, fostering collaboration with medical schools to promote AI through MD and PhD programs, and developing new disciplines in clinical informatics. It suggests using AI tools like Virtual Simulated Patients (AI-VSP) for clinical teaching to improve decision-making abilities. To effectively implement AI, educators must equip health care professionals (HCPs) with technical skills and critical thinking abilities related to AI. This approach aims to improve training efficiency, healthcare resource allocation, and the overall quality of care.

“FUTURE-AI: International Consensus Guideline for Trustworthy and Deployable Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare.” BMJ, Feb. 2025

The article emphasizes the integration of AI in medical education, recommending the development of AI competency for MD students. Key findings include embedding AI ethics, governance, and human-AI oversight in curricula, training students to use AI tools effectively, and ensuring data diversity. It advocates for hands-on sessions and periodic audits to refine AI applications, underscoring the importance of aligning AI competencies with clinical deployment to ensure patient safety and healthcare improvement.

Joint Research Centre (European Commission). Interrogating the research and development pipeline of artificial intelligence (AI) in health Diagnosis and prediction-based diagnosis. 2025

The article highlights the rapid advancement of AI in healthcare, emphasizing the need for medical educators to understand the challenges of safe and transparent AI implementation. Key findings suggest incorporating AI in medical curricula, improving patient care, addressing biases, ensuring data privacy, and integrating AI with existing systems. Educators should define AI competencies, including understanding AI applications in various medical fields, such as oncology and cardiology, and the importance of explainable AI. Collaboration between regions like China, the EU, and the USA indicates global research priorities that can enhance curricula.

Van Kolfschooten, Hannah, and Janneke Van Oirschot. “The Eu Artificial Intelligence Act (2024): Implications for Healthcare.” Health Policy, vol. 149, Nov. 2024

Excerpt from abstract: In August 2024, the EU Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act) entered into force. This legally binding instrument sets rules for the development, the placing on the market, the putting into service, and the use of AI systems in the European Union. As the world's first extensive legal framework on AI, it aims to boost innovation while protecting individuals against the harms of AI. Since healthcare is one of the top sectors for AI deployment, the new rules will significantly reform national policies and practices on health technology. In this article, we highlight the implications of the AI Act for the healthcare sector. We give a comprehensive overview of the new legal obligations for various healthcare stakeholders (tech developers; healthcare professionals; public health authorities). We conclude that, due to its horizontal approach, it is necessary to adopt further guidelines to address the unique needs of the healthcare sector. To this end, we make recommendations for the upcoming implementation and standardization phase.

European Parliament. Directorate General for Parliamentary Research Services. Artificial Intelligence in Healthcare: Applications, Risks, and Ethical and Societal Impacts. Publications Office, 2022

The report aims to assess the role of artificial intelligence (AI) in medicine and healthcare, highlighting its potential benefits and significant risks. Key applications include improving clinical practice, biomedical research, public health, and health administration. However, AI errors, biases, lack of transparency, privacy concerns, and accountability gaps pose serious risks. The report proposes methods to mitigate these concerns, including tailored regulatory frameworks, multi-stakeholder engagement, enhanced transparency through an 'AI passport,' and educational programs to improve AI literacy among healthcare professionals and the public. Ongoing research is also essential to address current limitations and reduce disparities across European countries in implementing medical AI.
