PURPOSE

This policy aids in clarifying the use and misuse of artificial intelligence generative tools at
UAMS. This policy applies across all UAMS departments and colleges, including education,
clinical care, research, and administration.

SCOPE

This policy applies to any work-related content created through an affiliation with UAMS or
on behalf of UAMS, by any paid or unpaid employees, faculty, academic visitors, and
trainees (including students, residents, postdoctoral fellows, etc.). Work-related content
(herein referred to as “work”) is defined as writing, images, or other creative products
presented for peer review, grading as an assignment, academic credit, patents and other
intellectual property, presentations, publications, and/or the media. The policy includes
guidance on if, how, and when artificial intelligence generative tools can be utilized and any
attribution or other information that is required when it is used. Protected Health Information
(PHI) may only be inputted and processed by an AI service if fully approved by UAMS and
a fully executed Business Associate Agreement (BAA). Refer to UAMS Policy 2.1.23
Safeguarding Protected Health Information. This policy applies across all UAMS
departments and missions, including education, clinical care, and research.

DEFINITIONS

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the ability of computer algorithms to perform tasks that
typically require human intelligence. Some types of AI perform mechanical skills
(punctuation, grammar, formatting, language translation, visual scanning, voice recognition
software, etc.). Other types of AI are generative.

Generative AI (GAI) is a subset of AI that leverages computers to mimic the problem-
solving and decision-making capabilities of the human mind. Some GAI tools synthesize data
and make recommendations (e.g., Clinical Decision Tools). Some GAI tools generate entire
written, audio, synthetic data, or visual works from a prompt. These GAI tools can make it
quite easy for an individual to enter a prompt (a question, a string of words, a description, an
instruction) and for the tool to generate a largely clear and coherent work (essay, short story,
poem, annotated bibliography, code, image, etc.) based on all of the data accessible to the
GAI software.

Work is defined as UAMS-related content such as writing, images, or other creative work
presented for peer review, grading as an assignment, or for public and/or scholarly
dissemination through publications, presentations, and the media.

POLICY

I. Contextual Information Regarding GAI Tools

a. GALis a rapidly evolving field that has many benefits, applications, and
implications for various disciplines. There are rapid advances in technology
that might be used by faculty, employees, trainees, and academic visitors,
among others. The use of GAI tools, including large language models
(LLMs), to help write clinical notes, manuscripts, student essays,
presentations, and research articles are noteworthy examples.

b. Using Al and GAI tools is becoming an important skill for diverse health
professions.

c. UAMS considers the presentation of Al-generated work as one’s own work
without appropriate attribution/citation to be plagiarism and is a violation of the
UAMS Basic Code of Conduct policy, the UAMS Responsible Conduct of
Research policy, the UAMS Student Code of Conduct, UAMS Core Values
(integrity) and each College’s Policies for trainees on Academic Misconduct or
Academic Integrity. Students should refer to each College’s policies related to
Academic Misconduct or Academic Integrity for the appropriate grievance
policy and process.

d. Like any tool, GAI tools can be appropriately or inappropriately used in the work
context and this policy is designed to clarify the use of these tools at UAMS.

e. Planned incorporation of AI tools in UAMS environments of education, clinical
care, and research should be submitted to the appropriate review process to
ensure compliance with Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act
(HIPAA), Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA), and other
compliance standards and safe use within UAMS Information Technology (IT)
systems infrastructure (details below). AI or GAI tools that are unsafe or
inappropriate will be blocked on UAMS computers and networks. A list of
approved AI detection tools and GAI tools approved for faculty, student, and/or
staff use in clinical, research, and/or educational contexts (and any limitations to
their use) can be obtained from the UAMS AI Governance Committee.

II. Legal and Ethical Considerations Across Contexts
a. GAI may produce inappropriately discriminatory output. GAI can inadvertently
(or intentionally) perpetuate existing biases present in the data it is trained on.
There are several reasons why GAI systems can perpetuate inappropriate
discrimination:
i. Quality of prompts: The quality of GAI output directly correlates
with the quality of input. To obtain high-quality output, master
“prompt engineering” by refining prompts, and make sure prompts do
not unintentionally introduce bias into the outcome.

ii. Bias in the training data: GAI models have built-in biases (1.e., they
are trained on limited underlying sources; they reproduce, rather than
challenge, errors in the sources). If the training data contains biases, the
GAI system may learn and replicate those biases in its decision-
making.

iii. Lack of diversity in the training data: If the training data does not
include a diverse range of examples, the GAI system may not perform
well on diverse inputs, which may lead to discrimination.

iv. Lack of critical thinking: GAI tools lack critical thinking to evaluate
and reflect on criteria; they lack abductive reasoning to make
judgments with incomplete information at hand.

v. Lack of transparency: Some GAI systems can be difficult to
understand and interpret, making it challenging to detect and
correct for biases.

vi. Lack of accountability: Without proper oversight and
accountability, it can be difficult to identify and address
inappropriate discrimination in GAI systems.

b. It is important to keep in mind that these biases can be unconscious,
unintended, and hard to detect, but they can have serious consequences if they
are not identified and addressed.

c. Use good judgement to determine if/where/when to use these tools. They
do not always make products easier and/or better.

d. Be aware that the texts and images produced by GAI prompts are currently not
protected by
U.S. copyright law. This means that products created from the use of GAI are
publicly available to anyone. Use of Al-based tools must not violate any
copyright or intellectual
property laws (please refer to UAMS Distribution of Royalties from Inventions
policy; U of A Board Patent and Copyright Policy; and U of A Board
Copyright and Distance Learning Policy).

e. Users are responsible for ensuring GAI outputs do not infringe third-party
rights; indemnification risk lies with the requester absent a vendor contractual
defense.

IV. The Use of AI and GAT in Research
a. No GAI tool is a substitute for human judgement. Researchers and students

involved in research must evaluate the accuracy and appropriateness of
recommendations made by GAI tools and make the final determination.

b.

Investigators must clearly disclose in consent forms any use of generative AI
tools when the outputs could potentially re-identify research participants.

Any use of external generative AI tools involving study data requires prior
Institutional Review Board (IRB) review and approval, when applicable.

AI tools must be assessed by Investigators for potential bias, especially in
datasets involving human subjects. Investigators must maintain a record of AI
tool versions, prompts, outputs, and decision rationales in the research file. The
UAMS Institutional Review Board should establish procedures for periodic
review of AI use in active studies to ensure compliance with evolving standards
and regulations.

Inputting identifiable participant data into public or non-secure AI tools is
prohibited. Any use of AI tools must be reviewed and approved by the
Chancellor’s AI Governance Committee.

V. The Use of AI and GAIT in the Education/Teaching Contexts

a.

Use of GAI by students to satisfy course requirements must be authorized

by the course faculty, trainer, or education supervisor. Use of such
applications of GAI must be specifically allowed by the instructor/mentor

for the given course or activity. Faculty should make a clear, conscious

effort to describe the expectations for a given course/activity with respect

to whether GAI is prohibited (i.e., not allowed in any capacity), restricted

(i.e., allowed for specific projects/activities with appropriate citation), or
unrestricted (i.e., allowed in any course activity with appropriate

citation).

No GAI tool is a substitute for human judgement. Teachers and/or students

must evaluate the accuracy and appropriateness of recommendations made by

GAI tools and make the final determination.

GAI tools can be useful assistive devices for learning. Trainees should use GAI
tools wisely and intelligently, aiming to deepen understanding of subject matter
and to support learning. Unless otherwise directed or specified by an instructor
or mentor, helpful ways to use GAI include analysis, rephrasing, essentializing,
synthesizing, and/or gathering information about the typical understanding of a
topic to assist with learning. However, it must be the trainee who guides,
verifies, and crafts the final answers on class assignments.

Violating this policy or the instructor’s directions about the use of GAI in their
course or on an assignment might be considered a violation of the College’s
Academic Misconduct Policy and/or Academic Integrity Policy and could
include disciplinary action up to and including dismissal.

Instructors may have additional requirements regarding citation or
acknowledgement of the use of GAI in assignments.

In accordance with the Family Educational Rights and Privacy

Act (FERPA), instructors may not input any student-identifiable

information or student work into GAI, plagiarism checking tools,

or Al-detection software without explicit written permission from

the student unless approved for use by UAMS. This includes

assignments, written responses, grades, or any material submitted

for evaluation or feedback. It is important to note that AI

VI. ‘The Use of AI and GAT in Clinical Contexts

a. AT has enhanced computerized clinical decision support (CDS) tools. AI-CDS
tools were created to alleviate the burden of data overload, enhance clinicians’
decision- making capabilities, and standardize care delivery processes. These
tools can offer diagnostic, treatment, and/or prognostic recommendations.
AI-CDS tools cannot be used to automatically interpret clinical results, integrate
findings, or conceptualize cases. No GAI tool is a substitute for clinical
judgement of healthcare providers and most AI-CDS tools make
recommendations to clinicians for their consideration. All clinicians must
evaluate the accuracy and appropriateness of recommendations made by AI-CDS
tools and make the final determination.

b. A few AI-CDS tools have been automated for use without clinical judgement
(.e., visual scanning detection software, chart summarization tools). This should
only occur when the literature regarding the use of that AI-CDS tool suggests
that it is safe to do so with approval from the Director of the Institute on Digital
Health and Innovation and the Chancellor’s Al Governance Committee. There
are also GAI tools that serve more mechanical functions that can be utilized (i.e.,
dictation software, language translation, spelling/grammar/punctuation,
formatting, alphabetizing items, formatting of citations, electronic health record
tools, etc.). Seek information the Director of the Institute on Digital Health and
Innovation or the Chancellor’s AI Governance Committee if there are any
questions about the use of a specific AI-CDS tool. Refer to UAMS policy 2.1.23
Safeguarding Protected Health Information for additional guidance on approval for
GAI tool use in a clinical context. Approval for tool use must also be required by
the Chancellor’s Al Governance Committee.

c. When writing and entering queries into any GAI model outside of an
electronic health record, PHI may only be inputted and processed by an AI
or GAI tool if fully approved by UAMS AI Governance Committee and only
when a fully executed BAA exists, compliant with UAMS Policy 2.1.23
Safeguarding Protected Health Information. Clinicians must disclose
whether patient consent was obtained before the use AI or GAI technology
(if applicable).
