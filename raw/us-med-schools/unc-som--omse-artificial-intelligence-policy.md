PURPOSE AND SCOPE
This policy defines and covers integration of Artificial Intelligence (AI) into the MD curriculum and
delineates acceptable uses of AI technologies in the SOM MD curriculum.

This policy reviews responsible integration of AI into the MD curriculum and applies to student use of AI
in the medical education program across academic and clinical learning environments. It applies to
coursework, independent study, formative learning activities, and assessments unless a course,
clerkship, or assignment states otherwise. Course and clerkship directors may establish alternative
expectations for specific learning activities or assessments. UNC guidance recommends that instructors
state their AI expectations clearly and align them with course learning goals.

RESPONSIBLE PARTY AND REVIEW CYCLE
The Education Committee will review and approve the policy in even-numbered years.

DEFINITION(S)

        •   Machine learning: systems that learn patterns from data, like spam filters.
        •   Natural language processing (NLP): tools that understand or generate text, like chatbots.
        •   Computer vision: systems that interpret images or video, like facial recognition.
        •   Speech recognition: converts spoken language into text, like voice assistants.
        •   Generative AI: creates new content, like text, images, or code.
        •   HIPAA-compliant: The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act protects the
            privacy and security of patients’ health information and limits how that information can be
            used or shared.
        •   FERPA-protected records: The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act protects education
            records that contain information directly related to a student such as grades, class
            schedules, and advising notes.
        •   Protected health information (PHI): any individually identifiable health information such as
            a patient’s name linked to a diagnosis, test results, medical record number, date of birth or
            address when connected to health information.
        •   Personally identifiable information (PII): information that can be used to identify, contact,
            or trace a person such as name, date and place of birth, address, email address, or biometric
            data.

POLICY

Artificial intelligence (AI) technologies (including but not limited to machine learning (ML), natural
language processing (NLP), computer vision, speech recognition, generative AI) may support learning
when used responsibly, transparently, and in ways that strengthen, not replace, student thinking, clinical
reasoning, and professional development. Consistent with UNC guidance, students remain responsible
for the accuracy, originality, and integrity of all the work they submit. AI should assist your learning, not
think for you.

Unless explicitly prohibited by the course director, students may use approved AI tools to support
learning in limited ways, such as:

         •   reviewing and reinforcing concepts, differential diagnoses, and treatment guidelines;
         •   exploring clinical reasoning and decision-making frameworks;
         •   preparing for examinations and shelf exams;
         •   generating study aids or practice questions for personal use; and
         •   brainstorming ideas.

Students must independently verify AI-generated information before relying on it.
Boundaries and Restrictions on AI Use
Limits on AI use are necessary to ensure students develop and demonstrate their own clinical reasoning,
knowledge, and professional judgment. Because AI outputs may be inaccurate, biased, or incomplete, AI
should support learning, not replace independent thinking, clinical decision-making, or accountability.

         •   Assessments and assignments (MK 1–5; PC 5–9; PBL 3–5; IC 2–4; PR 2): AI use is prohibited
             to complete assessments, assignments, and reflections intended to evaluate the student’s
             individual knowledge, judgment, or clinical reasoning unless explicitly authorized.

             AI use for routine spelling, grammar, punctuation, and formatting are permitted as long as it
             does not alter the content of the student’s original work and does not require disclosure
             unless otherwise specified.
             Use of citation-management assistance with AI should be disclosed.

         •   Clinical documentation and patient-related work (PC 1–9; IC 2–4; PR 1–2; SBP 4): AI use for
             clinical documentation must follow clinical-site policies and privacy requirements. Unless
             supported by approved institutional systems or explicitly permitted by the course, clerkship,
             clinical site, or supervisor, students may not use AI to generate, substantially modify, or
             fabricate H&Ps, patient notes, assessment and plans, or case presentations. These activities
             demonstrate data gathering, synthesis, communication, and clinical reasoning, not merely
             written documentation.
         •   Patient confidentiality and sensitive information (PR 1–2; PC 8; SBP 4): Students may not
             enter protected health information (PHI) into any AI system unless they have been explicitly
             informed by the institution that the system is HIPAA-compliant and approved for the use of
             PHI. PHI, FERPA-protected records, personally identifiable information (PII), and other

These restrictions are consistent with UNC guidance to align AI use with learning goals and with
institutional expectations regarding responsible use of AI and sensitive data.
Clinical Use
Students must not rely on AI-generated output as a substitute for clinical judgment. Institutional policies
at clinical sites may impose additional restrictions or limitations on AI use, which must be followed.
Disclosure and responsibility
AI should not be used in any assignment unless explicitly permitted. When AI use is allowed, students
must disclose that use in the manner required by the course director and remain fully responsible for:

        •      the accuracy of content
        •      appropriate citation or acknowledgment
        •      compliance with course expectations
        •      academic integrity and professionalism

Violations
Improper AI use may constitute a violation of course policy, professionalism standards, and/or academic
integrity expectations and may result in assignment failure, loss of professionalism points, course failure,
referral to the Student Progress Committee, and/or referral through university student conduct
processes.
Approved tools and updates
Students should use only UNC-approved AI tools for any permitted academic or clinical purpose.
Because AI technologies and institutional guidance are evolving rapidly, this policy is subject to periodic
review and revision.
