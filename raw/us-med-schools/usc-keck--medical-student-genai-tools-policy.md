Policy Statement:

As generative AI tools become more accessible, the Keck School supports the responsible use of such technologies to enhance learning and patient care. Understanding when to use, and when not to use these technologies in both the clinical and non-clinical settings is essential to safeguarding patient care and confidentiality, protecting academic integrity, and ensuring an optimal educational experience. Inappropriate or unauthorized use of generative AI tools is considered a violation of the Keck School Code of Professional Behavior.

Pre-Clerkship, Clerkship and Post-Clerkship Process:

The use of generative AI tools is prohibited for assignments, activities, and other required coursework that is intended to evaluate a student’s own knowledge, reasoning, skills or reflections unless explicitly permitted by the course director or supervising faculty.

When use of generative AI is permitted, it should be used as a tool, and not as a replacement for student reasoning or reflection. Students are responsible for verifying the accuracy, bias, and appropriateness of all submitted content. When generative AI is used, it must be appropriately cited in the student’s work.

The use of generative AI in clinical settings must comply with all policies and procedures as outlined by the applicable health system and clinical setting. Students cannot use generative AI tools for documentation of patient care, and it is never permissible to enter patient protected health information into a generative AI tool unless explicitly approved and sanctioned by the applicable health system and clinical setting.

The uploading of curricular materials to AI must comply with all policies, guidelines, and procedures in place as outlined by USC and Keck School IT. Students cannot use generative AI tools for the upload of curricular materials unless explicitly approved by the instructor. When allowable, it is the student’s responsibility to ensure that the use of these tools does not violate copyright or intellectual property laws.

When submitting scholarly work for publication or presentation, students must comply with the generative AI policies set forth by the relevant journals and organizations and must disclose how generative AI was used in their scholarship.
