Policy 645.00 | Use of Artificial Intelligence in Medical Education

Policy Statement

Purpose

The purpose of this policy is to outline the responsible use of generative Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools in Medical Education by students, faculty, and staff at the UVM Larner College of Medicine. This policy aims to ensure that AI tools are used ethically, transparently, and in ways that enhance the educational experience while maintaining academic integrity and protecting individual rights.

Persons Affected
Medical students
Faculty
Staff
Patients

Policy Elaboration
1. Definitions
Artificial Intelligence (AI): The use of machine learning models and/or algorithms trained or developed with pre-existing known and unknown sources of data to perform tasks with some degree of autonomy from human input
Traditional AI: A subset of AI used to analyze and interpret existing data to improve efficiency, accuracy, and decision-making within predefined boundaries
Generative AI: A subset of AI that utilizes models for the creation of novel text, image, or audio-based content using various known and unknown sources of training data as input.
Open System: a system where input data is accessible outside of UVM and provides no meaningful privacy protections.
Closed System: A model or data storage system where any input data is not used in training or made accessible to any non-authorized user or agent,
Closed Compliant System: A Closed System that complies with privacy laws and regulations including but not limited to FERPA and HIPPA.
UVM Instance: AI systems generally have both closed and open versions. A UVM instance means that the system has been reviewed, approved and its use sanctioned by COMTS or UVM ETS

2. Policy and Procedure Statement
General Principles:
Human-Centered Focus: AI tools should support and enhance human learning and decision-making, not replace these. Human judgment must remain central in all applications of AI.
Equity and Ethics: AI use must promote equity and adhere to ethical standards, avoiding biases and discrimination. Use of AI in medical education should align with principles of academic integrity and honesty.
Transparency and Accountability: AI operations must be transparent; systems and individual users must be accountable for their decisions.
Data Privacy and Security: If FERPA or HIPPA data will be input, only use Closed Compliant System
Continuous Learning and Adaptation: Ongoing education and adaptation to new AI technologies and methodologies are essential.
Collaboration and Stakeholder Engagement: Diverse stakeholders, including educators, students, and the community, must be involved in AI policy development.
Responsible Use: Users of AI systems are ultimately responsible for assuring the system's security, effectiveness, fairness, and accuracy.

Evidence-based Learning: AI tools for education should facilitate or utilize evidence-based learning methods

Specific Guidelines

Student use in Medical Education
Students must author their own work and cannot use generative AI tools to complete graded items (including but not limited to readiness assurance tests and summative exams) unless explicitly permitted by faculty.
Use of AI in graded activities without permission and/or citation is against the Code of Academic Integrity and will be responded to in accordance with the Enforcement of University and Larner Behavioral Standards Policy.
In any assignment, students must disclose their use of generative AI tools in completion of assignments including reflections and case reports, specifying tools used. Failure to do so is against the Code of Academic Integrity and will be responded to in accordance with the Enforcement of University and Larner Behavioral Standards Policy.
Use of AI in developing self-study and self-assessment tools distinct from graded assessments is permitted

Student is accountable for the accuracy and integrity of AI generated information that they use for assignments, self-study, presentation.

Faculty use in Medical Education
Faculty must disclose their use of generative AI tools when developing educational materials and student assignments, fostering a culture of transparency and setting an example for students by openly sharing information about the data used for training, the challenges encountered, and the evaluation methods employed.

Faculty must disclose if AI products are used for analysis of student performance (e.g. OSCE or note review and feedback). Faculty are ultimately responsible for ensuring the accuracy of the results generated.

Faculty and Student use in Clinical Training Environments:
Allowable AI use in clinical environments is primarily dictated by policies of the hosting facility
Protected patient health information may only be uploaded into HIPPAA compliant applications that are supported by the hosting facility
AI-generated clinical documentation outside of supported EHR systems is prohibited.

When used, patient-specific health information must always be completely and rigorously de-identified prior to use as input in a generative AI tool

Faculty and Student use of AI with Curricular Materials:
Faculty-created educational materials may be uploaded to closed systems OR to third party sites only with explicit approval by the faculty (see "Data Privacy")
Content that is specifically commissioned and funded by the University requires institutional approval prior to uploading/posting to third party sites

Users may upload third-party content if permitted under the applicable data use agreement or other agreement.

Assignments and Publication:
Students must follow faculty guidance when using AI technologies for specific assignments.
When submitting scholarly work for publication or presentation, AI use must be reviewed and approved by the faculty advisor, adhere to requirements for individual publication or venue and must be disclosed. Disclosure must include the specific AI tool and how it was used.

Use of student/learner data

Data generated from student activities including, but not limited to, student performance evaluations, student assessments, course evaluations, narrative assignments, demographic information may only be entered into secure COMTS approved closed systems (see "Data Privacy").

Bias Mitigation:

Users of AI systems should regularly assess output for evidence of bias

Human Oversight:
Decisions influenced by AI must be subject to human review.

Human reviewers must have the authority to override AI-generated outcomes if necessary.

Data Privacy:
Patient, student, or curricular data may only be input to Closed Compliant Systems.
Unless the AI system is a pre-approved, UVM instance, the determination on whether a system is closed must be made by COMTS. Prior authorization is required before using AI.
Users are solely responsible for verifying whether the version of the AI system is open or closed and must never enter NPPD (PHI, PII, and other data as defined in UVM's Privacy Policy) into an Open System.
Inputting NPPD into an open system is considered a breach of data security laws, such as HIPAA and the Vermont Data Breach Notification law, both of which require notification to government oversight agencies and to the individual.
Personal data must be collected minimally and securely deleted when no longer needed and in accordance with UVM's Records Management and Retention Policy.

3. Best practices

Training in the use of generative AI in the educational environment is encouraged before implementation. This training/education should involve:

Basic knowledge of how AI works
A basic understanding of how generative AI including large language models (LLM) work is important to ensure responsible usage. At the very least, the knowledge that a model's output might look truthful but not necessarily be so.
Examples of basic concepts learners/users should understand and be able to explain in their own words:
Model
Types of models (deep learning, generative AI, LLMs, etc.)
Agent/Instance
Dataset
Training
Input/output
Supervised/Unsupervised Learning
Model evaluation metrics (accuracy, benchmarks, AUC, etc.)
Hallucination

Awareness of caveats and pitfalls
Understanding that AI models are fallible tools and that each model will have specific biases, caveats and pitfalls.

Recognition that AI tools for education should fit into evidence-based learning methods
AI learning tools should align with current research on how we learn. Science-based learning means adhering to the latest scientific evidence regarding the most effective methodologies for learning, rather than relying on habits, opinions or preferences. AI tools should utilize techniques that have been scientifically proven to be effective for learning.
Examples of science-based learning, among others:
Retrieval practice (recall)
Interleaving
Spaced practice (spaced repetition)
Elaborative interrogation
Concrete examples
